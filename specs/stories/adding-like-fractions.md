# `adding-like-fractions` Adding fractions with the same denominator

- Level: beginner, building on the first ideas
- Needs: `unit-fractions`
- Leads to: no beginner story lists this one as a need. The end page suggests `mixed-numbers` and
  `equivalent-fractions`.
- Sections: adding counts of one size of part, the bottom stays the same, sums that fill one whole, sums past one
  whole
- In the world: two ribbons tied end to end, topping up a fuel tank
- Figure: two bars cut into the same number of parts, which the reader shades by tapping, and a sum bar below them
  that collects both and runs on into a second whole.
- Engine: `parts` `add` on its `join` stage with its `total` measure. The scene needs a new option that keeps the
  second fraction's bottom equal to the first's, with one slider for both, and the `sum` measure stays unused because
  it gives the simplest form. The challenges need a new goal `like-sum` that draws a bottom and a total, since the
  existing `sum` goal asks for different bottoms.

## Challenges

### Make the sum with two fractions
- Goal: both bars have at least one part shaded and the total equals the drawn fraction.
- Drawn each time: a bottom from 3 to 10 and a top from 2 to one less than the bottom. The bars are cut into the
  drawn bottom and start empty, so the page opens unsolved.
- Reached by: tapping parts of both bars.
- Hidden: the total readout and the label of the sum bar.
- Idea used: adding counts of one size of part.

### Fill the whole with the second fraction
- Goal: the total is one whole.
- Drawn each time: a bottom from 3 to 12 and a first fraction with a top from 1 to one less than the bottom, fixed for
  the round. The second bar starts empty, so the total is less than one whole.
- Reached by: tapping parts of the second bar.
- Hidden: the total readout, the label of the sum bar and the label of the second bar.
- Idea used: sums that fill one whole.

### Make a sum past one whole
- Goal: both bars have the drawn bottom, both have at least one part shaded, and the total equals the drawn fraction.
- Drawn each time: a bottom from 2 to 8 and a top from one more than the bottom to one less than twice the bottom. The
  bars start cut into a bottom that differs from the drawn one, with nothing shaded, so the page opens unsolved.
- Reached by: the slider for both bottoms and tapping parts.
- Hidden: the total readout and the labels of all three bars.
- Idea used: the bottom stays the same, sums past one whole.
