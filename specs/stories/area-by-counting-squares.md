# `area-by-counting-squares` Area by counting squares

- Level: beginner, building on the first ideas
- Needs: `equal-groups`, `centimetres-and-metres`
- Leads to: `area-of-rectangles`
- Sections: covering a surface, unit squares, counting in rows, square centimetres and square metres, the same area
  in a new shape
- In the world: a patchwork quilt sewn from square patches, rooms compared on a floor plan drawn on squared paper
- Figure: a square grid with a shape on it. The reader drags or taps square tiles onto the shape until it is
  covered, and the page counts the tiles.
- Engine: `geo` new scene `tiles`, shared with `perimeter`. It needs a tray of square tiles the reader drags onto
  the grid, a tile size of 1 or 2 cells, a target outline the tiles must cover, a highlight for a row the reader
  taps, a count of tiles in rows (3 rows of 5) and a unit label of squares, cm² or m².

## Challenges

### Build a shape with an area of 9 squares
- Goal: the shape is one piece, fits inside the frame and covers the drawn number of squares. The 9 in the title
  is drawn.
- Drawn each time: a number of squares from 5 to 20 and a frame of 3 to 6 cells by 3 to 6 cells that holds at least
  that many. The grid starts empty.
- Reached by: tapping cells on and off.
- Hidden: the count of squares.
- Idea used: unit squares.

### Make a rectangle with the same area as the shape
- Goal: the rectangle covers as many squares as the shape.
- Drawn each time: a shape of whole squares that is one piece, with an area from 6 to 24 that has a rectangle of
  sides from 2 to 8 squares, so a rectangle of one row never answers it. The rectangle's corner reaches 8 squares
  each way, and the rectangle starts with a different area.
- Reached by: counting the shape's squares by tapping them, dragging the rectangle's corner along the grid.
- Hidden: the count of the shape and the count of the rectangle.
- Idea used: counting in rows, the same area in a new shape.

### Make the patio 3 m² bigger than the lawn
- Goal: the patio is one piece and its area is the lawn's area plus the drawn number. The 3 in the title is
  drawn.
- Drawn each time: a lawn of whole square metres that is one piece, with an area from 4 to 15 m², and a difference
  from 1 to 6 m². The patio's space on the plan holds more squares than the answer needs. The patio starts empty.
- Reached by: tapping cells to lay and lift patio squares.
- Hidden: the areas of the lawn and of the patio.
- Idea used: square centimetres and square metres, counting in rows, covering a surface.
