# `fact-families` Fact families

- Level: beginner, first ideas
- Needs: `adding-within-20`
- Leads to: `missing-number`, `column-methods`
- Sections: three numbers make four facts, one operation undoes the other, a take away hides a missing part, a bar
  holds the family
- In the world: players on the pitch and on the bench, bikes and free places in a bike rack
- Figure: two ten frames with counters in two colours, and the four facts of the family written beneath. The
  reader drags counters, taps a fact to light the counters it speaks of, and on later pages drags the split of a
  part and whole bar.
- Engine: the `frames` and `jumps` scenes that `adding-within-20` asks for, with a readout that writes the four
  facts of the two groups. A new scene `bar` draws a whole bar split into two parts, with a draggable split and
  a draggable end, and labels on the whole and each part. `missing-number` and `word-problems` use the same scene.

## Challenges

### Find the missing part
- Goal: the red and blue counters together make the whole.
- Drawn each time: a whole from 11 to 19 and a red part from 3 to 9, so the missing part is from 2 to 16 and fits in
  the free places. The page opens with no blue counters.
- Reached by: dragging blue counters in and out, or the blue stepper.
- Hidden: the total under the frames and the facts.
- Idea used: a take away hides a missing part.

### Set the bar for the fact
- Goal: the bar's whole and parts are the three numbers of the drawn fact's family.
- Drawn each time: one fact of a family with a whole from 11 to 20 and two parts from 2 to 18, shown as a sum or a
  take away at random. The bar opens with a whole of 5 and no split, which matches no drawn family.
- Reached by: dragging the end of the bar from 1 to 20 and the split along it.
- Hidden: the four facts under the bar.
- Idea used: three numbers make four facts, a bar holds the family.

### Find a family that holds both numbers
- Goal: the bar's whole and two parts include both drawn numbers.
- Drawn each time: two different numbers from 2 to 18. The larger as the whole with the smaller as a part always
  works, and a pair whose sum is 20 or less also works as two parts. The bar opens with a whole of 1.
- Reached by: dragging the end of the bar from 1 to 20 and the split along it.
- Hidden: the facts under the bar.
- Idea used: three numbers make four facts, one operation undoes the other, a bar holds the family.
