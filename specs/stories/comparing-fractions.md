# `comparing-fractions` Comparing fractions

- Level: beginner, building on the first ideas
- Needs: `equivalent-fractions`
- Leads to: `pie-charts`
- Sections: the same bottom, the same top, against one half, common bottoms
- In the world: how full two cinema screens of different sizes are, which of two teams won a larger share of its games
- Figure: two bars of the same length above one number line, which the reader sets by tapping, with a button that
  cuts both bars into the same parts and the sign between them.
- Engine: `parts` `compare` with its `same` button, its `sign` measure and its `common` measures, and `parts`
  `between` for the second challenge. The scene needs a new option that marks one half on the bars and the line. The
  challenges need a new goal `larger` for two bars, and the `between` goal with targets drawn when the page opens.

## Challenges

### Set a larger fraction with the same bottom
- Goal: the second fraction is larger than the first.
- Drawn each time: a bottom from 3 to 12 and a first top from 1 to two less than the bottom. The second bar has the
  same bottom, which the reader cannot change, and starts with fewer shaded parts than the first.
- Reached by: tapping parts of the second bar.
- Hidden: the sign between the bars and the number line marks.
- Idea used: the same bottom.

### Set a fraction between this one and one half
- Goal: the reader's fraction lies strictly between the drawn fraction and one half.
- Drawn each time: a fraction with a bottom from 3 to 8 and a top from 1 to one less than the bottom that differs
  from one half, chosen so that some fraction with a bottom up to 12 lies between it and one half. The parts slider
  runs from 2 to 12. The bar starts at 0, which lies outside the gap.
- Reached by: the parts slider and tapping parts.
- Hidden: the name of the reader's fraction.
- Idea used: against one half, common bottoms.

### Beat this fraction with a smaller top
- Goal: the second fraction is larger than the first, less than one whole, and its top is smaller than the first's top.
- Drawn each time: a first fraction with a top from 3 to 6 and a bottom from one more than the top to 12, fixed for
  the round. The fraction stays below 2/3 for a top of 3, below 3/4 for a top of 4, below 4/5 for a top of 5 and
  below 5/6 for a top of 6, so a fraction with a smaller top can beat it. The second bar's slider runs from 2 to 12.
  The second bar starts with the first's bottom and nothing shaded, so the page opens unsolved.
- Reached by: the second bar's bottom slider and tapping parts.
- Hidden: the sign between the bars and the names of both fractions.
- Idea used: the same top, against one half, common bottoms.
