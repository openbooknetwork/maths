# `angles-add-up` Angles add up

- Level: beginner, building on the first ideas
- Needs: `angles`, `adding-tens-and-ones`
- Leads to: nothing at this level. The intermediate story `angles-in-polygons` follows it.
- Sections: angles side by side, angles on a straight line, angles round a point, two lines that cross
- In the world: the wings of a revolving door, a see-saw tilting on its post
- Figure: rays from one point with the angle between each pair of neighbours marked in degrees. The reader drags
  the rays round the point. On pages that find a missing angle, the reader sets a number with a stepper and a ghost
  ray appears at that angle to show whether it lands on the real ray.
- Engine: new scene `rays` in `geo`, a sibling of `angle` that shares its protractor. It draws two to five rays
  from one point, an optional fixed straight line or right angle frame through it, and the angle between each
  pair of neighbouring rays, each shown in degrees, as a question mark or not at all. A stepper sets a ghost ray at
  a chosen angle from a named ray. A page can let the protractor fit only some of the angles, so a hidden angle has
  to be worked out from the others.

## Challenges

### Make the hidden angle on the line
- Goal: the angle the card names has the size on the card, within 2°.
- Drawn each time: a size from 15° to 165° in steps of 5°, which of the two angles the card names, and a start
  for the ray at least 20° from the answer. The protractor fits only the other angle, so the reader sets it to
  180° take away the size on the card.
- Reached by: laying the protractor on the angle it fits, dragging the ray.
- Hidden: both degree labels and the total.
- Idea used: angles on a straight line.

### Make the hidden angle round a point
- Goal: the angle the card names, between the free ray and one fixed ray, has the size on the card, within 2°.
- Drawn each time: the angle between the two fixed rays, from 40° to 200° in steps of 10°, shown in degrees, and a
  size for the named angle of at least 30° in steps of 5° that leaves the third angle at least 30°. The
  protractor fits only the third angle, which lies between the free ray and the other fixed ray, and is less than
  180°. The free ray starts at least 20° from the answer.
- Reached by: laying the protractor on the third angle, dragging the free ray.
- Hidden: the labels of the named angle, the third angle and the total.
- Idea used: angles round a point, angles side by side.

### Turn the line to make the named angle
- Goal: the angle the card names, one of the four at the crossing, has the size on the card, within 2°.
- Drawn each time: a size from 20° to 160° in steps of 5°, the angle the card names, and the angle the protractor
  fits, which is either opposite it or beside it. The free line starts at least 20° from the answer. Across many
  draws about half need the opposite angle and half need 180° take away.
- Reached by: laying the protractor on the angle it fits, turning the free line.
- Hidden: every degree label at the crossing.
- Idea used: angles on a straight line, two lines that cross.
