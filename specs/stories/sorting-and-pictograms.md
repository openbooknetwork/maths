# `sorting-and-pictograms` Sorting, tallies and pictograms

- Level: beginner, first ideas
- Needs: `counting`, `skip-counting`
- Leads to: `bar-charts`
- Sections: sorting into groups, tally marks, a table of counts, a pictogram, a picture that stands for two
- In the world: a lost property box, birds at a garden feeder, vehicles passing on a street
- Figure: a set of objects the reader drags into labelled trays, with the same counts shown beside it as tally
  marks, a table or rows of pictures. The reader drags objects, taps to add or remove a mark or a picture, and
  switches the sorting rule or the key.
- Engine: new family `chart`, which every data story shares. This story needs its scenes `sort` (objects dragged
  into trays by a rule, each tray counting what it holds), `tally` (rows of marks that close into gates of five),
  `table` (counts in rows that follow the trays or the tallies) and `pictogram` (rows of equal pictures with a key
  of 1 or 2, where a half picture stands for one).

## Challenges

### Make the tally match the pile
- Goal: every tally row has as many marks as its tray holds things.
- Drawn each time: three or four kinds of object from the story's pictures, with 3 to 14 objects of each kind
  placed in sorted trays. The tally rows start empty, so the page never opens solved.
- Reached by: the plus and minus buttons on each tally row.
- Hidden: the count on each tray and the number beside each tally row.
- Idea used: tally marks.

### Draw the table as a pictogram
- Goal: every row of the pictogram stands for the number its table row shows, with a key of 2.
- Drawn each time: three or four kinds, each with a count from 1 to 16, with at least one odd count so a half
  picture is needed. The pictogram starts empty.
- Reached by: tapping a row to add a whole picture or a half picture, and tapping a picture to take it away.
- Hidden: the count each pictogram row stands for.
- Idea used: a pictogram, a picture that stands for two.

### Sort the box and draw its pictogram
- Goal: every thing sits in the tray its rule names, and the pictogram with a key of 2 shows each tray's count.
- Drawn each time: a heap of 12 to 20 things of three or four kinds and colours, and a rule, by kind or by colour.
  The draw keeps every group at one or more and at least one count odd. The trays and the pictogram start empty.
- Reached by: dragging things into trays, then tapping whole and half pictures into the pictogram.
- Hidden: the tray counts and the count each pictogram row stands for.
- Idea used: sorting into groups, a pictogram, a picture that stands for two.
