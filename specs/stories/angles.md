# `angles` Angles

- Level: beginner, building on the first ideas
- Needs: `turns-and-right-angles`, `hundreds-and-thousands`
- Leads to: `angles-add-up`, `triangles`, `quadrilaterals-and-polygons`
- Sections: an angle is an amount of turn, acute and obtuse, reflex, degrees, the protractor
- In the world: how far a laptop screen opens, leaning a ladder against a wall
- Figure: two arms that meet at a corner, called the vertex. The reader drags the end of one arm round, and on later
  pages drags and turns a protractor onto the angle. The panel names the angle and, where the page allows, gives
  its size in degrees.
- Engine: new scene `angle` in `geo`. It draws two arms from a vertex with draggable ends, shades the angle inside
  or the reflex angle outside, names it (acute, right, obtuse, straight, reflex, whole turn), and reads out degrees
  when the page shows them. A protractor with an inner and an outer scale drags and turns over it, and a page can
  set the arms into a picture such as a laptop or a ladder.

## Challenges

### Make an angle of this size
- Goal: the angle between the arms is the size on the card, within 2°.
- Drawn each time: a size from 10° to 170° in steps of 5°, at least 20° from the starting angle, and a direction for
  the fixed arm from any whole number of degrees.
- Reached by: dragging and turning the protractor, dragging the free arm.
- Hidden: the degree readout and the angle's name.
- Idea used: degrees, the protractor.

### Make a reflex angle of this size
- Goal: the reflex angle between the arms is the size on the card, within 2°.
- Drawn each time: a size from 190° to 350° in steps of 5°, a fixed arm in any direction, and a start with the
  arms at an acute or obtuse angle whose reflex angle is at least 20° from the size on the card.
- Reached by: dragging the free arm, laying the protractor on the arm or on the dashed line through the vertex.
- Hidden: the degree readout and the angle's name.
- Idea used: reflex, degrees, the protractor.

### Copy this angle
- Goal: the second angle has the same size as the drawn angle, within 2°, on the shaded side.
- Drawn each time: a drawn angle from 15° to 345° in whole degrees, with its arms in a random direction, and a
  second pair of arms turned a different way that starts at least 30° from the answer. When the drawn angle is
  reflex, the second pair shades its outside.
- Reached by: laying the protractor on the drawn angle, then on the second angle, and dragging the free arm.
- Hidden: every degree readout and both angles' names.
- Idea used: an angle is an amount of turn, reflex, degrees, the protractor.
