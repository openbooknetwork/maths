# `hundreds-and-thousands` Hundreds and thousands

- Level: beginner, building on the first ideas
- Needs: `tens-and-ones`, `more-and-less`
- Leads to: `column-methods`, `ten-times-the-place`, `large-numbers`, `mass`, `capacity`, `angles`
- Sections: ten tens make a hundred, three digit numbers, ten hundreds make a thousand, four digit numbers, a number
  as the sum of its places, comparing numbers
- In the world: setting the code of a bike lock with four wheels, a print shop packing an order of paper
- Figure: the place mat from `tens-and-ones` with hundreds flats and thousand cubes added, and four digit wheels
  under the columns. The reader drags blocks onto the mat, gathers ten of a kind into the next block, breaks a block
  into ten, and turns the wheels.
- Engine: `blocks` from `tens-and-ones`, with flats and big cubes, plus a new scene `wheels` in the same family. The
  wheels scene draws up to seven digit wheels that the reader turns by drag or by tapping above and below, and reads
  out the number, its sum of places and its name in words. Two sets of wheels can sit one above the other for
  comparing.

## Challenges

### Build the number with the blocks
- Goal: the blocks on the mat make the target in its usual form, with at most nine of each block.
- Drawn each time: a target from 1001 to 9999 with at least one digit 0 after the first. The mat starts empty.
- Reached by: dragging cubes, rods, flats and big cubes onto the mat and off it.
- Hidden: the digit wheels and the sum of places.
- Idea used: three digit numbers, four digit numbers.

### Set the wheels to the sum
- Goal: the wheels show the number the sum makes.
- Drawn each time: a number from 1000 to 9999 with at least two digits that are not 0, written as the sum of its non-zero places in a shuffled order that is
  not largest first. The wheels start at 0000.
- Reached by: turning the four wheels.
- Hidden: the wheels' own readout of the sum of places and the name in words.
- Idea used: a number as the sum of its places.

### Make a number between these two
- Goal: the wheels show a number greater than the lower number and less than the upper one.
- Drawn each time: two numbers from 1000 to 9999 that share their thousands digit and are 2 to 300 apart. The wheels
  start on the lower number.
- Reached by: turning the four wheels.
- Hidden: the word greater and the lit deciding place.
- Idea used: four digit numbers, comparing numbers.
