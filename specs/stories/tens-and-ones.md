# `tens-and-ones` Tens and ones

- Level: beginner, first ideas
- Needs: `counting`
- Leads to: `adding-tens-and-ones`, `hundreds-and-thousands`
- Sections: ten ones make a ten, the teen numbers, two digits for tens and ones, the place of a digit, breaking a
  ten
- In the world: a florist tying roses in bunches of ten, a stationery shop selling pencils in packs of ten and
  singly
- Figure: a mat with a tens column and a ones column, holding rods of ten and single cubes, with the digits under
  each column. The reader drags cubes onto the mat, gathers ten loose cubes into a rod, and taps a rod to break it
  into ten cubes.
- Engine: new scene `blocks` in the beginner family that holds `counters`. It draws a place mat of two to four
  columns with base ten blocks, snaps ten pieces of one place into one piece of the next, breaks a piece into ten
  on a tap, and shows the digits, the tens and ones count and the worth of each column.

## Challenges

### Build 58 with tens and ones
- Goal: the blocks on the mat make the target. The 58 in the title is the drawn target.
- Drawn each time: a target from 11 to 99. The mat starts empty.
- Reached by: dragging rods and cubes onto the mat and off it.
- Hidden: the digits under the columns and the worth of each column.
- Idea used: two digits for tens and ones.

### Show the number with this many ones
- Goal: the mat still shows the target, and its ones column holds the drawn number of cubes.
- Drawn each time: a target from 21 to 99 and a number of ones from 10 to 19 that the target allows, which is its
  ones digit plus 10. The mat opens with the target in its usual form, so the ones are fewer than asked.
- Reached by: tapping a rod to break it and dragging ten cubes together to make a rod.
- Hidden: nothing, because the digits do not give the way to the answer.
- Idea used: the place of a digit, breaking a ten.

### Pack the order with the packs on the shelf
- Goal: the counter holds exactly the pencils the order asks for.
- Drawn each time: an order from 21 to 99 and a shelf with 1 to 3 packs fewer than the order's tens and at least
  one pack. The box of single pencils holds enough to make up the rest, and the counter starts empty.
- Reached by: dragging packs and single pencils onto the counter and off it, and tying ten single pencils into a
  pack.
- Hidden: the count of pencils on the counter.
- Idea used: ten ones make a ten, two digits for tens and ones, breaking a ten.
