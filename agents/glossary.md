# Glossary

The terms the stories give their reader. A term is written one way across the book and translated one way per
language. The `terms` agent adds to this file as each story is planned, and a writer, a reviewer and a translator
follow it.

An entry is `term (scope)`: the scope is the kind of thing the term is said of. A meaning is written in words
simpler than the term and leaves the term's own words out. A word with two meanings has two
entries, one per scope, and a page that uses a second sense says what the word means there. An entry stays as it
is written once a story uses it. A change to one is agreed with the user, and then made in every story that uses
the term.

## Rules that tell two words apart

- Fewer is the word for objects, and less is the word for numbers. Before a noun the number that is less is "the
  smaller number", beside "the greater number", and a text does not say "the less number" or "the less".
- Two groups are "the same". Two numbers are "the same" before the story `more-and-less` reaches its section
  `signs`, and "equal" from there on.
- On a number line a mark stands for a number, and a gap lies between two marks. A jump crosses a gap, so the jumps
  from 0 to 5 are five while the marks are six.
- The step is a number, and the gap is a piece of the line.
- "The number at the point" names the number where a point sits, and "the number of" says how many.
- The term for the place halfway between two numbers on a line is "middle".
- The tens either side of a number are "the ten before" and "the ten after". `rounding-and-estimating` says "the ten
  below" and "the ten above" in its rough map, and takes this form.
- Join and take away are said of groups of objects, and add and subtract of numbers.
- The sign − is read "minus". "Take away" is what is done to objects, and a text does not read − as "take away".
- The number an addition gives is the sum. "Altogether" asks for it, and "total" is left out.
- "Left" is what stays after a take away. "Left over" is said of the objects with no partner when two groups are
  paired. "To the left" is a direction on a number line.
- A pair of objects is joined by a pair line, and a pair of numbers makes a given number. "Partner" is said of
  objects.
- A difference of numbers is the difference, and "gap" is said only of a number line.
- "Whole" is said of the number two parts make, and "whole number" is left to the story that introduces it.
- A number sentence is any row of numbers and signs, true or false. A fact is a true addition or subtraction. A
  number bond is a whole and its two parts written as an addition, and its fact family is the facts it gives.
- The facts of one family are "in the same family". "Related facts" and "inverse" are left out, and `missing-number`
  decides "inverse".
- An addition is undone by the subtraction that subtracts the number that was added, so 13 − 5 = 8 undoes
  8 + 5 = 13. 13 − 8 = 5 is in the same family and does not undo it.
- To check is to use the calculation that undoes. The Check button commits an exercise, and a text that names both
  says "the Check button".
- A part to be found is "the missing part", beside "the known part". "Unknown", "addend", "minuend" and
  "subtrahend" are left out.
- The missing part is found by counting on, and a text does not say "count up".
- Bars are longer and shorter, and numbers are greater and less. A part with a greater number is a longer part.
- "A line counts in tens" is said of a line's labels, and "you count in twos" of the jumps. "Count back" alone goes
  one by one, and "count back in fives" goes in jumps of 5.
- The step is what a jump to the next mark adds, and the size of a jump is what the jump adds. On a line with a mark
  for every number the step is 1 and a jump of 2 crosses two gaps.
- The size of a jump is a number. A jump of 10 is bigger than a jump of 2, and jumps are more and fewer, so "a bigger
  jump needs fewer jumps".
- A jump lands on a number, and "landing" is left out of a reader's text. The reader makes a jump and jumps back,
  and a text does not say "add a jump" or "take a jump away".
- The number of jumps is the number of groups, and the number the last jump from 0 lands on is how many objects
  there are altogether.
- "Pair" of things that come in twos, such as socks, is a group of 2. Even and odd are told by groups of 2. 0 is
  even and a multiple of every number.
- Up to 100 the multiples of 10 are "the tens", and past 100 they are "multiples of 10". "Previous" and "next
  multiple", "decade number" and "100s boundary" are left out.
- On a hundred square a count lights cells. "Mark" is said only of a number line, and "square" names the whole
  figure.
- "Last digit" is said before `tens-and-ones`, which introduces "ones digit" and "tens digit".
- The noun is "value", as "the value of the 4 in 42 is 40". A text may explain it once as what the digit is worth,
  and the noun "worth" is left out.
- Tens and ones are counted with a number before them, "1 ten", "4 tens and 2 ones". "The tens" are 10, 20, 30 and on,
  each a number of whole tens.
- Ten ones are bundled into a ten, and a ten is broken into ten ones. A number is split into parts. "Partition",
  "decompose", "unbundle" and "regroup" are left out, and `column-methods` decides "exchange".
- "Ten ones make a ten" uses "make" as two parts make a whole. "Make ten" without "a" is the term of making ten.
- A rod is a ten and a cube is a one. A text names the blocks when it speaks of the figure, and tens and ones when it
  speaks of the number.
- The ones outside the tens are "the ones", or "extra ones" where a text sets them apart from the ones inside the
  tens. "Loose" is left out, and "left over" stays with pairing.
- "Place" is said of a digit in a written number, and "column" of a place value chart.
- Two ways of showing one number "both show 42". A text does not say that 4 tens and 2 ones "equal" 3 tens and 12
  ones.
- A teen number is read "1 ten and 4 ones". "Ten and a bit" and "one-ten-one" are left out.
- Ten ones of an addition are bundled into a new ten, and a ten of a subtraction is broken. "Compose", "trade",
  "carry", "borrow" and "bridge" are left out with the words above.
- "Cross a ten" is said of a number passing one of 10 to 100, and "a new ten" of ten ones bundled into a ten. 38 + 2
  makes a new ten and reaches 40 without crossing it, and 38 + 5 does both. "The tens boundary" is left out.
- "The ten after 38" is the number 40, written in digits beside the words, and "a new ten" or "1 ten" is a group of
  10 ones. "The next ten" and "the previous ten" are left out.
- Ten more and ten less are numbers, so "24 has 1 ten fewer than 34". "Ten more" is said only of adding 10, written
  in words as "one more" is.
- A digit "goes up by 1", "goes down by 1" or "stays the same". "Increase" and "decrease" are left out.
- Where a ten has to be broken, a text says that the extra ones are fewer than the ones to take away, and that the
  number's other ones are inside its tens. "There aren't enough ones", "too few ones" and "you can't take a bigger
  number from a smaller one" are left out.
