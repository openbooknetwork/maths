You subtract tens the same way you subtract ones. If you subtract 3 tens from 7 tens, 4 tens are left, just as
`7 − 3 = 4`. When you subtract only tens from a two-digit number, its ones stay as they were, so only its tens digit
changes. Subtracting 1 ten gives ten less.

For `75 − 30`, think of 75 as 7 tens and 5 ones, and of 30 as 3 tens. Subtract 3 tens from 7 tens, and 4 tens are
left. 30 has no ones, so the 5 ones are still there, and 4 tens and 5 ones make 45. If you split 75 into 70 and 5, the
two number sentences are `70 − 30 = 40` and then `40 + 5 = 45`.

The red rods and cubes show 75. Drag a rod off the tens column, or tap the column, and the last rod goes to the
"Taken away" pile. It leaves a dashed outline, so you can still see the 7 tens that 75 had. "Taken away" shows
[0](@second) and "Left" shows [75](@left).

- Take away 3 rods, one at a time. The number sentence under the chart ends in 65, then 55, then 45, so each rod
  leaves ten less while the 5 ones stay.
- Tap the rod on the pile to put the last one back.
