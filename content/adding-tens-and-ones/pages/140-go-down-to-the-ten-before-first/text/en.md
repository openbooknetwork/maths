Subtracting ones can cross a ten too, as `43 − 5 = 38` crosses 40, which lies between 43 and 38. Making ten then works backwards. You split the number you subtract into two parts, and the first part is the ones digit of the number you start from. Subtracting it takes that number down to the ten before it. Then you subtract the rest from that ten.

Take `43 − 5`. 43 is 3 more than 40, the ten before it, so you split 5 into 3 and 2. The first step is `43 − 3 = 40`. Taking 2 from one of the 4 tens leaves 3 tens and 8 ones, so `40 − 2 = 38`. That gives `43 − 5 = 38`, which takes back the 5 that `38 + 5 = 43` added.

Two slips are common here. One is to take 30 as the ten before 43, but 30 is the ten before 40. The other is to write `40 − 2 = 48`, which keeps the 4 tens of 40. A number less than 40 has only 3 tens, so the answer is 38.

The point is on [43](@point).

- Press "Jump left" three times, a jump of 1 each time. The point lands on 40 and no steps appear, because the jumps reach 40 without crossing it.
- Press it twice more. The two steps appear under the number sentence, and "Down to ten" and "The rest" read 3 and 2.
- "Jump right" takes the last jump back. Drag the start ring to try another number.
