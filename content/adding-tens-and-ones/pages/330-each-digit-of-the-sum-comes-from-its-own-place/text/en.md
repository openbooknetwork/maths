When the ones of two numbers add to 9 or less, each digit of the sum comes from its own place. The tens digit counts the tens of both numbers, and the ones digit counts their ones. Nine ones or fewer do not make a ten, so the ones leave the tens digit alone.

Take `45 + 31`. 4 tens and 3 tens make 7 tens, and 5 ones and 1 one make 6 ones, so the sum is 76. Add 3 more ones to the second number to make 34, and the sum is 79. Its tens digit stays 7, because you only changed the ones. Put one more ten on the first number to make 55, and the sum is 89, with its ones digit still 9.

The 3 in 31 counts tens, so it goes with the 4 tens of 45. If you mix up the places and read 31 as 1 ten and 3 ones, you add 13 and get 58, which is wrong.

The Tens and Ones steppers change the first number, [45](@first), in red. "Tens added" and "Ones added" change the second, [31](@second), in blue.
