When you add two two-digit numbers, the tens go with the tens and the ones with the ones. If the ones make 10 or
more, you bundle ten of them into a ten, since ten ones are the same amount as one ten. This ten is called a new ten,
because it was not among the tens of either number. You count it with the other tens of the sum.

Take `38 + 25`. 3 tens and 2 tens make 5 tens. 8 ones and 5 ones make 13 ones, which are a new ten and 3 ones. The
new ten and the 5 tens make 6 tens, so the sum is 6 tens and 3 ones, 63.

The new ten is easy to forget, and here forgetting it gives 53. So look at the ones first, and if they make 10 or
more, the sum gets a new ten. When they make exactly 10, no ones are left, so the sum ends in 0, as in
`38 + 22 = 60`.

The chart holds 38 in plain red, and 25 waits beside it in blue with dots.

- Tap or drag every blue piece onto the chart. The counts under the columns go up to 5 and 13.
- Press "Bundle ten ones" and watch the "New ten" readout, [0](@new-ten). The counts now read 6 and 3, and the new
  rod has 8 red cubes and 2 blue ones, because its ten ones come from both numbers.
