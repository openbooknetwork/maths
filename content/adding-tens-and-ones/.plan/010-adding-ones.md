## Content

### 110 A bakery fills a new packet

The opening page of the story. A bakery packs its biscuits in packets of ten. A full packet is a ten and a biscuit
outside the packets is a one, so the packets and the loose biscuits show a number as tens and ones, as the place
value chart does. The bakery has 6 packets and 8 loose biscuits, 68 biscuits. A batch of 5 biscuits comes out of the oven and
joins the 8.

The question the page raises, which the story answers: how do the packets and the biscuits change when biscuits are
added? The 8 biscuits and the 5 make 13. That is more than ten, so 10 of them fill a new packet and 3 stay out. The
bakery then has 7 packets and 3 loose biscuits, 73 biscuits. 68 + 5 = 73. One more packet means the tens digit goes up
by 1, from 6 to 7, and the ones digit is 3, the biscuits left after the packet is filled.

The text closes with what the story does next: adding ones, then tens, then two two-digit numbers, then taking away.
It says this in one sentence.

What the figure stands for: a packet with its window of ten biscuits is a rod, the tray of biscuits is the ones
column, a plain biscuit is a red cube of 68, and a biscuit with a chocolate chip is a blue cube of the batch.

Example: 6 packets and 8 biscuits and a batch of 5. 8 + 5 = 13, a full packet and 3, so 7 packets and 3 biscuits,
68 + 5 = 73.

### 120 The ones change and the tens stay

When you add ones to a two-digit number, the ones join the ones. If the ones together make 9 or fewer, they fill no
ten, so the tens stay as they were. Only the ones digit changes, and an addition within ten gives it.

The example stays with tens and ones, and the part about the figure goes over the chart in a sentence: the rods are
the tens and the cubes are the ones. The chart shows 23, 2 rods and 3 cubes, with 5 blue cubes added after them. 3 ones and 5 ones make 8
ones, because 3 + 5 = 8. The tens are 2 rods still. So 23 + 5 = 28. The tens digit is 2 in 23 and in 28, and only the
ones digit changed, from 3 to 8. The reader does not need to count on 24, 25, 26, 27, 28, because 3 + 5 = 8
already gives the ones.

Then the text asks for two more ones. 23 + 7 has 3 + 7 = 10 ones, which is a full ten, so the ones column holds a
whole ten and the number lands on 30, the ten after 23. 30 has the tens digit 3. So the tens stay only while the ones
make 9 or fewer. The next page adds more ones than that.

Example: 23 + 5 = 28 because 3 + 5 = 8, and 23 + 7 = 30 because 3 + 7 = 10.

### 130 Go to the ten after first

An addition of ones crosses a ten when a ten lies between its first number and its answer, as 40 lies between 38
and 43. Making ten does such an addition in two steps. You split the ones you add into the part that makes ten with
the ones digit and the rest. The first step takes the number to the ten after it, and the second adds the rest to
that ten.

Take 38 + 5. 38 is 3 tens and 8 ones, and 8 needs 2 more to make ten. So 5 splits into 2 and 3. The first step is
38 + 2 = 40, which fills the ten and lands on 40, the ten after 38. The rest is 3, and 40 + 3 = 43. So 38 + 5 = 43.
These are the steps of 8 + 5 = 13 from `adding-within-20`, with 3 tens in front: 8 + 2 = 10 and 10 + 3 = 13 became
38 + 2 = 40 and 40 + 3 = 43. The text points back to that story by its title, "Adding and subtracting
within 20", and names none of its pages.

On the chart, the 8 red cubes and the first 2 blue cubes make a full ten, two rows of five with a gap after them, and
the other 3 blue cubes start the rows after the gap. 3 rods and a full ten of cubes are 4 tens, so the number is 43,
4 tens and 3 ones. Bundling the full ten into a rod shows 4 tens and 3 ones on the chart too.

Example: 38 + 5 = 43, in two steps, 38 + 2 = 40 and 40 + 3 = 43.

### 140 Go down to the ten before first

Taking ones away works the same way back. A subtraction of ones crosses a ten when a ten lies between its first
number and its answer. Going down to the ten before first does it in two steps. The first part you take away is the
ones digit, which takes the number down to the ten before it. Then you take the rest from that ten.

Take 43 − 5. The ten before 43 is 40, and 43 is 3 more than 40. So 5 splits into 3 and 2. The first step is
43 − 3 = 40. Then 40 − 2 = 38, because 8 and 2 make 10, so 2 less than 40 is 38, in the thirties. So 43 − 5 = 38. This
takes back the 5 of 38 + 5 = 43 from the page before, and the split 3 and 2 is the split of 13 − 5 from `adding-within-20`'s page
"Go down to ten first".

The text warns of two slips. The ten before 43 is 40, and 30 is the ten before 40. And 40 − 2 is 38, a number in the
thirties, where the slip 48 keeps the tens of 40.

On the line, each jump left is a jump of 1. The start ring is on 43, the first three jumps reach 40, and the next two
reach 38.

Example: 43 − 5 = 38, in two steps, 43 − 3 = 40 and 40 − 2 = 38.
