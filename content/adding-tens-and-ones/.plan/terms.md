# Terms of Adding tens and ones

File names without a folder are in `content/adding-tens-and-ones/.references/`.

## 010 adding-ones
- Introduces:
  - `cross a ten (two-digit numbers)`: an addition or a subtraction of ones crosses a ten when one of 10, 20, 30 and
    on to 100 lies between its first number and its answer, as 38 + 5 = 43 crosses 40 and 43 − 5 = 38 crosses 40. In
    an addition of two two-digit numbers it is said of the step that adds the ones, as 58 + 5 in 38 + 25 crosses 60.
    38 + 2 = 40 reaches 40 and does not cross it. A new sense beside `cross ten (calculations)`, which is the case of
    10, and the sense `adding-within-20` left to this story. NCETM: "Pupils add and subtract one to and from a
    two-digit number that crosses a tens boundary"
    (`ncetm.org.uk-classroom-resources-cp-year-2-unit-4-addition-and-subtraction-of-two-digit-numbers.txt`), and Oak
    "I can add two-digit numbers when the sum crosses the tens boundary"
    (`thenational.academy-f-two-2-digit-numbers-lessons-add-2-digit-numbers-when-crossing-the-tens-boundary.txt`).
    Written "cross a ten" over "the tens boundary", as `adding-within-20` wrote "cross ten" for Oak's "crosses the 10
    boundary", and over Oak's "bridging a multiple of ten", as the glossary chose making ten over bridging. The ten
    crossed is "the ten after" the first number in an addition and "the ten before" it in a subtraction, the
    glossary's `the ten before, the ten after (numbers)`, in place of the brief's "the next ten".
  - `making ten (two-digit numbers)`: adding ones to a two-digit number across a ten by going to the ten after it
    first, and subtracting ones by going down to the ten before it first. In an addition the part of the ones that
    makes ten with the ones digit takes the number to the ten after, and the rest is added to that ten, so for 38 + 5,
    2 makes ten with 8, 38 + 2 = 40, then 40 + 3 = 43. In a subtraction the part equal to the ones digit takes the
    number down to the ten before, and the rest is taken from that ten, so for 43 − 5, 43 − 3 = 40, then 40 − 2 = 38.
    A new sense beside `making ten (calculations)`, whose part brings a number to 10. "Make ten" said of the ones digit
    keeps its glossary sense, since 8 and 2 make 10, and "the rest" keeps its word. "Down to ten" becomes "down to 40".
    IM: "They draw on the idea of making a ten, which they learned when adding ones and when adding numbers within 20."
    (`curriculum.illustrativemathematics.org-k5-teachers-grade-1-course-guide-scope-and-sequence.txt`, Unit 5,
    Section B), and NCETM "Known strategies for addition or subtraction bridging ten can be applied to addition or
    subtraction bridging a multiple of ten."
    (`ncetm.org.uk-lassroom-resources-primm-113-addition-and-subtraction-two-digit-and-single-digit-numbers.txt`,
    teaching point 4).
- Uses: `ten, one (numbers)`, `tens digit, ones digit (two-digit numbers)`, `two-digit number (numbers)`,
  `place value chart (figure)`, `column (place value chart)`, `base ten blocks (figure)`, `rod, cube (base ten
  blocks)`, `bundle (place value)` for the biscuits that fill a new packet, `the ten before, the ten after (numbers)`,
  `making ten (calculations)`, `make ten, down to ten (making ten)` and `the rest (making ten)` where a text points
  back to `adding-within-20`, `cross ten (calculations)`, `add (numbers)`, `subtract (numbers)`, `sum (numbers)`,
  `addition, subtraction (calculations)`, `number sentence (calculations, comparisons)`, `plus, minus (signs)`,
  `split (numbers)` for "5 splits into 2 and 3", `count on, count back (numbers)`

## 020 tens
- Introduces:
  - `ten more, ten less (numbers)`: the number 10 greater than a number, and the number 10 less than it, as ten more
    than 34 is 44 and ten less than 34 is 24. Ten more has one more ten and the same ones, so its tens digit goes up
    by 1 and its ones digit stays the same. Ten more than 90 is 100. NCETM: "Teaching point 1: When finding ten more or
    ten less than any two-digit number, the ones digit does not change."
    (`ncetm.org.uk-room-resources-primm-114-addition-and-subtraction-two-digit-numbers-and-multiples-of-ten.txt`), and
    Oak "When finding 10 more than a number below 100, the tens digit increases by one."
    (`thenational.academy-ns-to-and-from-2-digit-numbers-lessons-find-10-more-or-less-than-a-2-digit-number.txt`, Key
    learning points). Written in words as NCETM writes it and as the glossary writes `one more, one less (numbers)`,
    over Oak's and the US "10 more".
- Uses: `ten, one (numbers)`, `tens digit, ones digit (two-digit numbers)`, `value (digits)`,
  `place value chart (figure)`, `rod, cube (base ten blocks)`, `hundred square (figure)`, `cell (hundred square)`,
  `row, column (hundred square)`, `number line (figure)`, `mark (number line)`, `label (number line)`,
  `point (number line)`, `start (number line)`, `jump of 2, 5, 10, 100 (number line)` for the jump of 10,
  `equal (jumps)`, `count in twos, fives, tens, hundreds (numbers)` for counting in tens from any start, as 34, 44,

## 040 new-ten
- Introduces:
  - `new ten (addition)`: the ten that ten ones of an addition make when its ones add to 10 or more. The ten ones
    are bundled into the new ten, and it is counted with the other tens of the sum, so in 38 + 25 the 8 ones and 5
    ones make 13 ones, which are 1 new ten and 3 ones, and 3 tens, 2 tens and the new ten make 6 tens, so the sum is
    63. A rod made of ten cubes on the chart is a new ten. IM: "where it is not necessary to compose a new ten"
    (`curriculum.illustrativemathematics.org-k5-teachers-grade-1-course-guide-scope-and-sequence.txt`, Unit 5), and
    "making a new tower of 10" (same file, Section B). The verb is the glossary's "bundle", over "compose" (US
    1.NBT.C.4, `.cache/curricula/us-common-core-math.pdf`, page 16), "regroup", "exchange" and "trade"
    (`sites.google.com-cesd73.ca-cesd-math-framework-all-concepts-additive-thinking-at-stop-saying.txt`) and "carry"
    (`isbe.net-Documents-number-base-ten-k-5.txt`, page 9), since `tens-and-ones` fixed "bundle" for the same act.
    Chosen over Oak's "the extra 10 made when combining the ones"
    (`thenational.academy-f-two-2-digit-numbers-lessons-add-2-digit-numbers-when-crossing-the-tens-boundary.txt`),
    because "extra ones" names the ones outside the tens.
- Uses: `ten, one (numbers)`, `bundle (place value)`, `rod, cube (base ten blocks)`, `place value chart
  (figure)`, `column (place value chart)`, `tens digit, ones digit (two-digit numbers)`, `cross a ten (two-digit
  numbers)` for the step of ones on the line, `making ten (two-digit numbers)`, `the ten before, the ten after
  (numbers)`, `sum (numbers)`, `add (numbers)`, `addition, subtraction (calculations)`, `number sentence
  (calculations, comparisons)`, `split (numbers)`, `jump of 2, 5, 10, 100 (number line)`, `jump (number line)`,
  `number line (figure)`, `start (number line)`

## 050 subtracting
- Introduces:
  - `ones inside the tens (place value)`: the ones a number's tens are made of, 10 in each ten, beside its extra ones,
    so 52 has 50 ones inside its 5 tens and 2 extra ones, 52 ones in all. Breaking a ten turns 10 of them into extra
    ones, and 52 is then 4 tens and 12 ones. A text says this where the extra ones are fewer than the ones to take
    away, as the 2 extra ones of 52 are fewer than the 7 ones of 17. The glossary's rule on "extra ones" names "the
    ones inside the tens", and this entry gives it its meaning. Kappan: "we need to decompose the higher unit value
    here so we can get at some of the 1’s that are composed into 10’s."
    (`kappanonline.org-wp-content-uploads-2019-08-PDK-95-2-Faulkner-59-tbl2.txt`, page 1). Chosen over the brief's
    "when the ones run short" and "too few ones", which Kappan and CESD warn against: "If they hear “we don’t have
    enough 1’s” whenever these problems are addressed, they begin to believe it." (same file).
- Uses: `break a ten (place value)`, `ten, one (numbers)`, `rod, cube (base ten blocks)`, `place value chart
  (figure)`, `column (place value chart)`, `tens digit, ones digit (two-digit numbers)`, `take away (groups)` for
  the rods and cubes, `left (groups)`, `subtract (numbers)`, `subtraction (calculations)` of `addition, subtraction
  (calculations)`, `minus (signs)` of `plus, minus (signs)`, `split (numbers)` for "17 splits into 10 and 7",
  `difference (numbers)`, `the greater number, the smaller number (numbers)`, `count on, count back (numbers)`,
  `the ten before, the ten after (numbers)`, `count in twos, fives, tens, hundreds (numbers)` for the tens,
  `jump of 2, 5, 10, 100 (number line)`, `cross a ten (two-digit numbers)`, `making ten (two-digit numbers)`,
  `ten more, ten less (numbers)`, `new ten (addition)` where a text compares breaking a ten with bundling one

## Rules
- Ten ones of an addition are bundled into a new ten, and a ten of a subtraction is broken into ten ones, with the
  glossary's verbs. "Compose", "decompose", "regroup", "exchange", "trade", "carry", "borrow" and "unbundle" are left
  out of the reader's text. `column-methods` decides "exchange", as the glossary has it.
- "Cross a ten" is said of a number passing one of 10 to 100, and "a new ten" of ten ones bundled into a ten. 38 + 2
  makes a new ten and reaches 40 without crossing it, and 38 + 5 does both. "Bridge", "bridging" and "the tens
  boundary" are left out.
- "The ten after" and "the ten before" name a number, 40 for 38, written in digits beside the words. A ten counted
  with a number before it, "1 ten", "a new ten", is a group of 10 ones. "The next ten" and "the previous ten" of the
  brief and of Oak are left out, as the glossary rules have it.
- Ten more and ten less are numbers, so a text says "ten less than 34 is 24" and "24 has 1 ten fewer than 34", with
  fewer for the tens counted. "Ten more" is said only of adding 10. Adding more tens is "add 30" or "add 3 tens", and
  "twenty more" is left out. "10 more" in digits is left out.
- A digit "goes up by 1" or "goes down by 1" and "stays the same". The brief's "raises" and "lowers" and Oak's
  "increases" and "decreases" are left out.
- "The tens" and "the ones" of a calculation are those of its two numbers, as "add the tens to the tens": 3 tens and
  2 tens make 5 tens. The numbers 10, 20, 30 are written in digits, and "multiple of 10", "multiple of ten" and
  "decade" are left out below 100, as the glossary rules have it.
- The ones outside the tens are the extra ones, and a text says that a number's other ones are inside its tens. Where
  a ten has to be broken, a text says the extra ones are fewer than the ones to take away. "There aren't enough
  ones", "not enough", "too few ones", "the ones run short" and "you can't take a bigger number from a smaller one"
  are left out.
- "Loose" is left out, as the glossary rules have it, so the brief's "loose biscuits" are the biscuits outside the
  packets, and its "no more than 9 loose ones" is "no more than 9 cubes in the ones column".
- On the line a text starts at the first number and splits only the second number into tens and ones. The brief's
  "keep the first number whole" is left out, since "whole" is the number two parts make.
- On a hundred square ten more is in "the cell just below", as the glossary rules have it. The brief's "the square
  below" is left out, since "square" names the whole figure.
- Jumps are written "a jump of 10" and "a jump of 1", in digits as the glossary writes `jump of 2, 5, 10, 100
  (number line)`. The brief's "jumps of ten" and "jumps of one" are left out. On a line with a mark for every number a
  jump of 1 is a jump to the next mark.
- Rods and cubes are taken away, as objects are, and numbers are subtracted. A text says "take away 1 rod and 7
  cubes" on the chart and "subtract 10, then 7" of the numbers. Tens counted with a number are subtracted, as
  "subtract 3 tens from 7 tens".
- The steps of a calculation are written one number sentence each, as `adding-within-20` rules, so 37 + 40 is
  `30 + 40 = 70` and `70 + 7 = 77`, and not one chain of = signs.
- "Partition", "recombine", "addend", "subtrahend", "like units", "unitising", "increase", "decrease" and
  "equation" are left out. `equals-sign` decides "equation".
- "The target" is the number a challenge asks the reader to reach, an everyday word as in `skip-counting`. "Points"
  and "halves" of a basketball game are everyday words, and a later story decides "half" of a number or a shape.
- Left to later stories:
  - `column-methods` introduces "exchange" and the written columns, where the new ten is written in the next place.
  - `hundreds-and-thousands` introduces the hundred made of ten tens. This story stops at 100 and says "ten more
    than 90 is 100" without naming a hundred.
