## Content

### 410 Ten ones make a new ten
Idea: to add two two-digit numbers, add the tens to the tens and the ones to the ones. When the ones add to 10 or
more, ten of them make a new ten. Bundle those ten ones into a ten and count the new ten with the other tens.

Example: 38 + 25. The tens: 3 tens and 2 tens make 5 tens, 30 + 20 = 50. The ones: 8 ones and 5 ones make 13 ones,
8 + 5 = 13. 13 ones are 1 ten and 3 ones, so the ones make a new ten. The new ten and the 5 tens make 6 tens, and 3
ones are left, so the sum is 6 tens and 3 ones, 50 + 13 = 63, and 38 + 25 = 63. Before the bundle the counts under
the columns read 5 and 13, and after it 6 and 3. The new rod holds 8 red cubes and 2 blue ones, since its ten ones
came from both numbers.

The ones digits tell before you add whether a sum gets a new ten, so check them first: 8 and 5 make 13, and the sum
gets a new ten, which is the ten that is easy to forget. Where the ones make exactly 10, they are a new ten and no
ones, so the sum ends in 0, as 38 + 22 = 60. The text says this in a sentence or two after the worked example.

On the figure the red rods and cubes are 38 and the blue rods and cubes with dots are 25.

### 420 Jump to the ten after on a number line
Idea: on a number line, start at the first number and split only the second number into its tens and ones. Jump the
tens first. When the ones cross the ten after, jump to that ten first and then on, as making ten does. The new ten
needs no step of its own on the line, since the jump to the ten after counts it.

Example: 38 + 25. 25 splits into 20 and 5. From 38, two jumps of 10 land on 48 and 58, so 38 + 20 = 58. The ten
after 58 is 60, and 8 needs 2 to make ten, so 5 splits into 2 and 3: 58 + 2 = 60, then 60 + 3 = 63. The line gives
38 + 25 = 63, the sum the chart gave on 410. The tags over the jumps read +20, +2 and +3.

### 610 Points in two halves of a game
Idea: the points a team scores in a game are the points of the first half and the second half added. A scoreboard has
one place for each digit, so 13 ones do not fit in the ones place, and the new ten has to join the tens first.

Example: a basketball team scores 28 points in the first half and 45 in the second half. The tens: 2 tens and 4 tens
make 6 tens. The ones: 8 ones and 5 ones make 13 ones, a new ten and 3 ones. With the new ten the team has 7 tens and
3 ones, so it scored 73 points in the game, 28 + 45 = 73.

On the figure the board's box "First half" shows 28 and "Second half" 45. The red blocks under the board are the
first half's points and the blue blocks with dots the second half's. The box "Game" writes the count of each column
in its own slot, and shows 13 in a dashed box that is too narrow until ten ones are bundled.

## Interactivity

### 410 Ten ones make a new ten
- Shows: the chart with 38 in red, 3 rods and 8 cubes, and 25 waiting beside it in blue, 2 rods and 5 cubes. Under
  the chart the line "38 + 25" stands open. The button "Bundle ten ones" rests.
- The reader taps or drags the waiting rods and cubes onto the chart, then presses "Bundle ten ones".
- In reply each blue piece lands after the red ones of its column. Once every piece has joined, the line reads
  "38 + 25 = 63", the place lines "30 + 20 = 50" and "8 + 5 = 13" show under it, and the counts under the columns
  read 5 and 13. The Bundle button wakes once the ones column holds 10 cubes. A press glides the first ten cubes,
  8 red and 2 blue, together into a rod that keeps their colours and dots, and the rod glides to the end of the tens
  column. The counts read 6 and 3, and "New ten" reads 1.
- In general words: join the second number's blocks, then bundle ten ones, and the ten ones of both numbers become a
  new ten in the tens column.

### 420 Jump to the ten after on a number line
- Shows: a number line from 30 to 70, a mark at every number and labels at the tens, the start ring on 38, the
  stepper "Size of a jump" at 10, the buttons Jump left and Jump right, and the written line under the line.
- The reader jumps right twice with the size at 10, steps the size to 2 and jumps once, then steps it to 3 and jumps
  once.
- In reply each jump draws its arc. A tag over each run of jumps of one size reads +20 over the two jumps of 10, +2
  over the jump to 60 and +3 over the last jump. The jumps made stay when the size changes. The written line reads
  "38 + 20 = 58", then "38 + 22 = 60", then "38 + 25 = 63". Jump left takes back the last jump, whatever its size.
- In general words: set the size of each jump, so that one jump lands on the ten after and the next goes on from it.

### 610 Points in two halves of a game
- Shows: a basketball scoreboard over the chart with the boxes "First half" 28, "Second half" 45 and "Game" with its
  two digit slots at 2 and 8. Under it the chart holds 28 in red, and 45 waits beside it in blue, 4 rods and 5
  cubes.
- The reader joins the second half's blocks, then presses "Bundle ten ones".
- In reply the Game box writes the count of each column as the pieces join. With every piece in, it reads 6 in the
  tens slot and 13 in a dashed box over the ones slot, too narrow for two digits. The bundle glides 8 red cubes and 2
  blue cubes into a new rod, the box reads 7 and 3, and "New ten" reads 1.
- In general words: add the halves on the chart, and the board shows the game's points once the new ten is bundled.

## Challenge

- Goal: build the sum of two drawn numbers on an empty chart, with 9 cubes or fewer in the ones column, then press
  Check. The page uses the goal `build-sum` as the brief designs it.
- Drawn each time: a first number and a second number from 15 to 69, whose ones add to 10 or more and whose sum is 99
  or less. The goal line names the addition, "Show the sum 38 + 25 with rods and cubes, then press Check."
- Reached: the reader drags rods and cubes from the supply onto the chart and bundles ten cubes where the ones pass
  9, or puts on the new ten as a rod at once. The goal holds when the blocks show the sum with 9 cubes or fewer. A
  wrong Check names the number the chart shows and says which way it is off. Where the chart shows 10 less than the
  sum, it says that the ones make a new ten to count with the other tens. Where the number is right with 10 or more
  cubes, it says to bundle ten ones. The hint adds the tens, then the ones, and counts the new ten with the tens.
- Hidden: the number above the chart, the counts under the columns, the values, the name, the steppers, the written
  lines and every readout. The sum and the line "38 + 25 = 63" show once the goal holds.
- Idea used: the ones of two numbers that add to 10 or more make a new ten, counted with the other tens. The controls
  are the supply and the button "Bundle ten ones", which `tens-and-ones` and 410 taught.
- For the writer: the page text works an example the goal cannot draw, as `agents/text.md` asks of a challenge, so
  it gives no drawn answer away. 38 + 25, the brief's goal line, can be drawn and stays out of the text. The text
  works 74 + 18, outside the draw since 74 is past 69: check the ones first, 4 and 8 make 12, so there is a new ten.
  7 tens and 1 ten make 8 tens, the new ten makes 9 tens, and 2 ones are left, so 74 + 18 = 92, built as 9 rods and
  2 cubes.
