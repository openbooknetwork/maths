## Content

### 310 Add tens to tens and ones to ones
The idea: to add two two-digit numbers, add the tens to the tens and the ones to the ones. A ten and a one are
different sizes, so each kind is counted on its own and the two counts are put together. The sum has the tens of
both numbers and the ones of both numbers. Either place can be added first, and the sum is the same. The teaching part
says this without the chart, and the chart's columns come in the part about the figure.

The example: 31 + 26. The chart shows 31 as 3 rods and 1 cube. 26 is 2 tens and 6 ones, so to add it the reader
puts on 2 rods and 6 cubes. The 2 rods go in the tens column with the 3 rods: 3 tens and 2 tens make 5 tens, 30 + 20
= 50. The 6 cubes go in the ones column with the 1 cube: 1 one and 6 ones make 7 ones, 1 + 6 = 7. The chart shows 5
tens and 7 ones, which is 57, since 50 and 7 make 57. So 31 + 26 = 57. Putting the 6 cubes on first and the 2 rods
after gives the same 57. The facts 3 + 2 = 5 and 1 + 6 = 7 are sums within 10 the reader knows, now of tens and of
ones.

On the figure the chart keeps rods and cubes in their own columns. The red blocks are the first number, 31, and the blue blocks with a dot are the second number, 26, as
the reader puts them on.

### 320 Jumps of 10, then jumps of 1
The idea: a sum can also be found on a number line. Start at the first number and split only the second number into
its tens and its ones. Add the tens with jumps of 10, then the ones with jumps of 1.

The example: 31 + 26 again, on a line from 30 to 60. 26 splits into 20 and 6. From 31, two jumps of 10 land on 41 and
51, so 31 + 20 = 51. From 51, six jumps of 1 land on 52, 53, 54, 55, 56 and 57, so 51 + 6 = 57. The line gives 57, the
sum the chart gave. Over the jumps of 10 a tag reads "+20" and over the jumps of 1 a tag reads "+6", the two parts of
26. Each step is one number sentence, as the story writes its steps.

### 330 Each digit of the sum comes from its own place
The idea: while the ones add to 9 or less, the tens digit of the sum counts the tens of both numbers and the ones
digit counts the ones of both numbers. A change to the ones of either number changes only the ones digit of the sum,
and a change to the tens changes only the tens digit. Each digit goes with the digit in the same place: the 3 of 31
counts 3 tens, so it adds to the 4 tens of 45.

The example: 45 + 31. 4 tens and 3 tens make 7 tens, 40 + 30 = 70, and 5 ones and 1 one make 6 ones, 5 + 1 = 6. The
sum is 7 tens and 6 ones, 76. With three more ones added the second number is 34: the ones make 5 + 4 = 9, the sum is
79, and its tens digit stays 7. With one more ten in the first number it is 55: the tens make 50 + 30 = 80, the sum is
89, and its ones digit stays 9. Mixing the places up reads 31 as 1 ten and 3 ones, adds 13 and gives 58, which is
wrong. The steppers keep the ones at 9 or less. Ones that make 10 or more are the next section's case.

### 710 Put on tens and ones to reach a number (challenge)
The reader uses the idea in reverse: the target's tens less the start's tens are the rods to put on, and the target's
ones less the start's ones are the cubes. From 31 to 57: 5 tens less 3 tens is 2 rods, 7 ones less 1 one is 6
cubes, and the chart then shows 57.

## Interactivity

### 310
- Shows: the place value chart with 31 in red, 3 rods in the tens column and 1 cube in the ones column, the digits
  under the columns and the number 31 above the chart. A supply of one blue rod and one blue cube stands beside the
  chart. Under the figure the whole line and the two place lines show a dash until a blue piece is on.
- The reader drags or taps the supply's rod and cube onto the chart, in any order, and drags a blue piece off to take
  it back.
- The figure: a blue rod lands in the tens column after the red rods and a blue cube in the ones column after the red
  cubes. The digit under that column and the number above the chart go up. The whole line reads "31 + 26 = 57" once
  2 rods and 6 cubes are on, and the line of a place shows once a blue piece is in that place, "30 + 20 = 50" and
  "1 + 6 = 7" at the end. The chips "Second number" and "Sum" follow.
- In general words: put a piece on the chart, and it lands in its own place's column, and the line of that place
  adds it.

### 320
- Shows: a number line from 30 to 60 with a mark for every number and labels at 30, 40, 50 and 60, the start's ring
  and the point on 31, the buttons "Size of a jump" with 1 and 10, 10 picked, Jump left and Jump right, and the
  written line "31 + 0 = 31".
- The reader presses Jump right twice, picks 1, and presses Jump right six times. The reader may also jump in ones
  first, and Jump left takes back the last jump of either size.
- The figure: each jump draws its arc and moves the point. A tag "+10" pops in over the first jump of 10 and turns
  "+20" with the second. A new tag "+1" pops in over the first jump of 1 and counts up to "+6". The written line
  reads "31 + 26 = 57" at the end. The chips "Point" and "Jumps" follow.
- In general words: pick a size and jump, and a tag over each run of jumps of one size says what the run adds.

### 330
- Shows: the chart with 45 in red and 31 in blue, the digits 7 and 6 under the columns, the number 76 above the
  chart, the lines "45 + 31 = 76", "40 + 30 = 70" and "5 + 1 = 6", and four steppers, "Tens" and "Ones" for the
  first number and "Tens added" and "Ones added" for the second.
- The reader steps any of the four.
- The figure: a step adds or takes away one rod or one cube of that number at the end of its row, red or blue. The
  line of that place, the digit under that column and that digit of the number above change, and the other place's
  line and digit stay as they are. The whole line follows. The chips "First number", "Second number" and "Sum"
  follow.
- In general words: step one place of either number, and only that place's line and that digit of the sum change.

## Challenge
- Goal: `add-to-target`. The goal line names the start and the target, "The chart shows 31. Put on rods and cubes
  until it shows 57, then press Check."
- Drawn each time: a start from 11 to 59 and an amount from 11 to 39 with 1 ten or more and 1 one or more, whose ones
  and the start's ones add to 9 or less. The target is the start plus the amount, 98 at most. "Another one" draws
  again.
- How it is reached: the chart opens with the start in red and a supply of blue rods and cubes. The goal never needs
  a new ten, so the chart has no Bundle button.
  The reader puts on the rods that make up the tens and the cubes that make up the ones, as page 310 taught, then
  presses Check. The goal holds when the blocks show the target with 9 cubes or fewer. A wrong Check names the column
  that is off, "The tens column has too few rods." or "The ones column has too many cubes.", and keeps the number
  back. The hint says "31 has 3 tens and 57 has 5 tens, so put on 2 rods. 31 has 1 one and 57 has 7
  ones, so put on 6 cubes."
- Hidden: the number above the chart, the digits under the columns, the values, the name, the steppers, the written
  lines and every readout. Once the goal holds, the number and the line "31 + 26 = 57" show.
- The idea used: tens go with tens and ones with ones, so the rods to put on are the difference of the tens and the
  cubes the difference of the ones.
