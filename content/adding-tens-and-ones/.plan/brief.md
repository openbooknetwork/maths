# Adding tens and ones

The brief of the story `adding-tens-and-ones`. `specs/stories/adding-tens-and-ones.md` was its rough map,
`research.md` holds what the sources say and `terms.md` the words the story gives its reader.

## Strategy

- **Subject:** adding and subtracting two-digit numbers in the head, by place value. Ten more and ten less, adding
  tens, adding tens and ones place by place, ten ones making a new ten, and subtracting tens and ones with a ten
  broken when the ones run short.
- **Level:** beginner. The reader knows `tens-and-ones` (a ten and ones, the tens digit and the ones digit, the value
  of a digit, breaking a ten, the rods and cubes on a place value chart) and `adding-within-20` (the number sentences
  of addition and subtraction, counting on and back, the difference, making ten).
- **At the end the reader can:** add ones to a two-digit number across the ten after, find ten more and ten less
  than a number up to 99, add a number of tens, add two
  two-digit numbers by adding tens to tens and ones to ones, bundle ten ones into a ten when the ones make ten or more,
  subtract tens and ones, break a ten when there are too few ones, and find a difference by jumping to the ten after
  and then in tens.
- **Family and figure:** `counters`, with the base ten blocks on a place value chart that `tens-and-ones` builds, and
  the number line of the family `line` from 0 to 100 with jumps of 10 and of 1. A hundred square shows ten more
  as the cell just below.
- **Interaction that suits the subject:** dragging rods and cubes on and off the chart, bundling ten cubes into a rod,
  breaking a rod into ten cubes, steppers for the numbers, jumps of 10 and of 1 on a line, and ten more on a
  hundred square.
- **Exercises that suit the subject:** the engine draws two numbers or a start and a target, and the reader builds
  the sum, the change or the take away on the chart with the number above it hidden.

## What the story takes from earlier stories

The story teaches no earlier idea again, as the user ruled on 2026-11-17. Where it needs an earlier idea, one page
goes over its logic.

- Tens and ones, the place value chart, bundling ten cubes into a rod and breaking a rod, from `tens-and-ones`. The first section
  goes over the chart in a sentence or two.
- The number sentences of addition and subtraction, counting on and back, and the difference found by counting on,
  from `adding-within-20`. The story uses them and points back where it counts on to find a difference.
- Making ten, from `adding-within-20`. Section 040 uses it for the ones that pass ten and points back to it.

## Sections

Page folders take the numbers of the section's range, in tens. World pages and challenges sit after the sections,
in the ranges given. Either the tens or the ones can be added first, and the texts say so where it matters.

### 010 `adding-ones` A two-digit number and ones

- **Pages:** 110 to 190. Opens the story at 110.
- **Needs:** tens and ones, making ten.
- **Teaches:** adding ones to a two-digit number changes its ones and keeps its tens, so 34 + 5 = 39. When the ones
  pass ten, the number crosses into the ten after: 38 + 5 makes the ten first, 38 + 2 = 40, then 40 + 3 = 43, as
  making ten did within 20. Taking ones away works the same way back.
- **Hands on:** a two-digit number and ones, crossing the ten after.
- **Arc:** the opening page is a bakery that packs biscuits in packets of ten, where biscuits outside the packets join the
  others and fill a new packet. No challenge.

### 020 `tens` Adding and taking away tens

- **Pages:** 210 to 290.
- **Needs:** a two-digit number and ones.
- **Teaches:** ten more makes the tens digit go up by 1 and leaves the ones digit as it was, and ten less makes it go down by
  1. On a hundred square the number ten more is in the cell just below. Tens add and take away like ones: 3 tens and 4
  tens make 7 tens, so 30 + 40 = 70, and 26 + 30 = 56 keeps its 6 ones. On a number line, jumps of ten from 34 land on
  44, 54, 64.
- **Hands on:** ten more and ten less, the hundred square, jumps of ten on a line.
- **Arc:** rods on the chart, the hundred square, then the line. No challenge.

### 030 `place-by-place` Adding tens and ones

- **Pages:** 310 to 390. Challenge at 710.
- **Needs:** adding tens.
- **Teaches:** to add two two-digit numbers, add the tens to the tens and the ones to the ones, so 31 + 26 is 5 tens
  and 7 ones, 57. On a line it is the first number, then jumps of ten, then jumps of one. While the ones add to 9 or
  less, each digit of the sum comes from its own place.
- **Hands on:** adding place by place.
- **Arc:** the challenge draws a start and an amount whose ones add to 9 or less, and the reader puts on the tens and
  ones that take the start to the target, with the number above the chart hidden.

### 040 `new-ten` Ones that make a new ten

- **Pages:** 410 to 490. World page at 610. Challenge at 720.
- **Needs:** adding tens and ones, crossing the ten after.
- **Teaches:** when the ones add to ten or more, ten of them make a new ten, so 38 + 25 is 6 tens and 3 ones, 63. The
  new ten has to be counted with the others, which is the step that is easy to forget. On a line, start at the first
  number and add the tens, then the ones, crossing the ten after.
- **Hands on:** a new ten made from ten ones in an addition.
- **Arc:** the world page is the points a basketball team scores in two halves. The challenge draws two numbers whose
  ones make ten or more, and the reader builds the sum on an empty chart with no more than 9 cubes in the ones column.

### 050 `subtracting` Subtracting tens and ones

- **Pages:** 510 to 590. World page at 620. Challenge at 730. Ends the story at 810.
- **Needs:** adding tens and ones, breaking a ten, the difference.
- **Teaches:** to subtract, take tens from the tens and ones from the ones, so 68 − 25 = 43. A number's ones are not
  only the extra ones: there are ten more inside each of its tens. To take 7 from 52, break one of its tens into ten
  ones first, and 52 is 4 tens and 12 ones, so 52 − 17 = 35. A difference can be found by counting on from the
  smaller number to the ten after, then in tens, then in ones.
- **Hands on:** nothing inside this story. The end page names `money` and `column-methods`.
- **Arc:** the world page is an album of stickers, with the stickers used on two days taken from the stickers in the
  album. The challenge draws a start and a target where a ten has to be broken, and the reader takes away to leave
  the target. It combines the story's ideas. The end page closes the story.

### Choices made in the user's place
- Ten more and ten less on the chart are the `tens` stepper, which puts on or takes off one rod, with the links
  `ten-more` and `ten-less` for the text. A button "Ten more" would repeat the stepper's act under another name.
- Ten more on the square is the square's jump of 10, which lands on the cell just below. The same two links light the
  cell just below and the cell just above, with one meaning in both scenes.
- `second` takes `{ tens, ones }` for pages that add tens or change both numbers, since a stepper of the second
  number one at a time needs 30 presses to add 30.
- `take` keeps the dashed outlines in their column, and a tap on the pile "Taken away" puts a piece back.
  `tens-and-ones` wrote that a tap fills an outline again. An outline in a column is narrower than a finger, and on
  the pages of 050 a tap on the tens column breaks a rod, so the tap sits on the pile.
- The line of a second number that waits writes the whole calculation open, "38 + 25", until every block has joined,
  as the seed packet of `counters/add` does, so a text can link the calculation as the page opens. The steps of
  making ten and the place lines show once every block has joined. On a `supply` page the reader builds the second
  number, so the place lines follow its blue pieces, settled at the order check after 030 and 040 read the two rules.
- The place lines of an addition add the tens and the ones of the two numbers and keep them after a bundle, as the
  terms' rule has "the tens" and "the ones" of a calculation. On a take away they read the columns as they stand, so a
  broken ten shows "40 − 10 = 30" and "12 − 7 = 5", the "4 tens and 12 ones" of 52 that 050 teaches.
- The new ten keeps the colours and dots of its cubes, so 8 red cubes and 2 blue ones show that it came from the ones
  of both numbers.
- On the line the split of a number shows as a tag over each run of jumps, "+20" and "+5", as children write it over
  an empty number line. Step lines would leave out the difference page, whose `written: difference` has one line.
- `written: ten` on the line has the meaning it has on `counters`, so 010 can take ones away across the ten before on
  the line without breaking a ten, which waits for 050.
- `build-sum` gives the reader a supply and an empty chart with the addition in the goal line. Dragging the two
  numbers' own blocks onto the chart would build the sum with no thought but the bundle.
- The scoreboard's Game box writes the column counts in two digit slots, so 5 tens and 13 ones read as a score the
  board cannot show until the ones are bundled, which is the step 040 calls easy to forget.
- The album takes the stickers of the two days one after the other, each day with its own line, since the brief takes
  the stickers of two days from the album. A piece that would pass a day's stickers goes back with a shake.
- The three goals hide the number, the digits, the values and the name, and accept a number only with 9 cubes or
  fewer, as `build-number` of `tens-and-ones` does.
- A line page with jumps of 1 keeps to 50 gaps, since a jump of 1 on a line of 100 gaps is 3.5px wide on a phone.
- "Makes ten", "Down to ten" and "The rest" move to the kit, since `line` reads them now as well as `counters`.
