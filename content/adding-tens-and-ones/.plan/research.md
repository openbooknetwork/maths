# Research for Adding tens and ones

The files named below sit in `content/adding-tens-and-ones/.references/`, except the curricula, which sit in
`.cache/curricula/`. Page numbers of PDFs are the pages of the PDF, which for the progression
`isbe.net-Documents-number-base-ten-k-5.txt` run one below the printed page. The text of that progression drops the
signs + and −, so its quotes leave those out. The IM files write equations in TeX, as in `\(37 + 25\)`, and the
quotes keep that form. The Oak lesson pages quote from their headings "Key learning points", "Keywords", "Common
misconception", "Teacher tip" and the quizzes.

What the curricula say, for all sections:

- US grade 1 adds within 100 by place: "Understand that in adding two-digit numbers, one adds tens and tens, ones
  and ones; and sometimes it is necessary to compose a ten." (us-common-core-math.pdf, page 16, 1.NBT.C.4). The same
  standard names the cases: "adding a two-digit number and a one-digit number, and adding a two-digit number and a
  multiple of 10".
- US grade 1, ten more: "Given a two-digit number, mentally find 10 more or 10 less than the number, without having
  to count; explain the reasoning used." (us-common-core-math.pdf, page 16, 1.NBT.C.5).
- US grade 1 subtracts only tens: "Subtract multiples of 10 in the range 10-90 from multiples of 10 in the range
  10-90 (positive or zero differences)" (us-common-core-math.pdf, page 16, 1.NBT.C.6).
- US grade 2: "Fluently add and subtract within 100 using strategies based on place value, properties of operations,
  and/or the relationship between addition and subtraction." (us-common-core-math.pdf, page 19, 2.NBT.B.5), and
  "sometimes it is necessary to compose or decompose tens or hundreds" (same page, 2.NBT.B.7), and "Mentally add 10
  or 100 to a given number 100–900" (same page, 2.NBT.B.8), and "Explain why addition and subtraction strategies
  work, using place value and the properties of operations." (same page, 2.NBT.B.9).
- England Year 2: "add and subtract numbers using concrete objects, pictorial representations, and mentally,
  including: a two-digit number and ones, a two-digit number and tens, two two-digit numbers"
  (england-ks1-2.pdf, page 12, Year 2, Number – addition and subtraction). Counting in tens: "count in steps of 2,
  3, and 5 from 0, and in tens from any number, forward and backward" (england-ks1-2.pdf, page 11).
- England Year 2 partitions for subtraction: "Pupils should partition numbers in different ways (for example,
  23 = 20 + 3 and 23 = 10 + 13) to support subtraction." (england-ks1-2.pdf, page 11, Notes and guidance).
- England Year 2 gives the words "sum and difference" (england-ks1-2.pdf, page 12, Notes and guidance) and puts
  columns after: "Recording addition and subtraction in columns supports place value and prepares for formal written
  methods with larger numbers." (same page).
- Japan grade 1: "To think about the ways to calculate simple cases of sums and differences involving 2-digit
  numbers." (japan-course-of-study-2008-en.pdf, page 7). Grade 2: "To understand that the addition and subtraction
  of 2-digit numbers are based on the basic addition and subtraction of single-digit numbers" and "To understand
  relative size of numbers by regarding 10 or 100 as a unit." (japan-course-of-study-2008-en.pdf, page 8).
- Bremen, end of year 2: "lösen Additions- und Subtraktionsaufgaben im Zahlenraum bis 100 unter Nutzung von
  Rechengesetzen" (germany-bremen-primary-2025.pdf, page 40), with "Bündeln und Entbündeln von Mengen" (page 20)
  and the difference found by adding on: "(z. B. Ergänzen bei 63-59)" (page 23).

## 010 adding-ones

- Order: facts within ten first, then bonds to ten that land on a multiple of ten, then bridging it, with subtraction
  beside addition at each step. "Teaching point 2: Known facts for the numbers within ten can be applied to
  addition/subtraction of a single-digit number to/from a two-digit number. Teaching point 3: Knowledge of numbers
  which sum to ten can be applied to the addition of a single-digit number and two-digit number that sum to a
  multiple of ten, or subtraction of a single-digit number from a multiple of ten. Teaching point 4: Known strategies
  for addition or subtraction bridging ten can be applied to addition or subtraction bridging a multiple of ten."
  (ncetm.org.uk-lassroom-resources-primm-113-addition-and-subtraction-two-digit-and-single-digit-numbers.txt,
  Teaching points). Topic 1.13 builds on the work within 20: "Build on segments 1.8, 1.9 and 1.10 to equip children
  with useful strategies for addition and subtraction of a single-digit number to/from two-digit numbers." (same
  file, Introduction).
- Order: NCETM's Year 2 unit gives each step with its subtraction after it: "4 Pupils use number facts to add a
  single-digit number to a two-digit number", "5 Pupils use number facts to subtract a single-digit number from a
  two-digit number", "7 Pupils use number bonds to ten to add a single-digit number to a two-digit number", "8 Pupils
  use number bonds to ten to subtract a single-digit number from a two-digit number", "9 Pupils use knowledge of
  ‘make ten’ to add a one-digit number to a two-digit number", "10 Pupils use knowledge of ‘make ten’ to subtract a
  multiple of ten or a single-digit from a two-digit number"
  (ncetm.org.uk-classroom-resources-cp-year-2-unit-4-addition-and-subtraction-of-two-digit-numbers.txt, Learning
  outcomes).
- Order: Oak's unit has the same order. The lesson on bonds to ten sits between "5 Use number facts to solve problems
  in measures and data contexts" and "7 Add by bridging a multiple of ten"
  (thenational.academy-rs-lessons-use-number-bonds-to-10-to-add-subtract-one-digit-and-two-digit-numbers.txt, end of
  page), and "Subtract by bridging a multiple of ten" sits between "7 Add by bridging a multiple of ten" and "9 Use
  bridging to solve addition and subtraction problems"
  (thenational.academy-d-tens-to-and-from-2-digit-numbers-lessons-subtract-by-bridging-a-multiple-of-ten.txt, end of
  page).
- Order: IM grade 1 first adds without a new ten, "students add a two-digit number and a one- or two-digit number
  without composing a ten" (curriculum.illustrativemathematics.org-k5-teachers-grade-1-course-guide-scope-and-sequence.txt,
  Unit 5, Section A), then "Section B: Make a Ten: Add One- and Two-digit Numbers" with the goal "Add a one-digit and
  a two-digit number within 100 with composing a ten." (same file, Unit 5, Section B).
- Order: the tens stay while no ten is made, as within 20. "They notice that the unit of ten doesn’t change and
  relate the sum to the adding or subtracting of ones." (same file, Unit 3, Section B, on "\(13-2\) and \(12+5\)").
- Order: making ten carries over from within 20. "They draw on the idea of making a ten, which they learned when
  adding ones and when adding numbers within 20." (same file, Unit 5, Section B). Within 20: "to find the value of
  \(9 + 5\), students can take 1 from the 5 and group it with the 9 to make 10, and then add the 4." (same file,
  Unit 3, Section C). "Students who rely on using known facts to add within 20 may apply the same method when adding
  within 100." (same file, Unit 5, Section B).
- Order: the rule on the ones digits. "When the ones digits in an equation sum to ten, the sum in the equation will
  always be a multiple of ten." and "The number being subtracted and the ones digit in the answer will be a number
  bond to ten."
  (thenational.academy-rs-lessons-use-number-bonds-to-10-to-add-subtract-one-digit-and-two-digit-numbers.txt, Key
  learning points).
- Order: going down to the ten before. "When we subtract a one-digit from a two-digit number, the equation may be
  solved more efficiently by 'bridging ten'." and "When bridging a multiple of ten, the number that is subtracted is
  partitioned to reach the previous multiple of ten."
  (thenational.academy-d-tens-to-and-from-2-digit-numbers-lessons-subtract-by-bridging-a-multiple-of-ten.txt, Key
  learning points).
- Examples: a fact within ten in a story. "Alex’s packet of seeds weighed 67 g and Sam’s weighed 4 g less." with
  "Correct answer: 7 − 4 = 3", and "there was 73 ml of water in the jug. Alex adds 5 ml more."
  (thenational.academy-rs-lessons-use-number-bonds-to-10-to-add-subtract-one-digit-and-two-digit-numbers.txt, Prior
  knowledge starter quiz, Q1 and Q2).
- Examples: bonds to ten that land on a ten. "Alex represents the equation 34 + 6 = ___ Which known fact could he use
  to solve this efficiently?" with "Correct answer: 4 + 6 = 10", "76 + 4 = ___" with "80", and the patterns "63 + 7 =
  70, 73 + 7 = 80, 83 + 7 = 90 ..." and "41 + 9 = 50, 42 + 8 = 50, 43 + 7 = 50 ..." (same file, Assessment exit quiz,
  Q1, Q3, Q4 and Q5).
- Examples: bridging. "How would 5 be partitioned to ‘bridge 10’ in this equation? 8 + 5 = 13" with "Correct answer:
  2 and 3", "18 + 8 = ?" with "Partition 8 into 2 and 6", "27 + 6 =" with "33", and "38 + = 43 What is the missing
  addend?" with "5" (thenational.academy-d-tens-to-and-from-2-digit-numbers-lessons-subtract-by-bridging-a-multiple-of-ten.txt,
  Prior knowledge starter quiz, Q1, Q2, Q5 and Q6). Taking away: "24 − 5 = ___" with "Partition 5 into 4 and 1", and
  "64 − 7 =" with "57", with ten frames and number lines (same file, Assessment exit quiz, Q1 to Q6).
- Examples: cubes. "Provide access to connecting cubes in towers of 10 and singles throughout the unit."
  (curriculum.illustrativemathematics.org-k5-teachers-grade-1-course-guide-scope-and-sequence.txt, Unit 5), and "They
  may show this awareness by making a new tower of 10 with connecting cubes or by marking or labeling a group of 10
  in their base-ten drawings." (same file, Unit 5, Section B). The count on that section shows: "Counting on: 68, 69,
  70, 74" (same file, Unit 5, Section B).
- Words: "Bridge - A strategy that involves using a multiple of ten as a 'bridge' so numbers can be partitioned to
  make use of bonds to ten and calculate efficiently." and "Partition - To split a whole up into parts."
  (thenational.academy-d-tens-to-and-from-2-digit-numbers-lessons-subtract-by-bridging-a-multiple-of-ten.txt,
  Keywords), and "the previous multiple of ten" (same file, Key learning points). "Sum - The result of adding two or
  more numbers" and "Difference - The result of subtracting one number from another."
  (thenational.academy-rs-lessons-use-number-bonds-to-10-to-add-subtract-one-digit-and-two-digit-numbers.txt,
  Keywords). NCETM: "‘make ten’" and "bridging a multiple of ten"
  (ncetm.org.uk-classroom-resources-cp-year-2-unit-4-addition-and-subtraction-of-two-digit-numbers.txt and
  ncetm.org.uk-lassroom-resources-primm-113-addition-and-subtraction-two-digit-and-single-digit-numbers.txt). IM:
  "compose a ten from ones" and "the next ten"
  (curriculum.illustrativemathematics.org-k5-teachers-grade-1-course-guide-scope-and-sequence.txt, Unit 5,
  Section B).
- Words: the sentence a child says. "We know that 5 plus 5 is equal to 10, so we also know that plus 5 is equal to
  30" (thenational.academy-rs-lessons-use-number-bonds-to-10-to-add-subtract-one-digit-and-two-digit-numbers.txt,
  Assessment exit quiz, Q2), and "'I know that 1 + 9 = 10, so I also know that....'" (same file, Teacher tip).
- Errors: counting without the ten. "Students may not initially consider composing a new unit of ten when adding
  numbers. They may count on by ones, or count on by place (count on by tens and by ones separately). They may also
  count on to the next ten, but not connect their counting to a new unit of ten."
  (curriculum.illustrativemathematics.org-k5-teachers-grade-1-course-guide-scope-and-sequence.txt, Unit 5,
  Section B).
- Errors: the wrong ten before. "Children may think the multiple of ten before 43 is 30, or partition the subtrahend
  incorrectly when bridging, meaning they do not use the most efficient strategy." The advice: "Display and use the
  stem sentences to support articulation of how to find the previous multiple of ten. Use ten frames, number lines
  and one hundred squares to support with appropriate partitioning of numbers."
  (thenational.academy-d-tens-to-and-from-2-digit-numbers-lessons-subtract-by-bridging-a-multiple-of-ten.txt, Common
  misconception). The same slip from counting in tens: "they may think 30 is before 40, so think 30 is the multiple
  of ten before 45", with the advice "Make deliberate errors to discuss and encourage children to visualise, then
  label, their own number lines marked in tens."
  (thenational.academy-ers-20-to-99-lessons-use-a-number-line-to-position-and-estimate-the-numbers-20-99.txt, Common
  misconception).
- Errors: the ten not taken. "children may use their number bonds to ten to find the correct ones digit, but may
  forget to consider the previous multiple of ten, e.g. say 40-2=48". The advice: "use their knowledge of decades to
  estimate (e.g. I know 40-2 will be in the thirties), then prove it using representations that highlight the tens
  structure, e.g. one hundred squares, ten frames and number lines."
  (thenational.academy-rs-lessons-use-number-bonds-to-10-to-add-subtract-one-digit-and-two-digit-numbers.txt, Common
  misconception).
- Errors: what to ask for. "Encourage children to focus on the numbers involved and to predict when solving
  equations, articulating their thinking and working towards generalisations based on the digits in the numbers
  involved." (thenational.academy-d-tens-to-and-from-2-digit-numbers-lessons-subtract-by-bridging-a-multiple-of-ten.txt,
  Teacher tip).

## 020 tens

- Order: ten more after a two-digit number and ones. In Oak's Year 2 unit the lesson "Find 10 more or less than a
  2-digit number" follows lesson "9 Use bridging to solve addition and subtraction problems" and comes before "11 Add
  and subtract 10 to and from a 2-digit number."
  (thenational.academy-ns-to-and-from-2-digit-numbers-lessons-find-10-more-or-less-than-a-2-digit-number.txt, end
  of page).
- Order: NCETM's Year 2 unit has the same order. Outcomes 1 to 11 add and subtract ones, then "12 Pupils find ten more
  or ten less than a two-digit number (1)", then "14 Pupils add and subtract ten to/from a two-digit number" and "15
  Pupils explain the patterns when adding and subtracting ten"
  (ncetm.org.uk-classroom-resources-cp-year-2-unit-4-addition-and-subtraction-of-two-digit-numbers.txt). Topic 1.13

- Order: IM grade 1 teaches ten more at the end of the unit on numbers to 99: "Later in the section, students use

  Unit 4, Section B).
- Order: the rule on the digits. "Teaching point 1: When finding ten more or ten less than any two-digit number, the

  ten than before." (isbe.net-Documents-number-base-ten-k-5.txt, page 7).

  Assessment exit quiz, Q1 and Q2). The same lesson adds a difference of ten: "When numbers have the same ones digit

- Order: tens added like ones, from facts within ten. "Teaching point 3: Knowledge of number facts within ten can be

## 030 place-by-place

- Order: first without a new ten, then with one. IM grade 1: "Students begin by adding a two-digit number with
  another two-digit number or with a one-digit number where it is not necessary to compose a new ten. Then, they
  observe cases in which adding some ones together require composing a new ten."
  (curriculum.illustrativemathematics.org-k5-teachers-grade-1-course-guide-scope-and-sequence.txt, Unit 5). NCETM
  Year 2: "3 Pupils add a two-digit number to a two-digit number when not crossing ten (i)" before "5 Pupils add a
  two-digit number to a two-digit number when crossing ten"
  (ncetm.org.uk-classroom-resources-cp-year-2-unit-8-addition-and-subtraction-of-two-digit-numbers.txt).
- Order: two methods. "Two broad methods for finding sums are explored: adding on by place (adding on tens, then
  ones), and adding units by place (combining tens with tens and ones with ones)."
  (curriculum.illustrativemathematics.org-k5-teachers-grade-1-course-guide-scope-and-sequence.txt, Unit 5). NCETM:
  "Teaching point 2: Two two-digit numbers can be added by partitioning one or both of them into tens and ones."
  (ncetm.org.uk-classroom-resources-primm-115-addition-two-digit-and-two-digit-numbers.txt).
- Order: the general method in the progression. "Adding tens and ones separately as illustrated in the margin is a
  general method that can extend to any sum of multi-digit numbers." (isbe.net-Documents-number-base-ten-k-5.txt,
  page 7).
- Order: either place first. "Either the tens or the ones can be added first, the sum will remain the same."

## Against the brief

These points were read against the first draft of the brief. Each was settled at the final brief, and
`decisions.md` says how.

- The order of ten more and the ones: Oak and NCETM teach a two-digit number and ones, with bridging a multiple of
  ten, before ten more. "9 Use bridging to solve addition and subtraction problems" precedes the ten more lesson
  (thenational.academy-ns-to-and-from-2-digit-numbers-lessons-find-10-more-or-less-than-a-2-digit-number.txt), and
  NCETM's outcomes 1 to 11 come before "12 Pupils find ten more or ten less than a two-digit number (1)"
  (ncetm.org.uk-classroom-resources-cp-year-2-unit-4-addition-and-subtraction-of-two-digit-numbers.txt). The brief
  opens on ten more.
- A two-digit number and ones: the US, England and IM name it as its own case. "adding a two-digit number and a
  one-digit number" (us-common-core-math.pdf, page 16, 1.NBT.C.4), "a two-digit number and ones"
  (england-ks1-2.pdf, page 12), "Section B: Make a Ten: Add One- and Two-digit Numbers" before "Section C: Make a Ten:
  Add Within 100" (curriculum.illustrativemathematics.org-k5-teachers-grade-1-course-guide-scope-and-sequence.txt,
  Unit 5). The brief has no section or page for it, and section 040 goes from making ten straight to 38 + 25.
- Subtracting tens: NCETM and Oak teach taking tens away beside adding them: "18 Pupils use number facts to subtract a
  multiple of ten from a two-digit number"
  (ncetm.org.uk-classroom-resources-cp-year-2-unit-4-addition-and-subtraction-of-two-digit-numbers.txt) and "Subtract
  multiples of ten from 2-digit numbers"
  (thenational.academy-o-and-from-2-digit-numbers-lessons-subtract-multiples-of-ten-from-2-digit-numbers.txt). The
  brief's section 020 adds tens only, and taking tens away appears first in 050.
- Which number to split in a subtraction: NCETM splits only the number taken away: "partitioning the subtrahend into
  tens and ones" (ncetm.org.uk-classroom-resources-primm-116-subtraction-two-digit-and-two-digit-numbers.txt). IM
  breaks a ten on the blocks of the whole: "decompose 1 ten from 63 and exchange it for 10 ones"
  (curriculum.illustrativemathematics.org-k5-teachers-grade-2-course-guide-scope-and-sequence.txt, Unit 2). The
  brief's 52 − 17 follows IM. The progression adds that breaking first "can help to inhibit the common error of
  subtracting a smaller digit on the top from a larger digit" (isbe.net-Documents-number-base-ten-k-5.txt, page 9).
- Which number to split in an addition that crosses a ten: Oak advises "partition only one addend"
  (thenational.academy-f-two-2-digit-numbers-lessons-add-2-digit-numbers-when-crossing-the-tens-boundary.txt), since
  splitting both leads children to "forget to add the extra 10 made when combining the ones". IM and the progression
  combine tens with tens and ones with ones and compose the new ten, as the brief does
  (isbe.net-Documents-number-base-ten-k-5.txt, page 7, "view 6+7 as 1 ten and 3 ones").
- The words for a ten that runs short: the brief says "When there are too few ones, one ten breaks into ten ones
  first". Kappan warns: "If they hear “we don’t have enough 1’s” whenever these problems are addressed, they begin to
  believe it." (kappanonline.org-wp-content-uploads-2019-08-PDK-95-2-Faulkner-59-tbl2.txt), and CESD lists "“There
  aren’t enough ones so we have to regroup.”" as a statement "that builds immediate misconceptions"
  (sites.google.com-cesd73.ca-cesd-math-framework-all-concepts-additive-thinking-at-stop-saying.txt). Both advise
  saying that the ones sit inside the tens.
- The words for the swap: the sources say "compose a ten" (us-common-core-math.pdf, page 16), "exchange it for 10
  ones" (IM grade 2 scope file), "Regroup, exchange or trade" (CESD file), "Unbundle" (isbe.net-Documents-number-base-ten-k-5.txt,
  page 15) and "bridging 10" (Oak crossing file). "carry" appears only as wording the progression sets aside, "rather
  than “write the 3 and carry the 1”" (isbe.net-Documents-number-base-ten-k-5.txt, page 9), and "borrow" as wording
  CESD advises against. The brief's "swap" and "break" match none of them, and `tens-and-ones` already fixes "breaking
  a ten".
- Adding 9 as a ten and one back: NCETM teaches this compensation in Year 5, "Using equivalence and the compensation
  property to calculate ... Topic 1.29"
  (ncetm.org.uk-stery-materials-primary-mastery-professional-development-number-addition-and-subtraction.txt), and
  the progression calls such strategies "practical in limited cases" (isbe.net-Documents-number-base-ten-k-5.txt,
  page 9). No read source teaches adding 9 this way at this level. The brief puts it in 040.
- The difference through the next ten: the brief jumps from 27 to 30, 60 and 63. Oak advises counting on "only ...
  when it is an efficient choice (when the numbers being compared are close in value)"
  (thenational.academy-nits-calculating-within-20-lessons-calculate-the-difference-in-different-contexts.txt, Teacher
  tip). The progression's adding on, "first add 6 to make 190, then add 10 to make 200", jumps to the next ten first
  as the brief does (isbe.net-Documents-number-base-ten-k-5.txt, page 9).
- Subtraction in grade 1: the US keeps two-digit subtraction other than whole tens for grade 2, "First graders are not
  expected to compute diﬀerences of two-digit numbers other than multiples of ten." (isbe.net-Documents-number-base-ten-k-5.txt,
  page 7), and US grade 1 takes tens only "in the range 10-90" (us-common-core-math.pdf, page 16, 1.NBT.C.6). The
  brief teaches addition and subtraction in one story, which fits grade 2 and England Year 2.
- The order of compare and take away: IM grade 2 starts two-digit subtraction with compare problems, "Students begin
  by solving Compare story problems."
  (curriculum.illustrativemathematics.org-k5-teachers-grade-2-course-guide-scope-and-sequence.txt, Unit 2, Section
  A). The brief takes away first and gives the difference the last page of 050.
- Tens first or ones first: Oak lets either come first, "Either the tens or the ones can be added first"
  (thenational.academy-2-digit-numbers-lessons-add-two-digit-numbers-without-crossing-the-tens-boundary.txt), and
  warns of children who think the order is fixed
  (thenational.academy-2-digit-numbers-lessons-solve-problems-involving-subtraction-of-two-digit-numbers.txt). Japan's
  worksheet adds the ones first (jica file, page 1). The brief puts tens first in every section.
