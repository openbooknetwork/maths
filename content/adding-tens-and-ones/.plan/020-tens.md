## Content

### 210 Each ten added is ten more
The idea: ten more than a number is the number that is 10 greater, and ten less than a number is the number that is
10 less. Ten more has one more ten than the number and the same ones, so its tens digit goes up by 1 and its ones
digit stays the same. Ten less has 1 ten fewer, so its tens digit goes down by 1. One rod put on does it, with no ten
ones to count. Adding more tens adds one ten more each time, and the tens add as the facts within 10 do, so 2 tens
and 3 tens make 5 tens, as 2 + 3 = 5. The ones stay as they are.

The example: 26 + 30. 26 is 2 tens and 6 ones. The first ten added gives 3 tens and 6 ones, 36, so ten more than 26
is 36, and 36 is ten more than 26. Ten less than 26 is 1 ten and 6 ones, 16. 30 is 3 tens, and 2 tens and 3 tens
make 5 tens, so 26 + 30 is 5 tens and 6 ones, 56. The figure writes the whole number sentence, and the text leaves the
steps 20 + 30 = 50 and 50 + 6 = 56 to page 230, which writes its own. The 6 in the ones place stays the same in 16, 26, 36, 46 and 56. The text says ten more both ways round
once, so the greater number is not always said second.

The red blocks are the first number, 26, and the blue rods with dots are the tens added.

### 220 Ten more is the cell just below
The idea: on the hundred square each row holds ten numbers, so a jump of 10 lands on the cell just below, as
`skip-counting` showed. The number in the cell just below is ten more, and the number in the cell just above is ten
less. A column of the square keeps its ones digit, and each row down the tens digit goes up by 1. Ten more than 90 is
100, the last cell, and ten less than 10 is 0, on the tile left of cell 1, which is outside
the rule of the cell just above. A number in the top row has no number
ten less on the square.

The example: 58. The cell just below 58 holds 68, so ten more than 58 is 68. The cell just above it holds 48, so ten
less than 58 is 48. The ones digit 8 stays the same in the whole column 8, 18, 28 and on to 98.

### 230 Take tens away from the tens
The idea: tens are taken from tens the way ones are taken from ones, so 3 tens taken from 7 tens leave 4 tens, as
7 − 3 = 4. Subtracting tens from a two-digit number changes its tens and keeps its ones. Taking away 1 ten finds ten
less.

The example: 75 − 30. 75 is 7 tens and 5 ones, and 30 is 3 tens. Subtract 3 tens from 7 tens, and 4 tens are left.
The 5 ones stay, so 75 − 30 = 45. As steps: 70 − 30 = 40, then 40 + 5 = 45. The first rod taken leaves 65, ten less
than 75. On the chart the reader takes away 3 rods, and numbers are subtracted, so the text says "take away 3 rods"
of the blocks and "subtract 30" of the numbers. The tens digit 4 and the ones digit 5 of 45 differ, so the reader
can tell the ones digit that stayed from the tens digit that changed.

The red blocks are 75, and each rod taken away leaves its dashed outline, so the column still shows the 7 tens the
number had.

### 240 Add tens in jumps of 10
The idea: on a number line, a jump of 10 to the right adds 10 and lands on ten more, and a jump of 10 to the left
subtracts 10 and lands on ten less. Adding 30 is three jumps of 10, and every number they land on has the same ones
digit. The jumps give the answer one ten at a time, and the facts of 210 give it at once.

The example: from 56, jumps of 10 land on 66, 76 and 86, so 56 + 30 = 86. The ones digit 6 stays the same at each
landing, and the tens digit goes up by 1 at each jump. 56 is 5 tens and 6 ones, and 5 tens and 3 tens make 8 tens,
so 86 comes without the jumps as well. A jump to the left from 56 lands on 46, so 56 − 10 = 46.

The start 56 is the sum of 210, so the text may say the line goes on from there.

## Interactivity

### 210
- Shows: the place value chart with 2 red rods and 6 red cubes, the digits 2 and 6, the number 26 above the chart,
  and the stepper "Tens added" from 0 to 6 at 0. Under the chart the written line "26 + 0 = 26". The readouts "Sum"
  at 26, "Ten more" at 36 and "Ten less" at 16. No supply and no Bundle button.
- The reader: reads "Ten more", then steps "Tens added" up once and finds the sum is the number "Ten more" named. The
  reader steps up twice more, to 3 tens added, and reads the written line, then steps down to take tens off again.
- The figure: each step up puts one blue rod with a dot on each of its ten cubes at the end of the tens column, after
  the red rods. The tens digit and the tens digit of the number go up by 1, and the 6 red cubes and the ones digit
  stay as they are. The written line follows: "26 + 10 = 36", "26 + 20 = 46", "26 + 30 = 56". "Sum" reads 36, 46,
  56, and "Ten more" and "Ten less" follow the sum: 46 and 26 at 36, 66 and 46 at 56. So each step makes the number
  that "Ten more" named before it, and a step down makes the number "Ten less" named. A step down takes the last blue
  rod off. At 6 tens added the tens column holds 8 rods, the number reads 86, "Ten more" reads 96, and the
  stepper rests. The stepper stops at 6 so that "Ten more" always has a number to show. Pointing at the link of "Ten more" or "Ten less" lights the tens column.
- Action: add tens one rod at a time, and each rod makes the ten more the readout named, while the written line
  counts the tens added and the ones stay.

### 220
- Shows: the hundred square with the start ring and the point on 58, and the dashed tile for 0 left of the cell 1.
  The buttons Jump back and Jump, the "Start" stepper at 58, and the size of a jump 10. The readouts "Ten more" at 68
  and "Ten less" at 48. No count line.
- The reader: points at "Ten more" and "Ten less" to light the cell just below 58 and the cell just above it, then
  taps other cells, such as 90 and 13, and reads their ten more and ten less. Jump and Jump back check a reading.
- The figure: a tap on a cell, or a step of the "Start" stepper, moves the start and the point there and clears the
  jumps. "Ten more" and "Ten less" read the numbers in the cell just below and the cell just above the point: 100 and
  80 on 90, 23 and 3 on 13, a dash for "Ten less" on a cell of the top row such as 7, 0 on 10 with the tile of 0 lit,
  and a dash for "Ten more" on a cell of the last row such as 95. The link of "Ten more" lights the cell just below
  the point and the link of "Ten less" the cell just above it. Jump moves the point to the cell just below and Jump
  back to the cell just above, as on `skip-counting` page 220, and the readouts follow the point.
- Action: pick a cell, and the cells just below and just above it hold its ten more and its ten less.

### 230
- Shows: the chart with 7 red rods and 5 red cubes, the digits 7 and 5, the number 75 above the chart, and beside
  the chart the pile "Taken away", empty, with one rod and one cube drawn at 0. Under the chart the written line
  "75 − 0 = 75". The readouts "Taken away" at 0 and "Left" at 75. No supply, no stepper, no Bundle button.
- The reader: takes away 3 rods, by a drag of the tens column off the chart or a tap on it, and reads the written
  line. A tap on the pile's rod puts one back.
- The figure: each rod taken glides to the pile and leaves its dashed outline at its place in the tens column, after
  the rods still on. The pile's number of rods counts it. The tens digit and the number follow the rods on the chart,
  and the 5 cubes and the ones digit stay. The written line follows: "75 − 10 = 65", "75 − 20 = 55",
  "75 − 30 = 45". "Taken away" reads 30 and "Left" 45. After 3 rods the tens column shows 4 rods and 3 outlines, so
  the 7 tens of 75 still show. A tap on the pile's rod puts the last rod taken back into its outline. A drag or a tap
  on the ones column takes a cube the same way, and the line then reads, for one cube, "75 − 31 = 44".
- Action: take rods off the chart, and each leaves an outline while the written line subtracts the tens taken.

### 240
- Shows: a number line from 0 to 100 with a mark at every number and labels at 0, 10, 20 and on to 100, the start
  ring and the point on 56, the buttons Jump left and Jump right, the size of a jump 10, and under the line the
  written line "56 + 0 = 56". The readout "Jumps" at 0.
- The reader: presses Jump right three times, then Jump left until the point is left of the start, and may drag the
  start ring to another mark and jump again.
- The figure: each press of Jump right draws an arc over ten gaps and the point lands 10 further on, at 66, 76, 86.
  The written line follows: "56 + 10 = 66", "56 + 20 = 76", "56 + 30 = 86". A fourth jump lands on 96, and Jump right
  then rests. The line runs from 46 to 96 with a mark every 10, so every mark is labelled and readable on a phone. Each press of Jump left moves the point 10 back: from 86 to 76, 66, 56, where the line reads
  "56 + 0 = 56", and then to 46, where it reads "56 − 10 = 46". "Jumps" counts the jumps the point stands from the
  start. A drag of the start ring moves the start and the point to another mark and clears the jumps.
- Action: jump in tens from a number, and the written line adds or subtracts the tens the jumps cover.
