Two numbers change the shape of a wave. The amplitude A is the radius of the circle. It sets how high the wave climbs and how low it falls. The frequency k says how fast the point turns. The point goes round k times while x runs through one turn.

The wave is y = A sin(kx). Here A is [1.5](@amplitude) and k is [2](@frequency). At x = [30°](@angle) the point has turned [60°](@turn), so

[y = A sin(kx) = 1.5 sin(2 × 30°) = 1.5 sin 60° ≈ 1.3](@formula-y)

One whole wave takes the x in which the point goes round once. That length is the period T, marked above the graph:

[T = 360°/k = 360°/2 = 180°](@formula-period)

Move the sliders. A larger A makes the wave taller. A larger k packs more waves into the same width. The dashed curve is y = sin x, to compare with.
