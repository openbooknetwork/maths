A shift moves the whole wave sideways. In y = sin(x + φ) the point does not start at 0°. It starts at the angle φ, the hollow dot on the circle, and the wave starts at that dot's height.

Here φ is [90°](@phase). At x = [30°](@angle) the point has turned to [120°](@turn):

[y = sin(x + φ) = sin(30° + 90°) = sin 120° ≈ 0.87](@formula-y)

Move the shift slider and watch the wave slide over the dashed sine wave. At 90° it lies on the cosine wave of the last page but one. At 180° it is the sine wave upside down.

Try a few shifts, and each time move the x slider up from 0°. The point turns the same way every time, and only its starting place changes.
