This circle has radius 1 and its centre at the origin. The point P sits on the circle, turned [30°](@angle) anticlockwise from the right end of the horizontal axis.

Drop a line from P to the axis. It makes a right triangle whose longest side is the radius, 1. The height of P is the sine of the angle, and its reach across is the cosine. Here the height is [0.5](@sin) and the reach across is [0.87](@cos).

An angle can also be measured in radians. The angle in radians is the length of the arc from the axis to P on a circle of radius 1. A whole turn is 2π, about 6.28.

[θ = 30° = 30 × π/180 = π/6 ≈ 0.52](@formula-radians)

The two legs and the radius obey Pythagoras' theorem, so they always fit together:

[sin² 30° + cos² 30° = 0.25 + 0.75 = 1](@formula-pythagoras)

Drag P round the circle, or move the θ slider under the figure. Below the axis the height is negative, and on the left the reach across is negative.
