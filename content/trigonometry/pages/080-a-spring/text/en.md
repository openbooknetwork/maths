Hang a weight on a spring, pull it down and let it go. It bobs up and down, and its height draws the same wave as the point on the circle.

Drag P round the circle. The weight stays level with P as P goes round. Now θ is [90°](@angle), and both stand at height [1](@sin).

Drag P round at a steady pace and follow the weight with your eye. It moves fastest through the middle and slows to a stop at the top and the bottom, just like the height of P.

Many things that swing back and forth draw this wave over time: a pendulum, a guitar string, the air in front of a loudspeaker. That is why sine waves turn up all over science.
