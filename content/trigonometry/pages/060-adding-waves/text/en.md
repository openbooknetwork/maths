Two waves can be added. Put a second circle on the point of the first. Its arm is A long and turns k times as fast. The tip of the second arm draws the sum of the two waves.

Here A is [0.5](@amplitude) and k is [3](@frequency). At x = [30°](@angle) the first wave is at [0.5](@first) and the second wave is at [0.5](@second), so the tip is at [1](@y):

[y = sin x + A sin(kx) = sin 30° + 0.5 sin(3 × 30°) = 0.5 + 0.5 × 1 = 1](@formula-y)

The faint curves are the two waves on their own. At every x the dark curve is their sum. On the graph the second bar stands on top of the first.

Set A to 0.3 and keep k at 3. The sum gets flatter tops. This is a first step towards a square wave. Sound works this way too. The note of a voice or a guitar is many waves added together.
