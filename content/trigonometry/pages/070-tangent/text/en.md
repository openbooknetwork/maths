The radius from the centre to P is a slope. It rises by the sine and runs by the cosine, so its steepness is their ratio, the tangent:

[tan 45° = sin 45° / cos 45° ≈ 0.71/0.71 = 1](@formula-tan)

Carry the radius on until it meets the dashed line that touches the circle on the right. It meets that line at the height tan θ, at the point T. Here θ is [45°](@angle) and tan θ is [1](@tan). At 45° the radius rises as much as it runs.

Drag P up towards 90°. The radius gets steeper and T climbs faster and faster. At 90° the radius stands upright and never meets the line, so tan 90° has no value. Just past 90°, T comes back from the bottom.

The graph shows these jumps. The dashed lines at 90° and 270° mark where tan x has no value. Between them the same curve repeats every half turn, 180°.
