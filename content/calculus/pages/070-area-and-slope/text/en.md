Start the area at x = 0 and sweep it to the right. The bottom view plots how much area you have swept so far. That is the area curve.

At x = [1](@x) the area is [1.5](@area). Move x and watch the area curve grow.

Look at how steeply it grows. The tangent on the area curve has slope [1](@slope), and the line on top has height [1](@height) at the same x. Each small step to the right adds a thin strip as tall as the line, so the slope of the area curve is the height of the line above it.

Past x = 2 the line dips below the axis. Area under the axis counts as negative, so the area curve falls.

Taking the slope turns a curve into its slope curve, and sweeping the area turns it back. This is the fundamental theorem of calculus.
