Every point of a curve has its own slope. Plot each slope as a height and you get a second curve, the slope curve. The bottom view draws it as you move x.

Now x = [−2.5](@x) and the slope there is [3.19](@slope). Drag P to the right, on either view, and draw the slope curve.

While the curve climbs, its slope is above 0 and the slope curve stays above the axis. The steeper the climb, the higher the slope curve.

At the top of the hump the tangent is flat. The slope is 0 there, so the slope curve crosses the axis right under it. The same happens at the bottom of the dip. Between the two the curve falls and the slope curve drops below the axis.
