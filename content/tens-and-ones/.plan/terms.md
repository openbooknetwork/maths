# Terms of Tens and ones

File names without a folder are in `content/tens-and-ones/.references/`.

## 010 ten-ones
- Introduces:
  - `ten, one (numbers)`: a ten is a group of 10 ones taken as one thing, as a bundle of 10 pencils or a rod,
    and a one is a single object, as one pencil or one cube. A number of them is written in digits, "1 ten",
    "4 tens", "1 one", "2 ones", and 4 tens are 40 ones. A new sense beside `ten, one (numbers)`, which names the numbers
    10, 20, 30 of the count. Oak gives the reader "Tens - A ten is a group of 10 ones." and "Ones - Individual
    objects, grouped in ones."
    (`thenational.academy-11-to-19-lessons-explain-that-the-digits-in-the-numbers-11-to-19-express-quantity.txt`,
    Keywords), and US 1.NBT.B.2a "10 can be thought of as a bundle of ten ones — called a “ten.”"
    (`.cache/curricula/us-common-core-math.pdf`, page 15). The DfE guidance moves from "1 group of 10" to "1 ten"
    (`assets.publishing.service.gov.uk-ads-system-uploads-attachment-data-file-954511-Maths-guidance-year-1.txt`,
    page 21, 1NF–2), so the glossary's `group of 2, 5, 10, 100 (objects)` holds and a ten is a group of 10 counted
    as one. A number is said as its tens and its ones with "and", "4 tens and 2 ones", as the DfE and IM write it,
    over the US "3 tens 2 ones".
  - `bundle (place value)`: put ten ones together so that they make one ten, as tying 10 pencils with a band or
    joining 10 cubes into a rod. The ten made so is a bundle. Bundling keeps the number the same. US 1.NBT.B.2a
    "a bundle of ten ones" (`.cache/curricula/us-common-core-math.pdf`, page 15) and England's "These sticks are
    grouped into bundles of 10."
    (`assets.publishing.service.gov.uk-ads-system-uploads-attachment-data-file-954511-Maths-guidance-year-1.txt`,
    page 22, 1NF–2). Chosen over the brief's "gather" and the verb "group", since "bundle" names both the action and
    the ten it makes, as the progression's "bundling in groups of 10" does
    (`isbe.net-Documents-number-base-ten-k-5.txt`, page 8).
  - `base ten blocks (figure)`: the rods and cubes the figure counts with. Oak gives the reader "Jun has these Base 10
    blocks." (`thenational.academy-ng-and-representing-the-numbers-20-to-99-lessons-represent-a-number-from-20-to-99.txt`,
    Prior knowledge starter quiz). Written in words as the book writes "ten frame".
  - `rod, cube (base ten blocks)`: a rod is a block of 10 cubes joined in a row, which is one ten, and a cube is a
    single block, which is one one. England gives "Daisy has used 10cm rods and 1cm cubes to measure the length of
    this toy boat."
    (`assets.publishing.service.gov.uk-ads-system-uploads-attachment-data-file-954796-Maths-guidance-year-2.txt`,
    page 13, 2NPV–1). Chosen over IM's "towers of 10 and singles"
    (`coreknowledge.org-wp-content-uploads-2023-08-CKMath-G1U4-NumbersTo99-TG-W2.txt`), which are connecting cubes,
    since the figure draws base ten blocks and England names their pieces so.
- Uses: `ten, one (numbers)`, `count in twos, fives, tens, hundreds (numbers)` for the tens, `count on, count back
  (numbers)`, `group of 2, 5, 10, 100 (objects)` for the group of 10

## 020 teens
- Introduces:
  - `teen number (numbers)`: a number from 11 to 19, which is 1 ten and 1 to 9 ones, as 15 is 1 ten and 5 ones. 11
    and 12 are teen numbers too, though their names do not end in "teen", and 10 and 20 are not. IM's glossary gives
    "teen number A number with 1 ten and between 1 and 9 ones. Example: Fifteen is 1 ten and 5 ones."
    (`im.kendallhunt.com-k5-teachers-grade-1-unit-4-glossary.txt`). Chosen over Oak's "ten and a bit"
    (`thenational.academy-11-to-19-lessons-explain-that-the-digits-in-the-numbers-11-to-19-express-quantity.txt`),
    since "1 ten and 5 ones" says how big the bit is.
  - `number name (numbers)`: the word or words a number is said with, as fourteen for 14 and forty-three for 43. A
    number written with digits is "in digits", and its name written out is "in words". England's guidance: "Because
    number names in English do not always reflect the structure of the numbers"
    (`assets.publishing.service.gov.uk-ads-system-uploads-attachment-data-file-954511-Maths-guidance-year-1.txt`,
    page 12, 1NPV–1), and England Year 1 "read and write numbers from 1 to 20 in numerals and words."
    (`.cache/curricula/england-ks1-2.pdf`, page 6).
- Uses: `ten, one (numbers)`, `rod, cube (base ten blocks)`, `bundle (place value)`, `digit, last digit
  (numbers)` for "digit", `count on, count back (numbers)`, `full ten (ten frame)` and `a full ten and some more
  (numbers)` only where a text points back to `number-bonds`

## 030 two-digits
- Introduces:
  - `two-digit number (numbers)`: a number written with two digits, 10 to 99. A number from 0 to 9 has one digit.
    Oak gives the reader "Two-digit number - A digit is a single symbol used to make a numeral. A two-digit number
    uses two symbols to make one numeral."
    (`thenational.academy-ng-and-representing-the-numbers-20-to-99-lessons-represent-a-number-from-20-to-99.txt`,
    Keywords). Written with its hyphen, as every source writes it.
  - `tens digit, ones digit (two-digit numbers)`: the digit on the left, which counts the tens, and the digit on the
    right, which counts the ones, so 43 has the tens digit 4 and the ones digit 3. The ones digit is the last digit of
    the glossary's `digit, last digit (numbers)`. A number with one digit has only a ones digit, so 7 has no tens.
    Oak gives the reader "Tens digit - The symbol used to represent the number of tens in a numeral." and "Ones digit
    - The symbol used to represent the number of ones in a numeral."
    (`thenational.academy-ng-and-representing-the-numbers-20-to-99-lessons-represent-a-number-from-20-to-99.txt`,
    Keywords).
  - `place, tens place, ones place (digits)`: the place of a digit is where it is written in a number. In a
    two-digit number the tens place is on the left and the ones place on the right, so the tens digit is the digit in
    the tens place. Japan's grade 1 terms are "ones place" and "tens place"
    (`.cache/curricula/japan-course-of-study-2008-en.pdf`, page 8).
  - `place value chart (figure)`: a chart of two columns headed Tens and Ones, with the rods of a number in the tens
    column, its cubes in the ones column, and the digit for each column under it. After a page names it, "the chart"
    refers back to it. Oak: "We can use a Gattegno chart or a place value chart to help us remember what the digits on
    a two-digit number represent."
    (`thenational.academy-essons-partition-two-digit-numbers-into-tens-and-ones-using-place-value-resources.txt`), and
    NCETM "A place value chart may help to support his understanding of where each digit needs to be"
    (`ncetm.org.uk-media-alenk0du-pska-num-q4.txt`, page 2). Chosen over the brief's "place value mat", which no
    source read says.
  - `column (place value chart)`: one of the two upright parts of the chart, the tens column on the left, which
    holds rods, and the ones column on the right, which holds cubes. A new sense beside `row, column (hundred
    square)`. The progression gives "tens column" (`isbe.net-Documents-number-base-ten-k-5.txt`, page 9).
- Uses: `ten, one (numbers)`, `rod, cube (base ten blocks)`, `bundle (place value)`, `teen number (numbers)`,
  `number name (numbers)`, `digit, last digit (numbers)` for "digit", `ten, one (numbers)`, `count in twos, fives, tens,
  hundreds (numbers)` for the tens

## 040 place
- Introduces:
  - `value (digits)`: the number a digit stands for in its place. A digit in the tens place stands for that many
    tens and a digit in the ones place for that many ones, so in 42 the value of the 4 is 40 and the value of the 2 is
    2, and a 7 in the tens place has the value 70. NCETM's teaching point "The digits in the numbers 20–99 tell us
    about their value." (`ncetm.org.uk-classroom-resources-primm-109-composition-of-numbers-20-100.txt`), Oak's key
    learning point "The order of the digits tells us their value."
    (`thenational.academy-essons-partition-two-digit-numbers-into-tens-and-ones-using-place-value-resources.txt`), and
    England Year 2 "the value of each digit in two-digit numbers" (`.cache/curricula/england-ks1-2.pdf`, page 11).
    Chosen over the brief's "worth", which no source read uses. A text may explain "value" once as what the digit is
    worth.
  - `place value (numbers)`: the way numbers are written, where the place of a digit sets its value. The name of the
    idea, and the first part of "place value chart". England Year 2: "recognise the place value of each digit in a
    two-digit number (tens, ones)" (`.cache/curricula/england-ks1-2.pdf`, page 11).
- Uses: `tens digit, ones digit (two-digit numbers)`, `place, tens place, ones place (digits)`, `two-digit number
  (numbers)`, `place value chart (figure)`, `column (place value chart)`, `ten, one (numbers)`,
  `rod, cube (base ten blocks)`, `bundle (place value)`, `teen number (numbers)`, `split (numbers)` for "56 splits
  into 50 and 6", `number sentence (calculations, comparisons)` and `plus, minus (signs)` where a text writes
  `56 = 50 + 6`

## 050 breaking-a-ten
- Introduces:
  - `break a ten (place value)`: turn one ten into ten ones, as taking the band off a bundle of 10 pencils or
    tapping a rod so that it falls into 10 cubes. It undoes a bundle, and the number stays the same, so 42 is 4 tens
    and 2 ones and also 3 tens and 12 ones. CKMath: "Every time break apart a ten into ones, the number of ones
    increases by 10." and "started with 3 tens and broke one ten apart at a time"
    (`coreknowledge.org-wp-content-uploads-2023-08-CKMath-G1U4-NumbersTo99-TG-W2.txt`, Lesson 19). Written
    "break a ten into ten ones" over CKMath's "break apart", since "into ten ones" says where the ten goes, as the
    brief and `adding-tens-and-ones` have it. Chosen over "decompose" (`.cache/curricula/us-common-core-math.pdf`,
    page 19, 2.NBT.B.7), the teacher's word for every kind of split, over "unbundle"
    (`isbe.net-Documents-number-base-ten-k-5.txt`, page 15), which the progression writes only in a grade 4 division
    diagram, and over
    "exchange", which England keeps for column subtraction
    (`assets.publishing.service.gov.uk-ads-system-uploads-attachment-data-file-954796-Maths-guidance-year-2.txt`,
    page 10) and `column-methods` takes.
- Uses: `ten, one (numbers)`, `bundle (place value)`, `rod, cube (base ten blocks)`, `place value chart
  (figure)`, `column (place value chart)`, `tens digit, ones digit (two-digit numbers)`, `value (digits)`,
  `two-digit number (numbers)`, `split (numbers)` for "42 splits into 30 and 12", `number sentence (calculations,
  comparisons)` and `plus, minus (signs)` where a text writes `42 = 30 + 12`, `count in twos, fives, tens, hundreds
  (numbers)` for the tens, `count on, count back (numbers)`

## Rules
- The noun is "value": "the value of the 4 in 42 is 40", "a 7 in the tens place has the value 70". A text may say
  once, where it brings in "value", that the value is what the digit is worth. Elsewhere it says value, and the noun
  "worth" is left out. "The value of a number" is left out too: a text says "the number" or "how many".
- A ten and a one of `ten, one (numbers)` are counted with a number before them, "1 ten", "4 tens", "1 one",
  "0 ones", written in digits with "and" between, "4 tens and 2 ones". The tens of a number are "its tens", as "the
  4 tens of 42". `ten, one (numbers)` names 10, 20, 30, and this story writes such a number in digits, so "40" and not
  "the ten 40". "The tens" alone, the glossary's name for the multiples of 10, is left out of this story.
- "A one" is a single object or cube, and "ones" is its plural. The number one is written 1.
- A ten is a group of 10 counted as one. A group of 10 is counted in tens with one jump, and a ten is counted as
  "1 ten". The DfE's "1 group of 10" becomes "1 ten" once the section has said so.
- Ten ones are bundled into a ten, and a ten is broken into ten ones. Both keep the number the same. A number is
  split into two parts, as the glossary has it, so 42 splits into 40 and 2, or into 30 and 12 once a ten is broken.
  "Partition", "decompose", "compose", "unbundle", "exchange" and "regroup" are left out of the reader's text.
- "Ten ones make a ten" uses "make" as "two parts make the whole" does. "Make ten" without "a" stays the term of
  `adding-within-20`, the part that brings a number to 10.
- A rod is a ten and a cube is a one. A text names the blocks when it speaks of the figure, and tens and ones when it
  speaks of the number. "Stick", "long", "tower", "single", "unit" and "Dienes" are left out.
- The ones outside the tens are "the ones". Where a text sets them apart from the ones inside the tens, it says
  "extra ones", as Oak and the DfE do. "Loose" and "left over" are left out, since "left over" is said of pairing.
- Real tens keep their own names, a bunch of 10 roses or a pack of 10 pencils, and a text says that each is a ten.
- "Place" is said of a digit in a written number, and "column" of the chart. The tens digit is in the tens place, and
  the rods are in the tens column. "Position" is left out.
- Of a two-digit number the text says "ones digit". "Last digit" stays in `skip-counting`, and this story says once
  that the ones digit is the last digit.
- Two ways of showing one number "both show 42". "Equal" and "the same" are said of numbers, so a text does not say
  that 4 tens and 2 ones "equal" 3 tens and 12 ones. Bundling and breaking a ten "keep the number the same".
- A number is written "in digits" and said by its number name, written "in words". "Numeral" and "symbol" are left
  out of the reader's text.
- A teen number is read "1 ten and 4 ones". The brief's "ten and one, ten and two", the DfE's "one-ten-one" and Oak's
  "ten and a bit" are left out, so every number is read the one way. "-teen" in a name says 1 ten, and "-ty" says
  tens, as NSW gives "‘teen’ (ten more than) and ‘ty’ (groups of ten)"
  (`education.nsw.gov.au-cy-and-numeracy-teaching-and-learning-resources-numeracy-connecting-number-names.txt`).
  "Decade" is left out, as the glossary rules have it.
- A number from 11 to 20 shown on two ten frames is "a full ten and some more" in `number-bonds`. This story says
  "1 ten and 3 ones", and a rod is "a ten", not "a full ten".
- 34 and 43 are "different numbers" with the same digits. Which of two two-digit numbers is greater is left to the
  story that compares them.
- "Unitising", "expanded form" and "place holder" are left out. A 0 in the ones place "shows there are no ones".
- Left to later stories:
  - `adding-tens-and-ones` adds and subtracts with these words. Its brief says "swap ten cubes for a rod", and this
    story's word for that is "bundle".
  - `column-methods` introduces "exchange", which England uses for the column methods.
  - `hundreds-and-thousands` introduces the hundred as ten tens, the hundreds digit, the hundreds place and the 0 as a
    place holder.
