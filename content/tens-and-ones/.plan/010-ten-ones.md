# Ten ones make a ten

## Header

- Assumes: from `counting`, one number for each object, counting on from a number you know, counting to 100, and
  counters in rows of ten whose rows end on 10, 20, 30 up to 100 (pages 080 and 090). The tap that gives an object
  the next number of the count, from `counting`.
- Teaches: ten ones put together make one ten, a group made only of tens is counted in tens, and bundling a large
  group into tens keeps the number the same and makes it quick to count, by counting the tens first and then counting
  on the extra ones, wherever they lie.
- Introduces:
  - Terms: `ten, one (numbers)` and `bundle (place value)` on page 110, `base ten blocks (figure)` and
    `rod, cube (base ten blocks)` on page 120. The phrase "extra ones" of the terms' rules first shows on page 120.
    The section uses `ten, one (numbers)`, `count on, count back (numbers)` and `group of 2, 5, 10, 100 (objects)` for the
    group of 10, and counts in tens as the rows of `counting` do.
  - Controls: the button "Tie a bundle" and the readout "Bundles" (page 110). The tap that gives a rod or a cube the
    next number of the count, a rod adding 10 and a cube 1, and the readout "Last number said" (page 120). The button
    "Mix up the blocks" (page 130).
- Uses:
  - A jar of 60 pencils, tied into 6 bundles of 10 pencils and counted 10, 20, 30, 40, 50, 60 (page 110).
  - 5 rods and 2 cubes, counted rods first: 10, 20, 30, 40, 50, then 51, 52. 52 is 5 tens and 2 ones, and also 52
    cubes, 50 of them inside the rods (page 120).
  - 4 rods and 6 cubes, mixed up among each other and counted rods first: 10, 20, 30, 40, then 41 to 46. A cube
    counted first gives 1 and a rod after it 11, numbers hard to keep track of (page 130).

The section has three pages, 110 to 130, and no challenge. Pages 140 to 190 stay free. Page 110 counts whole tens
only, and pages 120 and 130 add extra ones below 10.

## Content

figure, as the terms rule. A number of tens or ones is written in digits, "6 tens", "5 tens and 2 ones". The section

### 110 Tie the pencils in bundles of ten

The story opens here, on a glass jar full of pencils on a table. The question is how many pencils there are.
Counting them one by one is slow, and in a heap it is easy to count a pencil twice or skip one. A quicker way is to
tie the pencils in bundles of ten. Ten pencils tied together make one bundle. Each pencil is a one, and a bundle of
10 ones is a ten. Once the pencils are tied, you count the bundles in tens, since each bundle adds ten more pencils.
This is how the rows of ten counters in `counting` ended on 10, 20, 30, and the text points back to that in one
sentence. Tying the pencils changes how they lie and keeps their number the same.

Example: the jar holds 60 pencils, which the text does not say until the count. Tying them gives 6 bundles and leaves
the jar empty. 6 bundles are 6 tens. Counting in tens, 10, 20, 30, 40, 50, 60, gives 60 pencils, the same number a
count one by one would reach.

### 120 Count the rods in tens, then the cubes

Base ten blocks show tens and ones. A cube is one block, and it is a one. A rod is ten cubes joined in a row, and the
lines across it show its ten cubes, so a rod is a ten. The rod is the bundle of page 110 made from cubes in place of
pencils. To count blocks, count the rods in tens first, then count on the extra cubes in ones from the last ten.

Example: 5 rods and 2 cubes. The rods count 10, 20, 30, 40, 50, and that is 5 tens. The 2 cubes count on from 50:
51, 52. So there are 52, which is 5 tens and 2 ones. The same 52 are also 52 cubes, since the 5 rods hold 50 cubes
and 2 cubes are extra. Counting them one by one gives 52 too, only more slowly.

### 130 Count the tens first wherever they lie

The rods do not have to lie on the left. However the blocks are set out, you find the rods and count them in tens
first, then count on the cubes in ones. The number stays the same when the blocks move. Each cube is a one, so
once the rods are counted the count goes up by one for each cube. Counting the rods first is easier, since the
count switches from tens to ones just once.

Example: 4 rods and 6 cubes. The rods count 10, 20, 30, 40, that is 4 tens. The cubes count on from 40: 41, 42, 43,
44, 45, 46. So there are 46, which is 4 tens and 6 ones. A cube tapped first gives 1 and a rod after it 11, and the
count goes on jumping between tens and ones, with numbers hard to keep track of. The figure adds each piece's own
value in any order, so it shows this awkward count and does not show the slip of counting a cube as a ten.
After the blocks are mixed up, the rods are somewhere among the cubes, and counting the rods first gives 46 again.

## Interactivity

### 110

- Shows: the look `jar`. A glass jar on a table holds 60 pencils standing in a heap, too many to count at a glance,
  and the table beside it is empty. The panel holds the button "Tie a bundle" and the readout "Bundles" at 0. No
  number in digits.
- The reader: presses "Tie a bundle".
- The figure: ten pencils rise out of the jar, glide together, and a band goes round them. The bundle lies on the
  table beside the jar, after the bundles already there. "Bundles" goes up by 1. After 6 presses the jar is empty and
  the button rests.
- Action: press a button, and ten ones are tied into one ten.

### 120

- Shows: the look `blocks` without the chart. 5 red rods side by side on the left, each drawn as ten cubes with a line
  between each two, and 2 red cubes after them, apart from each other. The readout "Last number said" at 0.
- The reader: taps a rod or a cube.
- The figure: the tapped piece takes the next number of the count and carries it: a rod adds 10 and a cube adds 1.
  Tapped rods first, the pieces carry 10, 20, 30, 40, 50, 51, 52. A cube tapped first carries 1, and a rod tapped
  after it 11. A tapped piece keeps its number. "Last number said" shows the last number given.
- Action: tap a ten or a one, and it takes the next number of the count, ten more for a ten and one more for a one.

### 130

- Shows: the look `blocks` without the chart. 4 red rods on the left and 6 red cubes after them, the readout "Last
  number said" at 0, and the button "Mix up the blocks".
- The reader: presses "Mix up the blocks", then taps the pieces as on page 120, rods first. After a second mix the
  reader taps a cube and then a rod, and sees 1 and then 11.
- The figure: the rods and cubes move to new places among each other, and every number of the count comes off.
  "Last number said" goes back to 0. Taps number the pieces as on page 120, so the rods tapped first carry 10, 20, 30,
  40 wherever they lie, and the cubes 41 to 46. Each new press mixes the blocks again and takes the numbers off.
- Action: mix the tens and ones among each other, and count them again from 0, tens first.
