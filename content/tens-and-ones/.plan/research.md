# Research for Tens and ones

The files named below sit in `content/tens-and-ones/.references/`, except the curricula, which sit in
`.cache/curricula/`. Page numbers of PDFs are the pages of the PDF. The CKMath file is the Core Knowledge edition of
Illustrative Mathematics grade 1 unit 4, and its PDF pages differ from its printed page numbers. The text of
`isbe.net-Documents-number-base-ten-k-5.txt` drops the signs + and = from its equations, so its quotes leave those out.

What the curricula say, for all sections:

- The United States starts the teen numbers in kindergarten: "Compose and decompose numbers from 11 to 19 into ten
  ones and some further ones, e.g., by using objects or drawings, and record each composition or decomposition by a
  drawing or equation (e.g., 18 = 10 + 8)" (.cache/curricula/us-common-core-math.pdf, page 12, K.NBT.A.1).
- Grade 1 in the United States: "Understand that the two digits of a two-digit number represent amounts of tens and
  ones." with "a. 10 can be thought of as a bundle of ten ones — called a “ten.”", "b. The numbers from 11 to 19 are
  composed of a ten and one, two, three, four, five, six, seven, eight, or nine ones." and "c. The numbers 10, 20, 30,
  40, 50, 60, 70, 80, 90 refer to one, two, three, four, five, six, seven, eight, or nine tens (and 0 ones)."
  (.cache/curricula/us-common-core-math.pdf, page 15, 1.NBT.B.2).
- Grade 1 in the United States composes a ten when adding: "sometimes it is necessary to compose a ten."
  (.cache/curricula/us-common-core-math.pdf, page 16, 1.NBT.C.4). Decomposing comes in grade 2: "sometimes it is
  necessary to compose or decompose tens or hundreds." (same file, page 19, 2.NBT.B.7). Expanded form is grade 2:
  "Read and write numbers to 1000 using base-ten numerals, number names, and expanded form." (same file, page 19,
  2.NBT.A.3).
- England Year 1 reads and writes to 20 in words and starts place value beyond 20: "read and write numbers from 1 to
  20 in numerals and words." and "Pupils begin to recognise place value in numbers beyond 20 by reading, writing,
  counting and comparing numbers up to 100, supported by objects and pictorial representations."
  (.cache/curricula/england-ks1-2.pdf, page 6, Year 1, Number and place value).
- England Year 2 has the digits and partitioning: "recognise the place value of each digit in a two-digit number
  (tens, ones)" and "Pupils should partition numbers in different ways (for example, 23 = 20 + 3 and 23 = 10 + 13) to
  support subtraction." and "They begin to understand zero as a place holder." (.cache/curricula/england-ks1-2.pdf,
  page 11, Year 2, Number and place value).
- Japan grade 1: "e. To understand how to represent 2-digit numbers." and "g. To consider numbers using ten as a
  unit." (.cache/curricula/japan-course-of-study-2008-en.pdf, page 6, Grade 1, A (1)). Its terms for grade 1 are
  "ones place" and "tens place" (same file, page 8, Grade 1, Terms / Symbols).
- Bremen, years 1 and 2: "Bündeln und Entbündeln von Mengen in unterschiedlichen Darstellungsformen", "Nutzen der
  Stellenwertschreibweise (z. B. H, Z, E)", "Zerlegen von Zahlen in Stellenwerte", with the materials
  "Mehrsystemblöcke, Stellenwerttafel, Zahlwort, Ziffernschreibweise"
  (.cache/curricula/germany-bremen-primary-2025.pdf, page 20, Stellenwertverständnis).

What the reader must hold from `counting`:

- Counting by ones and by tens to 100 comes first. England Year 1: "count, read and write numbers to 100 in numerals;
  count in multiples of twos, fives and tens" (.cache/curricula/england-ks1-2.pdf, page 6). The DfE guidance moves
  from counting in tens to naming tens: "“Ten, twenty, thirty…”", "“1 group of 10, 2 groups of 10, 3 groups of
  10…”", "In time, shortened to:", "“1 ten, 2 tens, 3 tens…”"
  (assets.publishing.service.gov.uk-ads-system-uploads-attachment-data-file-954511-Maths-guidance-year-1.pdf, page 21,
  1NF–2 Language focus).

## 010 ten-ones

- Order: ten ones become one ten, then a large group is bundled in tens, then the tens are counted before the ones.
  Oak's key learning points, in order: "Ten ones are equivalent to one ten.", "When counting larger groups, it is more
  efficient to group objects into tens, then count the tens and extra ones." and "We can work out how many there are
  by counting in ones, or by counting the groups of ten and extra ones."
  (thenational.academy-and-representing-the-numbers-20-to-99-lessons-count-groups-of-ten-and-extra-ones.txt, Key
  learning points).
- Order: NCETM topic 1.9 bundles before it names the digits: "Teaching point 2: Objects can be counted efficiently by
  making groups of ten. The digits in the numbers 20–99 tell us about their value."
  (ncetm.org.uk-classroom-resources-primm-109-composition-of-numbers-20-100.txt, Teaching points).
- Order: IM counts whole tens first, then tens and ones, then counts on from the tens: "Students count collections
  with a multiple of 10 objects in each. As they represent the quantity in each collection, they see that counting by
  one and counting by ten yields the same number." (Section A) and "For example, to count to 73, they may count 7 tens
  and count on—71, 72, 73." (curriculum.illustrativemathematics.org-k5-teachers-grade-1-course-guide-scope-and-sequence.txt,
  Unit 4).
- Order: the progression puts the ten as a new unit in grade 1, after the teen numbers of kindergarten: "Grade 1
  students take the important step of viewing ten ones as a unit called a “ten.”"
  (isbe.net-Documents-number-base-ten-k-5.pdf, page 6, Grade 1).
- Examples: packs of pencils and bundles of sticks. "Figure 13: counting in tens – 9 packs of 10 pencils" with
  "“The pencils are in groups of 10, so we will count in tens.”"
  (assets.publishing.service.gov.uk-ads-system-uploads-attachment-data-file-954511-Maths-guidance-year-1.pdf, page
  20), and "These sticks are grouped into bundles of 10. How many sticks are there altogether?" (same file, page 22,
  1NF–2 Example assessment questions).
- Examples: straws and pencils in Oak's quiz. "Andeep has 54 straws. He puts them into groups of ten and there are
  some more ones left over." and "Andeep counts his pencils in ones. He has 37 pencils. If he puts them into groups of
  ten, how many extra ones will he have left over."
  (thenational.academy-numbers-20-to-99-lessons-count-a-large-group-of-objects-by-counting-tens-and-ones.txt,
  Prior knowledge starter quiz).
- Examples: a bag of 52 cubes counted with cups, plates or ten frames. "draws 5 groups of 10 and 2 leftovers, counts
  10, 20, 30, 40, 50, 51, 52" (coreknowledge.org-wp-content-uploads-2023-08-CKMath-G1U4-NumbersTo99-TG-W2.pdf, page
  85, Lesson 6).
- Examples: IM uses towers of ten connecting cubes: "Connecting cubes in towers of 10 and singles are used instead of
  base-ten blocks, which will be used in later grades, so units of ten can be physically composed and decomposed with
  the cubes." (curriculum.illustrativemathematics.org-k5-teachers-grade-1-course-guide-scope-and-sequence.txt, Unit 4).
- Words: "ten" for the bundle, "a bundle of ten ones — called a “ten.”" (.cache/curricula/us-common-core-math.pdf,
  page 15, 1.NBT.B.2a). Oak's keywords: "Tens - A ten is a group of 10 ones." and "Ones - Individual objects, grouped
  in ones." (thenational.academy-11-to-19-lessons-explain-that-the-digits-in-the-numbers-11-to-19-express-quantity.txt,
  Keywords).
- Words: "groups of ten" and "extra ones", as in "There are 5 groups of ten and 4 extra ones." Oak also names
  "Unitising - Unitising means treating groups that contain, or represent, the same number of things as ‘ones’ or
  ‘units’." (thenational.academy-numbers-20-to-99-lessons-count-a-large-group-of-objects-by-counting-tens-and-ones.txt,
  Keywords and quiz). The DfE guidance keeps "unitising" for teachers: "Recognising that a group of 5, for example,
  can be treated as a single unit is called unitising"
  (assets.publishing.service.gov.uk-ads-system-uploads-attachment-data-file-954511-Maths-guidance-year-1.pdf, page 20).
- Errors: counting by ones after bundling. "Some students may count by one as they do not yet trust that counting by
  ten yields the same result." and "Some students may continue to count by one after they have organized the cubes
  into tens" (coreknowledge.org-wp-content-uploads-2023-08-CKMath-G1U4-NumbersTo99-TG-W2.pdf, page 85, Lesson 6).
- Errors: counting the ones as tens, and counting by layout. "Children may continue counting in tens when they reach
  the ones, e.g. count 2 tens and 3 ones completely in tens. They may think arrangement of tens and ones affects how
  to count them." The advice: "Present tens and ones in non-standard arrangements, so children practise always
  counting tens first."
  (thenational.academy-numbers-20-to-99-lessons-count-a-large-group-of-objects-by-counting-tens-and-ones.txt, Common
  misconception).
- Errors: losing the ones inside the tens. "Children may only think of 'ones' in terms of the extra ones after the tens
  have been grouped, so may fail to see 45, for example, as 45 ones as well as 4 tens and 5 ones." The advice: "use
  deliberate errors and discussion to draw attention to the total number of ones represented by the number"
  (thenational.academy-and-representing-the-numbers-20-to-99-lessons-count-groups-of-ten-and-extra-ones.txt, Common
  misconception). DfE: "Pupils should recognise that 42, for example, can be composed either of 42 ones, or of 4 tens
  and 2 ones." and "Within these representations the relative positions of the tens and the ones should be varied."
  (assets.publishing.service.gov.uk-ads-system-uploads-attachment-data-file-954796-Maths-guidance-year-2.pdf, page 12,
  2NPV–1).

## 020 teens

- Order: the teen numbers come before the other two-digit numbers in the United States and IM. Kindergarten has
  K.NBT.A.1 (.cache/curricula/us-common-core-math.pdf, page 12). IM teaches them in unit 3 before unit 4: "Students
  see that a new unit, a ten, is composed from 10 ones, and that teen numbers are composed of 1 unit of ten plus some
  number of ones. Double 10-frames are used here as they encourage students to see this structure (MP7)."
  (curriculum.illustrativemathematics.org-k5-teachers-grade-1-course-guide-scope-and-sequence.txt, Unit 3, Section B).
- Order: NCETM teaches 11 to 19 after 20 to 100. The spine lists "Composition of numbers: multiples of 10 up to 100"
  as Topic 1.8, "Composition of numbers: 20–100" as Topic 1.9 and "Composition of numbers: 11–19" as Topic 1.10
  (ncetm.org.uk-stery-materials-primary-mastery-professional-development-number-addition-and-subtraction.txt, Year 1).
- Order: within the topic, the digits first, then the ten and ones: "Teaching point 1: The digits in the numbers 11–19
  tell us about their value. Teaching point 2: The numbers 11–19 can be formed by combining a ten and ones, and can be
  partitioned into a ten and ones." (ncetm.org.uk-classroom-resources-primm-110-composition-of-numbers-11-19.txt).
- Order: objects grouped first, then the numeral. "Children can count out a given teen number of objects, e.g., 12,
  and group the objects to see the ten ones and the two ones." (isbe.net-Documents-number-base-ten-k-5.pdf, page 5,
  Kindergarten).
- Examples: ten frames filled in order. "The student then says the number and draws that many dots beginning with the
  first 10-frame. When the first 10-frame is filled, the student continues drawing the remaining dots in the next
  10-frame." (illustrativemathematics.org-content-standards-tasks-1404.txt, Action).
- Examples: a ten that is hard to count by ones. "Provide concrete and pictorial examples where it is more difficult
  to count the ones in a group of 10 e.g. a bundle of straws or box of pencils."
  (thenational.academy-to-19-lessons-identify-the-quantity-shown-in-a-representation-of-numbers-11-to-19.txt, Common
  misconception).
- Examples: 17 as 10 and 7, with layered cards. "Layered place value cards can help children see the 0 “hiding” under
  the ones place and that the 1 in the tens place really is 10 (ten ones)." (isbe.net-Documents-number-base-ten-k-5.pdf,
  page 5).
- Words: "teen number". IM's glossary: "teen number A number with 1 ten and between 1 and 9 ones. Example: Fifteen is
  1 ten and 5 ones." (im.kendallhunt.com-k5-teachers-grade-1-unit-4-glossary.txt). Oak: "Teen numbers - A number made
  of a ten (a group of 10 ones) and some additional ones." and "We can say there is one ten and --- ones."
  (thenational.academy-11-to-19-lessons-explain-that-the-digits-in-the-numbers-11-to-19-express-quantity.txt).
- Words: "ten and a bit". "Explore the ten-and-a-bit nature of the numbers 11–19"
  (ncetm.org.uk-classroom-resources-primm-110-composition-of-numbers-11-19.txt, Introduction), "The teen numbers can be
  represented as ten and a bit."
  (thenational.academy-11-to-19-lessons-explain-that-the-digits-in-the-numbers-11-to-19-express-quantity.txt).
- Words: counting with names that show the ten. "“…seven, eight, nine, one-ten, one-ten-one, one-ten-two,
  one-ten-three… two-tens, two-tens-one, two-tens two…”", because "number names in English do not always reflect the
  structure of the numbers"
  (assets.publishing.service.gov.uk-ads-system-uploads-attachment-data-file-954511-Maths-guidance-year-1.pdf, page 12,
  1NPV–1). The progression: "Children could learn to say numbers in this East Asian way in addition to learning the
  standard English number names." (isbe.net-Documents-number-base-ten-k-5.pdf, page 5).
- Errors: the English names. "“eleven” and “twelve” do not sound like “ten and one” and “ten and two.” The numbers
  “thirteen, fourteen, fifteen, . . . , nineteen” reverse the order of the ones and tens digits by saying the ones
  digit first. Also, “teen” must be interpreted as meaning “ten” and the prefixes “thir” and “fif” do not clearly say
  “three” and “five.”" (isbe.net-Documents-number-base-ten-k-5.pdf, page 5).
- Errors: reading the numeral as two single digits. "initially, a numeral such as 16 looks like “one, six,” not “1 ten
  and 6 ones .”" (isbe.net-Documents-number-base-ten-k-5.pdf, page 5).
- Errors: teen and decade names mixed. "Pupils confuse e.g. 18 and 80 because they sound similar." The advice: "You may
  wish to practise the teen and decade numbers side by side so pupils can hear the difference."
  (thenational.academy-11-to-19-lessons-explain-that-the-digits-in-the-numbers-11-to-19-express-quantity.txt, Common
  misconception). NSW: "make meaning from the suffixes ‘teen’ (ten more than) and ‘ty’ (groups of ten), comparing and
  contrasting pairs or trios of numbers like 14, 40 and 41"
  (education.nsw.gov.au-cy-and-numeracy-teaching-and-learning-resources-numeracy-connecting-number-names.txt,
  Representations). The study codes it as an error: "teens and decades were substituted with each other (seventy →
  17)" (eprints.whiterose.ac.uk-181380-1-6929-Article-63081-2-10-20211130.pdf, page 7, Analysis).
- Errors: the teens are where English children reverse digits. "These errors occurred more often for number words that
  are inverted at the item level, i.e., teen numbers." (pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov-articles-PMC8615580.txt, Introduction).
- Errors: counting all. "Pupils use one to one correspondence to count all rather than counting on from a group of 10
  to identify a teen number." The advice: "Encourage pupils to use the phrase 'one ten and ...' to focus attention on
  counting on from 10"
  (thenational.academy-to-19-lessons-identify-the-quantity-shown-in-a-representation-of-numbers-11-to-19.txt, Common
  misconception).
- Errors: counting back across the teens. "Counting backwards from 20 down to 10 requires additional focused practice,
  due to the irregularity of these number names."
  (assets.publishing.service.gov.uk-ads-system-uploads-attachment-data-file-954511-Maths-guidance-year-1.pdf, page 12).

## 030 two-digits

- Order: whole tens first, then tens and ones. IM: Section A "Represent the base-ten structure of multiples of 10 up to
  90 using towers of 10, drawings, numbers, or words." then Section B "Understand that the two digits of a two-digit
  number represent amounts of tens and ones."
  (curriculum.illustrativemathematics.org-k5-teachers-grade-1-course-guide-scope-and-sequence.txt, Unit 4). NCETM 1.8
  on multiples of 10 comes before 1.9, whose introduction says "Build on multiples of ten, by introducing non-zero
  values in the ones place" (ncetm.org.uk-classroom-resources-primm-109-composition-of-numbers-20-100.txt).
- Order: building and reading a number come before writing it. "At this time, students are not expected to write
  two-digit numbers, but continue to make sense of how to read and say them based on their base-ten structure."
  (coreknowledge.org-wp-content-uploads-2023-08-CKMath-G1U4-NumbersTo99-TG-W2.pdf, page 100). Writing comes
  in Lesson 10, "Write Two-digit Numbers" (same file, page 252).
- Order: a sentence for each digit. "Pupils need to be able to connect the way two-digit numbers are written in
  numerals to their value. They should demonstrate their reasoning using full sentences."
  (assets.publishing.service.gov.uk-ads-system-uploads-attachment-data-file-954796-Maths-guidance-year-2.pdf, page 12,
  2NPV–1).
- Order: the 0 in the decade numbers. "The numbers 10, 20, 30, 40, 50, 60, 70, 80, 90 refer to one, two, three, four,
  five, six, seven, eight, or nine tens (and 0 ones)." (.cache/curricula/us-common-core-math.pdf, page 15). CKMath
  compares 7, 17, 70 and 71: "“How are 70 and 71 the same and different?” (Both have 7 tens, but 71 has 1 one instead
  of 0 ones.)" (coreknowledge.org-wp-content-uploads-2023-08-CKMath-G1U4-NumbersTo99-TG-W2.pdf, page 120, Lesson 10).
- Examples: 42 in the DfE sentence, 28 as 20 and 8 (assets.publishing.service.gov.uk-ads-system-uploads-attachment-data-file-954796-Maths-guidance-year-2.pdf,
  page 12), 67 as "6 tens and 7 ones" and 86 as "eight tens and six ones" (isbe.net-Documents-number-base-ten-k-5.pdf,
  page 6), 73 as "7 tens and 3 ones"
  (curriculum.illustrativemathematics.org-k5-teachers-grade-1-course-guide-scope-and-sequence.txt, Unit 4).
- Examples: lengths with rods. "Daisy has used 10cm rods and 1cm cubes to measure the length of this toy boat. How long
  is the boat?" (assets.publishing.service.gov.uk-ads-system-uploads-attachment-data-file-954796-Maths-guidance-year-2.pdf,
  page 13, 2NPV–1 Example assessment questions).
- Words: Oak's keywords "Tens digit - The symbol used to represent the number of tens in a numeral.", "Ones digit - The
  symbol used to represent the number of ones in a numeral." and "Two-digit number - A digit is a single symbol used to
  make a numeral. A two-digit number uses two symbols to make one numeral."
  (thenational.academy-ng-and-representing-the-numbers-20-to-99-lessons-represent-a-number-from-20-to-99.txt,
  Keywords). Japan gives "ones place" and "tens place" in grade 1 (.cache/curricula/japan-course-of-study-2008-en.pdf,
  page 8).
- Words: the sentence a child says. "“This is the number 42. The 4 shows we have 4 groups of ten. The 2 shows we have 2
  extra ones.”" (assets.publishing.service.gov.uk-ads-system-uploads-attachment-data-file-954796-Maths-guidance-year-2.pdf,
  page 12, Language focus). IM: "Students also begin to use words such as “___ tens ___ ones.”"
  (curriculum.illustrativemathematics.org-k5-teachers-grade-1-course-guide-scope-and-sequence.txt, Unit 4, Section B).
- Words: the mat. Oak says "place value chart": "We can use a Gattegno chart or a place value chart to help us remember
  what the digits on a two-digit number represent."
  (thenational.academy-essons-partition-two-digit-numbers-into-tens-and-ones-using-place-value-resources.txt). Bremen
  says "Stellenwerttafel" (.cache/curricula/germany-bremen-primary-2025.pdf, page 20).
- Words: IM keeps the digit words out of its first section: "terms such as “two-digit number,” “digits,” “multiples,”
  “tens place,” and “ones place” are not used."
  (curriculum.illustrativemathematics.org-k5-teachers-grade-1-course-guide-scope-and-sequence.txt, Unit 4, Section A).
- Errors: writing what is heard. "Eighty-six is shown as “eight tens and six ones” and written as 86 and not as 806, a
  common Grade 1 error of writing what is heard." (isbe.net-Documents-number-base-ten-k-5.pdf, page 6). The study finds
  this the largest error in English: "In English, additive composition errors were clearly the largest error
  category, while inversion errors were rare" (eprints.whiterose.ac.uk-181380-1-6929-Article-63081-2-10-20211130.pdf,
  page 11, Summary).
- Errors: the 0 as place holder. "Jack may not have understood that zero is required as a place holder when there is
  no digit. A place value chart may help to support his understanding of where each digit needs to be within his whole
  number." (ncetm.org.uk-media-alenk0du-pska-num-q4.pdf, page 2).
- Errors: counting past 29. "children frequently make count errors such as “twenty-nine, twenty-ten, twenty-eleven,
  twenty-twelve.”" (isbe.net-Documents-number-base-ten-k-5.pdf, page 6).
- Errors: the decade names. "The decade words, “twenty,” “thirty,” “forty,” etc., must be understood as indicating 2
  tens, 3 tens, 4 tens, etc." and "“fourteen” and “forty” sound very similar"
  (isbe.net-Documents-number-base-ten-k-5.pdf, page 6).
- Errors: building each number from nothing. "When representing two-digit numbers practically, children may start each
  number anew, instead of using what they know about number patterns to adapt existing representations."
  (thenational.academy-ng-and-representing-the-numbers-20-to-99-lessons-represent-a-number-from-20-to-99.txt, Common
  misconception).

## 040 place

- Order: the place of a digit matters in the numeral, and the place of a block in a picture does not. "In the previous
  activity, students saw that the order of the digits matters when writing a two-digit number. In this activity,
  students see that although the order matters when writing a number, the position of tens or ones in a drawing or
  diagram does not change their value."
  (coreknowledge.org-wp-content-uploads-2023-08-CKMath-G1U4-NumbersTo99-TG-W2.pdf, page 96, Activity 2).
  Oak: "The order in which tens and ones are arranged does not affect the value of the number."
  (thenational.academy-ng-and-representing-the-numbers-20-to-99-lessons-represent-a-number-from-20-to-99.txt).
- Order: the worth of each digit written as a sum, the ones also written first. "To highlight the meaning of the
  digits, the expressions are shown in expanded form (30 + 5) and with the value of the ones before the tens (5 +
  30)." (curriculum.illustrativemathematics.org-k5-teachers-grade-1-course-guide-scope-and-sequence.txt, Unit 4,
  Section B).
- Order: the tens digit decides which number is greater. "They notice that if a two-digit number has more tens it will
  be greater than another number with fewer tens, no matter how many ones there are."
  (curriculum.illustrativemathematics.org-k5-teachers-grade-1-course-guide-scope-and-sequence.txt, Unit 4, Section C).
  The progression: "recognize that the digit in the tens place is more important for determining the size of a
  two-digit number." (isbe.net-Documents-number-base-ten-k-5.pdf, page 6).
- Order: Oak prepares partitioning by naming the decade: "Two-digit numbers are now also referred to as the multiple of
  ten and ___ more, e.g. 50 and 2 more, to prepare for later partitioning and recombining work."
  (thenational.academy-ng-and-representing-the-numbers-20-to-99-lessons-represent-a-number-from-20-to-99.txt, Teacher
  tip).
- Examples: 24 and 42. "“Tyler’s drawing shows 42, not 24. They both have the digits 2 and 4, but they are in different
  places, which makes them different numbers.”"
  (coreknowledge.org-wp-content-uploads-2023-08-CKMath-G1U4-NumbersTo99-TG-W2.pdf, page 96).
- Examples: one digit in different places. "“How are 7 and 17 the same and different?” (They both use the digit 7 but
  only 17 has 1 ten. Seven has no tens.)" and "17 has 1 ten 7 ones and 71 has 7 tens 1 one."
  (coreknowledge.org-wp-content-uploads-2023-08-CKMath-G1U4-NumbersTo99-TG-W2.pdf, page 120). NSW swaps the digits of a
  teen number: "Discuss with students what happens if we “swap” the numerals around for example, 14 now becomes 41.
  Ask students, “What does the one represent? Does it still represent ten? Why or why not?"
  (education.nsw.gov.au-cy-and-numeracy-teaching-and-learning-resources-numeracy-connecting-number-names.txt, Teen
  number puzzle).
- Examples: Oak's quiz pairs "3 tens 8 - 38" with "8 tens 3 - 83" and "67 - 6 tens 7" with "76 - 7 tens 6", and asks
  "Which of these numbers has the most tens?" of 34, 43, 53 and 35
  (thenational.academy-essons-partition-two-digit-numbers-into-tens-and-ones-using-place-value-resources.txt, Prior
  knowledge starter quiz).
- Examples: 28 written as parts. "28 = 20 + 8" and "8 + 20 = 28" with the subtractions, under "Figure 2: partitioning
  28 into 20 and 8" (assets.publishing.service.gov.uk-ads-system-uploads-attachment-data-file-954796-Maths-guidance-year-2.pdf,
  page 12). England: "23 = 20 + 3" (.cache/curricula/england-ks1-2.pdf, page 11).
- Words: "value". "The digits in the numbers 20–99 tell us about their value."
  (ncetm.org.uk-classroom-resources-primm-109-composition-of-numbers-20-100.txt), "The order of the digits tells us
  their value." (thenational.academy-essons-partition-two-digit-numbers-into-tens-and-ones-using-place-value-resources.txt),

## 050 breaking-a-ten

- Order: breaking a ten is the last section of IM's unit 4, after the digits and comparing. "Students begin by making a
  number with towers of 10 and singles, using more than 9 single cubes. This prompts students to consider how to
  represent numbers in different ways, for instance, by composing a ten from 10 ones, or decomposing a ten into 10
  ones." with "3 tens 2 ones" and "2 tens 12 ones"
  (curriculum.illustrativemathematics.org-k5-teachers-grade-1-course-guide-scope-and-sequence.txt, Unit 4, Section D).
- Order: IM's purpose is adding later. "Students learn that there are different ways to represent a two-digit number
  with tens and ones which will be important in a later unit when they compose a ten to add within 100."
  (coreknowledge.org-wp-content-uploads-2023-08-CKMath-G1U4-NumbersTo99-TG-W2.pdf, page 186, Lesson 19).
- Order: England and the DfE put it in Year 2, for subtraction. "Pupils should partition numbers in different ways (for
  example, 23 = 20 + 3 and 23 = 10 + 13) to support subtraction." (.cache/curricula/england-ks1-2.pdf, page 11).
  "Recognise the place value of each digit in two-digit numbers, and compose and decompose two-digit numbers using
  standard and non-standard partitioning." (assets.publishing.service.gov.uk-ads-system-uploads-attachment-data-file-954796-Maths-guidance-year-2.pdf,
  page 12, 2NPV–1).
- Order: the progression names both moves from the start: "decomposing 1 ten into 10 ones and composing a ten from 10
  ones." (isbe.net-Documents-number-base-ten-k-5.pdf, page 2). Grade 1 subtracts only whole tens: "First graders are
  not expected to compute differences of two-digit numbers other than multiples of ten." (same file, page 7).
- Order: Bremen bundles and unbundles in years 1 and 2: "Bündeln und Entbündeln von Mengen in unterschiedlichen
  Darstellungsformen: enaktiv, ikonisch, symbolisch" (.cache/curricula/germany-bremen-primary-2025.pdf, page 20).
- Examples: 73 in five ways. "7 tens and 3 ones", "3 ones and 7 tens", "70 + 3", "63 + 10", "60 + 13"
  (curriculum.illustrativemathematics.org-k5-teachers-grade-1-course-guide-scope-and-sequence.txt, Unit 4).
- Examples: 68 split many ways. "68 can be partitioned into 50 and 18, into 40 and 28, and so on" and "68 can be
  partitioned into 67 and 1, 66 and 2, and so on"
  (assets.publishing.service.gov.uk-ads-system-uploads-attachment-data-file-954796-Maths-guidance-year-2.pdf, page 13).
- Examples: comparing numbers shown with extra ones. "7 tens 29 ones" against "50 + 39"
  (curriculum.illustrativemathematics.org-k5-teachers-grade-1-course-guide-scope-and-sequence.txt, Unit 4, Section D).
- Examples: 17 split six ways and more. "Sam says he has found six ways to decompose 17. He challenges you to find
  more." (ncetm.org.uk-media-alenk0du-pska-num-q4.pdf, page 1).
- Words: "partition" and "non-standard partitioning" (DfE, above). "decompose" and "compose"
  (.cache/curricula/us-common-core-math.pdf, page 12). Oak's "Partition - To split a whole up into parts."
  (thenational.academy-teachers-lessons-partition-2-digit-numbers-in-different-ways.txt, Keywords). The DfE uses
  "regrouping" and "exchanging" for the column methods of later years: "Subtract within a column during columnar
  subtraction when the minuend of the column is smaller than the subtrahend (exchanging)"
  (assets.publishing.service.gov.uk-ads-system-uploads-attachment-data-file-954796-Maths-guidance-year-2.pdf, page 10).
- Errors: one way only. "Children may think two-digit numbers can only be partitioned into tens and ones." The advice:
  "Use practical equipment to manipulate tens and ones on part-part-whole models to help children to understand the
  many ways that a number can be partitioned." (thenational.academy-teachers-lessons-partition-2-digit-numbers-in-different-ways.txt,
  Common misconception). NCETM: "only decomposing by place value (300 + 20 + 4) and not understanding numbers can be
  decomposed differently (250 + 70 + 3 + 1)." (ncetm.org.uk-media-alenk0du-pska-num-q4.pdf, page 3).
- Errors: the number seems to change. Oak's tip: "Partitioning two-digit numbers in different ways is important because
  it supports the recognition that no matter how a number is partitioned, the total value is conserved."
  (thenational.academy-teachers-lessons-partition-2-digit-numbers-in-different-ways.txt, Teacher tip).
- Errors: a ten that cannot be opened. Oak: "unitising (where one object represents ten ones that cannot physically be
  separated)" (thenational.academy-numbers-20-to-99-lessons-count-a-large-group-of-objects-by-counting-tens-and-ones.txt,
  Teacher tip). IM picks cubes "so units of ten can be physically composed and decomposed with the cubes."
  (curriculum.illustrativemathematics.org-k5-teachers-grade-1-course-guide-scope-and-sequence.txt, Unit 4).
- Errors: the start of the next story. "It is common for children to look at the ones and tens as separate bonds, not
  taking into consideration the tens created by the ones bond. For example, incorrectly stating 62 + 48 would bond to
  100." (ncetm.org.uk-media-alenk0du-pska-num-q4.pdf, page 2).

## Against the brief

- The order of sections 010 and 020: NCETM teaches 20 to 100 before 11 to 19, "Composition of numbers: 20–100" Topic
  1.9 and "Composition of numbers: 11–19" Topic 1.10
  (ncetm.org.uk-stery-materials-primary-mastery-professional-development-number-addition-and-subtraction.txt). The
  United States and IM teach the teens first, K.NBT.A.1 in kindergarten (.cache/curricula/us-common-core-math.pdf, page
  12) and IM unit 3 Section B before unit 4
  (curriculum.illustrativemathematics.org-k5-teachers-grade-1-course-guide-scope-and-sequence.txt). The brief follows
  the United States and IM.
- The brief opens with ones bundled straight into tens and ones. IM and NCETM first count collections of whole tens:
  "Students count collections with a multiple of 10 objects in each." (curriculum.illustrativemathematics.org-k5-teachers-grade-1-course-guide-scope-and-sequence.txt,
  Unit 4, Section A) and Topic 1.8 "Composition of numbers: multiples of 10 up to 100"
  (ncetm.org.uk-stery-materials-primary-mastery-professional-development-number-addition-and-subtraction.txt). The
  brief's opening jar of 34 pencils has 4 loose ones.
- The word "worth": the sources say "value", as in "The digits in the numbers 20–99 tell us about their value."
  (ncetm.org.uk-classroom-resources-primm-109-composition-of-numbers-20-100.txt) and "The order of the digits tells us
  their value." (thenational.academy-essons-partition-two-digit-numbers-into-tens-and-ones-using-place-value-resources.txt).
  No source read uses "worth".
- The words "tens digit" and "ones digit": Oak gives them as keywords
  (thenational.academy-ng-and-representing-the-numbers-20-to-99-lessons-represent-a-number-from-20-to-99.txt) and Japan

  leaves them out of the first section: "terms such as “two-digit number,” “digits,” “multiples,” “tens place,” and

  .cache/curricula/germany-bremen-primary-2025.pdf, page 20). No source read says "mat".
- Rods and cubes: IM uses towers of ten connecting cubes in grade 1 "instead of base-ten blocks, which will be used in
  later grades, so units of ten can be physically composed and decomposed with the cubes."
  (curriculum.illustrativemathematics.org-k5-teachers-grade-1-course-guide-scope-and-sequence.txt, Unit 4). The brief's

  inversion-related errors was very low." (eprints.whiterose.ac.uk-181380-1-6929-Article-63081-2-10-20211130.pdf, page
  2), and common in German. The English swaps come in the teens, "These errors occurred more often for number words
  that are inverted at the item level, i.e., teen numbers." (pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov-articles-PMC8615580.txt). The IM test
  still lists switching the digits of 53 as an error (coreknowledge.org-wp-content-uploads-2023-08-CKMath-G1U4-NumbersTo99-TG-W2.pdf,
  page 244). The commonest English writing error is "forty-three" as 403: "written as 86 and not as 806, a common Grade
  1 error of writing what is heard." (isbe.net-Documents-number-base-ten-k-5.pdf, page 6). The brief has no page for it.
- 56 as 50 and 6: expanded form is grade 2 in the United States, "Read and write numbers to 1000 using base-ten
  numerals, number names, and expanded form." (.cache/curricula/us-common-core-math.pdf, page 19, 2.NBT.A.3), and
  England's "23 = 20 + 3" is Year 2 (.cache/curricula/england-ks1-2.pdf, page 11). IM shows "(30 + 5)" in grade 1
  (curriculum.illustrativemathematics.org-k5-teachers-grade-1-course-guide-scope-and-sequence.txt, Unit 4, Section B).
  All are within the beginner level.
- Breaking a ten: England and the DfE place non-standard partitioning in Year 2 "to support subtraction"
  (.cache/curricula/england-ks1-2.pdf, page 11), and the United States decomposes a ten in grade 2, 2.NBT.B.7
  (.cache/curricula/us-common-core-math.pdf, page 19). IM teaches it in grade 1 unit 4 Section D for adding
  (curriculum.illustrativemathematics.org-k5-teachers-grade-1-course-guide-scope-and-sequence.txt). The brief points it

- The order of the teen names in section 020: the progression and the DfE add a second way of saying the numbers,
  "one-ten-one, one-ten-two" (assets.publishing.service.gov.uk-ads-system-uploads-attachment-data-file-954511-Maths-guidance-year-1.pdf,
  page 12) and "ten one, ten two, ten three" (isbe.net-Documents-number-base-ten-k-5.pdf, page 5). The brief names the

  education.nsw.gov.au-cy-and-numeracy-teaching-and-learning-resources-numeracy-connecting-number-names.txt,
