# Breaking a ten

## Header

- Assumes: from `ten-ones`, the reader knows a ten and a one, the rod and the cube, bundling ten ones into a ten with
  the button "Bundle ten ones", counting the tens first and then counting on the ones, and dragging a rod or a cube
  from the supply. From `teens`, each number from 11 to 19 as 1 ten and some ones, and number names. From
  `two-digits`, the place value chart with its tens column and ones column, the count under each column, the number
  written above the chart, and the tens digit and the ones digit of a two-digit number. From `place`, the value of a
  digit, a two-digit number as the value of its tens and the value of its ones together, so 56 is 50 and 6, and the
  written line under the chart, "50 + 6 = 56".
- Teaches: a ten can be broken into ten ones, which keeps the number the same, so 34 is 3 tens and 4 ones and also
  2 tens and 14 ones, and one number can be shown with tens and ones in several ways.
- Introduces:
  - Terms: `break a ten (place value)` (510).
  - Controls: a tap on the tens column that breaks its last rod into ten cubes, and the ones column holding more than
    9 cubes, so the count under it reads 14 or 24 (510). The list "Ways found", which logs each way the blocks have
    shown the number, and its readout (520). The stationery shop: the shelf of packs and pencils, the tray labelled
    "Tray" that the pencils of an order go into, with its columns "Packs" and "Pencils", the slip of the order, and a tap on the Packs column that opens a
    pack into ten pencils (620). The goals `show-with-ones` (720) and `fill-order` (730), with Check and Another
    one.
- Uses:
  - 34 as 3 rods and 4 cubes. One ten broken gives 2 tens and 14 ones, and a second gives 1 ten and 24 ones. The
    number stays 34 (page 510).
  - 23 as 2 tens and 3 ones, 1 ten and 13 ones, and 0 tens and 23 ones, written 20 + 3 = 23, 10 + 13 = 23 and
    0 + 23 = 23, the three ways a list logs (page 520).
  - A stationery shop with 6 packs of ten pencils and 2 pencils on the shelf, 62 pencils in all, and an order of 37
    pencils. 3 packs and 2 pencils make 32, so a fourth pack is opened: 3 packs and 12 pencils make 42, and 5
    pencils go back to the shelf. The tray holds 3 packs and 7 pencils, and the shelf keeps 2 packs and 5 pencils
    (page 620).
  - The challenge `show-with-ones` draws a number from 31 to 99 and a number of ones from 10 to 29 that ends in its
    ones digit, such as 57 with 17 ones or 74 with 24 ones (page 720).
  - The challenge `fill-order` draws an order from 31 to 79 and one or two missing packs, such as 43 pencils with 2
    packs on the shelf, filled with 2 packs and 23 pencils (page 730).
  - 64 as 6 tens and 4 ones, "sixty-four", 60 + 4 = 64, and 5 tens and 14 ones after a ten is broken (page 810).

The section has two teaching pages, 510 and 520, then the world page 620, the challenges 720 and 730 and the end page
810 of the story. The pages 530 to 590 stay free.

## Content

### 510 Break a ten into ten ones

A ten is ten ones joined together, so it can be broken back into ten ones. Breaking a ten undoes a bundle. The
blocks change and the number stays the same, since no block is added or taken away.

The chart shows 34: 3 rods in the tens column and 4 cubes in the ones column, 3 tens and 4 ones. The text asks the
reader to tap the tens column. Its last rod breaks into ten cubes, and they join the 4 cubes in the ones column. The
chart now shows 2 tens and 14 ones. Counting the tens first and then counting on the ones gives 10, 20, then 21, 22
and on to 34, so the blocks still show 34. The number above the chart reads 34 throughout.

The counts under the columns read 2 and 14 now. They are the tens digit and the ones digit only while each column
holds 9 or fewer, so 34 is still written 34. Bundling ten ones into a ten gives back 3 tens and 4 ones. A second
break gives 1 ten and 24 ones, and that shows 34 too. The chart has no room for a third break, since the ones
column holds 29 cubes. The text says so as a limit of the chart, and says that 34 is also 0 tens and 34 ones, so the
reader does not take it that 34 cannot be all ones.

Example: 34 is 3 tens and 4 ones, 2 tens and 14 ones, and 1 ten and 24 ones.

### 520 One number, many ways

A number can be shown with tens and ones in more than one way. Each way has one ten fewer than the way before and
ten ones more, because a ten breaks into ten ones. Each way splits the number into two parts, the value of the tens
and the value of the ones.

The chart shows 23 as 2 tens and 3 ones, and the list beside it holds that way, 20 + 3 = 23. The text asks the
reader to find every way to show 23 with tens and ones. Breaking a ten gives 1 ten and 13 ones, so 23 splits into 10
and 13 as well, 10 + 13 = 23. Breaking the last ten gives 0 tens and 23 ones, 0 + 23 = 23, which is 23 counted one
by one. With no ten left to break there is no fourth way, so the list is full at three. Bundling goes back up the
list.

The text points to the pattern down the list: the tens go 2, 1, 0 and the ones go 3, 13, 23. The way with the most
tens and fewer than 10 ones, 2 tens and 3 ones, is the one the digits of 23 give.

Example: 23 is 2 tens and 3 ones, 1 ten and 13 ones, and 0 tens and 23 ones.

### 620 A shop opens a pack

The world page. A stationery shop sells pencils in packs of ten and one by one. A pack is a ten and a single pencil
is a one, so the shop counts its pencils as tens and ones. The shelf holds 6 packs and 2 pencils. A customer asks for
37 pencils, and the slip of the order lies on the counter.

The shopkeeper puts the pencils of an order in a tray on the counter. The text says what the tray is the first time
it names it, and keeps "order" for what the customer asks for. 37 is 3 tens and 7 ones, but the shelf has only 2
pencils outside the packs. The shopkeeper opens a pack, which
breaks a ten into ten ones, and sells pencils from it. Opening a pack keeps the number of pencils in the shop the
same. Before filling the tray, the text asks the reader to work out how many packs go into the tray, how many packs
to open, and how many pencils go in. Then the reader fills the tray and checks it against the slip.

The text gives none of the answers. The readout "Pencils in the tray" tells the reader when the tray holds 37. A
reader who opens two packs and fills the tray with 2 packs and the rest in pencils has 37 too, and the text says so
after the work: both show 37.

Answer, for the writer and the engine author and kept out of the text: 3 packs go into the tray and 1 pack is opened.
3 packs and 2 pencils make 32, and with the fourth pack in the tray and opened, 3 packs and 12 pencils make 42. 5
pencils go back to the shelf, so the tray holds 3 packs and 7 pencils, 37, and the shelf keeps 2 packs and 5
pencils, 25. 62 − 37 = 25.

### 720 Show a number with more ones

The challenge. The engine draws a number and a number of ones from 10 to 29 that ends in the number's ones digit,
such as 57 with 17 ones. The chart shows the number as its tens and its ones. The reader breaks tens and presses
Check.

The text says how. The ones of the number are its ones digit. Each ten broken adds ten ones. Its example is 27
shown with 17 ones, a number the draw from 31 cannot give: 27 is 2 tens and 7 ones, 17 ones are those 7 and 10 more,
so 1 ten is broken, which leaves 1 ten and 17 ones, still 27. An example such as 20 asked as 20 ones would work only
by a coincidence of its numbers, a ones digit of 0 and ones equal to the number. A reader who breaks too many bundles
ten ones back. The number stays the same throughout. Another one draws again.

### 730 Fill an order with too few packs

The challenge. The shop again, with the shelf short of packs. The engine draws an order, such as 43 pencils, and a
shelf with 1 or 2 packs fewer than the order's tens, such as 2 packs, and enough pencils to make up the rest. The
reader fills the tray and presses Check.

The text says how. Ten pencils make as many pencils as a pack, so ten pencils can stand in for each missing pack.
The packs on the shelf make 20, and 43 needs 23 more, so the tray takes 2 packs and 23 pencils. The text says what
the tray is, where the pencils of the order go, and keeps "order" for what the customer asks for. It gives this
example in its own numbers and leaves the drawn order to the reader. Another one draws again.

### 810 What comes after tens and ones

The end page of the story. The chart shows 64, 6 tens and 4 ones, with its name "sixty-four" and the written line
60 + 4 = 64. The text sums the story up in the order it was taught. Ten ones make a ten, and a large group is quick
to count in tens first and then ones. A teen number is 1 ten and some ones. In a two-digit number the tens digit
counts the tens and the ones digit counts the ones. The place of a digit sets its value, so 64 is 60 and 4. A ten
breaks into ten ones and the number stays the same, so 64 is also 5 tens and 14 ones. The text asks the reader to
break a ten on the chart and watch the written line become 50 + 14 = 64.

Then it names the stories that follow. "Adding tens and ones" adds and takes away numbers such as these, and
breaks a ten when a number has too few ones to take away. "Hundreds and thousands" bundles ten tens into a hundred
and writes numbers with three and four digits.

Example: 64 is 6 tens and 4 ones, 60 + 4 = 64, and 5 tens and 14 ones, 50 + 14 = 64.

## Interactivity

### 510 Break a ten into ten ones

- Shows: the place value chart with 3 rods in the tens column and 4 cubes in the ones column, the counts 3 and 4
  under the columns, and 34 written large above the chart. The panel holds the button "Bundle ten ones" and the
  readouts "Tens", "Ones" and "Number", which read 3, 4 and 34.
- The reader: taps the tens column, or presses Enter or Space on it, and presses "Bundle ten ones".
- The figure: a tap breaks the last rod of the tens column. Its ten cubes come apart along the lines of the rod and
  glide into the ones column, where they take their places in rows of five after the cubes there. The counts under
  the columns and the readouts "Tens" and "Ones" follow, 2 and 14, then 1 and 24. The number above and the readout
  "Number" stay 34. A tap that would take the ones column past 29 cubes, the third tap from 34, breaks no rod and
  the column shakes. "Bundle ten ones" glides ten cubes together into a rod that joins the tens column. It rests
  while the ones column holds fewer than 10 cubes.
- Action: tap a ten, and it breaks into ten ones while the number stays the same.

### 520 One number, many ways

- Shows: the chart with 2 rods and 3 cubes, the counts 2 and 3, and 23 above it. Beside the chart, or under it on a
  phone, the list "Ways found" has one row, "2 tens and 3 ones", with "20 + 3 = 23" beside it. The panel holds
  "Bundle ten ones" and the readouts "Tens", "Ones" and "Ways found", which read 2, 3 and 1.
- The reader: taps the tens column to break a ten and presses "Bundle ten ones".
- The figure: the blocks break and bundle as on 510. Each way the blocks show that the list does not hold yet joins
  it as a new row, "1 ten and 13 ones" with "10 + 13 = 23", then "0 tens and 23 ones" with "0 + 23 = 23". The rows
  stand in order of their tens, from the most tens to the fewest, so the list reads 2, 1, 0 tens down its rows
  whatever order the reader finds them in. The row of the way the chart shows now is lit. "Ways found" counts the
  rows. A tap on an empty tens column does nothing.
- Action: find every way to show one number, and a list logs each new way.

### 620 A shop opens a pack

- Shows: a shop counter. On the shelf, beside the counter or under it on a phone, one pack of ten pencils with a band
  round it and "6" beside it, and one pencil with "2" beside it. The numbers say how many of each the shelf holds. On the counter, the tray
  labelled "Tray" with its columns "Packs" and "Pencils", empty, the counts 0 and 0 under them and 0 written above it,
  and the slip of the order with 37 on it. The readouts "Order", "Pencils in the tray", "Packs on the shelf" and
  "Pencils on the shelf" read 37, 0, 6 and 2.
- The reader: drags a pack or a pencil from the shelf onto the tray, or taps one on the shelf to put it in. Taps the
  Packs column to open its last pack. Drags a pack or a pencil off the tray to send it back to the shelf.
- The figure: a pack or a pencil lands in its column, and the shelf's stack or heap loses one. A tap on the Packs
  column takes the band off its last pack, and the ten pencils glide into the Pencils column. The pencils in the
  tray stay the same. A pencil sent back joins the heap on the shelf, so the shelf can hold more pencils than it
  opened with. The counts under the columns, the number above the tray and the readouts follow. With 3 packs and 7
  pencils in the tray they read 3, 7, 37, and the shelf 2 and 5.
- Action: open a pack in the tray to get the pencils an order needs, and send back the ones it does not.

## Challenge

The section has two challenges.

### 720 Show a number with more ones

- Goal: when the reader presses Check, the ones column holds the drawn number of ones. The blocks show the drawn
  number throughout, since breaking and bundling keep it.
- Drawn each time: a number from `number: [31, 99]` and a number of ones from `ones: [10, 29]` that ends in the
  number's ones digit and is at most the number, so the drawn ones are the ones digit and one or two tens broken,
  such as 57 with 17 ones, 74 with 24 ones and 99 with 29 ones. The chart opens with the number in tens and ones,
  its ones digit at most 9, so the page does not open solved.
- Reached by: taps on the tens column and the Bundle button, then Check. The reader works out how many tens to break
  from the ones digit: each ten broken adds ten ones.
- Hidden: the counts under the columns, the values, the written line, the name and every readout. The counts would
  read the ones column out and let the reader tap until they match, so the reader counts the cubes in their rows of
  five or reasons from the ones digit. The number above the chart shows, since it stays the same.
- Wrong answer: "The ones column holds 7 ones, and 17 are asked. Break a ten to get more ones." or "The ones column
  holds 27 ones, and 17 are asked. Bundle ten ones into a ten."
- Hint: "17 ones are 7 ones and 10 more. Break 1 ten into ten ones." With two tens to break: "24 ones are 4 ones and
  20 more. Break 2 tens into ten ones."
- When the goal holds: the tick, and the counts under the columns show. The done line reads "57 is 5 tens and 7
  ones, and also 4 tens and 17 ones."
- Idea used: breaking a ten keeps the number and adds ten ones, from 510.

### 730 Fill an order with too few packs

- Goal: when the reader presses Check, the tray holds as many pencils as the order asks, counting each pack as 10.
- Drawn each time: an order from `order: [31, 79]` and a number of missing packs from `short: [1, 2]`. The shelf holds
  the order's tens less the missing packs, and pencils enough for the rest, the order's ones and 10 for each missing
  pack, with up to 9 more so the heap is not the answer, 29 at most. For 43 with 2 missing packs, the shelf holds 2
  packs and from 23 to 29 pencils. The tray opens empty.
- Reached by: drags and taps from the shelf, drags back, then Check. The reader works out the value of the packs on
  the shelf and the pencils the order needs besides, and counts that many pencils in. The counts under the tray's
  columns help the reader count the pencils, and the total stays hidden.
- Hidden: the number above the tray, the values, the name and every readout. The slip, the numbers beside the shelf's
  stack and heap, and the counts under the tray's columns show.
- Wrong answer: "The tray holds 47 pencils, more than 43." or "The tray holds 38 pencils, fewer than 43."
- Hint: "The 2 packs make 20. 43 needs 23 more, so put 23 pencils in the tray."
- When the goal holds: the tick, and the number above the tray shows. The done line reads "2 packs and 23 pencils
  make 43 pencils."
- Ideas used: tens and ones and their values, from `ten-ones` and `place`, and ten ones in the place of a ten, the
  idea of breaking a ten read the other way, from this section. A reader who fills the order by counting pencils
  one by one up to 43 has too few pencils on the shelf, and has to count from the packs' 20.
