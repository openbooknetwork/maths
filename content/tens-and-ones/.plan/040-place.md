## Content

### 410 A 7 in the tens place has the value 70

The value of a digit is the number it stands for in its place, what the digit is worth there. A digit in the tens
place counts tens, so its value is that many tens. A digit in the ones place counts ones, so its value is that many
ones. The place of a digit sets its value, and this way of writing numbers is called place value. The place value
chart takes its name from it.

Example: 70 is 7 tens and 0 ones. Its 7 stands in the tens place and counts 7 rods, so the value of the 7 is 70. The
0 in the ones place shows there are no ones, and its value is 0. One step of the ones up to 7 cubes gives 77. Now the
number has two 7s. The 7 on the left counts 7 rods and has the value 70, and the 7 on the right counts 7 cubes and
has the value 7. One digit, two places, two values. Each step of the tens adds 10 to the value of the tens, and each
step of the ones adds 1 to the value of the ones.

### 420 17 and 71 are different numbers

Two numbers can have the same digits in different places, and then they are different numbers, because the place of
each digit sets its value. To read a two-digit number, start from the places: the left digit counts tens and the
right digit counts ones.

Example: 17 is 1 ten and 7 ones. The 1 is in the tens place, so its value is 10, and the 7 is in the ones place, so
its value is 7. Swapping the digits gives 71, which is 7 tens and 1 one. Now the 7 has the value 70 and the 1 has the
value 1. Both numbers use a 1 and a 7, yet 17 has 1 rod and 71 has 7 rods. The names can mislead: "seventeen" says
"seven" first, though its 7 is in the ones place, and its "-teen" says 1 ten. In "seventy-one", "seventy" says 7
tens. A second swap brings back 17. The 7 of 17 has the same value as the right 7 of 77 on page 410, and the 7 of 71
the same value as its left 7.

### 430 56 is 50 and 6

A two-digit number is the value of its tens and the value of its ones together. So a number splits into two parts,
the value of its tens and the value of its ones, and the two parts make the number. The text says what the
signs mean, since the story needs only `counting`: + puts the two parts together, and = says that both sides are the
same number.

Example: 56 has 5 tens, whose value is 50, and 6 ones, whose value is 6. 50 and 6 together make 56, so 56 splits
into 50 and 6, written 50 + 6 = 56 and read "50 plus 6 is equal to 56". Adding the digits gives 5 + 6 = 11, which
counts the 5 tens as 5 ones. A rod is a ten wherever it lies, so 5 rods are 50 and 6 cubes are 6 in any order, even
with the cubes first, and 6 + 50 = 56 as well. Blocks may lie in any order, and digits may not: 56 is written with
the tens digit 5 on the left, and 65 is another number.

### 610 Roses in bunches of ten

A florist ties roses in bunches of ten. A bunch is a ten and a rose is a one. Bunches stand in a bucket, the tens
column, and roses wait in a jug, the ones column. Once the jug holds 9 roses or fewer, the florist reads the stock
from the two places: the bunches give the tens digit and the roses in the jug give the ones digit.

Example: a delivery leaves 2 bunches in the bucket and 28 roses in the jug, 48 roses in all. The counts 2 and 28 do
not make the digits of the stock, since 28 is more than 9. Tying one bunch takes 10 roses from the jug: 3 bunches
and 18 roses. Tying a second gives 4 bunches and 8 roses. Now the 4 bunches have the value 40 and the 8 roses the
value 8, and 40 + 8 = 48. The florist knows there are 48 roses from 4 bunches and 8 roses, without counting 48 roses
one by one.

On the figure a bunch is a rod, a rose is a cube, the bucket is the tens column and the jug is the ones column.

## Interactivity

### 410

- Shows: the place value chart with 7 rods in the tens column and no cubes in the ones column. The digits 7 and 0
  stand under the columns, and under each digit its value, 70 and 0. The number 70 stands large above the chart. The
  steppers "Tens" at 7 and "Ones" at 0 stand in the panel.
- The reader: steps the ones up to 7, and then steps either column up or down.
- The figure: each step adds a piece at the end of its column or takes the last one away. The digit under the
  column, its value and the number above follow. A step of the tens changes the value of the tens by 10 and a step of
  the ones changes the value of the ones by 1.
- Action: step a column up or down, and the value under its digit follows, 10 for each rod and 1 for each cube.

### 420

- Shows: the place value chart with 1 rod and 7 cubes, the digits 1 and 7, the values 10 and 7, the number 17 above
  the chart with its name "seventeen" under it. The chip "Tens and ones" reads "1 ten and 7 ones". The button "Swap the
  digits" stands in the panel.
- The reader: presses "Swap the digits", reads the new number, and presses it again.
- The figure: the pieces that change glide, so the chart holds 7 rods and 1 cube. The digits read 7 and 1, the
  values 70 and 1, the number 71, the name "seventy-one" and the chip "7 tens and 1 one". A second press brings back
  17.
- Action: press a button, and the tens digit and the ones digit trade places, so the blocks, the values, the number
  and its name all change.

### 430

- Shows: 5 rods side by side and 6 cubes after them, without the chart. The number 56 stands above the blocks. The
  written line "50 + 6 = 56" stands under the figure. The chips "Value of the tens" and "Value of the ones" read 50
  and 6. No button.
- The reader: taps the 50 or the 6 of the written line.
- The figure: the tapped number lights its blocks, 50 the 5 rods and 6 the 6 cubes. The text says in words that the
  blocks show 56 in any order and that 6 + 50 = 56 as well.
- Action: tap a part of the line, and the blocks whose value it is light up.

### 610

- Shows: the florist's counter as the place value chart. The tens column is a bucket with 2 bunches of roses, each
  tied with a ribbon, and the ones column is a jug of 28 roses. The counts 2 and 28 stand under the bucket and the
  jug, with the values 20 and 28 under them. The written line reads "20 + 28 = 48". The chips "Bunches" and "Roses in
  the jug" read 2 and 28. The button "Tie a bunch" stands in the panel.
- The reader: presses "Tie a bunch" until it rests.
- The figure: each press takes ten roses from the jug, a ribbon goes round them, and the bunch glides into the
  bucket. The counts read 3 and 18, then 4 and 8. The values read 30 and 18, then 40 and 8, and the line reads
  "30 + 18 = 48", then "40 + 8 = 48". With 8 roses in the jug the button rests.
- Action: the world page joins the bundling of `ten-ones` with the values of this section, so tying bunches turns
  the counts of the bucket and the jug into the digits of the stock.
