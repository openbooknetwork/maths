### 210 A teen number is a ten and some ones

The numbers from 11 to 19 are the teen numbers. Each of them is 1 ten and some ones: 11 is 1 ten and 1 one, 12 is 1
ten and 2 ones, and so on up to 19, which is 1 ten and 9 ones. 10 is 1 ten and no ones, and 20 is 2 tens, so
neither is a teen number. The 1 in 16 stands for the ten, and the 6 for the ones, so 16 is read "1 ten and 6 ones".
A reader who sees "one, six" in 16 has read two separate digits, the error the progression names.

A number name is the word a number is said with, such as sixteen for 16. The names from thirteen to nineteen say
the ones first and the ten last: sixteen is six and "teen", and "teen" stands for the ten. "Thir" in thirteen says
three, and "fif" in fifteen says five. Eleven and twelve have names of their own, which do not say their 1 one and
2 ones.

Example: the rod is 1 ten and the 6 cubes are 6 ones. To find how many, start at 10 for the rod, without counting
its ten cubes again, and count on the cubes: 11, 12, 13, 14, 15, 16. So 1 ten and 6 ones make 16, sixteen.

The figure shows one rod and the cubes, the number in digits and its name above them. The reader steps the ones
from 1 to 9 and watches the number, the name and "1 ten and 6 ones" change together, and the text asks the reader
to step down to 1 and 2 to see eleven and twelve, the two names that do not say their ones.

The text keeps within 210 words, so it works one example, 16, and names the others in a line.

### 220 Fourteen and forty

Fourteen and forty sound alike, and they are very different numbers. The end of a name tells them apart.
"-teen" says 1 ten, so fourteen is 1 ten and 4 ones, 14. "-ty" says tens, so forty is 4 tens, 40, with 0 ones. In
fourteen the 4 counts ones, and in forty the 4 counts tens. A "-ty" name says its tens first and its ones after,
as forty-one is 4 tens and 1 one, 41, the opposite order to a teen name, which says its ones first.

Example: 14 is 1 rod and 4 cubes. 40 is 4 rods and no cubes, and its 4 rods hold 40 cubes where fourteen has 4
cubes beside its rod. 41 is 4 rods and 1 cube. The three numbers 14, 40 and 41 all have the digit 4, and the 4
counts ones in 14 and tens in 40 and 41.

The figure shows the blocks of 14 with the number and its name above them. The reader sets the Tens stepper to 4
and the Ones stepper to 0, and the rods, the number and the name change to forty. Setting the ones to 1 makes
forty-one. The text then asks the reader to make fifteen and fifty and hear the "-teen" and "-ty" of each.

Example in numbers: 14 = 1 ten and 4 ones, 40 = 4 tens and 0 ones, 41 = 4 tens and 1 one. The reader's own try:
15 = 1 ten and 5 ones, 50 = 5 tens and 0 ones.

### 210

- Shows: base ten blocks without the chart. One rod lies on the left, ten cubes high with a line between each two
  cubes, and 6 cubes stand after it, apart from each other on the grid. Above the blocks the number 16 is written
  large in digits, and under it the number name "sixteen". The stepper "Ones" stands at 6, from 1 to 9, and the
  readout "Tens and ones" reads "1 ten and 6 ones". The rod stays one rod throughout.
- The reader: presses + or − on the Ones stepper.
- The figure: a press adds a cube after the others or takes the last cube away. The number, the name and the
  readout follow at once, so at 1 the figure shows 11, "eleven" and "1 ten and 1 one", and at 9 it shows 19,
  "nineteen" and "1 ten and 9 ones". The stepper stops at 1 and at 9, so the blocks always show a teen number.
- Action: change the ones beside one ten, and the number, its name and its tens and ones change together.

### 220

- Shows: base ten blocks without the chart, 1 rod and 4 cubes, with 14 written large above them and "fourteen"
  under it. The steppers "Tens" at 1, from 1 to 9, and "Ones" at 4, from 0 to 9. The readout "Tens and ones" reads
  "1 ten and 4 ones".
- The reader: presses + or − on either stepper.
- The figure: a press of Tens adds a rod at the end of the row of rods or takes the last rod away, and a press of
