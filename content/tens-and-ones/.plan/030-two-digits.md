# Two digits for tens and ones

## Header
- Assumes: from section `ten-ones`, the reader knows that ten ones make a ten, that a rod is a ten and a cube is a
  one, that ten cubes are bundled into a rod with the button "Bundle ten ones" and the number stays the same, that a
  rod or a cube is dragged from the supply onto the stage, and that a group is counted tens first, 10, 20, 30, then
  on by ones. From section `teens`, the reader knows that a teen number is 1 ten and some ones, read "1 ten and 4
  ones", that a number is written in digits and said by its number name, that "-ty" in a name says tens, and the
  stepper "Ones". From `counting`, the reader counts to 100.
- Teaches: in a two-digit number the left digit counts the tens and the right digit counts the ones, each digit runs
  from 0 to 9 so the two digits write every number up to 99, a 0 in the ones place shows there are no ones, and
  eighty-six is written 86, its tens digit and its ones digit side by side, and not 806 as it is heard.
- Introduces:
  - Terms: `place value chart (figure)` and `column (place value chart)` (310), `tens digit, ones digit (two-digit
    numbers)` (310), `two-digit number (numbers)` (320), `place, tens place, ones place (digits)` (330).
  - Controls: the place value chart with its two columns, the pieces dragged from the supply landing in their column,

    ones" on the chart, where the new rod glides into the tens column (330). The two cards under the chart, where a
    tap slides the ones card over the 0 of the tens card and back (340). The Check button and "Another one" of a
    challenge (710).
- Uses:
  - 2 tens and 6 ones, 26, and one more rod, 36 (310).

## Content

### 310 The left digit counts the tens

The idea: a digit is one of the ten symbols 0 to 9 that numbers are written with, and the text says so first, since
no earlier page has. A place value chart has two columns. The tens column on the left holds the rods, and the ones column on the
right holds the cubes. Under each column stands a digit that counts the pieces in it. Read together, left then right,
the two digits write the number. The digit on the left is the tens digit and counts the tens, and the digit on the
right is the ones digit and counts the ones. The ones digit is the number's last digit, which the text says once, as
the terms' rules ask.

The example: 2 rods and 6 cubes on the chart are 2 tens and 6 ones. The digit under the tens column is 2, the digit
under the ones column is 6, and the number above the chart is 26, twenty-six. The text says it in full sentences, as
the DfE asks: "This is the number 26. The 2 shows 2 tens. The 6 shows 6 ones." Counting tens first, 10, 20, then on,
21 to 26, gives the same 26, which the text says in one sentence and does not teach again.

The new case: a rod dragged onto the chart makes 3 tens and 6 ones. The tens digit turns to 3, the ones digit stays 6,
and the number reads 36.

On the figure each rod is a ten and each cube a one.

### 320 Every number up to 99

The idea: each column of the chart holds from 0 to 9 pieces, so each of the two digits is one of the digits 0 to 9.
With 0 to 9 tens and 0 to 9 ones the two digits write every number up to 99. A number from 10 to 99 is a two-digit
number. A number from 0 to 9 has only a ones digit, so it is written with one digit.

The example: the chart opens at 2 tens and 8 ones, 28, twenty-eight. The tens go up to 9, and only the tens digit
changes: 98, ninety-eight. The ones go up to 9, and only the ones digit changes: 99, ninety-nine, 9 tens and 9 ones. 99 is the
last two-digit number of the count, and the next number, 100, needs three digits. The ones stepper stops at 9, since
ten ones make a ten, which the next page shows.

One sentence names the one-digit case: with the tens down to 0 and 9 ones, the number is 9, written with its ones
digit alone.

The text adds that a new number is set by changing only the digit that differs, with no need to build it again from an
empty chart.

### 330 A 0 shows there are no ones

The idea: in a written number each digit has its place. In a two-digit number the tens digit is in the tens place on
the left, and the ones digit is in the ones place on the right. When the ones are bundled into a ten and no ones are
left, the number is written with a 0 in the ones place. The 0 shows there are no ones, and it keeps the tens digit in
the tens place.

The example: the chart shows 5 tens and 9 ones, 59. One more cube makes 10 cubes in the ones column. The row under the
chart reads 5 and 10, which do not write a number with two digits, while the number above reads 60. Ten ones make a
ten, so they are bundled: a rod joins the tens column and the ones column is empty. The row reads 6 and 0, the number
60, sixty, 6 tens and 0 ones, and the number stayed 60 through the bundle. The 0 in the ones place shows there are no
ones. Written without it, the 6 alone would be six.

The new case: one more cube makes 6 tens and 1 one, 61. The 1 takes the place of the 0.

### 340 Eighty-six is written 86

The idea: the name of a two-digit number says its tens and then its ones. Eighty-six says eighty, which is 8 tens,
80, and then six. Writing what is heard, 80 and then 6, gives 806, a number with three digits, far more than
eighty-six. In 86 the 6 is written in the ones place, where the 0 of 80 stood, so the tens digit 8 and the ones digit 6
stand side by side.

The example: the chart holds 8 rods and 6 cubes, 8 tens and 6 ones, eighty-six. Under it lie two cards, the card 80
for the rods and the card 6 for the cubes. Side by side they read 806. The 6 laid over the 0 of 80 makes 86, the same
digits as the row under the columns, 8 and 6.

On the figure the card 80 stands for the 8 rods and the card 6 for the 6 cubes.

### 710 Build a number with rods and cubes (challenge)

The goal and the idea are under "Challenge" below. The text says the goal, works one example in numbers, "58 is 5 tens
and 8 ones, so 5 rods go in the tens column and 8 cubes in the ones column", says how to drag rods and cubes on and off
the chart and how to bundle ten cubes, and says when to press Check.

## Interactivity

### 310
- Shows: the place value chart with the heads "Tens" and "Ones", 2 rods standing in the tens column and 6 cubes in the
  ones column, in a row of five and a row of one. Under the columns the digits 2 and 6, above the chart the number 26,
  its 2 over the tens column and its 6 over the ones column. Beside or under the chart the supply, "Blocks to use",
  with one rod and one cube to take. The readout "Tens and ones" at "2 tens and 6 ones".
- The reader drags a rod or a cube from the supply onto the chart, or taps it in the supply, and it lands in its
  column. The digit under that column counts one more, and the number above follows. A rod or a cube dragged off the
  chart goes back to the supply, and its column's digit counts one fewer. The page has no Bundle button, so the engine
  stops each column at 9 pieces, and the text says a column stops at 9 because 9 is the largest digit. Ten ones are
  page 330's matter.
- In general: put a piece on the chart or take one off, and it goes to its column, the digit under that column counts
  it, and the number follows.

### 320
- Shows: the chart with 2 rods and 8 cubes, the digits 2 and 8, the number 28 above the chart and its name
  "twenty-eight" under the number. The steppers "Tens" from 0 to 9 at 2 and "Ones" from 0 to 9 at 8. No supply and no
  Bundle button.
- The reader steps the tens or the ones. A step adds or takes away one rod at the end of the tens column, or one cube
  at the end of the ones column, and only that column's digit changes. The number and its name follow. Each stepper
  stops at 0 and at 9. With 0 tens the number above stands over the ones column alone, such as 9, "nine".
- In general: set the tens and the ones on two steppers, and the blocks follow, one digit at a time, through every
  number from 0 to 99.

### 330
- Shows: the chart with 5 rods and 9 cubes, the digits 5 and 9, the number 59, the supply, and the button "Bundle ten
  ones" in the panel, resting while the ones column holds fewer than 10 cubes. The readout "Tens and ones" at "5 tens
  and 9 ones".
- The reader drags one more cube onto the chart. The ones column holds 10 cubes, the row under it reads 10, the number
  above reads 60, and the readout reads "5 tens and 10 ones". The Bundle button wakes. A press takes the ten cubes,
  which glide together into a rod, and the rod glides into the tens column. The row reads 6 and 0, the number stays 60,
  and the readout reads "6 tens and 0 ones". One more cube makes 61. Dragging pieces off works as on 310.
- In general: fill the ones column past 9 and bundle the ten ones, and the ones digit turns to 0 while the tens digit
  grows by one, with the number unchanged.

### 340
- Shows: the chart with 8 rods and 6 cubes and the digits 8 and 6 under the columns, the name "eighty-six" above the
  chart, and no number in digits above it. Under the chart the two cards side by side, "80" under the tens column and
  "6" under the ones column, so they read 80 6. No steppers, no supply, no Bundle button.
- The reader taps the ones card, and it slides onto the tens card over its 0, so the cards read 86. A tap on the cards
  slides the ones card back, and they read 80 6 again.
- In general: tap a card, and it slides over the 0 of the other card to write the number, and a tap slides it back.

### 710
- Shows: the empty chart with no digits, the supply, the Bundle button, the goal line naming the number, Check and
  "Another one". See "Challenge".
- The reader drags rods and cubes onto the chart and off it, and may bundle ten cubes into a rod. Then the reader
  presses Check.
- In general: build a named number on an empty chart, then commit it.

## Challenge

- Goal: build the drawn number on the chart with rods and cubes, then press Check. The goal line: "Build 47 with rods
  and cubes, then press Check."
- Drawn each time: a number from 11 to 99, as the goal `build-number` draws it from `number: [11, 99]`. The chart opens
  empty, so the number, at least 11, is not there as the page opens. "Another one" draws again and empties the chart.
- Reached: the reader drags a rod or a cube from the supply onto the chart, where it lands in its column, drags one off
  to take it away, and may press "Bundle ten ones". The goal holds when the blocks show the drawn number with 9 cubes or
  fewer, and the reader commits with Check. A wrong Check says in which direction the blocks are off, as the brief
  writes it: "The blocks show 52, more than 47.", "The blocks show 38, less than 47.", or with the number right and 10
  or more cubes, "The blocks show 47 with 17 ones. Bundle ten ones into a ten." The hint: "47 is 4 tens and 7 ones. Put
  4 rods in the tens column and 7 cubes in the ones column." A change after Check takes the check back.
- Hidden: the digits under the columns, the number, the name, the cards, the steppers, every readout and every link,
  since each gives the number's tens and ones away or counts the blocks for the reader.
- The idea used: the tens digit counts the tens and the ones digit counts the ones, so a number is built as its tens
  digit in rods and its ones digit in cubes. Reading 47 as 4 tens and 7 ones is the knowing that reaches the goal,
  where adding cubes one at a time to 47 takes 47 drags and leaves the cubes to bundle. It is the first challenge of
  the story and needs this one idea.
