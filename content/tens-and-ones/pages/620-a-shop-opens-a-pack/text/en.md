A stationery shop sells pencils in packs of ten, and also one at a time. A pack is a ten and a single pencil is a one. When a customer wants more single pencils than the shelf has, the shopkeeper opens a pack. That breaks a ten into ten ones, so the shop has just as many pencils as before.

The shopkeeper puts the pencils for each order in a tray on the counter. Say a customer wants 16 pencils, which is 1 ten and 6 ones, and the shelf has 5 packs but only 3 single pencils. 3 pencils are too few for the 6 ones, so the ones have to come from a pack. The shopkeeper puts 2 packs in the tray, 1 for the ten and 1 to open. Once it is open, the tray holds 1 pack and 10 pencils, which is 20. That is 4 more than 16, so 4 pencils go back to the shelf, and the tray holds 1 pack and 6 pencils.

The slip on the counter is for an order of [37](@order). On the shelf, the number beside the pack is [6](@supply-tens) and the number beside the pencil is [2](@supply-ones). Work out how many packs go into the tray, how many to open and how many pencils to put in. Then fill the tray:

- Drag a pack or a pencil onto the tray, or tap it on the shelf.
- Tap the Packs column to open its last pack. The "Pencils in the tray" readout stays the same, since the pack's ten pencils are still in the tray.
- Drag a pencil off the tray to send it back.

Opening one pack more than you need still works, because a tray with one pack fewer and ten pencils more shows 37 as well.
