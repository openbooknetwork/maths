A two-digit number is the value of its tens and the value of its ones together.

Take 56. Its 5 tens have the value 50, and its 6 ones have the value 6. Counting on 6 from 50 gives 56, so 56 splits into 50 and 6. You write this as `50 + 6 = 56` and say "50 plus 6 is equal to 56". The + sign puts the two parts together, and the = sign says that both sides are the same number. If you add the digits instead, `5 + 6 = 11` counts the 5 tens as if they were 5 ones.

A rod is a ten wherever it lies, so 5 rods and 6 cubes show 56 in any order. You can even write the ones first, as `6 + 50 = 56`. But the tens digit of 56 has to stay on the left, because 65 is another number.

The line under the blocks reads [50 + 6 = 56](@written). Each part of it belongs to some of the blocks.

- Tap the 50 in the line, and the 5 rods light up, because together they have the value 50.
- Tap the 6, and the 6 cubes light up.
- The "Value of the tens" and "Value of the ones" readouts show the same two parts.
