A ten is ten ones joined together, so you can take it apart into ten ones again. This is called breaking a ten, and it undoes a bundle. You have fewer tens and more ones, but the number stays the same, because nothing has been added or taken away.

Take 34, which is 3 tens and 4 ones. When you break one of the tens, its ten ones join the 4 ones, so you have 2 tens and 14 ones. Count the tens first, 10, 20, and then count on the 14 ones: 21, 22, and on up to 34. So 2 tens and 14 ones show 34 too.

On the chart, 34 is 3 rods and 4 cubes.

- Tap the tens column. Its last rod breaks into ten cubes, which move into the ones column. The Number readout stays at [34](@number).
- The counts under the columns now read 2 and 14. They are the digits of the number only while each column holds 9 or fewer, so 34 is still written 34.
- Tap again for 1 ten and 24 ones. The chart has no room for a third break, but 34 is also 0 tens and 34 ones.
- Press "Bundle ten ones" to join ten cubes back into a rod.
