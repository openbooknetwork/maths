Every number is written with the ten digits 0 to 9. A place value chart has two columns. The tens column on the left holds the rods, and the ones column on the right holds the cubes. Under each column is a digit that counts the pieces in it. The left digit counts the rods, which are tens, so it is called the tens digit. The right digit counts the cubes, which are ones, so it is the ones digit, and it is also the number's last digit. The two digits, read from left to right, make the number.

For 2 rods and 6 cubes the digits are 2 and 6. You can say it like this: "This is the number 26. The 2 shows 2 tens. The 6 shows 6 ones." Counting tens first gives the same number: 10, 20, then 21 to 26. If one more rod goes in the tens column, only the tens digit changes, and the number is 36.

"Blocks to use" has a rod and a cube to take.

- Drag the rod onto the chart, or tap it, and 26 turns into 36.
- Add a cube the same way, and only the ones digit changes, while the tens digit stays. A column stops at 9 pieces, since 9 is the largest digit.
- Drag a piece off the chart to send it back.
