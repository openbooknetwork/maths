In a two-digit number, the spot on the left is called the tens place, and the spot on the right is the ones place. When there are no ones, you write a 0 in the ones place. The 0 shows there are no ones, and it keeps the tens digit in the tens place. If you left the 0 out, 6 tens would be written 6, which is six.

Take 5 rods and 9 cubes, which make 59. One more cube makes 60, but now there are 10 cubes in the ones column, and 10 does not fit in the ones place. Ten ones make a ten, so you bundle them into a rod for the tens column. Bundling keeps the number the same, so 6 tens and 0 ones are still 60, written with a 0 in the ones place.

The chart starts at 59, and you take cubes from "Blocks to use".

- Drag one more cube onto the chart. The row under the ones column reads 10, while the number above already reads 60.
- Press "Bundle ten ones". The ten cubes join into a rod in the tens column, and the row reads 6 and 0.
- Add another cube to make 61, and watch the 1 take the place of the 0.
