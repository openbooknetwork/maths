Base ten blocks show tens and ones. A cube is one block on its own, so it is a one. A rod is ten cubes joined end to end, and the lines across it show the ten cubes. So a rod is a ten, just like a bundle of ten pencils.

To count the blocks, you count the rods in tens first. Then you count on the cubes in ones, starting from the last number you said.

Take 5 rods and 2 cubes. You count the rods 10, 20, 30, 40, 50, which is 5 tens. The 2 cubes are extra ones, and each adds just one, so you count on from 50: 51, 52. The blocks show 52, which is 5 tens and 2 ones. They are also 52 cubes, because the 5 rods are made of 50 cubes and there are 2 more. Counting every cube one by one gives 52 too.

When you tap a piece, the next number of the count appears above it. A rod gets 10 more than the last number said, and a cube gets 1 more.

- Tap the rods first, then the cubes, and say each number as you go.
- Watch the "Last number said" readout, now [0](@count). It goes up by 10 for each rod and by 1 for each cube.
