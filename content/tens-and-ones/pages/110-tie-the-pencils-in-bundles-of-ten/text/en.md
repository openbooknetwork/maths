When you count a big heap of pencils one by one, it takes a long time, and it is easy to count a pencil twice or miss one. A quicker way is to tie the pencils in bundles of ten. Each pencil is a one. Ten pencils tied together make a bundle, and a bundle of 10 ones is called a ten.

Once every pencil is in a bundle, you count the bundles in tens, because each bundle adds ten more pencils. Rows of ten counters end on 10, 20, 30 for the same reason. Tying the pencils changes how they lie, but their number stays the same.

Say you tie up all the pencils in a jar and get 6 bundles. 6 bundles are 6 tens, so you count 10, 20, 30, 40, 50, 60, and there are 60 pencils. Counting them one by one would give 60 too, only much more slowly.

The jar on the table is full of pencils, and each bundle you tie is laid on the table beside it.

- Press "Tie a bundle" until the jar is empty.
- Watch the Bundles readout, now [0](@tens). It goes up by 1 with each bundle, while the jar loses ten pencils.
- Then count the bundles in tens to find how many pencils were in the jar.
