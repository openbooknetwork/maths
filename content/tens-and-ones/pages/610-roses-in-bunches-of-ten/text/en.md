A florist ties roses in bunches of ten, because bunches are quicker to count than single roses. A bunch is a ten and a single rose is a one. The bucket of bunches is the tens column, and the jug of roses is the ones column. When the jug holds 9 roses or fewer, the bunches give the tens digit of how many roses there are, and the roses in the jug give the ones digit.

Say a delivery leaves 2 bunches in the bucket and 28 roses in the jug, which is 48 roses in all. 28 is more than 9, so 2 and 28 are not the digits of 48. Each new bunch moves 10 roses from the jug to the bucket, so there are still 48 roses. After two bunches there are 4 bunches and 8 roses, and 8 roses are too few for another bunch. The 4 bunches are 40 roses, and `40 + 8 = 48`, so the counts 4 and 8 are now the digits of 48.

The line under the figure reads [20 + 28 = 48](@written).

- Press "Tie a bunch" until the jug has fewer than ten roses.
- Watch the line. The two parts change with each bunch, and the number after the = sign stays 48.
