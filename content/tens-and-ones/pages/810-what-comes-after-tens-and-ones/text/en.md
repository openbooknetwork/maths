Ten ones make a ten, so a large group is quick to count in tens first and then in ones. A teen number is 1 ten and some ones. In a two-digit number, the tens digit counts the tens and the ones digit counts the ones. The place of a digit sets its value, so 64 is 60 and 4. And a ten can be broken into ten ones, which keeps the number the same.

Take 64. It is 6 tens and 4 ones, so it splits into 60 and 4, and `60 + 4 = 64`. Break one ten and you have 5 tens and 14 ones. The 5 tens are 50, and with the 14 ones that makes 64 again, so `50 + 14 = 64`.

The chart shows 64, sixty-four, with the line [60 + 4 = 64](@written) under it. Tap the tens column to break a ten, and watch the line and the "Tens and ones" readout, [6 tens and 4 ones](@tens-and-ones), change while the number stays the same.

"Adding tens and ones" adds and takes away numbers like these, and it breaks a ten when a number's extra ones are fewer than the ones to take away. "Hundreds and thousands" bundles ten tens into a hundred and writes numbers with three and four digits.
