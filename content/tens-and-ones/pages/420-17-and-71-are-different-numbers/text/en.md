Two numbers can use the same digits and still be different numbers, because the place of each digit sets its value.

Take 17, which is 1 ten and 7 ones. The 1 is in the tens place, so its value is 10. The 7 is in the ones place, so its value is 7. Now swap the two digits to get 71. The 7 is now in the tens place, so its value is 70. The 1 is now in the ones place, so its value is 1. Both numbers are made of a 1 and a 7, but 17 has 1 ten and 71 has 7 tens.

The names can trick you. Seventeen says "seven" first, yet its 7 is in the ones place, and its "-teen" says the 1 ten. In seventy-one, "seventy" says the 7 tens and "one" says the 1 one.

The chart starts with 1 rod and 7 cubes. Above it are [17](@number) and its name, [seventeen](@name).

- Press "Swap the digits". The tens digit and the ones digit change places, and the blocks change to match. Press it again to go back.
- Watch the 7 as it moves. The value under it is 7 in the ones place and 70 in the tens place, and the "Tens and ones" readout, [1 ten and 7 ones](@tens-and-ones), changes with it.
