# Terms of Fact families

## 010 four-facts
- Introduces:
  - `fact (calculations)`: an addition or a subtraction written with its answer as a number sentence, such as
    9 + 4 = 13 or 13 − 4 = 9. England Year 2 gives "recall and use addition and subtraction facts to 20 fluently"
    (`.cache/curricula/england-ks1-2.pdf`, page 12), and Eureka Math "Pick an addition fact from the chart."
    (`content/fact-families/.references/onlinemathlearning.com-relate-addition-subtraction-facts.txt`). A fact is a
    true number sentence, so 9 + 4 = 12 is a number sentence and not a fact.
  - `fact family (calculations)`: the facts that a whole and its two parts give: the two parts added in either
    order make the whole, and either part taken from the whole leaves the other. 9, 4 and 13 give 9 + 4 = 13,
    4 + 9 = 13, 13 − 4 = 9 and 13 − 9 = 4. When the two parts are equal, the family has two facts, as 6 + 6 = 12 and
    12 − 6 = 6. A family is named by its three numbers, "the family of 9, 4 and 13", since one whole has many
    families. After a page names it, "the family" refers back to it. The IM task gives "these are often known as
    "fact families"" and chose the word because "it is one that connects with students' lives"
    (`content/fact-families/.references/tasks.illustrativemathematics.org-content-standards-OA-1-B-tasks-1214.txt`,
    IM Commentary). Chosen over England's, IM's and Eureka's "related facts", because that names how two facts stand
    to each other and gives no noun for the group of four, which every section of the story builds on. The family
    holds four facts with the answer after the = sign, as the IM task's frames do.
- Uses: `whole, part (numbers)`, `number bond (numbers)`, `split (numbers)`, `addition, subtraction (calculations)`,
  `number sentence (calculations, comparisons)`, `true, false (number sentences)`, `sign (calculation)`,
  `plus, minus (signs)`, `is less than, is greater than, is equal to (signs)` for "is equal to", `sum (numbers)`,
  `add (numbers)`, `subtract (numbers)`, `join (groups)`, `take away (groups)`, `left (groups)`,
  `ten frame (figure)`, `cell (ten frame)`

## 020 missing-part
- Introduces:
  - `the known part, the missing part (whole and parts)`: the glossary sense as written, from `number-bonds`. This
    section reads a subtraction as a question about it: 13 − 8 asks for the missing part when the whole is 13 and
    the known part is 8, which is how many to add to 8 to make 13. Oak gives "In problems, if the whole and one part
    of the whole are known, subtraction can be used to find the missing part."
    (`content/fact-families/.references/thenational.academy-res-addition-lessons-understand-the-relationship-between-addition-and-subtraction.txt`,
    Key learning points). The places that the missing part fills are the empty cells of the ten frames.
- Uses: `fact (calculations)`, `fact family (calculations)`, `whole, part (numbers)`, `subtract (numbers)`,
  `add (numbers)`, `addition, subtraction (calculations)`, `count on, count back (numbers)`, `difference (numbers)`,
  `the greater number, the smaller number (numbers)`, `number line (figure)`, `jump (number line)`,
  `start (number line)`, `ten frame (figure)`, `cell (ten frame)`, `take away (groups)`, `left (groups)`,
  `ten, one (numbers)`

## 030 undoing
- Introduces:
  - `undo (calculations, groups)`: a calculation undoes another when it starts from the other's answer and gives
    back the number the other started from. Subtracting the number that was added undoes an addition, so 13 − 5 = 8
    undoes 8 + 5 = 13, and adding the number that was taken away undoes a subtraction. With counters, taking away
    the counters that were joined undoes the join. Oak Year 1 gives "Subtraction can 'undo' addition." and "When a
    given amount is added to the start of a story, to 'undo' the change, we must subtract the same amount."
    (`content/fact-families/.references/thenational.academy-and-subtraction-lessons-know-that-addition-and-subtraction-are-inverse-operations.txt`,
    Key learning points). Chosen over Oak's and NCETM's "inverse", which Oak has to define as "the opposite in
    effect", because "undo" says the same in a word the reader already knows.
  - `check (calculations)`: find out whether the answer of a calculation is right with the calculation that undoes
    it. 7 + 6 = 13 is checked by 13 − 6, which has to leave 7. England Year 2 gives "use this to check calculations"
    and "They check their calculations, including by adding to check subtraction" (`.cache/curricula/england-ks1-2.pdf`,
    page 12).
- Uses: `start (number line)`, where a jump and the same jump back land, `fact (calculations)`, `fact family (calculations)`, `add (numbers)`, `subtract (numbers)`,
  `join (groups)`, `take away (groups)`, `left (groups)`, `sum (numbers)`, `whole, part (numbers)`,
  `addition, subtraction (calculations)`, `number sentence (calculations, comparisons)`, `plus, minus (signs)`,
  `number line (figure)`, `mark (number line)`, `point (number line)`, `jump (number line)`,
  `to the right, to the left (number line)`, `ten frame (figure)`, `cell (ten frame)`

## 040 bar
- Introduces:
  - `bar model (figure)`: a long bar for the whole, split into two parts, each part as long as its number. The
    whole's number is above the bar and each part's number is in its part. A bar is a row of counters drawn as one
    length, so the part for 9 is as long as 9 counters in a row, and the whole is as long as both parts together.
    After a page names it, "the bar" is the whole bar and "a part of the bar" is one of its two parts. A part to be
    found carries a question mark. Oak gives the keyword "Bar model - A bar model is a pictorial representation of a
    problem where bars are used to represent the known and unknown quantities."
    (`content/fact-families/.references/thenational.academy-rs-0-to-5-lessons-use-a-bar-model-to-represent-a-whole-partitioned-into-two-parts.txt`,
    Keywords), NCETM "the whole bar" and "A question mark is used to indicate the part that is unknown."
    (`content/fact-families/.references/ncetm.org.uk-classroom-resources-ca-the-bar-model.txt`). Chosen over the
    "tape diagram" of the US progression and the Japanese lessons and Singapore's "part-whole model", because it is
    the word England and Oak give a reader in Year 1, and the rough maps of `missing-number` and `word-problems` say
    "bar".
  - `split (bar model)`, coined: the line across the bar where it splits into its two parts, which the reader drags.
    No source names the place on the bar where the parts meet. The noun follows the verb `split (numbers)` and Oak's
    keyword "Partition - To split a whole up into parts.", so moving the split moves where the whole splits.
  - `end (bar model)`, coined: the right end of the bar, which the reader drags to set the whole. No source names a
    handle on the bar. A new sense beside `end (number line)`, in the same plain word.
- Uses: `whole, part (numbers)`, `the known part, the missing part (whole and parts)`, `fact (calculations)`,
  `fact family (calculations)`, `split (numbers)`, `addition, subtraction (calculations)`, `subtract (numbers)`,
  `add (numbers)`, `undo (calculations, groups)`, `greater (numbers)`, `less (numbers)`, `ten frame (figure)`

## Rules
- A number sentence is any row of numbers and signs, true or false, and a comparison such as 3 < 5 is one too. A
  fact is a true addition or subtraction. A number bond is a whole and its two parts written as an addition, and its
  fact family is the two additions and two subtractions it gives. Eureka joins the last two: "write the two
  subtraction facts that would have the same number bond".
- The facts of one family are "in the same family" or "facts of the family". The story does not say "related facts"
  or "inverse". `missing-number` is left to decide "inverse", whose rough map says "the inverse operation".
- Each addition of a family has one subtraction that undoes it, the one that takes away the number that was added.
  8 + 5 = 13 is undone by 13 − 5 = 8. 13 − 8 = 5 is in the same family and does not undo it, as Oak warns: "they
  may try to undo 6 + 4 = 10 with 10 - 6 = 4".
- Undoing is said of calculations and of what is done to a group. Join and take away stay the words for groups, and
  add and subtract the words for numbers, so a page with counters says "taking the 5 counters away undoes joining
  them" and a page with numbers says "subtracting 5 undoes adding 5".
- "Start" is the glossary sense on a number line. With counters the text says "the number you started with".
- To check is to use the calculation that undoes. The Check button commits an exercise. A text that names both
  says "the Check button".
- A subtraction can be read two ways, and both give the same number. 13 − 8 takes 8 away from 13 and asks what is
  left, and it asks for the missing part when 13 is the whole and 8 the known part. The text says "the known part"
  or "the part you know", and "missing" for the other, and leaves out "unknown", "addend", "minuend" and
  "subtrahend", as the US progression does for grade 1.
- The missing part is found by counting on from the known part to the whole, the same count that finds the
  difference of the two numbers. The text says "count on" and does not say "count up".
- Bars are longer and shorter, and numbers are greater and less. A part with a greater number is a longer part. The
  parts of a bar are equal only when their numbers are equal, as Oak warns: "Children may not see the bar model as
  proportional, so may think that the parts are equal." "Half" is said only of a domino, whose two halves are the
  same size.
- On the world page "piece" is said of the chocolate, which breaks into two pieces. In a bar model the pieces are the
  parts of the bar.
- The two additions of a family have the parts in either order, NCETM's form, and the text points back to
  `number-bonds`, where the parts swap places and the whole stays the same. "Any order" and "commutative" are left
  to `any-order`.
- Left to later stories:
  - `equals-sign` introduces "equation" and the facts with the whole before the = sign, such as 13 = 9 + 4, the
    other four of the IM task's eight.
  - `missing-number` introduces the box that covers a number. This story marks a part to be found with a question
    mark, and its end page shows the box as the next story's.
  - `word-problems` or the story that compares two amounts introduces the comparison bar model. This story's bar
    model is the part-whole one.
