# Subtracting undoes adding

## Header
- Assumes: from sections `four-facts` and `missing-part`, the reader writes the four facts of a whole and its two
  parts, and knows the words fact and fact family. From `adding-within-20`, the reader joins waiting counters into
  two ten frames, writes the join as an addition and a take away as a subtraction, and adds and subtracts on a number
  line from 0 to 20 with a start and jumps to the right and to the left.
- Teaches: adding a number and then subtracting the same number gets you back to the number you started with, and
  subtracting and then adding the same number does too, for every start and every number, so a subtraction can
  check an addition and an addition can check a subtraction.
- Introduces:
  - Terms: undo (page 310), check (page 340).
  - Controls: counters joined and then taken back to the tray, with a second written line for the take away (page
    310). On a number line, one long jump that counts on or back as many numbers as the page gives, where the second
    press counts back or on as many numbers to the start (page 320). The "Size of a jump" stepper on that line, which
    sets how many numbers a jump counts (page 330). The "Sum" stepper, which sets the sum
    an addition claims, with a second line that checks it at once (page 340).
- Uses:
  - Two ten frames with 6 round red counters and 3 square blue counters in the tray. The join writes 6 + 3 = 9.
    Taking the 3 blue counters back writes 9 − 3 = 6, and 2 of them back writes 9 − 2 = 7. The fact 9 − 6 = 3 of
    the same family does not get back to 6 (page 310).
  - A number line from 0 to 20 with the start on 9 and jumps of 8: 9 + 8 = 17, then 17 − 8 = 9 (page 320).
  - The same line with the start on 19 and jumps of 8: 19 − 8 = 11, then 11 + 8 = 19. From 11 a jump of 7 to the
    right lands on 18, so only a jump of 8 gets back to 19. The reader then sets other sizes and starts, such as
    20 − 3 = 17 and 17 + 3 = 20 (page 330).
  - Two ten frames with 12 red and 6 blue counters and the claimed addition 12 + 6 = 17. Its check 17 − 6 = 11 does
    not give back 12, so 17 is wrong. Stepped to 18, the check 18 − 6 = 12 gives back 12, so 12 + 6 = 18 is right

## Content

### 310 Taking away undoes joining

The idea: when you join some counters to a group and then take those same counters away, the group is back to the
number you started with. Taking the counters away undoes the join. One calculation undoes another when it starts
from the other's answer and gives back the number the other started from.

The example: 6 red counters are in the frames and 3 blue counters wait in the tray. Joining the 3 makes 6 + 3 = 9.
Taking the same 3 away again makes 9 − 3 = 6, the 6 you started with, and the frames look as they did before the
join. So 9 − 3 = 6 undoes 6 + 3 = 9. The number taken away has to be the number joined. Taking away only 2 of the
blue counters leaves 9 − 2 = 7, which is not 6 yet. And 9 − 6 = 3 is a fact of the same family, but it takes away 6,
the number you started with, rather than the 3 that was joined, so it leaves 3 and does not get you back to 6.

The figure draws counters alone, so no object stands for another.

### 320 The same jump back undoes a jump

The idea: on a number line, adding 8 is one long jump to the right that counts on 8 numbers at once, and
subtracting 8 is one long jump to the left that counts back 8 numbers. Each number counted back takes back one number
counted on, so 8 numbers back take back all 8 and land on the start. Subtracting 8 undoes adding 8.

The example: the start is 9 and each jump is a jump of 8. A jump to the right counts on 10, 11, 12, 13, 14, 15, 16, 17
and lands on 17, so 9 + 8 = 17. The jump back from 17 counts back 16, 15, 14, 13, 12, 11, 10, 9, which takes back the
8 numbers counted on one by one, from 17 first to 10 last, and lands on 9, so 17 − 8 = 9. The arc of the jump to the right is
over the line and the arc of the jump back is under it, and the two make a loop that ends where it began.

### 330 Undo a jump of any size from any start

The idea: it works the other way round too. A jump to the left followed by the same jump to the right also lands on
the start, so adding a number undoes subtracting it. And it works for every start and every size of jump, because
the jump back counts on as many numbers as the first jump counted back. Only the same size gets back, so the number
added has to be the number subtracted.

The example: the start is 19 and each jump is a jump of 8. The first jump counts back 8 numbers to 11, so
19 − 8 = 11. The jump back counts on as many numbers, 8 numbers to 19, so 11 + 8 = 19, and adding 8 undoes
subtracting 8. A jump of 7 to the right from 11 would land on 18, short of 19, so it would not undo the subtraction.
Then the reader sets a size and a start of their own, such as a jump of 3 to the left from 20, which makes
20 − 3 = 17 and 17 + 3 = 20.

### 340 Check a sum by subtracting

The idea: because subtracting the number you added gets you back to the number you started with, you can check an
addition with a subtraction. Take the second number away from the sum. If you get the first number back, the sum is
right. An addition checks a subtraction the same way: add back the number you took away, and you should get the
number you started with.

The example: someone works out 12 + 6 and writes 12 + 6 = 17. To check it, take the 6 away from 17. That leaves
11, and the addition started from 12, so 17 is wrong. Try 18 instead: 18 − 6 = 12 gives back the 12 the addition
started from, so 12 + 6 = 18 is right. All the check needs is the subtraction that undoes the addition.

The figure draws counters alone: the 12 red counters are the first number, the 6 blue counters the number added,
and the "Sum" stepper sets the sum that the addition claims. The counters always show the true parts, so the reader
can count them to see why 18 is right.

## Interactivity

### 310

- Shows: two ten frames with 6 round red counters in the first frame and a tray beside them with 3 square blue
  counters. Under the frames the written line reads 6 + 0 = 6. The readouts "Taken back" and "Left" read a dash.
- The reader: drags or taps the 3 blue counters into the frames, then drags or taps blue counters back to the tray.
- The figure: each blue counter joined fills the next empty cell and the written line counts it, up to 6 + 3 = 9.
  The join is done once the tray is empty, and that line stays. From then on each blue counter dragged or tapped back
  to the tray is taken away, and a second written line under the first writes it: 9 − 1 = 8, 9 − 2 = 7, and
  9 − 3 = 6 once all 3 are back, when the frames look as they did when the page opened. "Taken back" counts the
  counters back in the tray and "Left" the counters in the frames. A counter joined again takes one off the second
  line. The red counters take no taps.
- Action: join counters, then take them back one at a time, and a second line writes each take away until the group
  is back to where it began.

### 320

- Shows: a number line from 0 to 20 with every mark labelled, the start ring and the point on 9, and the buttons Jump
  left and Jump right. No written line shows yet.
- The reader: presses Jump right, then Jump left. A further press starts again.
- The figure: the first press jumps 8 from the start in one long arc, above the line for a jump to the right, and
  writes 9 + 8 = 17 under the line. The button of that direction then rests, so the other button jumps the same 8
  back, draws its arc under the line, lands on 9 and writes 17 − 8 = 9 under the first line. A press
  after the jump back clears both and makes a new first jump in the direction pressed, so Jump left first writes
  9 − 8 = 1 and then 1 + 8 = 9. A button rests where its jump would leave the line.
- Action: press a jump button, then the other one, and the second jump goes the same size back to the start.

### 330

- Shows: the number line from 0 to 20, the start ring and the point on 19, the "Size of a jump" stepper on 8, the
  buttons Jump left and Jump right, and the "Start" readout. Jump right rests, since a jump of 8 from 19 would leave
  the line. No written line shows yet.
- The reader: presses Jump left and then Jump right, then changes the size with the stepper or drags the start to
  another mark, and jumps and jumps back again.
- The figure: the jumps and lines work as on page 320, 19 − 8 = 11 and then 11 + 8 = 19. A change of the size or a
  drag of the start clears both jumps and puts the point on the start. Each new pair of jumps loops back to the
  start, whatever the size and the start.
- Action: change the size of a jump or the start, and the jump and the jump back still land on the start.

### 340

- Shows: two ten frames with 12 red counters, a full first frame and 2 in the second, then 6 blue counters in the
  second frame. The "Sum" stepper is on 17. Under the frames the first line reads 12 + 6 = 17 and the second line,
  the check, reads 17 − 6 = 11, marked wrong because 11 is not 12.
- The reader: steps the "Sum" stepper up and down.
- The figure: each step writes the claimed sum into the first line and the check into the second at once, the claim
  minus 6. The check is marked right when it gives back 12, which happens only at 18, and wrong at every other sum:
  19 − 6 = 13 and 16 − 6 = 10 are wrong as well. The counters do not change.
- Action: set the sum an addition claims, and a subtraction under it checks the claim and says whether it gives back
  the first number.

The approach:

- Counters before the number line, and the same things added and taken back before numbers alone. Nuffield: "First
  they understand that adding and subtracting the same stuff restores the physical status quo. Then they extend
  this knowledge to quantity" and "young children are readier to use the inversion principle in concrete than in
  abstract problems" (`content/fact-families/.references/nuffieldfoundation.org-sites-default-files-P2.pdf`, pages 24
  and 25). Oak works with cubes: "What amount would we subtract to undo the addition? Use the cubes to help you."
  (`content/fact-families/.references/thenational.academy-and-subtraction-lessons-know-that-addition-and-subtraction-are-inverse-operations.txt`).
  Page 310 takes this order.
- The word undo and the rule of the same amount, both ways. Oak Year 1: "Subtraction can 'undo' addition.", "When a
  given amount is added to the start of a story, to 'undo' the change, we must subtract the same amount." and "When a
  given amount is subtracted from the start of a story, to 'undo' the change we must add the same amount." (same Oak
  file, Key learning points). Pages 310 to 330.
- The wrong number undone. Oak: "they may try to undo 6 + 4 = 10 with 10 - 6 = 4" with the advice "Use practical
  equipment to illustrate the importance of the number added and subtracted being the same."
  (`content/fact-families/.references/thenational.academy-ction-lessons-represent-the-inverse-relationship-between-addition-and-subtraction.txt`,
  Common misconception). Page 310 shows the same error with its own numbers: 9 − 6 = 3 does not get back to 6.
- Cases where the same number is not taken back, beside cases where it is. Nuffield's children overgeneralised "a + b
  + b" after drills, and its taught group had bricks where "The number of bricks added and subtracted was either the
  same or differed by one" (Nuffield file, pages 23 and 25). Page 310 writes 9 − 2 = 7 for a part taken back, and
  page 330 names a jump of 7 that misses 19.
- The number line after objects. Oak asks "What number is missing from the number line?" in its exit quiz (Oak
  represent file, Exit quiz Q2), and NCETM has "They can start at a fixed point on a number line and will either
  count on (augmentation) or back (reduction)."
  (`content/adding-within-20/.references/ncetm.org.uk-media-pkxnnz0r-pska-add-q2.txt`). The digest finds no source
  that teaches undoing first on a number line, so the line comes second.
- For every number. Nuffield states the law with numbers: "equal additions and subtractions cancel each other out:
  27 + 19 – 19 = 27 and 27 – 19 + 19 = 27." (Nuffield file, page 22). Page 330.
- Checking. England Year 2: "use this to check calculations" and "They check their calculations, including by adding
  to check subtraction" (`.cache/curricula/england-ks1-2.pdf`, page 12). Singapore Primary 2 checks by working back:
  "Work Backwards 2. Using your answer, can you check if the total is 235?"
  (`content/fact-families/.references/isomer-user-content.by.gov.sg-0Drawing-20for-20Young-20Learners-Mathematics-Parents-20Workshop-202026.txt`,
