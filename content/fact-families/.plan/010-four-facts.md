# Three numbers make four facts

## Header

- Assumes: from `number-bonds`, a whole and its two parts, and that the two parts can swap places while the whole
  stays the same (page 050). From `adding-within-20`, the signs + and − and =, a number sentence and its reading
  aloud, such as "5 plus 3 is equal to 8", joining and taking away counters in two ten frames, and the steppers.
- Teaches: a whole and its two parts give four facts, the two parts added in either order and either part subtracted
  from the whole, which together are a fact family, so knowing one fact gives the other three, and two equal parts
  give a family of two facts.
- Introduces:
  - Terms: fact (calculations) on page 110, fact family (calculations) on page 130. The section uses whole and part,
    number bond, number sentence, plus and minus, "is equal to", add, subtract, join, take away, left, ten frame and
    cell from earlier stories.
  - Controls: the facts written under the figure, and the tap on a fact that picks it and lights the eggs or counters
    it speaks of (page 110). The card that covers a half of a domino with a tap and lifts with a second tap (page
    120). The steppers "First part" and "Second part" and the readout "Whole" (page 130).
- Uses:
  - A box of ten eggs, 6 brown and 4 white. One count gives 6 + 4 = 10, 10 − 4 = 6 and 10 − 6 = 4 (page 110).
  - A domino with 4 pips on one half and 3 on the other, 4 + 3 = 7. Covering the half with 3 gives 7 − 4 = 3, and
    covering the half with 4 gives 7 − 3 = 4 (page 120).
  - Counters in two ten frames, 9 red and 4 blue, the family of 9, 4 and 13: 9 + 4 = 13, 4 + 9 = 13, 13 − 4 = 9 and
    13 − 9 = 4. Then the steppers set 6 and 6, the family of 6, 6 and 12, which has two facts, 6 + 6 = 12 and
    12 − 6 = 6 (page 130).

The section has three pages, 110 to 130, and no challenge. Pages 140 to 190 stay free. The wholes stay within 10 on
pages 110 and 120 and go past 10 on page 130, as the brief asks of a section.

## Content

The text says "join" and "take away" of eggs and counters, and "add" and "subtract" of numbers, as the terms rule.
"Fact" first shows on page 110 and "fact family" on page 130. "Half" is said only of the domino. The text does not
say "related facts", "inverse", "missing part" or "known part", which belong to later sections and stories.

### 110 Brown and white eggs in one box

The story opens here. A box holds 10 eggs, 6 brown and 4 white. The brown eggs are the first part, the white eggs the
second part, and the full box is the whole. One count of the two sorts gives three number sentences, and each is
true. 6 brown eggs and 4 white eggs make 10, so 6 + 4 = 10. Take the 4 white eggs out of the box and 6 brown eggs are
left, so 10 − 4 = 6. Take the 6 brown eggs out and 4 white eggs are left, so 10 − 6 = 4. An addition or a subtraction
written with its right answer is a fact. The three facts stand under the box, and each number has the colour of its
part, with 10 in ink.

The reader taps a fact to see what it says about the box. 6 + 4 = 10 lights every egg. 10 − 4 = 6 marks the 4 white
eggs as taken out and lights the 6 brown eggs left. The text asks the reader to tap 10 − 6 = 4 and say which eggs are
taken out. The page ends on the question the story answers: one count gave an addition and two subtractions, so how
are adding and subtracting linked, and how many facts do three numbers give? The empty place beside 6 + 4 = 10 is the
place of a fourth fact, 4 + 6 = 10, which page 130 brings in.

Example: 6 + 4 = 10, 10 − 4 = 6, 10 − 6 = 4.

### 120 Cover half a domino and subtract

A domino has two halves, here one with 4 pips and one with 3. The pips of each half are a part, and the pips of the
whole domino are the whole, 7, written above it. The line under it reads 4 + 3 = 7. When you know the whole and one
part, a subtraction gives the other part: take the part you can see away from the whole. The text asks the reader to
cover the half with 3 pips. The 3 is hidden, but 7 and 4 are still there, and 7 − 4 finds the hidden half. Since
4 + 3 = 7, taking 4 away from 7 leaves 3, so 7 − 4 = 3, without counting a pip. The reader lifts the card and finds 3
pips. Covering the half with 4 pips gives 7 − 3, and 4 + 3 = 7 answers it as well: 7 − 3 = 4. One addition answered
both subtractions, so knowing one fact gives other facts.

Example: 4 + 3 = 7, with 7 − 4 = 3 and 7 − 3 = 4.

### 130 Three numbers make a fact family

The frames hold 9 red counters and 4 blue counters, 13 altogether. Two parts and their whole give four facts. The
parts added in either order make the whole: 9 + 4 = 13 and 4 + 9 = 13. `number-bonds` showed that two parts can
swap places and the whole stays the same, and the second addition is that swap. Either part subtracted from the whole
leaves the other: 13 − 4 = 9 and 13 − 9 = 4. These four facts are a fact family, the family of 9, 4 and 13. Each
fact of the family uses the same three numbers, and the whole comes first in each subtraction. Knowing one fact of
the family gives the other three, as 4 + 3 = 7 gave two subtractions on the domino.

The steppers "First part" and "Second part" set the two parts, and the facts rewrite with the new numbers. Tapping a
fact lights the counters it speaks of, as on the eggs.

The last point of the page is a family whose two parts are equal. The text asks the reader to set both steppers to 6.
Swapping two equal parts gives the same addition, so 6 + 6 = 12 is written once. Taking either 6 away from 12 leaves
the other 6, so 12 − 6 = 6 is written once too. The family of 6, 6 and 12 has two facts, in the first column of the
block, and the second column stays empty. A whole with two different parts gives four facts, and a whole with two
equal parts gives two.

The text keeps within 210 words, so it names the 6 and 6 family and does not work a second pair of different parts.

Example: the family of 9, 4 and 13: 9 + 4 = 13, 4 + 9 = 13, 13 − 4 = 9, 13 − 9 = 4. As the page's last point, the
family of 6, 6 and 12: 6 + 6 = 12 and 12 − 6 = 6.

## Interactivity

### 110

- Shows: a box of ten eggs, two rows of five cups, with 6 brown speckled eggs in the first six cups, the first row
  first, and 4 white eggs in the last four. Under the box the facts block in two rows of two: 6 + 4 = 10 in the top
  left, an empty place in the top right, 10 − 4 = 6 in the bottom left and 10 − 6 = 4 in the bottom right. The
  numbers 6 and 4 carry the colours of their parts and 10 is ink. No readout and no stepper.
- The reader: taps a fact, taps the picked fact again, or taps another fact.
- The figure: a tap picks the fact and lights it. A picked addition lights every egg. A picked subtraction draws the
  eggs of the part it takes away as dashed rings and lights the eggs left. A tap on the picked fact lets it go, and a
  tap on another fact picks that one in its place. A link to a fact in the text does the same while the reader points
  at it.
- Action: tap a fact, and the figure shows the objects it joins or takes away and the objects left.

### 120

- Shows: one domino with its two halves side by side, 4 pips on the first half and 3 on the second, and the whole's
  number 7 above it. Under it one written line, 4 + 3 = 7. No readout.
- The reader: taps a half to cover it, taps the card to lift it.
- The figure: a tap on a half covers it with a card that carries a question mark, and the line turns into the open
  subtraction that finds the covered half: 7 − 4 for the half with 3 pips, 7 − 3 for the half with 4. A tap on the
  card lifts it, and the line takes its answer, 7 − 4 = 3. The line keeps it until the reader covers a half again.
  One half is covered at a time, so a tap on the other half moves the card there.
- Action: cover a part, and the addition turns into the subtraction that finds the covered part, answered once the
  part shows again.

### 130

- Shows: the two ten frames with 9 round red counters and 4 square blue counters after them. Under the frames the
  facts block with all four facts, 9 + 4 = 13 and 4 + 9 = 13 above, 13 − 4 = 9 and 13 − 9 = 4 under them. The steppers
  "First part" at 9 and "Second part" at 4, each from 1 to 10, and the readout "Whole" at 13.
- The reader: presses + or − on either stepper, and taps a fact.
- The figure: a press adds or removes a counter of that part, the facts rewrite with the new numbers, the readout
  follows, and a picked fact lets go. When the two parts are equal, such as 6 and 6, the block holds two facts in its
  first column, 6 + 6 = 12 and 12 − 6 = 6, with the second column empty. When they differ again it holds four. A tap
  picks a fact as on page 110, and with 6 and 6 the fact 12 − 6 = 6 takes away the blue counters.
- Action: set the two parts, and every fact of the family rewrites with the new numbers, two facts when the parts
  are equal and four when they differ.
