# Fact families

The brief of the story `fact-families`. `specs/stories/fact-families.md` was its rough map, `research.md` holds
what the sources say and `terms.md` the words the story gives its reader.

## Strategy

- **Subject:** how addition and subtraction are linked. Three numbers, a whole and its two parts, give two
  additions and two subtractions. Taking away undoes adding, and adding undoes taking away. A subtraction can ask
  for the part that is missing from a whole.
- **Level:** beginner. The reader knows `adding-within-20`, and through it `number-bonds`, `more-and-less` and
  `counting`: a whole and its two parts, the signs +, − and =, a number sentence and how to read it aloud, adding
  and subtracting within 20, the difference found by counting on, and making ten.
- **At the end the reader can:** write the four facts of three numbers, use a known addition to answer a
  subtraction, undo an addition with a subtraction and the other way round, check a sum by taking a part away,
  read a subtraction such as `13 − 8` as "8 and what make 13", and draw a bar for a story and find its missing part.
- **Family and figure:** `counters`, with the two ten frames and the number sentences of `counters/add`, and the
  four facts of a family written under them. The number line of the family `line` shows a jump and the same jump
  back. A new scene draws a bar: a whole split into two parts, with each part's number in it and the whole's number
  above it.
- **Interaction that suits the subject:** the reader taps one of the four facts and sees the counters or the part
  of the bar it speaks of light up, sets the two parts with steppers and watches all four facts change, drags
  counters in and out again, covers a part, and drags the split and the end of a bar.
- **Exercises that suit the subject:** the engine draws a whole and a part, or one fact, or two numbers, and the
  reader builds the missing part or the bar that holds the family, then commits with Check. The facts are hidden
  until the goal holds.

## What the story takes from earlier stories

The story teaches no earlier idea again, as the user ruled on 2026-11-17. Where it needs an earlier idea, one page
goes over its logic.

- A whole and its two parts, from `number-bonds`. The opening section goes over it in a sentence or two, since the
  four facts are built on it.
- The signs +, − and =, the number sentence and its reading, from `adding-within-20`. The story uses them from the
  first page.
- Counting on to find a difference, from `adding-within-20`. Section 030 uses it to find a missing part and points
  back to it in one sentence. It does not teach it again.

## Sections

Page folders take the numbers of the section's range, in tens. World pages and challenges sit after the sections,
in the ranges given. The first pages of each section keep the whole within 10, and later pages go up to 20, as the
sources do.

### 010 `four-facts` Three numbers make four facts

- **Pages:** 110 to 190. Opens the story at 110.
- **Needs:** a whole and its two parts, the parts in either order (`number-bonds` page 050), the number sentences
  of addition and subtraction.
- **Teaches:** the whole and its two parts give four facts: the parts added in either order make the whole, and
  either part subtracted from the whole leaves the other. The four are a fact family, and knowing one fact gives
  the other three. Two equal parts give a family of two facts, as `6 + 6 = 12` and `12 − 6 = 6`.
- **Hands on:** the words fact and fact family, the four facts written under the figure, and the tap on a fact
  that lights the counters it speaks of.
- **Arc:** the opening page is a box of eggs, some brown and some white, where one count gives an addition and two
  subtractions. A page on a domino covers one half to turn the sum into a subtraction. The swap of the parts points
  back to `number-bonds` in one sentence. No challenge.

### 020 `missing-part` A subtraction can ask for a missing part

- **Pages:** 210 to 290. World page at 610. Challenge at 710.
- **Needs:** the four facts, and counting on to find a difference.
- **Teaches:** with 13 as the whole and 8 as the known part, `13 − 8` asks for the missing part: how many to add
  to 8 to make 13. So a subtraction can be answered with an addition of its family. The missing part is the whole
  minus the known part, and counting on from the known part to the whole finds it, as finding a difference did.
- **Hands on:** the known part and the missing part, which the bar of 040 and the story `missing-number` use.
- **Arc:** outlined empty places beside the counters show the part that is missing. The world page is a squad of
  players, some on the pitch and the rest on the bench: the coach knows the squad and the players on the pitch,
  and finds the bench with a subtraction. The challenge draws a whole and a known part, and the reader puts in the
  missing part.

### 030 `undoing` Subtracting undoes adding

- **Pages:** 310 to 390.
- **Needs:** the four facts and the missing part.
- **Teaches:** adding a number and then subtracting the same number gets you back to the number you started with,
  and the other way round. On a number line a jump forward and the same jump back land on the start, for every
  start and every size of jump. So a subtraction can check an addition, and an addition can check a subtraction.
- **Hands on:** the word undo, and checking a fact with another fact of its family.
- **Arc:** counters first, joined and taken away again, then the number line, then a sum checked by subtracting one
  part. No world page and no challenge.

### 040 `bar` A bar model holds the family

- **Pages:** 410 to 490. World page at 620. Challenges at 720 and 730. Ends the story at 810.
- **Needs:** the four facts and the missing part.
- **Teaches:** a bar model draws the whole and its two parts as lengths. The long bar is the whole, the two parts
  of the bar are the parts, drawn to scale, and one bar model holds all four facts. Moving the split keeps the
  whole and gives a new family. A story's numbers set the whole and one part, and the bar model shows the part
  that is missing, marked with a question mark.
- **Hands on:** the bar model, which `missing-number` and `word-problems` use. The end page names `missing-number`
  and `column-methods`.
- **Arc:** the bar model comes in two steps, as the sources bring it in: first counters in a row with a bar drawn
  beside them, then the bar alone. The early pages give the reader a bar ready made, and only the challenges ask
  the reader to set one. The world page is a bar of chocolate with a row of squares, broken into two pieces. The
  first challenge draws one fact, and the reader sets the bar model that holds its family. The second draws two
  numbers, and the reader sets a family that holds both: the larger as the whole and the smaller as a part, or the
  two as the parts. Either answer counts. It combines the four facts, the missing part and the bar model. The end
  page shows a bar with a question mark on one part, as the story `missing-number` will.

## Settled with the research and the terms

- The word is "fact family", and the title "Fact families" stays. "Related facts" and "inverse" stay out of the
  story, and `missing-number` decides "inverse".
- A family has four facts. The four with the whole before the = sign, such as `13 = 9 + 4`, are left to
  `equals-sign`.
- The missing part comes before undoing, in the order NCETM teaches them.
- The bike rack of the rough map is out, and a bar of chocolate takes its place.

## Engine

The story takes two families. `counters` gets a new scene, `bar`, for the bar model, and new fields, looks and a goal
in `add`. `line` gets jumps of a size and a jump back. The four facts of a family are a new part of the kit's written
line, which `counters/add` and `counters/bar` draw. Section 020 may also take `line/line` with `start` and
`written: difference` as `adding-within-20` built it, to count on from the known part to the whole. The story has no
page of `bonds`, `count`, `match` or `order`.

### The four facts (kit `written.js`, `new`)
Draws: the facts of a family under a figure, in two rows of two, the two additions above and the two subtractions
under them, or in one column of four where that shows the figure at a larger scale. For a first part 9, a second part
4 and a whole 13 they are 9 + 4 = 13, 4 + 9 = 13, 13 − 4 = 9 and 13 − 9 = 4, in this order, as the glossary writes
them. Each fact is a written line as the kit draws it, with each number in the colour of its part and carrying the
link id of that number, `first`, `second` or `whole`. Two equal parts give two facts, 6 + 6 = 12 and 12 − 6 = 6,
written in the first column. Used by: `counters/add`, `counters/bar`.

- A tap on a fact picks it, and a tap on the picked fact or a change of the numbers lets it go. One fact is picked at
  a time. The picked fact is lit and shows in the figure: an addition lights both parts, and a subtraction draws the
  part it takes away as taken away and lights the part left. Each scene below says how its figure draws this. A link
  to a fact does the same while the reader points at it.
- Each fact is a button named by its reading, "13 minus 4 is equal to 9", with a touch area at least 44px tall at
  the figure's scale. The group of four is named "Facts of the family".
- The model half gives the facts of a first part and a second part, with their words and readings, so a text and its
  live value agree. It also gives the calculation that finds a covered number: the first part plus the second part
  for the whole, and the whole minus the other part for a part.
- The readings take the kit's four strings of the written line, and the facts add one string, the name of the group.

| Link id | Reads |
|---|---|
| `written` | The first part plus the second part, such as "9 + 4 = 13". |
| `written-swap` | The second part plus the first part, such as "4 + 9 = 13". |
| `written-minus-second` | The whole minus the second part, such as "13 − 4 = 9". |
| `written-minus-first` | The whole minus the first part, such as "13 − 9 = 4". |
| `reading`, `reading-swap`, `reading-minus-second`, `reading-minus-first` | Each fact in words, such as "13 minus 4 is equal to 9". |

With two equal parts, `written-swap` reads and lights the fact of `written`, and `written-minus-first` the fact of
`written-minus-second`. A page with `written: family` offers the eight ids. They take no readout, since the figure
writes them.

### counters/bar (`new`)
Draws: a bar model across the stage, a long bar for the whole split into two parts drawn to scale. The first part is
on the left in the red of the family's first group and the second part on the right in its blue, with a line across
the bar at the split. Each part's number stands in its part, and the whole's number stands above the bar over a
bracket as long as the bar. Every 1 has the same length. On a page whose end drags, 20 fill the width the stage gives
the bar, so the bar grows and shrinks as the end moves. On a page whose end stays, the whole fills that width. A 1 is
about 17px on a 390px phone and at most 48px on a wide stage. A number too wide for its part stands under the part
with a short line up to it. Used by: `bar` (its pages, 620, 720, 730 and the end page 810), and later `missing-number`
and `word-problems`.

It is a scene of `counters` because it draws a row of the family's counters over the bar, and takes the family's
colours, its lighting and the four facts with `counters/add`. Its fields are its own, so a later story takes the
scene without any field of `add`.

### line/line (as it is, with jumps of a size and a jump back)
Draws: the number line of `adding-within-20`, from 0 to 20 in ones with every mark labelled, with the start's ring
and the written line. New: jumps of a size, each one arc over several gaps, and a jump back that undoes a jump. Used
by: `undoing`.

| Field | Values | Draws and does |
|---|---|---|
| `jump` | adds a whole number from 1, or `{ stepper: [lo, hi], start }` | The size of a jump, the widening `skip-counting` was to make. Each press of Jump left or Jump right moves the point that many gaps and draws one arc over them. A stepper sets the size, and a change of it clears the jumps and puts the point on the start. `true` stays a jump of one gap. A button rests where its jump would leave the line. |
| `undo` | `true`, `false` (default) | A jump and the same jump back. The first press of either button jumps from the start, and the button of that direction then rests, so the next jump goes back. The jump back lands on the start, and both arcs stay: a jump to the right above the line and a jump to the left below it, as on any page, so the two make a loop. Under the line the first written line shows once the first jump is made, such as 8 + 5 = 13, and the second under it once the jump back is made, 13 − 5 = 8. A first jump to the left writes 8 − 5 = 3 and then 3 + 5 = 8. A press after the jump back clears both jumps and makes a new first jump. A drag of the start or a change of the size clears both and puts the point on the start. |

| Link id | Reads |
|---|---|
| `size` | The size of a jump, on a page whose `jump` has a size. |
| `written`, `reading` | On an `undo` page, the line of the first jump and its words. A dash until the first jump. |
| `written-back`, `reading-back` | On an `undo` page, the line of the jump back and its words. A dash until the jump back. |

With a size, `jumps` counts jumps. An `undo` page offers no `jumps`, since the loop ends where it began. The stepper
and the readout of `size` are named "Size of a jump", and the readouts of the lines as on any page with `written`.

Fields that go together: `undo` takes `start`, `jump` with a size and `written: true`, and leaves out `point`,
`second`, `flag`, `bracket`, `open`, `find`, `estimate`, `moved` and `draw`.

### Turkish strings
The strings take their words from `i18n/terms/tr.yaml`. It has "bütün" and "parça" for the whole and a part, and
"bilinen" and "bilinmeyen" for the known part and the missing part. It has no word for these terms, so each string
that names one waits, in English only, until the file has it:
- `fact (calculations)` and `fact family (calculations)`: the name of the facts' group and the goal lines of
  `bar-for-fact` and `bar-for-two`.
- `bar model (figure)`: the goal lines of `bar-for-fact` and `bar-for-two`.
- `split (bar model)` and `end (bar model)`: the names of the two handles.
- `jump (number line)`, which the file marks unsourced: the stepper and the readout "Size of a jump".
- `undo (calculations, groups)` and `check (calculations)` have no entry either. No engine string names them.

### Build order
Three engine authors work side by side.
1. `counters`, 010, first author: the kit's model of the four facts and of the calculation that finds a covered
   number, then the facts block in the family, `written: family`, `facts`, the pick by tap, the link `whole` and the
   look `eggs`. Then the look `domino` with `cover`.

### Choices made in the user's place
- The bar model is the scene `counters/bar`, in the family whose counters, colours and facts it draws. A family of
  its own would need the counters and the facts block moved into the kit.
- The four facts' model goes into the kit beside the written line it extends. The block that draws and picks them
  stays in `counters`, the one family that draws it.
- The bar's handles are listed in `drag` rather than named as fields `split` and `end`, since `split` on
  `counters/add` already means a dropped counter that joins the frame under it.
- A covered number is `cover`, a field of its own beside `cup` of `bonds`: the cup hides one fixed part, and a cover
  goes on any number the reader taps and writes the calculation that finds it.
- A covered number's line is open while the card is on and takes its answer when the card is lifted, by the kit's
  rule that a line adds its answer once the figure shows it.
- The challenge `missing-part` draws no outlined places and no numbers on its counters, since either gives the
  answer away.
- On counters, `undo` joins first and takes away the same counters, as Nuffield's children learn with the same
  bricks before the same number. The other order, a take away undone by adding, is on the number line, whose `undo`
  takes a first jump either way.
- `facts` lets the opening page write an addition and two subtractions, as its arc asks, with the swap left for the
  page that points back to `number-bonds`.
- The egg box holds ten, so the opening page keeps its whole within 10, and the readouts of the eggs name parts
  rather than colours.
- The challenges' bar opens at 10 split at 5, and their goals leave out the draws that bar already holds, so no page
  opens solved. `bar-for-two` draws two different numbers, so the two answers are two different families.
- A domino half shows 1 to 6 pips, so each half is a part and each fact takes away a number.
