This story has shown you how addition and subtraction belong together.

- A whole and two different parts give four facts, two additions and two subtractions, and two equal parts give two. Together they are a fact family.
- A subtraction can ask for a missing part, and counting on from the known part finds it.
- Subtracting a number undoes adding it, so one fact of a family can check another.
- A bar model holds a whole fact family, and a question mark marks the number to find.

This bar has a whole of 14, a known part of 8 and a question mark on the other part. The line under it reads [14 − 8](@written). When you count on from 8 to 14, you say 9, 10, 11, 12, 13, 14. That is six numbers, so `14 − 8 = 6`, and `6 + 8 = 14` checks it. Lift the card to see the 6, or tap the 14 or the 8 to move the question mark.

These stories come next:

- **The missing number** puts a box where this bar has its question mark, as in `8 + ▢ = 14`. The family of 8, 6 and 14 finds the box.
- **Adding and subtracting in columns** works with numbers up to the thousands, and checks a subtraction with an addition.
