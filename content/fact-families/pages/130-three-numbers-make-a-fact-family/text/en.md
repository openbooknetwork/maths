A whole and two different parts give four facts. The two parts added in either order make the whole, and either part subtracted from the whole leaves the other part. These four facts are called a fact family, and you name a family by its three numbers.

9 round red counters and 4 square blue counters make 13, so `9 + 4 = 13`. Two parts can swap places and the whole stays the same, so `4 + 9 = 13` too. Taking the blue counters away leaves the red ones, and taking the red ones away leaves the blue, so `13 − 4 = 9` and `13 − 9 = 4`. That is the family of 9, 4 and 13. Both subtractions start with the whole, because you subtract a part from the whole. Any one of the four facts tells you the whole and both parts, so it gives you the other three.

Two equal parts give only two facts. Swapping 6 and 6 gives the same addition, `6 + 6 = 12`, and subtracting either 6 from 12 gives the same subtraction, `12 − 6 = 6`.

The "First part" and "Second part" steppers set the two parts, and all four facts change with them. Tap a subtraction to see which part it subtracts from the whole. Then set both steppers to 6 and watch the right column of facts: it empties, because its two facts would only repeat the two on the left.
