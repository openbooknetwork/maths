A bar model can show a story, with a question mark on the number the story asks for. The place of the question mark tells you which calculation finds it. A missing part is the whole minus the known part, and a missing whole is the sum of the two parts.

"You have 15 pencils and give 6 away. How many do you have now?" On a bar model of the story, the bar is the 15 pencils you had, the first part is the 6 you gave away, and the second part is the pencils you have now. The story does not say how many that is, so the second part carries the question mark. It is a missing part, so the whole minus the known part, `15 − 6`, finds it. 6 and 9 make 15, so `15 − 6 = 9`, and you have 9 pencils now.

The line under the bar reads [15 − 6](@written).

- Tap the card to lift it. The 9 appears, and the line gets its answer.
- Now turn the story round: "You gave 6 pencils away and have 9 left. How many did you have?" Tap the 15 to cover it. You know both parts now, so the line turns into the addition that finds the whole. Work it out before you lift the card.
