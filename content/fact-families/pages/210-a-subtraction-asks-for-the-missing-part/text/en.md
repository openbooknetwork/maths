You can read a subtraction such as `7 − 2` as the question "2 and what make 7?" The whole comes first and the known part after the − sign. Subtracting 2 from 7 leaves the missing part, and since the two parts make the whole, the missing part is also the number you add to 2 to make 7.

You answer it by counting on from the known part to the whole, as you did for a number bond and for a difference. For `7 − 2` you start at 2 and say 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, and each number you say is one more counter added to the 2. You said five numbers, so `2 + 5 = 7`, and that answers the subtraction: `7 − 2 = 5`. Adding the two numbers you know gives `7 + 2 = 9`, which is greater than the whole, and a part cannot be greater than its whole.

The 2 round red counters are the known part, and the outlined places after them are for the missing part.

- Tap the frames to put a square blue counter in the next place. It carries the number your count has reached. Tap a blue counter to take the last one out.
- When every place is full, the last blue counter carries the whole, and the answer is how many blue counters there are. The "Counters put in" readout counts them, and the four facts of the family replace the subtraction under the frames.
- Set the "Whole" stepper to another number and count on again from 2.
