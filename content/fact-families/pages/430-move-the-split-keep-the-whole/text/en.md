The split is the line across the bar where its two parts meet. If you move the split, both parts change, but the whole stays the same, because the end of the bar does not move. So each place of the split gives a family with the same whole.

Take a bar of 11 split into 7 and 4. Move the split one step to the left. The first part loses 1 and becomes 6, and the second part gains that 1 and becomes 5. The bar is as long as before, so the whole is still 11, and the facts change to the family of 6, 5 and 11.

The "First part" readout shows [7](@first) and the "Second part" readout shows [4](@second), and the two always make 11.

Drag the split by its handle until the first part is 4. The parts are now 4 and 7, so this is the family of 7, 4 and 11 again, with its parts the other way round.

Each family turns up at two of the 10 places of the split, once each way round. So a bar of 11 gives 5 families: 1 and 10, 2 and 9, 3 and 8, 4 and 7, and 5 and 6.
