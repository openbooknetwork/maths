A bar of chocolate works like a bar model. All its squares together are the whole, and when you break it, the two pieces are the two parts. When you break off a piece for a friend, that piece is the known part and the squares you keep are the missing part.

Say a bar has 10 squares and you break off 4 for a friend. The 10 squares are the whole and the 4 are the known part, so the squares you keep are `10 − 4`. 4 and 6 make 10, so `10 − 4 = 6`, and you keep 6 squares.

The bar on the figure has 12 squares, and you will break off 5 for a friend. Before you break it, work out how many you keep and which subtraction tells you.
