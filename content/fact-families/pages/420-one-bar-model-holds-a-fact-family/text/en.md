One bar model holds every fact of its family. The two parts put together make the whole, in either order. Taking either part off the whole leaves the other part.

Take 8 marbles, 5 red and 3 blue, drawn as a bar split into 5 and 3. Putting the two parts together gives `5 + 3 = 8`. The parts in the other order fill the same bar, so `3 + 5 = 8` as well. Now start from the whole bar and take the part of 3 off it. The part of 5 is left, so `8 − 3 = 5`. If you take the part of 5 off instead, the part of 3 is left, so `8 − 5 = 3`. On a bar, taking away means taking a length off the whole.

The four facts are under the bar. Tap a fact to see what it does to the bar.

- Tap [5 + 3 = 8](@written). Both parts and the 8 light up, since the parts together are the whole.
- Tap [8 − 3 = 5](@written-minus-second). The part of 3 turns pale with a dashed outline, because it is taken away, and the part of 5 that is left lights up.
- Before you tap `8 − 5 = 3`, say which part comes off the whole. Then tap it and see.
