Checking an addition means finding out whether its sum is right. Subtract the second number from the sum. If the sum is right, this subtraction undoes the addition and gives back the first number. Any other number means the sum is wrong. You check a subtraction the same way, by adding back the number you subtracted.

Say someone writes `12 + 6 = 17`. Subtracting 6 from 17 leaves 11, but the addition started from 12, so 17 is wrong. 11 is 1 less than 12, so 17 is 1 too small. Try 18 instead. `18 − 6 = 12` gives back the 12, so `12 + 6 = 18` is right.

The frames hold 12 round red counters and 6 square blue ones. They show the real addition, so you can count them to see the true sum. The "Sum" stepper sets the sum the addition claims. The first line under the frames reads [12 + 6 = 17](@written), and the check under it reads [17 − 6 = 11](@written-back).

- Press + and − on the "Sum" stepper. Both lines change at once, and a note under them says whether the sum is right.
- Go through the sums from 12 to 20. Only at 18 does the check give back 12.
