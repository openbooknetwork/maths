In ten-pin bowling, a player rolls a ball down a lane at ten pins. The ball knocks some pins down and the rest stay standing. Every pin is either down or standing, so the pins down and the pins standing make 10 together. That is why a player who knows the pairs that make 10 can tell at once how many pins are left for the second ball.

Say the first ball knocks down 7 pins. 7 and 3 make 10, so 3 pins are still standing. If you do not know that pair yet, count on from 7 to 10: 8, 9, 10. Each number you say counts one of the pins still standing, so 3 pins are left.

All ten pins are standing at first.

- Press "Roll the ball". The ball knocks down at least one pin.
- Count the pins that fell, and work out how many are still standing before you look at the readouts.
- Roll again to knock down more of the standing pins, and press "Set up the pins" to stand all ten up again.

The Standing readout now shows [10](@first) and the "Knocked down" readout shows [0](@empty). After every roll, the two make 10.
