A ten frame is a box of ten cells, in two rows of five. Each cell holds one counter or is empty. Every cell is one or the other, so the counters and the empty cells make 10 together, however many counters there are.

Put 6 counters in a ten frame, and 4 cells are left empty. So 6 and 4 make 10, and the 4 empty cells tell you how many more counters you need to fill the frame. When two numbers are the two parts of 10, like 6 and 4, they are called a pair that makes 10. Now put in one more counter. It takes one of the empty cells, so there are 7 counters and 3 empty cells, and 7 and 3 are a pair that makes 10 too.

The ten frame on the figure starts empty. Tap an empty cell to put a counter in it, and tap a counter to take it out again. The Counters readout shows [0](@first) and the "Empty cells" readout shows [10](@empty). Watch both as you tap. When one goes up by 1, the other goes down by 1, so the two always make 10.
