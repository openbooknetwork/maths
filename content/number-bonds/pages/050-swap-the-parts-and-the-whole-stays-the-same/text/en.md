The two parts of a number bond can swap places. When they do, you have the same counters as before, only in different places, so the whole stays the same.

9 splits into 3 and 6. Swap the parts and you have 6 and 3. These are the same nine counters, with 6 of them now on the left and 3 on the right, so 6 and 3 make 9 as well. Each split of 9 comes with another one that has the parts the other way round. So when you know that 1 and 8 make 9, you know that 8 and 1 make 9 too.

The whole, 9, is in the circle at the top. The left part starts with 3 red counters, and the right part with 6 blue ones.

- Press Swap and watch the readouts. "First part" and "Second part", now [3](@first) and [6](@second), trade their numbers, and Whole stays 9.
- Drag a few counters across to make another split, then press Swap again.
