A pair that makes 20 fills two ten frames exactly. Whatever the first part is, the second part is the number of cells it leaves empty, so each number from 0 to 20 has just one number that makes 20 with it. You can find that number from a pair that makes 10.

Take 12. Put 12 counters in two ten frames. You count 10 of them into the first frame, and then 11 and 12 go into the second, so 2 counters are in the second frame. The first frame is full, so the second part has to fill the empty cells of the second frame. 2 and 8 make 10, so 8 of its cells are empty. That means 12 and 8 make 20.

The first part is red, and it fills the cells from the start of the first frame, the upper one. The second part is blue and fills the cells that are left. Move the "First part" slider to any number from 0 to 20. The parts are now [12](@first) and [8](@second).

- When the first part is over 10, look for the pair that makes 10 in the second frame.
- When it is under 10, look for that pair in the first frame.
