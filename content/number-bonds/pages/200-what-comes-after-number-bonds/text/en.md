This story has shown you how a number splits into two parts, and how the two parts make the whole again.

- A whole and its two parts make a number bond. The parts can swap places, and the whole stays the same.
- A ten frame holds 10, so the counters in it and its empty cells make 10.
- Every number from 11 to 20 is a full ten and some more.
- When you add a full ten to one part of a pair that makes 10, you get a pair that makes 20.
- To find a missing part, you count on from the known part to the whole.

The figure shows the whole, 10, split into 4 and 6. If the parts swap places, 6 and 4 make 10 too. If you add a full ten to the 4, you get 14 and 6, a pair that makes 20.

The next story, "Adding and subtracting within 20", builds on number bonds. There you put two parts together to find the whole, and you learn to write that with the + sign. You also take one part away from the whole to find the other part, and write that with the − sign.
