Choose two numbers with the sliders. The multiples of a turn blue and the multiples of b turn orange. A number that is a multiple of both wears both colours.

The numbers they share are the common multiples of a and b. For [4](@a) and [6](@b) there are [8](@count-common) of them up to 100.

The smallest one is the least common multiple, circled on the grid. For these two numbers it is [12](@lcm). Every other common multiple is a multiple of it.

Multiplying the two numbers always gives a common multiple: [4](@a) × [6](@b) = [24](@product). It is the least one only for some pairs. Find a pair whose least common multiple is their product, such as 4 and 5. Then find a pair where one number is a multiple of the other.
