Write 1 in the middle, then wind 2, 3, 4 and onwards round it in a square spiral. The primes are purple. This is the Ulam spiral, first drawn by the mathematician Stanisław Ulam in 1963.

Among the first [121](@numbers) numbers, [30](@count-primes) are prime. Move the slider to grow the spiral up to 441 numbers. The cells shrink and the primes gather along slanting lines.

Tap a number to light up the two diagonals that cross at it, and read how many primes they hold. Tap 9 and look at the diagonal from 1 down to the right. It holds 9, 25, 49 and 81, which are the odd squares, and no prime at all.

Some diagonals hold far more primes than others. Look for the longest line of purple cells you can find.
