The hundred square has rows of ten, but any width works. Move the slider to change the number of columns and watch the numbers flow into new rows. The primes are purple.

With [6](@columns) columns, the primes after the first row stand in just [2](@prime-columns) columns. At this width they are the first column and the fifth.

To see why, set the width to 6 and tap 2, then tap 3. The multiples of 2 fill three columns and the multiples of 3 fill two. Every number in those columns has a factor of 2 or 3, so no prime past the first row can stand there.

Try other widths. The multiples of a number line up in columns when the width is one of its multiples. When the width is one more or one less than a multiple, they run down slanting lines.
