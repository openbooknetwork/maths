Counting in hundreds means saying 100, 200, 300 and so on, and each jump adds 100. When you say it aloud, it is the
count 1, 2, 3 with "hundred" after each number: one hundred, two hundred, three hundred. Just as ten jumps of 10
reach 100, ten jumps of 100 reach 1000, which you say as "one thousand".

A flat of 100 small squares is a group of 100, so you can count flats in hundreds, one jump for each flat. For three
flats you say 100, 200, 300. Take care not to mix up the two numbers: you counted 3 groups, and they hold 300 small
squares.

The line has a mark every 100, and the point starts on 0.

- Say the next hundred, then press "Jump right" to check it. A flat appears over the jump, and the number you land
  on joins the count under the line.
- Watch the two readouts after three jumps. Groups counts the flats and reads 3, and Point counts the squares and
  reads 300. Now they read [0](@groups) and [0](@point).
- Make ten jumps to reach 1000. "Jump left" takes back the last jump and its flat.
