Socks come in pairs, so you can count them a pair at a time. Each pair adds 2 socks, so you say every second number and leave out the one in between: 2, 4, 6, 8. This is called counting in twos. When you count by adding the same number each time, and that number is more than 1, it is called skip counting.

Take 7 pairs of socks on a washing line. Counting in twos, you say one number for each pair: 2, 4, 6, 8, 10, 12, 14. After 10 comes 12, because 10 and 2 more is 12. As in any count, the last number you say, 14, is how many socks there are. The 7 is how many pairs there are, so take care not to mix up the two.

Under the washing line is a number line, with a mark for each number from 0 to 20 in order from left to right. The dot on it is called the point, and it starts on 0. Each pair you hang moves the point one jump of 2, to the number two marks on, and the pair hangs over its jump as a group of 2. The jumps are equal, because each one adds 2.

- Press "Jump right" to hang the next pair, and say out loud the number you land on. It appears in a box and in the count under the line.
- Watch the numbers you land on: 2, 4, 6. Each jump passes over one number, first 1, then 3, then 5.
- The number at the point counts the socks, and the Groups readout counts the pairs. "Jump left" takes the last pair down again.
