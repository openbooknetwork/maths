A hand has five fingers, so the fingers of one hand are a group of 5. You count the fingers on many hands in fives, one jump of 5 for each hand: 5, 10, 15, 20. Two jumps of 5 make 10, so every second jump lands on a ten. The other jumps land halfway between two tens, on 5, 15, 25. That is why every number you say ends in 5 or 0.

When you count back in fives, each jump subtracts 5, so after a ten you say the number halfway down to the ten
before, as 35 after 40. It is easy to say 30 after 40, but that is a jump of 10.

Say 10 children each hold up one hand. The count goes 5, 10, 15, 20, 25, 30, 35, 40, 45, 50, so 10 hands have 50
fingers. To check, count back in fives from 50 and put a hand down at each jump: 45, 40, 35, 30, 25, 20, 15, 10, 5,
0. You reach 0 after 10 jumps, one for each hand, so the count was right.

The line has labels only at 0 and the tens. The number at the point counts the fingers, and the Groups readout counts the
hands.

- Press "Jump right" ten times and say each number aloud. A hand appears over each jump. Watch the labels: every
  second jump lands on a labelled ten, and the jumps between land on the numbers that end in 5.
- Press "Jump left" ten times and say each number. Each press takes a hand away.
