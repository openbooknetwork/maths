A number is even when that many objects can be put in groups of 2 with none left over. It is odd when one object is left over. A count in twos from 0 lands only on even numbers, because each jump adds a whole group. A count from 1 keeps its one object on its own, so it lands on the odd numbers in between.

Start with 1 counter and count in twos: 3, 5, 7, 9. At 9 there are 4 groups of 2, one for each jump, and the counter you started with is left over, so 9 is odd. From 0 the count goes 2, 4, 6, 8, and 8 counters make 4 groups of 2 with none left over, so 8 is even. Add one counter to an odd number and it makes a group of 2 with the one left over, so the next number is even. Add one to an even number and the new counter is left over, so the next number is odd. That is why even and odd numbers take turns.

The point starts on 1, with one counter at the left end. The "Left over" readout shows [1](@left-over).

- Each press of "Jump right" adds a group of 2, and the counter on its own stays.
- Drag the start ring, the circle where the jumps begin, from mark to mark. Watch "Left over": it reads 1 on every odd number and 0 on every even one.
