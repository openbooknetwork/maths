A print shop has an order for 700 flyers, and each flyer takes one sheet of paper. Paper comes in packs of 100
sheets, so each pack is a group of 100 and you count the sheets in hundreds, one jump for each pack. To find how many
packs the shop needs to fetch, start the count at the sheets it already has and count on in hundreds to the sheets
it needs. Each jump is one more pack, so the number of jumps is the number of packs to fetch.

There are 2 packs on the shelf, which is 200 sheets. Start at 200 and count on: 300, 400, 500, 600, 700. You said
five numbers, so that is 5 jumps, and the shop fetches 5 more packs. With the 2 on the shelf it uses 7 packs, which
hold the 700 sheets. If you counted from 0 instead, you would fetch 7 packs, which is 2 too many, because 2 are on the
shelf already.

Each pack over the line is a pack of 100 sheets, and the number at the point counts the sheets.

- Drag the start to 200, the sheets on the shelf. Two packs appear between 0 and 200.
- Press "Jump right" until the count under the line says 700.
- Watch the Jumps readout, [0](@jumps), count only the packs you fetch, while the Groups readout, [0](@groups),
  counts the packs in all.
