A count in tens can start on any number. Numbers are written with ten signs, 0 to 9, called digits, and the last digit of a number is the one on the right, like the 3 in 43. A jump of 10 keeps the last digit and adds one to the digit before it, so from 43 you land on 53. To see why, count on in ones from 43 to 53. The last digit passes 0 once, at 50, where the 4 becomes a 5, and it ends back on 3. So on the hundred square a count in tens goes straight down the column of its start.

Count in tens from 3. The number 3 has no digit before it, so think of a 0 there. The first jump makes that 0 a 1,
and you land on 13. Then come 23, 33, and on to 93, each ending in 3. Thirteen and thirty sound alike, so take
care: 13 is under 3, and 30 is in the last column.

The point starts on 3.

- Press Jump until the point is on 93 in the bottom row, then press "Jump back" to go back up.
- Tap another cell, or use the Start stepper, to move the start, then count down and up its column. Watch the last
  digit of each number you land on: it stays the start's last digit, while the digit before it goes up or down by one.
