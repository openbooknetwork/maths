A count in hundreds can start on any number. You read a number past 100 with its hundreds first, so 863 is "eight
hundred and sixty-three", which is 8 hundreds and 63 more. A jump of 100 keeps the last two digits, the 63, and adds
one to the digit before them, so from 863 you land on 963. A jump back keeps them too and takes one away from that
digit, so from 863 you land on 763.

To see why the last two digits stay, split a jump of 100 into ten jumps of 10, since ten jumps of 10 take you from 0
to 100. Each jump of 10 keeps the last digit, so from 863 you go 873, 883, 893, 903, 913 and on to 963. You read 903
as "nine hundred and three". The digit before the 3 goes 7, 8, 9, then 0 at 903, where the 8 at the front turns into
a 9, then 1 and on to 6. So after ten jumps the last two digits are 63 again, and the digit before them has gone up by one.

Counting back in hundreds from 863, you say 763, 663, 563, 463, 363, 263, 163. One more jump back lands on 63, where
no digit is left before the 63.

The line has a mark every 10 from 3 to 993, so every mark ends in 3. The point starts on 863.

- Press "Jump left" until the count reaches 63, and watch the last two digits stay 63.
- Drag the start to another mark, such as 413 or 43, and count on or back. For 43, think of a 0 before the 43, so
  the first jump lands on 143.
