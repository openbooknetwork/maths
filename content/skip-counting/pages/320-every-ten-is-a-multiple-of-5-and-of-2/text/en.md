When you count in fives from 0, the numbers you land on, 5, 10, 15, 20 and on, are called the multiples of 5. In
the same way, the count in twos lands on the multiples of 2, which are the even numbers, and the count in tens lands
on the multiples of 10, which are the tens.

Two jumps of 5 make one jump of 10, so the count in fives lands on every ten, and every ten is a multiple of 5.
Five jumps of 2 make 10 as well, so every ten is a multiple of 2 too.

Every row of the hundred square ends with a ten. In each row the fives land on the number ending in 5 and on the
ten, so they fill two columns. 60 is a ten, so it is in all three counts. 65 is only in the fives, because it is
odd and not a ten. 64 ends in 4, so it is even and in the twos, but neither the fives nor the tens land on a number
that ends in 4.

Each button lights a whole count, and a second press turns it off.

- Press "Count in fives", then "Count in tens". The tens get a tint and a ring, and the "In both counts" readout
  counts them.
- Press "Count in twos" too and find the cells with a tint, a ring and a thick border. The readout, now "In all three
  counts", still reads 10, because those cells are the tens.
