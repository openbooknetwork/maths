The bigger your jumps are, the fewer of them you need to get from one number to another, because each one adds more. Ten jumps of 10 add
100 altogether, so one jump of 100 gets you as far as ten jumps of 10.

Start at 520 and count in tens: 530, 540, 550, 560, 570, 580, 590. The next jump of 10 lands on 600, because 590 and
10 more make 600, just as 90 and 10 make 100. Then come 610 and 620. That is ten jumps to 620. One jump
of 100 from 520 lands on 620 straight away. The farther you go, the more jumps you save: three jumps of 100 take you
from 520 to 820, and jumps of 10 would take 30.

The line runs from 500 to 700 with a mark every 10, and the point starts on 520. The "Size of a jump"
buttons start on 10.

- Press "Jump right" ten times and say each number. The count under the line keeps every number you land on.
- Press 100. The jumps disappear and the point goes back to 520. Press "Jump right" once, and it lands on 620 with
  one arc over the same ten gaps.
- Watch the Jumps readout, [0](@jumps) now. It reads 10 after the tens and 1 after the hundred, with the point on
  620 both times.
