When you count in tens, you add 10 at each jump. From 0 you land on 10, 20, 30 and on to 100, and every one of these numbers ends in 0. They are the tens. Counting back in tens takes 10 away at each jump and brings you back to 0.

The hundred square has a cell for each number from 1 to 100. The numbers go left to right along a row of ten cells
and carry on in the next row, so every row ends with a ten. A column is ten cells one under another. A jump of 10
moves you on ten numbers, which is one whole row, so you land on the cell below.

Start at 0. The first jump crosses the first row to 10, its last cell. Each jump after that goes one cell down, so
20, 30 and the other tens up to 100 fill the last column. Counting back from 100 goes up the same column, 90, 80 and
on to 10, and then back to 0.

The point starts on the 0 left of the square.

- Press Jump ten times, and say each number aloud. Watch the cells that light up: after 10, each one is under the one
  before, and together they fill the last column.
- Then press "Jump back" ten times and count back up the column.
