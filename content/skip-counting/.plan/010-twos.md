## Content

### 110 How many socks are on the line?

The idea: to count objects you give each one a number, and the last number you say is how many there are. When
the objects come in pairs, you can count a pair at a time. Each pair adds 2, so you say every second number, 2, 4, 6,
8, and the numbers in between are left out. That is counting in twos. On a number line it is a row of jumps of 2,
each from one number to the number two marks on. The jumps are equal, since each adds the same number. Counting in
jumps of one number other than 1 is called skip counting. A group of 2 is two objects that one jump counts.

The example: 7 pairs of socks hang on a washing line. One sock at a time takes fourteen numbers. In twos you say
2, 4, 6, 8, 10, 12, 14, one number for each pair, and the last number, 14, is how many socks there are. After 10
comes 12, which is 10 and 2 more. 7 is how many pairs there are, and 14 is how many socks.

On the figure, each pair of socks hangs over one jump. The number line under the washing line has a mark for each
number from 0 to 20, in order from left to right, and the number at the point is how many socks hang so far. The
"Groups" readout counts the pairs.

### 120 Count back in twos

The idea: counting back in twos says every second number going down. Each jump back subtracts 2, so a count back
from 18 says 16, 14, 12. When you take objects away a pair at a time, the count back says how many are left after
each pair. A count back in twos to 0 also checks a count in twos, since it takes back the same jumps.

The example: 18 counters stand in 9 groups of 2. Take one group away at a time and count back in twos: 16, 14, 12,
10, 8, 6, 4, 2, 0. After 12 comes 10, which is 12 and 2 less. Nine jumps back reach 0, so 9 groups of 2 make 18,
and a count in twos from 0 to 18 is right when it takes nine jumps as well.

The figure draws counters, a row of 2 counters over each jump. A group taken away stays as a dashed outline, so the
groups that are left hold the number at the point.

### 130 Odd numbers have one left over

The idea: a number is even when that many objects can be put in groups of 2 with none left over. It is odd when one
object is left over. The count in twos from 0 lands on the even numbers, 2, 4, 6, 8. The count in twos from 1 keeps
the one counter that is left over, so it lands on 3, 5, 7, 9, the odd numbers that the count from 0 jumped over.

The example: start the count on 1, one counter on its own. Count in twos from 1: 3, 5, 7, 9. 9 counters are 4
groups of 2 with 1 left over, so 9 is odd, and so are 3, 5 and 7. From 0, the count goes 2, 4, 6, 8, and 8 counters
are 4 groups of 2 with none left over, so 8 is even. The odd and even numbers take turns along the line: 8 is even,
9 is odd and 10 is even.

The figure draws counters, a row of 2 over each jump and a loose counter at the left end for the one left over.

### 610 Wheels in a bike rack

The idea: a bike has 2 wheels, so the wheels of a row of bikes count in twos, one jump for each bike. When some
bikes are in the rack already, count on in twos from their wheels. The start is no jump, so the count begins with
the number after it.

The example: in the morning 5 bikes stand in the rack. Count their wheels in twos: 2, 4, 6, 8, 10. Then 3 more bikes
come in. Count on in twos from 10: 12, 14, 16. There are 8 bikes and 16 wheels.

On the figure, each bike stands in one slot of the rack over one jump, and its two wheels are the group of 2. The
number at the point is how many wheels are in the rack, and the "Groups" readout counts the bikes.

## Interactivity

### 110

- Shows: the number line from 0 to 20 with every mark labelled, a washing line with pegs over its whole length and
  no socks, the start ring and the point on 0, and the buttons Jump left, which rests, and Jump right. The count line
  under the line is empty. The "Groups" readout reads 0.
- The reader: presses Jump right one press at a time, and Jump left to take a jump back.
- The figure: each press of Jump right moves the point two marks in one arc over the line. A pair of socks pops in
  on the pegs over that arc, a box with the number the jump lands on pops in at the end of the arc, the label of
  that mark lights, and the count line adds the number in bold, so after seven presses it reads "2, 4, 6, 8, 10, 12,
  14". "Groups" goes up by one at each jump. Jump left takes back the last jump: the point goes back two marks, the
  last pair, its box and its number leave. Jump right rests once the point is on 20.
- Action: make a jump, and a group of 2 appears over it and the count says the next number.

### 120

- Shows: the number line from 0 to 20 with every mark labelled, the start ring and the point on 18, 9 rows of 2
  counters over the line from 0 to 18, each over the stretch of one jump, and the buttons Jump left and Jump right.
  The count line is empty. "Groups" reads 9.
- The reader: presses Jump left one press at a time down to 0.
- The figure: each press of Jump left moves the point two marks to the left in one arc under the line. The group
  over that stretch turns into a dashed outline, a box with the number pops in under the arcs, and the count line
  adds it in bold: "16, 14, 12" and on to "16, 14, 12, 10, 8, 6, 4, 2, 0". "Groups" goes down by one at each jump.
  Jump left rests once the point is on 0. Jump right takes back the last jump back, and its group stands again.
- Action: jump back, and the group over the jump is taken away and the count says the number left.

### 130

- Shows: the number line from 0 to 20 with every mark labelled, the start ring and the point on 1, a loose counter
  at the left end over the stretch from 0 to 1, and the buttons Jump left and Jump right. The count line is empty.
  "Groups" reads 0 and "Left over" reads 1.
- The reader: presses Jump right a few times, then drags the start ring to 0 and jumps again, then drags it along
  the line from mark to mark.
- The figure: from 1, each jump adds a row of 2 counters over its arc and the loose counter stays at the left end, so
  the boxes and the count line read 3, 5, 7, 9 and "Left over" stays 1. A drag of the start clears the jumps and
  puts the point on the start. Over the line from 0 to the start stand whole groups of 2, with one loose counter at
  the left end when the start is odd and none when it is even, and "Groups" and "Left over" follow: on 8 they read 4
  and 0, on 9 they read 4 and 1. From 0 the count reads 2, 4, 6, 8 and "Left over" stays 0.
- Action: move the start, and the objects up to it stand in groups of 2 with one left over or none, and that one
  stays at every number the count lands on.

### 610

- Shows: the number line from 0 to 20 with every mark labelled, a bike rack of ten slots over it, 5 bikes in the
  first five slots, the start ring and the point on 10, and the buttons Jump left and Jump right. The count line is
  empty. "Groups" reads 5 and "Point" reads 10.
- The reader: presses Jump right three times. The reader may drag the start ring to another number of bikes, a
  whole bike at a time, and count on from there.
- The figure: each jump to the right stands a bike in the next slot, which pops in, and the count line reads "12, 14,
  16" after three jumps. "Groups" reads 8 and "Point" 16. A jump to the left from the start takes the
  bike over its arc out as a dashed outline. A drag of the start moves the ring two marks at a time, fills the rack
  up to it and clears the jumps.
- Action: count on in twos from the objects already there, and the count starts at the number after them.
