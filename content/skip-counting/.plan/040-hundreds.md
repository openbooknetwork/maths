# Counting in hundreds

## Header
- Assumes: from `twos`, `tens` and `fives`, the reader counts in twos, tens and fives on a number line, from 0 and
  from another start and back, with a group over each jump. The reader presses Jump left and Jump right, drags the
  start, reads the count line, and picks the size of a jump on a row of buttons. From `tens`, a jump of 10 keeps the
  last digit and adds one to the digit before it, and one count can take jumps of 10 and then jumps of 1, as the
  pencils of page 620. From `fives`, the numbers a count lands on are its multiples, and a number has digits and a
  last digit.
- Teaches: counting in hundreds says 100, 200, 300 and reaches 1000 after ten jumps, a jump of 100 keeps the last
  two digits from any start, and a bigger jump reaches a number in fewer jumps.
- Introduces:
  - Terms: `count in twos, fives, tens, hundreds (numbers)` for the hundreds, from 0 (page 410) and from any start
    and back (page 420). `group of 2, 5, 10, 100 (objects)` for the group of 100, a flat of 100 small squares (page
    410) and a pack of 100 sheets of paper (page 630). `Size of a jump (figure)`, the row of buttons that picks the
    size, 10 or 100 (page 430).
  - Controls: the line to 1000 with marks every 100 (page 410) and every 10 (pages 420 and 430). The flats of 100
    over the jumps (page 410), the packs of paper over the jumps (page 630). The goal `few-jumps` with its Check and
    "Another one" (page 730).
- Uses:
  - A line from 0 to 1000 with a mark every 100. Ten jumps of 100 from 0 say 100, 200, 300, 400, 500, 600, 700,
    800, 900, 1000, with a flat of 100 over each jump. 3 flats stand for 300 (page 410).
  - A line from 3 to 993 with a mark every 10, so every mark ends in 3. The count back in hundreds from 863 says
    763, 663, 563, 463, 363, 263, 163, 63 in eight jumps, and a jump to the right from 863 lands on 963 (page 420).
  - A line from 0 to 1000 with a mark every 10. From 520, ten jumps of 10 say 530, 540, 550, 560, 570, 580, 590,
    600, 610, 620, and one jump of 100 lands on 620. Three jumps of 100 reach 820, which jumps of 10 reach in 30
    (page 430).
  - A print shop prints 700 flyers, one sheet of paper each. Paper comes in packs of 100, and 2 packs, 200 sheets,
    are on the shelf. The count on in hundreds from 200 says 300, 400, 500, 600, 700, so the shop fetches 5 more
    packs and uses 7 in all (page 630).
  - The challenge's worked example, outside its draw: from 8 to 528 in seven jumps, five jumps of 100 to 508 and two
    of 10 to 528, where jumps of 10 alone take 52 (page 730).
  - The end page: a line from 0 to 20 with jumps of 5 and a row of 5 counters over each jump. Four jumps say 5, 10,
    15, 20, so 4 groups of 5 are 20 counters (page 810).

The section has three teaching pages, 410 to 430, the world page 630, the challenge 730 and the end page of the
story 810. The pages 440 to 490 stay free. No example's numbers come from the brief's lines of `twos`, `tens` or

## Content

### 410 Count in hundreds

The idea: counting in hundreds says the hundreds, 100, 200, 300, and each jump adds 100. Said aloud, it is the count
1, 2, 3 with "hundred" after each number: one hundred, two hundred, three hundred. The count in tens says 10, 20, 30
and reaches 100 after ten jumps. The count in hundreds reaches 900 after nine jumps, and the tenth jump lands on
1000, which is said "one thousand". So ten jumps of 100 from 0 land on 1000.

The example: the line runs from 0 to 1000 with a mark every 100, and the start is 0. Each jump of 100 puts a flat
over its arc, and each flat holds 100 small squares, so it is a group of 100. Jump by jump the count says 100, 200,
300, 400, 500, 600, 700, 800, 900, 1000. After three jumps, 3 flats stand over the line and the point is on 300.
The number of groups is 3, and the number of squares is 300. After ten jumps, 10 flats hold 1000 squares.

The figure: each flat stands for 100 things, here 100 small squares.

### 420 Count in hundreds from any start

The idea: a count in hundreds can start on any number. A jump of 100 keeps the last two digits and adds one to the
digit before them, and a jump back of 100 keeps the last two digits and takes one from the digit before them. So
counting back in hundreds from 863 gives numbers that all end in 63. The reason: a jump of 100 is ten jumps of 10, since ten tens
make 100, and each jump of 10 keeps the last digit, as page 220 shows. So from 863 the count goes 873, 883, and on
to 963, and the digit before the 3 comes round to 6 again.

The example: this line has a mark every 10 from 3 to 993, so every mark ends in 3, and labels at 3, 103, 203 and on
to 903, and at 993. A jump of 100 crosses ten gaps under one arc. The start is 863. Counting back in hundreds says
763, 663, 563, 463, 363, 263, 163. The last two digits stay 63, and the digit before them goes 7, 6, 5, 4, 3, 2, 1.
One more jump back takes 100 from 163 and lands on 63, where no digit stands before the 63. A jump back from 63
would go past the left end, and the count stops. Counting on in hundreds from 863 lands on 963, which ends in 63 as
well. Any other start keeps its last two digits the same way, such as 413, 513, 613.

### 430 The bigger the jumps, the fewer you need

The idea: the bigger your jumps are, the fewer of them you need to get from one number to another. From 520, ten jumps of 10 land on
620, and one jump of 100 lands on 620 too. So ten jumps of 10 add as much as one jump of 100.

The example: the line runs from 500 to 700 with a mark every 10 and labels at 500, 550, 600, 650 and 700, and the
start is 520. Counting in tens from 520 says 530, 540, 550, 560, 570, 580, 590, then 600, 610, 620. After 590 the next jump of 10
lands on 600, a multiple of 100, and the count goes on to 610. That is ten jumps. Picking 100 as the size of a jump
starts the count again from 520, and one jump lands on 620. Further on the difference grows: three jumps of 100 say
620, 720, 820, and counting from 520 to 820 in tens would take 30 jumps. The text says this, and the line, which
ends at 700, does not draw it.

The figure: the line counts in tens, and the jumps are of 10 or of 100, as the row "Size of a jump" picks.

### 630 Paper comes in packs of a hundred

The idea: things that come in packs of 100 are counted in hundreds. To find how many more packs you need, start the
count at what you already have and count on in hundreds to what you need. The number of jumps is the number of
packs to add.

The example: a print shop prints 700 flyers for a school fair, one sheet of paper for each flyer. Paper comes in
packs of 100 sheets, and 2 packs are on the shelf, which is 200 sheets. The count starts at 200 and goes on in
hundreds: 300, 400, 500, 600, 700. That is 5 jumps, so the shop fetches 5 more packs, and 7 packs hold the 700
sheets.

The figure: each pack over the line stands for a pack of 100 sheets. The packs between 0 and the start are the packs
on the shelf, and each jump to the right is one pack fetched. The number at the point is the number of sheets.

### 730 Get to the target in nine jumps or fewer

The idea: to reach a target in few jumps, make the big jumps first. Jump in hundreds while the count stays short of
the target, then jump in tens the rest of the way. A jump of 100 keeps the last two digits, so the hundreds bring
the digit before them to the target's, and the tens then bring the last two digits to the target's. Going back works
the same way, in hundreds first while the count stays past the target.

The example, which the challenge does not draw since its starts run from 11 to 99: from 8 to 528. Counting in
hundreds says 108, 208, 308, 408, 508. The next jump would land on 608, past 528, so the hundreds stop at 508.
Counting in tens then says 518, 528. That is five jumps of 100 and two of 10, seven jumps in all. Jumps of 10 alone
would take 52. From 528 back to 8 the count says 428, 328, 228, 128, 28, then 18, 8, again seven jumps.

### 810 What comes after skip counting

The end page of the story. It sums the story up in the order it was taught. Counting in twos says every second
number and lands on the even numbers from 0. Counting in tens from 0 lands on the numbers that end in 0, and a jump
of 10 keeps the last digit. Counting in fives lands on the numbers that end in 5 or 0, and the numbers a count lands
on are its multiples. Counting in hundreds reaches 1000 in ten jumps, and a jump of 100 keeps the last two digits. A
bigger jump reaches a number in fewer jumps, and each count goes back the same way.

The page then names the stories that follow and what each does with the count. `equal-groups` counts groups that
hold the same number, such as 4 rows of 5 cupcakes on a tray, and writes the count 5, 10, 15, 20 as 5 + 5 + 5 + 5
and as 4 × 5. `telling-the-time` counts the minutes in fives as the minute hand goes from one number of the clock to
the next. `sorting-and-pictograms` draws one picture for two things and counts the pictures in twos.
`number-patterns` finds the rule of a pattern, such as "add 5", and comes back to odd and even.

The example: four jumps of 5 from 0 say 5, 10, 15, 20, with a row of 5 counters over each jump. The number of groups
is 4 and the number of counters is 20. The figure draws counters, which stand for the cupcakes of `equal-groups`.

## Interactivity

### 410 Count in hundreds
- Shows: a line from 0 to 1000 with a mark and a label every 100, the start ring and the point on 0, the buttons
  Jump left and Jump right, and the readouts "Number at the point" 0 and "Groups" 0. The count line reads a dash.
- The reader: presses Jump right up to ten times, then Jump left.
- The figure: each Jump right draws one arc of 100 over the line, the point lands on the next hundred, a box with
  that number pops in where the arcs meet and its label lights, a flat of 100 squares pops in over the arc, and the
  count line adds the number in bold, "100, 200, 300". The readouts follow, 300 and 3 after three jumps. At 1000
  Jump right rests. Jump left takes back the last jump, and its flat goes, as a hand does on page 310. The reader may drag
  the start to another hundred, which clears the jumps and stands that many flats from 0.
- Action: make a jump, and a group of 100 joins over it and the count says the next hundred.

### 420 Count in hundreds from any start
- Shows: a line from 3 to 993 with a mark every 10, long marks with labels at 3, 103, 203 and on to 903, and at 993.
  The start ring and the point are on 863. The readouts "Start" 863 and "Number at the point" 863. The count line
  reads a dash. No groups.
- The reader: presses Jump left until it rests, then drags the start to another mark, such as 413, and jumps either
  way.
- The figure: each jump crosses ten gaps under one arc, under the line for a jump to the left, and the box of the
  number it lands on pops in. The count line lists 763, 663, 563 and on to 63, where Jump left rests. A drag of the
  start clears the jumps, the start snaps to a mark and its readout says the number, and the jumps from there keep
  that start's last two digits.
- Action: move the start and count back or on in hundreds, and every number keeps the start's last two digits.

### 430 The bigger the jumps, the fewer you need
- Shows: a line from 500 to 700 with a mark every 10 and labels at 500, 550, 600, 650 and 700. The start ring and the
  point are on 520. The row "Size of a jump" with the buttons 10 and 100, on 10. The readouts "Number at the point" 520 and
  "Jumps" 0. The count line reads a dash.
- The reader: presses Jump right ten times, then picks 100 and presses Jump right once.
- The figure: each jump of 10 draws a small arc over one gap and the count line adds the number. The count line
  keeps every number, 530 to 620, with "Jumps" 10.
  A tap on 100 clears the jumps and puts the point back on 520. Each jump of 100 then draws one arc over ten gaps,
  and the first lands on 620 with "Jumps" 1.
- Action: pick another size of jump, and the count starts again from the start and reaches the same number in fewer
  jumps.

### 630 Paper comes in packs of a hundred
- Shows: a line from 0 to 1000 with a mark and a label every 100, the start ring and the point on 0, the buttons Jump
  left and Jump right, and the readouts "Start" 0, "Jumps" 0 and "Groups" 0. The count line reads a dash.
- The reader: drags the start to 200, then presses Jump right until the count says 700.
- The figure: the start moves a whole hundred at a time, and 2 packs of paper with 100 on them stand from 0 to 200.
  Each jump pops a pack in over its arc and adds its number to the count line, 300, 400, 500, 600, 700. "Jumps" reads
  5, the packs fetched, and "Groups" 7, the packs in all.
- Action: set the start to what you already have, then count on, and the jumps say how many groups to add.

### 730 Get to the target in nine jumps or fewer
- Shows: the line the goal draws, the start ring and the point on the drawn start, the row "Size of a jump" with 10
  and 100 on 10, the jump buttons, the goal line, Check and "Another one". No readout and no count.
- The reader: picks 100 and jumps, picks 10 and jumps, takes a jump back where needed, and presses Check.
- The figure: the jumps of both sizes stay in one count, and the other button takes back the last jump. A jump or a
  change of the size after a Check takes the Check back. Once the goal holds, the count line and the boxes show.
- Action: reach a drawn number in nine jumps or fewer, with jumps of two sizes, and commit.

## Challenge

### 730 Get to the target in nine jumps or fewer
- Goal: the point is on the target after nine jumps or fewer, committed with Check. The goal line reads "Jump from
  35 to 365 in 9 jumps or fewer, then press Check." for a draw of 35 and 365.
- Drawn each time, by the goal `few-jumps` with `start: [11, 99]`, `most: 9` and `back: true`: a start from 11 to 99,
  and a target with the same last digit, a hundreds and b tens above it, with a and b each 1 or more and a + b at
  most 9, so the target is from 121 to 909 and every draw needs both sizes. Half the draws swap the two, so the count
  goes back. The line runs from the start's last digit in steps of 10 to the last mark under 1000, or to 1000, and is
  labelled at its ends and at every hundredth number from the left end, such as 5, 105, 205. The size of a jump is
  a segment of 10 and 100 on 10, with `mixed`. The target is never the start, so the page does not open solved.
- Reached by: picking 100 and jumping while the count stays short of the target, then picking 10 and jumping to it,
  and the reverse for a draw that goes back. The jump button of the count's way rests after nine jumps. A wrong
  Check says "The point is short of 365. Make another jump.", "The point is past 365. Take back a jump." or, at nine
  jumps, "That is 9 jumps, and the point is short of 365. Take back some jumps of 10 and jump in hundreds." The hint
  says "Pick 100 and jump from 35 while you stay under 365: 135, 235, 335. Then pick 10 and jump until you say 365."
- Hidden: the count is `reveal`, so the boxes and the count line show only once the goal holds. No readout, so the
  number at the point and the number of jumps stay hidden. Only the ends and every hundredth number carry labels.
- Idea used: it combines the counts. A jump of 100 keeps the last two digits (420), a jump of 10 keeps the last digit
  and moves the digit before it (`tens`), the bigger the jumps, the fewer you need (430), and one count takes jumps of two
  sizes (`tens`, page 620).
