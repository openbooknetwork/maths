# Research for Skip counting

File names without a folder are in `content/skip-counting/.references/`. Curricula are in `.cache/curricula/`. Two
files are read in `content/number-line/.references/` and carry that folder.

## Curricula

- England Year 1: "count in multiples of twos, fives and tens" and "counting in twos, fives and tens from different
  multiples to develop their recognition of patterns in the number system (for example, odd and even numbers)"
  (.cache/curricula/england-ks1-2.pdf, page 6, Year 1 Number and place value)
- England Year 2: "count in steps of 2, 3, and 5 from 0, and in tens from any number, forward and backward"
  (.cache/curricula/england-ks1-2.pdf, page 11, Year 2 Number and place value)
- England Year 3: "count from 0 in multiples of 4, 8, 50 and 100; find 10 or 100 more or less than a given number"
  and "pupils continue to count in ones, tens and hundreds, so that they become fluent in the order and place value
  of numbers to 1000" (.cache/curricula/england-ks1-2.pdf, page 18, Year 3 Number and place value)
- United States, kindergarten: "Count to 100 by ones and by tens." (.cache/curricula/us-common-core-math.pdf, K.CC.A.1)
- United States, grade 2: "Count within 1000; skip-count by 5s, 10s, and 100s." (.cache/curricula/us-common-core-math.pdf,
  2.NBT.A.2) and "Determine whether a group of objects (up to 20) has an odd or even number of members, e.g., by
  pairing objects or counting them by 2s" (.cache/curricula/us-common-core-math.pdf, 2.OA.C.3)
- Japan grade 1: "Counting concrete objects by categorizing or making equal size groups, and representing the
  results of counting in an organized manner." (.cache/curricula/japan-course-of-study-2008-en.pdf, Grade 1,
  Mathematical Activities (1)a)
- Japan grade 2: "To count objects by rearranging them into groups of the same size or by classifying them." and
  "To understand relative size of numbers by regarding 10 or 100 as a unit." (.cache/curricula/japan-course-of-study-2008-en.pdf,
  Grade 2, A(1)a and A(1)c)

## 010 twos

- Order: England counts in multiples of 2, 5 and 10 forwards and backwards from the start, and adds the odd numbers
  in the same criterion. "Count forwards and backwards in multiples of 2, 5 and 10, up to 10 multiples, beginning
  with any multiple, and count forwards and backwards through the odd numbers."
  (assets.publishing.service.gov.uk-ads-system-uploads-attachment-data-file-954511-Maths-guidance-year-1.txt, page 19,
  1NF–2)
- Order: England practises the spoken count first, then the count on a picture, then a start other than the step.
  "reciting just the number names (for example, “ten, twenty, thirty…”), without the support of visual
  representations", "counting with the support of visual representations and gestural patterns, for example pupils
  can point to numerals on a number line or 100 square" and "starting the forwards counting sequence with numbers
  other than 2, 5 or 10" (assets.publishing.service.gov.uk-ads-system-uploads-attachment-data-file-954511-Maths-guidance-year-1.txt,
  page 19, 1NF–2 Teaching guidance)
- Order: England counts objects in groups only after the counting sequence is secure. "When pupils are confident
  with the counting sequences, they should learn to enumerate objects arranged in groups of 2, 5 or 10 by skip
  counting" (assets.publishing.service.gov.uk-ads-system-uploads-attachment-data-file-954511-Maths-guidance-year-1.txt,
  page 20, 1NF–2 Teaching guidance)
- Order: Oak opens its Year 1 unit with the count in twos forwards and backwards, then counts objects in groups of
  two. "Skip count in twos forwards and backwards" is lesson 1 and "Count efficiently in groups of two" is lesson 2
  (thenational.academy-recognitions-counting-in-2s-5s-and-10s-lessons-count-efficiently-in-groups-of-two.txt, lesson
  navigation)
- Order: the odd numbers come from a count of twos on a line, with odd and even taken as pairs. "When you count in
  odds or evens you count in jumps of two." and "Numbers that can be made out of pairs are even numbers. Numbers that
  cannot be made out of pairs are odd numbers."
  (thenational.academy-ary-ks1-units-composition-of-numbers-6-to-10-lessons-skip-count-in-odds-and-evens.txt, Key
  learning points)
- Order: the odd number count is first shown on a line with the odd numbers marked. "This can initially be supported
  by a number line with odd numbers highlighted."
  (assets.publishing.service.gov.uk-ads-system-uploads-attachment-data-file-954511-Maths-guidance-year-1.txt, page 21,
  1NF–2 Teaching guidance)
- Order: NCETM teaches two more and two less before the count, from odd and even numbers. "identify the number that
  is two more or two less than a given odd or even number, identifying that two more/ less than an odd number is the
  next/ previous odd number" (axis.ncetm.org.uk-media-zclpoezm-mastering-number-year-1-overview.txt, Term 2)
- Order: in the US, counting by 2s belongs with odd and even and is the first count-by. "2.OA.3 relates doubles
  additions up to 20 to the concept of odd and even numbers and to counting by 2s (the easiest count-by in Level 2)"
  (isbe.net-Documents-counting-cardinality-k-5.txt, page 26)
- Examples: wheels counted in twos. "How many wheels are there altogether? Count in groups of 2."
  (assets.publishing.service.gov.uk-ads-system-uploads-attachment-data-file-954511-Maths-guidance-year-1.txt, page 22,
  1NF–2 Example assessment questions) and "Jacob counts the wheels in twos and Sam counts backwards to check."
  (thenational.academy-recognitions-counting-in-2s-5s-and-10s-lessons-count-efficiently-in-groups-of-two.txt, exit
  quiz Q6)
- Examples: counters put into twos, up to 20. "Provide each pupil with an even number of counters up to 20, then ask
  pupils to: a. put the counters into groups of 2 b. count in multiples of 2 find out how many counters there are"
  (assets.publishing.service.gov.uk-ads-system-uploads-attachment-data-file-954511-Maths-guidance-year-1.txt, page 22,
  1NF–2 Example assessment questions)
- Examples: a count back checks a count forward. "14, 12, 10, 8, 6, 4, 2, 0"
  (thenational.academy-recognitions-counting-in-2s-5s-and-10s-lessons-count-efficiently-in-groups-of-two.txt, exit
  quiz Q6) and "Counting back in twos from the amount altogether can be used to check that the count was accurate."
  (same file, Key learning points)
- Examples: the count from a start that is not 2. "Sam counts forward in twos starting from 8 What are the next
  numbers she says? 10, 12, 14"
  (thenational.academy-tions-counting-in-2s-5s-and-10s-lessons-skip-count-in-twos-forwards-and-backwards.txt, exit
  quiz Q2)
- Examples: odd and even counts side by side. "1, 3, 5, 7, - 9" and "11, 9, 7, 5, - 3"
  (thenational.academy-on-solving-problems-involving-money-lessons-counting-in-multiples-of-two-five-ten.txt, exit quiz
  Q2)
- Examples: the last digit repeats in the count. "When counting forwards in twos, the ones digit follows the pattern
  0, 2, 4, 6, 8" and "When counting backwards in twos, the ones digit follows the pattern 8, 6, 4, 2, 0"
  (thenational.academy-tions-counting-in-2s-5s-and-10s-lessons-skip-count-in-twos-forwards-and-backwards.txt, Key
  learning points)
- Words: "skip count", "twos". "Skip count - Counting forwards or backwards by a number other than 1."
  (thenational.academy-ary-ks1-units-composition-of-numbers-6-to-10-lessons-skip-count-in-odds-and-evens.txt,
  Keywords) and "Twos - We can skip count in 'twos', or the multiples of two."
  (thenational.academy-tions-counting-in-2s-5s-and-10s-lessons-skip-count-in-twos-forwards-and-backwards.txt,
  Keywords)
- Words: "groups of 2", "altogether". "Groups of 2 - Groups of two objects that can be counted in twos." and
  "Altogether - The amount in the whole set or group."
  (thenational.academy-recognitions-counting-in-2s-5s-and-10s-lessons-count-efficiently-in-groups-of-two.txt,
  Keywords)
- Words: "pairs", "odd", "even". "Odd - Any number that cannot be divided exactly by 2. The last digit is 1, 3, 5, 7
  or 9." and "Even - Any number that can be divided exactly by 2. The last digit is 0, 2, 4, 6 or 8."
  (thenational.academy-ary-ks1-units-composition-of-numbers-6-to-10-lessons-skip-count-in-odds-and-evens.txt,
  Keywords)
- Words: "jumps of two". "When you count in odds or evens you count in jumps of two."
  (thenational.academy-ary-ks1-units-composition-of-numbers-6-to-10-lessons-skip-count-in-odds-and-evens.txt, Key
  learning points)
- Words: "multiples of 2" in England, "counting by 2s" in the US. "count in multiples of 2"
  (assets.publishing.service.gov.uk-ads-system-uploads-attachment-data-file-954511-Maths-guidance-year-1.txt, page 22)
  and "counting them by 2s" (.cache/curricula/us-common-core-math.pdf, 2.OA.C.3)
- Errors and advice: learners leave out 12. "Children often miss out 12 when reciting the multiples of two due to the
  irregularity and not hearing the 'two' in twelve." and "Make deliberate errors around this and use practical
  objects to show the effect of adding two to ten. Orally rehearse counting in twos, both through and from 10."
  (thenational.academy-tions-counting-in-2s-5s-and-10s-lessons-skip-count-in-twos-forwards-and-backwards.txt, Common
  misconception)
- Errors and advice: learners slip back into counting in ones. "Children may be so familiar with counting in ones
  that they might find it hard to count in twos." and "Try saying all the numbers but whisper or clap the odd numbers
  to emphasise the even numbers and vice versa."
  (thenational.academy-ary-ks1-units-composition-of-numbers-6-to-10-lessons-skip-count-in-odds-and-evens.txt, Common
  misconception)
- Errors and advice: learners give the number of groups as the total. "they may count 3 groups of 2 as 3 not 6 They
  may also confuse the number of groups with the group size." and "Draw a ring around each group of 2 whilst using
  the stem sentence to help describe what you are seeing as you identify each group."
  (thenational.academy-recognitions-counting-in-2s-5s-and-10s-lessons-count-efficiently-in-groups-of-two.txt, Common
  misconception)
- Errors and advice: learners miss out numbers in a skip count. "Children miss out numbers when skip counting." and
  "Children could have resources like number lines to refer to when counting. These can be removed once children
  become more confident."
  (thenational.academy-on-solving-problems-involving-money-lessons-counting-in-multiples-of-two-five-ten.txt, Common
  misconception)
- Errors: on a line, a jump from 0 covers one more mark than its size, so learners count marks in place of gaps.
  "the beginning of the initial jump of ‘‘5’’ starts at zero, and thus includes six tick marks over the total jump of
  five spaces." and the advice "You may wish to highlight the fact that in this model, spaces are counted, not points
  on the number line." (content/number-line/.references/fisme.science.uu.nl-publicaties-literatuur-2013-teppo-number-line-zdm.txt,
  4.1)
- Errors: learners read the line as a count of marks. "some students’ explanations suggest that they do not
  appreciate that the number line is a measurement model rather than a counting model."
  (merga.net.au-0files-Annual-20Conference-20Proceedings-2006-20Annual-20CP-RP172006-20DIezmann-20Lowrie.txt,
  abstract)
- Errors: a source states a false fact about zero. "Zero is neither odd nor even and you may wish to discuss this
  with your pupils." (thenational.academy-ary-ks1-units-composition-of-numbers-6-to-10-lessons-skip-count-in-odds-and-evens.txt,
  Teacher tip). Zero is even, as the same file's own definition gives: "The last digit is 0, 2, 4, 6 or 8."

## 020 fives

- Order: Oak teaches the count in fives after the count in tens, and uses tens to explain the fives. Lesson 3 is
  "Count efficiently in groups of ten" and lesson 4 "Skip count in fives forwards and backwards"
  (thenational.academy-ions-counting-in-2s-5s-and-10s-lessons-skip-count-in-fives-forwards-and-backwards.txt, lesson
  navigation). Its starter quiz checks the count in tens: "When counting forwards in tens, which of the following is
  correct?" (same file, starter quiz Q3)
- Order: the spoken count comes first, then objects that come in fives, then objects put into fives. "This lesson
  progresses from counting items that usually appear in fives to those that are simply grouped in fives."
  (thenational.academy-n-recognitions-counting-in-2s-5s-and-10s-lessons-count-efficiently-in-groups-of-5.txt, Teacher
  tip)
- Order: forwards and backwards are taught together, between 0 and 50. "Ensure children have lots of opportunities
  to continue counting forwards and backwards in fives, between 0 and 50, beyond this lesson."
  (thenational.academy-n-recognitions-counting-in-2s-5s-and-10s-lessons-count-efficiently-in-groups-of-5.txt, Teacher
  tip)
- Order: in Year 2 each landing is the last one plus five. "If you add 5 to a multiple of 5 you get the next multiple
  of 5" and "If you subtract 5 from a multiple of 5 you get the previous multiple of 5"
  (thenational.academy-10-times-tables-lessons-explain-the-relationship-between-adjacent-multiples-of-5.txt, Key
  learning points)
- Examples: the landings end in 0 or 5, and two fives make a ten. "When counting in fives, each number ends in either
  0 or 5" and "Each time there is an even number of fives, they fit together to makes a ten."
  (thenational.academy-ions-counting-in-2s-5s-and-10s-lessons-skip-count-in-fives-forwards-and-backwards.txt, Key
  learning points)
- Examples: counters on a tens frame, five at a time. "Sam is using counters on a tens frame to count forwards in
  fives. When she adds five more counters, what is the next number she will say?"
  (thenational.academy-ions-counting-in-2s-5s-and-10s-lessons-skip-count-in-fives-forwards-and-backwards.txt, exit
  quiz Q1)
- Examples: slices of pizza counted back to check. "Sam says that there are 25 slices of pizza, so Jacob counts
  backwards to check." with "25, 20, 15, 10, 5"
  (thenational.academy-n-recognitions-counting-in-2s-5s-and-10s-lessons-count-efficiently-in-groups-of-5.txt, exit
  quiz Q5)
- Examples: hedgehogs, dots on tokens and coins. "There are 5 hedgehogs in each group. How many hedgehogs are there
  altogether?" and "There are 5 dots on each token."
  (assets.publishing.service.gov.uk-ads-system-uploads-attachment-data-file-954511-Maths-guidance-year-1.txt, page 22,
  1NF–2 Example assessment questions) and "“I have four 5p coins. How much money do I have altogether?”" (same file,
  page 40, Calculation and fluency 1NF–2)
- Examples: a count back in fives from 60. "Aisha is counting back in 5s: 60, 55, 50, What multiple of 5 comes next?"
  (thenational.academy-10-times-tables-lessons-explain-the-relationship-between-adjacent-multiples-of-5.txt, starter
  quiz Q1)
- Examples: the 100 square is a picture for the count, pointed at a number at a time. "pupils can point to numerals
  on a number line or 100 square" (assets.publishing.service.gov.uk-ads-system-uploads-attachment-data-file-954511-Maths-guidance-year-1.txt,
  page 19, 1NF–2 Teaching guidance)
- Words: "fives", "groups of 5". "Fives - Another name for the multiples of five." and "Group of 5 - Group of five
  objects that can be counted in fives."
  (thenational.academy-n-recognitions-counting-in-2s-5s-and-10s-lessons-count-efficiently-in-groups-of-5.txt,
  Keywords)
- Words: "decade number". "Decade number - These are the multiples of ten which mark the tens boundary where the
  pattern of counting changes."
  (thenational.academy-ions-counting-in-2s-5s-and-10s-lessons-skip-count-in-fives-forwards-and-backwards.txt,
  Keywords)
- Words: "multiple", "next", "previous". "Multiple - A multiple is the result of multiplying a number by another
  whole number." (thenational.academy-10-times-tables-lessons-explain-the-relationship-between-adjacent-multiples-of-5.txt,
  Keywords)
- Words: "skip-count by 5s" in the US. "skip-count by 5s, 10s, and 100s" (.cache/curricula/us-common-core-math.pdf,
  2.NBT.A.2)
- Errors and advice: after a ten, learners switch to the count in tens. "When counting in fives, children may revert
  to the pattern of counting in tens after reciting a decade number." and "Use practical equipment such as ten frames
  and base ten number boards to highlight why the next number in our count could not be a decade number."
  (thenational.academy-ions-counting-in-2s-5s-and-10s-lessons-skip-count-in-fives-forwards-and-backwards.txt, Common
  misconception)
- Errors and advice: learners give the number of fives as the total. "Children may confuse the number of groups with
  the total, so 3 groups of 5 may be counted as 3 and not 15" and "Provide opportunities for children to identify
  both the number of groups as well as the total throughout."
  (thenational.academy-n-recognitions-counting-in-2s-5s-and-10s-lessons-count-efficiently-in-groups-of-5.txt, Common
  misconception)
- Errors: counting back past a ten is hard. "When counting backwards, pupils often find it challenging to identify
  which number they should say after they have said a multiple of 10. A partially marked number line can be used for
  support." (assets.publishing.service.gov.uk-ads-system-uploads-attachment-data-file-954511-Maths-guidance-year-1.txt,
  page 12, 1NPV–1 Teaching guidance)

## 030 tens

- Order: the count by tens to 100 is the first skip count in the US, in kindergarten. "Count to 100 by ones and by
  tens." (.cache/curricula/us-common-core-math.pdf, K.CC.A.1)
- Order: England counts in tens from a multiple of ten in Year 1 and from any number in Year 2. "beginning with any
  multiple" (assets.publishing.service.gov.uk-ads-system-uploads-attachment-data-file-954511-Maths-guidance-year-1.txt,
  page 19, 1NF–2) and "in tens from any number, forward and backward" (.cache/curricula/england-ks1-2.pdf, page 11)
- Order: the spoken count of tens comes before the groups, and "tens" replaces "groups of 10" later. "“Ten, twenty,
  thirty…”" then "“1 group of 10, 2 groups of 10, 3 groups of 10…” In time, shortened to: “1 ten, 2 tens, 3 tens…”"
  (assets.publishing.service.gov.uk-ads-system-uploads-attachment-data-file-954511-Maths-guidance-year-1.txt, page 21,
  1NF–2 Language focus)
- Order: IM counts by 10 aloud and writes ten numbers to a row, then asks for patterns. "Record 10 numbers in each
  row. Then start a new row directly below." and "“What patterns do you see?”"
  (curriculum.illustrativemathematics.org-k5-teachers-grade-2-unit-5-lesson-2-lesson.txt, Warm-up)
- Order: the 100 square lays out the base-ten structure in columns of ten. "In the classroom, a list of the numerals
  from 1 to 120 can be shown in columns of 10 to help highlight the base-ten structure."
  (isbe.net-Documents-number-base-ten-k-5.txt, page 7)
- Examples: packs of ten pencils. "Figure 13: counting in tens – 9 packs of 10 pencils" and "“The pencils are in
  groups of 10, so we will count in tens.”" (assets.publishing.service.gov.uk-ads-system-uploads-attachment-data-file-954511-Maths-guidance-year-1.txt,
  page 20, 1NF–2)
- Examples: sticks in bundles of ten. "These sticks are grouped into bundles of 10. How many sticks are there
  altogether?" (assets.publishing.service.gov.uk-ads-system-uploads-attachment-data-file-954511-Maths-guidance-year-1.txt,
  page 22, 1NF–2 Example assessment questions)
- Examples: ten more from a start that is not a ten. "10 more than 17 is - 27", "10 more than 46 is - 56"
  (thenational.academy-ns-to-and-from-2-digit-numbers-lessons-find-10-more-or-less-than-a-2-digit-number.txt, exit
  quiz Q4)
- Examples: cherries, fingers, 10p coins. "There are ten cherries on each cake. How many cherries are there altogether
  in the picture?" (thenational.academy-recognitions-counting-in-2s-5s-and-10s-lessons-count-efficiently-in-groups-of-ten.txt,
  exit quiz Q4) and "There are 4 children. How many fingers are there altogether?"
  (thenational.academy-he-2-and-10-times-tables-lessons-represent-counting-in-tens-as-the-10-times-table.txt, exit
  quiz Q4)
- Examples: the last digit of a ten. "All multiples of 10 have a zero in the ones column."
  (thenational.academy-he-2-and-10-times-tables-lessons-represent-counting-in-tens-as-the-10-times-table.txt, Key
  learning points)
- Examples: a jump of ten keeps the ones digit. "When finding 10 more or 10 less than any two-digit number, the ones
  digits does not change." and "When finding 10 more than a number below 100, the tens digit increases by one."
  (thenational.academy-ns-to-and-from-2-digit-numbers-lessons-find-10-more-or-less-than-a-2-digit-number.txt, Key
  learning points)
- Examples: a count chosen by the group. "Which is the most efficient way to count the number of cups given to
  children at lunchtime?" with "count in tens"
  (thenational.academy-on-solving-problems-involving-money-lessons-counting-in-multiples-of-two-five-ten.txt, starter
  quiz Q6)
- Examples: the fives counted again in tens. "True or false. These groups of 5 could also be counted in tens." with
  "True" (thenational.academy-n-recognitions-counting-in-2s-5s-and-10s-lessons-count-efficiently-in-groups-of-5.txt,
  exit quiz Q6)
- Words: "tens", "groups of 10", "decade numbers". "Tens - Another name for the multiples of ten." and "Groups of 10 -
  Groups of ten objects that can be counted in tens."
  (thenational.academy-recognitions-counting-in-2s-5s-and-10s-lessons-count-efficiently-in-groups-of-ten.txt,
  Keywords)
- Words: "10 more", "10 less", "increase", "decrease". "10 more - 10 more than is when there is a greater amount by
  10 than another item in the set."
  (thenational.academy-ns-to-and-from-2-digit-numbers-lessons-find-10-more-or-less-than-a-2-digit-number.txt,
  Keywords)
- Words: "previous" and "next" multiple of 10. "If you add 10 to a multiple of 10 you get the next multiple of 10"
  (thenational.academy-10-times-tables-lessons-explain-the-relationship-between-adjacent-multiples-of-10.txt, Key
  learning points)
- Words: "unitising". "Recognising that a group of 5, for example, can be treated as a single unit is called
  unitising" (assets.publishing.service.gov.uk-ads-system-uploads-attachment-data-file-954511-Maths-guidance-year-1.txt,
  page 20, 1NF–2 Teaching guidance). A word for teachers.
- Errors and advice: learners count in ones to find ten more. "Children may default to counting in ones to find ten
  more or ten less." and "Use representations that highlight the structure of the base 10 system, e.g. Base 10
  blocks/one hundred squares" (thenational.academy-ns-to-and-from-2-digit-numbers-lessons-find-10-more-or-less-than-a-2-digit-number.txt,
  Common misconception)
- Errors and advice: learners give the number of tens as the total. "they may count 4 groups of 10 as 4 not 40" and
  "Draw a ring around each group of 10 whilst using the stem sentence"
  (thenational.academy-recognitions-counting-in-2s-5s-and-10s-lessons-count-efficiently-in-groups-of-ten.txt, Common
  misconception)
- Errors and advice: learners struggle with the count of tens. "Children may struggle to count in multiples of 10s"
  and "Use of concrete resources: 10 frames, number lines and 100 squares and can support children to practise skip
  counting in groups of 10" (thenational.academy-he-2-and-10-times-tables-lessons-represent-counting-in-tens-as-the-10-times-table.txt,
  Common misconception)
- Errors: the spoken count of tens runs ahead of its use for a while. "children's counting by 10s is initially a
  verbal string with little or no concrete significance" (content/number-line/.references/lchcautobio.ucsd.edu-uploads-2015-10-Petitto-1990-Development-of-Numberline-and-Measurement-Concepts.txt,
  Discussion)
- Errors: "forty" and "fourteen" sound alike. "“fourteen” and “forty” sound very similar, as do “fifteen” and “fifty,”
  and so on to “nineteen” and “ninety.”" (isbe.net-Documents-number-base-ten-k-5.txt, page 7)
- Errors and advice: the challenge's count of jumps. Grade 2 learners say the count but do not track the number of
  jumps. "This skip counting is not yet true multiplication because students don’t keep track of the number of groups
  they have counted." (isbe.net-Documents-number-base-ten-k-5.txt, page 9). England's advice: "they may keep track of
  the number of twos, fives or tens using their fingers or by tallying."
  (assets.publishing.service.gov.uk-ads-system-uploads-attachment-data-file-954796-Maths-guidance-year-2.txt, page 41,
  2MD–1)

## 040 hundreds

- Order: 10 tens make a hundred before the count of hundreds. "making a unit of 1 hundred out of 10 units of 10, for
  example using 10 bundles of 10 straws to make 100" and "“10 tens is equal to 1 hundred.”"
  (assets.publishing.service.gov.uk-ds-system-uploads-attachment-data-file-1017431-Maths-guidance-year-3.txt, page 13,

## Against the brief

- Order of 020 and 030: Oak teaches tens before fives and explains the fives through the tens, and the US counts by
  tens in kindergarten. The brief puts fives first and gives tens the need "counting in fives and twos". "When
  counting in fives, children may revert to the pattern of counting in tens after reciting a decade number."
  (thenational.academy-ions-counting-in-2s-5s-and-10s-lessons-skip-count-in-fives-forwards-and-backwards.txt, Common
  misconception) and "Count to 100 by ones and by tens." (.cache/curricula/us-common-core-math.pdf, K.CC.A.1)
- Counting back in 010: the sources teach the count back with the count forward for every step, twos included. The
  brief counts back first in 020. "I can skip count in twos going forwards and backwards"
  (thenational.academy-tions-counting-in-2s-5s-and-10s-lessons-skip-count-in-twos-forwards-and-backwards.txt,
  Learning outcome)
- Limit on the jumps: England counts up to 10 multiples in Year 1 and up to 10 groups in Year 2. The map's page 4
  takes twelve jumps of two to 24, and page 13 takes thirty jumps of two to 60. "up to 10 multiples, beginning with
  any multiple" (assets.publishing.service.gov.uk-ads-system-uploads-attachment-data-file-954511-Maths-guidance-year-1.txt,
  page 19, 1NF–2) and "up to 10 groups of each"
  (assets.publishing.service.gov.uk-ads-system-uploads-attachment-data-file-954796-Maths-guidance-year-2.txt, page 41)
- Hundreds from any start: England's statute counts in hundreds from 0, and reaches other starts as "100 more or
  less". "count from 0 in multiples of 4, 8, 50 and 100; find 10 or 100 more or less than a given number"
  (.cache/curricula/england-ks1-2.pdf, page 18). The US standard names no start: "skip-count by 5s, 10s, and 100s"
  (.cache/curricula/us-common-core-math.pdf, 2.NBT.A.2)
- The word "multiple": every English source names the landings "multiples of 2", "multiples of five", and the brief
  does not use the word. "count in multiples of twos, fives and tens" (.cache/curricula/england-ks1-2.pdf, page 6)
- Zero: an Oak lesson says zero is neither odd nor even. The brief's "From 0 the count lands on the even numbers"
  holds, since 0 is even. "Zero is neither odd nor even"
  (thenational.academy-ary-ks1-units-composition-of-numbers-6-to-10-lessons-skip-count-in-odds-and-evens.txt, Teacher
  tip)
- Fives on the hundred square: no source read says the fives fill two columns. The sources state the last digits and
  show the 100 square only as a chart to point at. "When counting in fives, each number ends in either 0 or 5"
  (thenational.academy-ions-counting-in-2s-5s-and-10s-lessons-skip-count-in-fives-forwards-and-backwards.txt, Key
  learning points)
- The 2s in the US: the grade 2 standard for skip counting names 5s, 10s and 100s, and counting by 2s sits with odd
  and even. "Count within 1000; skip-count by 5s, 10s, and 100s." and "by pairing objects or counting them by 2s"
  (.cache/curricula/us-common-core-math.pdf, 2.NBT.A.2 and 2.OA.C.3)
