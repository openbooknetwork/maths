# Counting in fives

## Header
- Assumes: from `counting`, the reader counts on and back in ones and counts to 100 in rows of ten. From section
  `twos`, the reader counts in twos from 0 and from another start and back, presses Jump right and Jump left on a line
  in ones where each jump adds or subtracts its size, reads the count line and the number boxes, sees a group stand
  over each jump and go at each jump back, and knows even and odd. From section `tens`, the reader counts in tens
  from 0 and from any start and back, knows that the tens end in 0 and that a jump of 10 keeps the last digit, and
  knows the hundred square with its cells, rows and columns.
- Teaches: counting in fives says every fifth number, 5, 10, 15, landing on each ten and on the number halfway
  between, so every number it lands on ends in 5 or 0, and counting back in fives from 50 goes 45, 40 and reaches 0
  after ten jumps, and the numbers a count from 0 lands on are its multiples, where every multiple of 10 is a
  multiple of 5 and of 2 too.
- Introduces:
  - Terms: `count in twos, fives, tens, hundreds (numbers)` for the fives, forward and back (page 310).
    `group of 2, 5, 10, 100 (objects)` for the group of 5, the five fingers of a hand (page 310). `multiple
    (numbers)` (page 320).
  - Controls: the groups of 5 drawn as hands over the jumps (page 310). The chips "Count in fives", "Count in tens"
    and "Count in twos" on the hundred square, each of which lights a whole count from 0 to 100 at once with its own
    mark, and the readout "In both counts", "In all three counts" of the cells the lit counts share (page 320).
- Uses:
  - A number line from 0 to 50 in ones, labelled at the tens, with jumps of 5 from 0 and a hand over each jump. Ten
    children each hold up one hand, and the count 5, 10, 15, 20, 25, 30, 35, 40, 45, 50 finds 50 fingers on 10
    hands. The count back in fives from 50, 45, 40, 35, 30, 25, 20, 15, 10, 5, 0, takes a hand away at each jump and
    reaches 0 after 10 jumps, which checks the count (page 310).
  - The hundred square with nothing lit. The fives light 20 cells in two columns. The tens lit too share 10 cells
    with the fives, and the twos lit as well share the same 10 cells with both. 60 is a multiple of 2, 5 and 10, 65
    of 5 alone and 64 of 2 alone (page 320).
  - The challenge 720 draws a size of 2, 5 or 10, a start at 0 or at the end of the line, and a target 2 to 9 jumps
    away.

## Content

### 310 Count the fingers in fives and back

The idea: a hand has five fingers, so the fingers of a hand are a group of 5. To count many hands of fingers, you
count in fives. Each jump adds 5, and you say every fifth number: 5, 10, 15, 20. The count lands on each ten, 10, 20,
30, and on the number halfway between two tens, 5, 15, 25. So every number the count in fives lands on ends in 5 or
0. To count back in fives, each jump back subtracts 5 and lands on the same numbers in the other order. After a ten
the count back says the number ending in 5 just below it, so after 40 comes 35, and 30 comes after 35.

The example: 10 children each hold up one hand. How many fingers go up? Make a jump of 5 for each hand and say the
number you land on: 5, 10, 15, 20, 25, 30, 35, 40, 45, 50. 10 jumps land on 50, so 10 hands hold 50 fingers. The 10
is the number of hands and of jumps, and the 50 is the number of fingers altogether. To check, count back in fives
from 50 and take a hand away at each jump: 45, 40, 35, 30, 25, 20, 15, 10, 5, 0. The count back reaches 0 after 10
jumps, one for each hand, so the count was right.

On the figure each hand stands over the jump that counts it, and the line's numbers are the fingers counted so far.
The labels at the tens show the count landing on every ten, and the boxes of the numbers between show the numbers
halfway.

### 320 Every ten is a multiple of 5 and of 2

The idea: the numbers a count from 0 lands on are the multiples of its jump. The count in fives lands on 5, 10, 15,
20 and on to 100, so these are the multiples of 5. The count in twos lands on the multiples of 2, which are the even
numbers, and the count in tens on the multiples of 10, the tens. Every ten is a multiple of 5, because two jumps of 5
make a jump of 10. Every ten is a multiple of 2 as well, because five jumps of 2 make a jump of 10. So every
multiple of 10 is a multiple of 5 and of 2 too.

The example: on the hundred square the 20 multiples of 5 fill two columns, the column of numbers that end in 5 and
the last column. The 10 tens fill the last column, and all 10 of them are in both counts. The 50 multiples of 2 fill
every second column, and the same 10 tens are in all three counts. So 60 is a multiple of 2, of 5 and of 10. 65 is a
multiple of 5 and is not a ten, and it is odd. 64 is a multiple of 2 alone.

The text says how the chips work, since this is the first page that has them: a chip lights a whole count at once,
pressed again it puts the count out, and each count has its own mark.

The figure draws the numbers 1 to 100, so no object stands for another.

## Interactivity

### 310

- Shows: a number line from 0 to 50 with a mark at every number and labels at 0, 10, 20, 30, 40 and 50. The start
  ring and the point are on 0. The buttons Jump left and Jump right, the "Groups" readout on 0, and an empty count
  line under the line. Jump left rests, since a jump of 5 left of 0 leaves the line.
- The reader: presses Jump right ten times, once for each hand, and says each number aloud. Then presses Jump left
  ten times and says the number the point lands on at each jump, back to 0.
- The figure: each press of Jump right draws an arc over 5 gaps and moves the point to the end of it. A hand with
  five fingers spread pops in over the arc, a box with the number pops in where the arc lands, and the count line adds
  the number in bold: "5, 10, 15". At 10, 20, 30, 40 and 50 the mark's label lights as well. "Groups" counts the
  hands. Jump right rests on 50, after 10 jumps. Each press of Jump left takes back the last jump: the point moves 5
  back, the arc, the box and the hand of that jump go, the count line drops its last number, and "Groups" goes down
  by one, so the number at the point and the hands left always agree. On 0 the line is as it was when the page
  opened, and Jump left rests.
- Action: jump on in fives and back again, and a group of five stands over each jump and goes as you jump back.

### 320

- Shows: the hundred square with no cell lit. The chips "Count in fives", "Count in tens" and "Count in twos", each
  with a swatch of its mark and reading a dash. The readout "In both counts" reads a dash.
- The reader: presses "Count in fives", then "Count in tens", then "Count in twos", and presses a chip again to put
  its count out.
- The figure: "Count in fives" lights the multiples of 5 in a wave from 5 to 100, each of the 20 cells tinted, and
  reads 20. "Count in tens" lights the tens in a wave from 10 to 100, each with a ring round its number, and reads
  10. "In both counts" then reads 10 and lights the 10 tens, which carry the tint and the ring. "Count in twos" lights
  the multiples of 2 in a wave, each with a thick border round its cell, and reads 50. The readout is named "In all
  three counts" and still reads 10, the tens, which carry all three marks. A count put out takes its marks away, its
  chip reads a dash again, and the readout counts the cells the lit counts still share, or reads a dash with fewer
  than two counts lit.
- Action: light whole counts on the square, one over another, and the cells they all share stand out and are
  counted.

## Challenge

Page 720, "Land on a number in twos, fives or tens".

- Goal: land the point on the target with jumps of the size the goal names, counting from the start, and press
  Check.
- Drawn each time: a size of 2, 5 or 10. The line from 0 to ten jumps of that size, in ones, with only its two ends
  labelled, so 0 to 20, 0 to 50 or 0 to 100. Half the draws start at 0 and count forward, half start at the right
  end and count back. The target is 2 to 9 jumps from the start and never 5 jumps, so no label and no middle gives it
  away. Example draws: "Count in fives from 0 and land on 35, then press Check." and "Count back in tens from 100 and
  land on 20, then press Check."
- Reached: the reader presses the jump button of the count's way once for each number of the count, saying each
  number, counting the jumps and not the marks, and stops on the target. Check holds when the point is on the target.
  A wrong Check takes the jumps back to the start with a note on how far off the point was, so the reader counts to
  the target before pressing Check. The hint says the first three numbers of the count and to stop at the target.
  "Another one" draws again.
- The text's example: count back in fives from 50 to 25, five jumps, which the goal does not draw. It says the number
  after 40 is 35, and that a jump of 5 goes over five gaps, against the error of counting marks in place of jumps.
- Hidden: the number boxes, the count line and every label but the two ends, until the goal holds. No readout.
- The idea used: a count in equal jumps from 0 or back from the end lands on the multiples of its size, so the reader
  says the count of the drawn size to know when the point is on the target. It is the story's one `land-on`
  challenge and combines the twos, the tens and the fives.
