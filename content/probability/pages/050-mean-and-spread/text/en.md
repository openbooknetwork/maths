Roll some dice and take the mean of what they show. To find the mean, add the numbers and divide by how many dice there are. That mean is one dot on the plot.

Begin with a sample size of 1 and press Start. Each dot is a single roll, so the dots fill the six columns about evenly. The triangle under the axis marks the mean of all the dots, the point where the plot would balance. It settles near [3.5](@expected-mean).

The bracket shows the spread, which says how far a typical dot sits from the mean. For a sample size of [1](@size) it comes close to [1.71](@expected-spread).

Now pick a sample size of 4, then 16. The mean stays near 3.5, and the dots crowd closer to it. In a sample of many dice the high and low rolls cancel out, so its mean rarely lands far from 3.5. The spread shrinks with the square root of the sample size:

[spread ≈ 1.71/√1 ≈ 1.71](@formula-spread)
