A fair coin lands heads or tails, and the two faces have the same chance. The probability of heads is [1/2](@p-heads), because heads is one face out of two.

Press Start. The coin flips again and again. It shows how the last flip landed, and each flip adds a mark to the tally. Every fifth mark crosses the four before it, so you can count in fives.

So far you have [0](@flips) flips, with [0](@heads) heads and [0](@tails) tails.

Stop at ten flips. Ten flips often give six and four, or seven and three. A probability of 1/2 says what happens on average over many flips. A short run can lean either way.

When the flips end, look at the longest run of one face. The Flips slider takes you back through the same flips. In 100 flips, a run of five or more is normal. Press Start again for a fresh set of flips and compare its longest run.
