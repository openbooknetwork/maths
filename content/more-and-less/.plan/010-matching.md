# Matching one to one

## Header

- Assumes: the reader counts objects one by one and knows that the last number says how many, from the story
  `counting`. The reader drags and taps objects, uses a stepper, and knows Check and Another one from that story's
  challenges.
- Teaches: pairing each object of one group with one object of another shows whether the two groups are the same
  size, with no counting.
- Introduces:
  - Terms: pair, partner, pair line, a counter without a partner, the same.
  - Controls: dragging a cup onto a saucer, dragging from a counter of one row to a counter of the other to draw
    a pair line, tapping a paired counter to take its line away, a stepper on each row, the Spacing slider of the
    top row.
- Uses:
  - 5 cups and 6 saucers. One saucer stays empty.
  - A top row of 7 counters and a bottom row of 5. Two top counters stay without a partner.
  - Two rows on steppers from 1 to 10, opening at 4 and 7. Three bottom counters open without a partner.
  - Two rows of 6, with the top row spread out to twice its length.
  - The challenge draws a bottom row of 3 to 10 and a top row of 1 to 10.

## Content

### 110 Is there a cup for every saucer?

The opening page of the story. A table is laid with saucers in a row, and the cups stand together on a tray. The
page asks whether there is a cup for every saucer and tells the reader to put one cup on each saucer. With 5 cups
and 6 saucers every cup finds a saucer and one saucer stays empty, so the answer is no. The page says that the
reader found this out with no counting. Its last paragraph says what the story does: it compares two groups, then
two numbers, and ends on the signs <, > and =.

### 120 A line pairs two counters

The cups become counters that stay in their rows, and a line does the work of putting one on the other. The page
names the pair, the partner and the pair line. A counter takes one line, so each counter has one partner at most.
The reader pairs a top row of 7 with a bottom row of 5 until no more pairs fit. Every bottom counter has a
partner and two top counters have none. The figure rings the counters without a partner. The page says that
counters left without a partner show that the two groups are not the same.

### 130 Every counter has a partner

The figure draws the pair lines itself, and the reader changes the rows. The rows open at 4 and 7, with three
bottom counters without a partner. The page tells the reader to add counters to the top row until no ring is left,
and names the result. When every counter of both rows has a partner, the two groups are the same. It then asks the
reader to add one counter to each row and see that the groups are still the same, and to take one away from one
row and see a counter lose its partner.

### 140 A row spread out is still the same

Two rows of 6 open paired, one straight above the other. The reader spreads the top row out until it is twice as
long as the bottom row. The rows line up at the left, so the first line stays upright, the lines after it slant,
and every counter keeps its partner. The page says that the space a row
takes does not decide whether two groups are the same. The pair lines decide it. A longer row looks like a
bigger group, and the lines show that it is the same.

### 710 Make the two rows the same

The challenge. The page says that the two rows are not the same and that the reader changes the top row until
they are, then presses Check. It reminds the reader that the rows stand at different spacings, so their ends do
not show whether the rows are the same, and that pair lines show which counters lack a partner.

## Interactivity

### 110

- Shows: 6 saucers in a row, and 5 cups close together on a tray above them.
- The reader drags a cup onto a saucer, or taps a cup to send it to the first empty saucer. A tap on a placed cup
  sends it back to the tray.
- The figure seats the cup on the saucer. A saucer holds one cup, and a cup dropped on a full saucer goes back to
  the tray. When the tray is empty the saucer left without a cup takes a ring.
- Action: put each object of one group on an object of the other, and see which objects stay empty.

### 120

- Shows: a top row of 7 counters and a bottom row of 5 counters, each row in a straight line, with no lines
  between them.
- The reader drags from a counter of one row to a counter of the other. A tap on one counter and then on a counter
  of the other row does the same. A tap on a paired counter takes its line away.
- The figure draws a line that follows the finger and stays when it is let go on a counter that has no line. Let
  go anywhere else, the line goes away. When one row has no counter left to pair, each counter without a partner
  takes a ring.
- Action: draw a line between two objects that stay in place, one line for each object, until no more pairs fit.

### 130

- Shows: a top row of 4 and a bottom row of 7, with the pair lines drawn and a ring on each of the three bottom
  counters without a partner. Two steppers stand in the panel under the figure, labelled "Top row" and "Bottom
  row". A readout shows the number of counters without a partner.
- The reader taps + and − on either stepper.
- The figure adds or removes the last counter of that row, redraws the lines and the rings, and shows the words
  "The same" in a box over the middle of the lines when every counter has a partner.
- Action: add or remove an object in a group, and the pairs and the leftover objects follow.

### 140

- Shows: two rows of 6, paired with upright lines, and a Spacing slider for the top row. The readout shows the
  number of counters without a partner, which is 0, and the words "The same" stand in a box over the middle of
  the lines.
- The reader moves the Spacing slider.
- The figure moves the top counters apart or together, from the spacing of the bottom row to twice that spacing.
  The rows line up at the left, so the first line stays upright and the lines after it slant. Each line stays on
  its two counters. The readout and the words stay as they are.
- Action: change how much room a group takes, and every pair stays.

### 710

- Shows: the two drawn rows with no lines, the top row's stepper in the panel under the figure, and the buttons
  Check and Another one. The stepper shows no number until the goal holds. One row stands close and the other
  wide, so the ends of the rows do not line up.
- The reader draws pair lines as on page 120, changes the top row with its stepper, and presses Check.
- The figure keeps the drawn lines while the row changes. A counter the stepper removes takes its line with it,
  and a counter it adds arrives with no line. The rings appear as on page 120. Check shows a tick, or says which
  row has counters without a partner. A change to the top row takes the check back.
- Action: change one group until it matches the other, and commit the answer.

## Challenge

Make the two rows the same, at page 710.

- Goal: the top row has as many counters as the bottom row when the reader presses Check.
- Drawn each time: a bottom row from 3 to 10 counters and a top row from 1 to 10 counters that differs from the
  bottom row. The engine also draws which of the two rows stands wide. The other stands close.
- Reached by: the pair drag and the tap of page 120 to find the counters without a partner, the top row's stepper
  from 0 to 12 to add or remove counters, and Check to commit. The stepper is the one way to change a row.
- Hidden: every readout, and the words "The same", since a challenge page sets no `word`. The stepper shows no number
  until the goal holds, so no count of either row shows before then.
- Wrong answer: "Some counters of the bottom row have no partner." or "Some counters of the top row have no
  partner."
- Hint: "Draw a line from each top counter to a bottom counter. The counters left without a line show what to
  change."
- Idea used: matching one to one, with rows whose lengths mislead, from page 140.

## Contract

Everything in this section's `hesap` blocks is `new`. The family `counters` has the scenes `bonds` and `count`
today.

### The scene `counters/match` (`new`)

Two rows of objects, one above the other, named the top row and the bottom row. The family's two colours tell the
rows apart as a second cue, red for the top row and blue for the bottom row. A stepper or a slider stands in the
panel under the figure.

| Field | Values | What it draws and does |
|---|---|---|
| `look` | `rows` (default), `cups` | `rows` draws two rows of counters. `cups` draws the bottom row as saucers on a table and the top group as cups on a tray, and a drag seats a cup on a saucer in place of a line. |
| `top`, `bottom` | a whole number from 0 to 12, or `{ stepper: [low, high], start: n }` | The objects of each row as the page opens. A stepper adds and removes the last object of its row. |
| `pair` | `drag` (default), `auto` | `drag`: the reader draws each pair, by a drag between two counters of different rows or by a tap on each. A counter holds one line, and a tap on a paired counter removes its line. `auto`: the scene draws the lines itself, from the first counter of the top row to the first of the bottom row and on, and redraws them when a row changes. |
| `spread` | `top`, `bottom` | A Spacing slider sets the gap between the counters of the named row, from the other row's gap to twice that gap. The page opens with both rows at the same gap. It needs `look: rows`, `pair: auto` and 6 counters at most in the named row. |
| `word` | `same` | The words "The same" show in a box over the middle of the pair lines when every counter of both rows has a partner and each row has at least one counter. |
| `readout` | a list of `unpaired`, `pairs` | The measures shown under the figure. |
| `challenge` | `{ goal: same-rows, top: [low, high], bottom: [low, high] }` | The goal and the ranges it draws from. The page leaves every other field out. |

Rules of the scene on every page:

- A ring, drawn in ink, marks each counter without a partner once one of the rows has no counter left to pair.
  In the `cups` look the ring marks a saucer without a cup once the tray is empty, or a cup left on the tray once
  every saucer is full.
- A row stays in one straight line, and 12 counters fit the width of a phone.

Measures a text can link:

| Id | What it counts |
|---|---|
| `unpaired` | the counters of both rows that have no pair line, or the cups and saucers that stand alone |
| `pairs` | the pair lines, or the cups on saucers |

The goal `same-rows` (`new`), for `look: rows`:

- It draws `{ top, bottom, wide }`: `bottom` from the page's `bottom` range, `top` from the page's `top` range and
  different from `bottom`, and `wide` as `top` or `bottom`.
- The page opens with `pair: drag`, no lines, and a stepper on the top row from 0 to 12 that shows no number
  until the goal holds.
- It holds once the reader has pressed Check with the top row as long as the bottom row. A wrong check gives the
  note `top` when the top row has more counters and `bottom` when the bottom row has more. The words of the two
  notes are in the Challenge part above.
- `kitap check` fails a page whose ranges lie outside 1 to 10 for `top` and 3 to 10 for `bottom`, or whose ranges
  leave no pair of different numbers.

Words the scene needs in `i18n/en.yaml`: the stepper labels "Top row" and "Bottom row", the slider label
"Spacing", the readout labels "Without a partner" and "Pairs", the words "The same", the goal line "Make the top
row the same as the bottom row, then press Check.", the two notes and the hint.

## Sources

Each entry names the file it was read in. The curricula are in `.cache/curricula/`, read with `pdftotext`. The
fetched pages are in `content/more-and-less/.references/`.

- Japan, Course of Study 2008, grade 1, A(1)a: "To compare numbers of objects in groups by making one-to-one
  correspondence between objects." It is the first item of the grade's content on numbers. Read in
  `japan-course-of-study-2008-en.pdf`. The curriculum that `specs/beginner-subjects.md` names for this story is
  Japan.
- US Common Core, kindergarten, K.CC.C.6: "Identify whether the number of objects in one group is greater than,
  less than, or equal to the number of objects in another group, e.g., by using matching and counting
  strategies." Its footnote says "Include groups with up to ten objects." Read in `us-common-core-math.pdf`. The
  rows that the pages set and the challenge draws hold 10 counters at most for that reason.
- England, national curriculum, year 1: "use the language of: equal to, more than, less than (fewer), most,
  least". Read in `england-ks1-2.pdf`.
- Bremen, Bildungsplan Mathematik 2025, under "Anzahlerfassung und -darstellung": "Vergleichen von Mengen (z. B.
  mehr, weniger, gleich viel)". Read in `germany-bremen-primary-2025.pdf`.
- NCETM, "Exploring comparison in Early Years". Read in
  `ncetm.org.uk-features-exploring-comparison-in-early-years.txt`.
  https://ncetm.org.uk/features/exploring-comparison-in-early-years
  - Children compare quantities before the numbers that stand for them, and compare close sets "by matching or
    counting". Matched one to one, the set with one left over shows up. Pages 120 and 130 use this.
  - "Similarly, sets with items spread far apart do not always contain more." Page 140 uses this.
  - Children match real objects in daily routines, such as sharing snacks. Page 110 starts from such a case.
  - "The language of 'fewer than' should be used when comparing countable sets of objects. 'Less than' should be
    used when comparing numbers or measures."
- NCETM, Early Years typical progression chart, Comparison, December 2018. Read in
  `ncetm.org.uk-media-wvqgcfqm-typical-progression-comparison.txt`.
  https://www.ncetm.org.uk/media/wvqgcfqm/typical-progression-comparison.pdf
  - "Children can check that groups are equal, by matching objects on a one-to-one basis." Page 130 uses this.
  - It suggests "asking children to convert two unequal groups into two that have the same number". The challenge
    at 710 is this task.
  - It lists as a common error "children not comparing the numerosity of the group and considering more in terms
    of size".
  - It places "More than / less than" before "Identifying groups with the same number of things". The brief
    orders them the other way, with matching and "the same" in 010 and more and fewer in 020, as the Japanese
    course of study does.
- NRICH, early years activity "Show Me". Read in `nrich.maths.org-12747.txt`. https://nrich.maths.org/12747
  Its list "Counting and cardinality" has "matching two groups one-to-one to show that they have the same
  number" and "conservation - rearranging items and explaining that the number is the same because the
  arrangement can be returned to the original and none have been added or taken away". Page 140 uses the second.
- Unread: https://escholarship.org/content/qt1z39h57r/qt1z39h57r.pdf answered 403. A search summary described it
  as a study of Piaget's conservation task, in which children judge two rows of the same length and number well
  and a longer row misleads them. No part of this plan rests on it alone.

The order of the pages is this plan's own: objects placed on objects (110), lines between objects that stay in
place (120), the rule for "the same" (130), a row spread out (140). The sources support each step and give no
order for them.
