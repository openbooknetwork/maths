# More and less

The brief of the story `more-and-less`. `specs/stories/more-and-less.md` was its rough map.

## Strategy

- **Subject:** comparing two groups and two numbers.
- **Level:** beginner. The reader knows the story `counting`: one number for each object, the last number says how
  many, counting on and back, and counting to 100 in rows of ten.
- **At the end the reader can:** tell which of two groups has more without counting, say how many more, name the
  number one more and one less than a number up to 100, say which of two numbers is greater, and read and write a
  comparison with <, > and =.
- **Family and figure:** `counters`. The story needs two new scenes. `match` draws two rows of counters, one above
  the other, with their pair lines. `order` draws two numbers as markers on one count or as blocks of rows of ten,
  and number cards put in order. Both take the sign between two numbers.
- **Interaction that suits the subject:** the reader pairs counters by dragging from one row to the other, changes a
  row with a stepper, sets a number with a slider, and taps a sign tile to change it. Every comparison is made on
  objects first and on numbers after.
- **Exercises that suit the subject:** the engine draws two groups or two numbers, the reader makes them match, or
  one less, or sets the sign, and commits with Check. The counts are hidden, so the reader has to pair or compare.

## Names the sections share

- The rows are `top` and `bottom`, in both scenes.
- `unpaired` is the count of counters without a partner.
- `spread: top | bottom` is the Spacing slider of that row.
- `word` is the tag of a row: `word: same` draws "The same", `word: true` draws more, fewer or the same.
- `reading` is the line of words under two numbers. Equal numbers read "is equal to" on a page with a `sign` and
  "is the same as" on every other page.
- `sign` is the sign tile, in `match` and in `order`.

## Sections

Page folders take the numbers of the section's range, in tens. World pages and challenges sit after the sections,
in the ranges given.

### 010 `matching` Matching one to one

- **Pages:** 110 to 190. Opens the story at 110. Challenge at 710.
- **Needs:** counting objects, from the story `counting`.
- **Teaches:** pairing each object of one group with one object of another shows whether the groups are the same
  size, with no counting.
- **Hands on:** the pair line, the counter left without a partner, and "the same" for two groups where every
  counter has a partner.
- **Arc:** the opening page raises the question on a real situation, cups and saucers. The challenge asks the reader
  to make two rows the same.

### 020 `more-and-fewer` More and fewer

- **Pages:** 210 to 290. World page at 610.
- **Needs:** pairing, and the counters left without a partner.
- **Teaches:** the group with counters left over has more, and the number left over is how many more it has and how
  many fewer the other has.
- **Hands on:** the words more, fewer and the same, and the difference between two groups as a count, under that
  name. Fewer is the word for objects, and less the word for numbers.
- **Arc:** the world page is a game of musical chairs, where one chair fewer than players leaves one player
  without a seat. No challenge.

### 030 `one-more-one-less` One more and one less

- **Pages:** 310 to 390. Challenge at 720.
- **Needs:** counting on and back, and more and fewer.
- **Teaches:** one more than a number is the next number of the count, and one less is the number before it, across
  a ten too.
- **Hands on:** stepping a group by one counter, and the numbers either side of a number.
- **Arc:** the challenge asks the reader to make the top row one less than the bottom row.

### 040 `comparing-numbers` Comparing two numbers

- **Pages:** 410 to 490.
- **Needs:** counting to 100 in rows of ten, and more and fewer.
- **Teaches:** of two numbers, the one that comes later in the count is the greater. With each number as counters in
  rows of ten, the number with more full rows is the greater, and with the same full rows the last row decides.
  Comparing two at a time puts three numbers in order.
- **Hands on:** the words greater and less for numbers up to 100, and full rows before the last row, which the
  story `tens-and-ones` turns into tens and ones.
- **Arc:** no world page and no challenge.

### 050 `signs` The signs <, > and =

- **Pages:** 510 to 590. World page at 620. Challenge at 730. Ends the story at 810.
- **Needs:** greater, and the same, for two numbers and for two groups.
- **Teaches:** a sign between two numbers says which is greater or that they are equal, and the line it makes is
  true or false. The sign = says that both sides are the same number.
- **Hands on:** nothing inside this story. The end page names the stories that follow: `adding-within-20`,
  `comparing-lengths`, `hundreds-and-thousands` and `times-as-many`.
- **Arc:** the world page is the scoreboard of a football match. The challenge asks for the right sign between two
  groups whose counts are hidden, and combines pairing, comparing and the signs. The end page closes the story.
