# One more and one less

## Header

- Assumes: from the story `counting`, the reader counts on and back, knows the counters in rows of ten up to 100
  and knows that 40 comes after 39. From section `matching` the reader knows the pair line, the ring on a counter
  without a partner, the stepper on a row, and Check on a challenge. From section `more-and-fewer` the reader knows
  that the row with a counter left over has more and the other has fewer, and that fewer is the word for objects
  and less the word for numbers.
- Teaches: a group that gains a counter has one counter more, and its number is one more, the next number of the
  count. A group that loses a counter has one counter fewer, and its number is one less, the number before. Both
  hold across a ten.
- Introduces:
  - Terms: one more, one less, the number before, the number after.
  - Controls: the row labelled "before", which holds the group as it was one step ago. The tiles on either side of
    a number, which step the group by one counter. The outline of the place where the next counter goes.
- Uses:
  - A group of 7 counters above the same group one step ago, 6 counters. The group steps from 1 to 10 (page 310).
  - 38 counters in rows of ten, between 37 and 39. The reader steps over 39, 40 and 41, back over 41 to 40 and
    39, and on down to 30 and 29. The number runs from 1 to 99 (page 320).
  - The challenge draws a bottom row of 3 to 10 counters and a top row of 1 to 10 (page 720).

The section has two teaching pages. Page 310 works on objects and page 320 on numbers. The pages 330 to 390 stay
free.

## Content

### 310 One more is the next number

The top row is a group of counters as it is now. The bottom row is the same group as it was one step ago. The
page opens with 7 counters now and 6 before. Every counter of the bottom row has a partner, and one counter of the
top row is left over, so the group now has one counter more. Its number, 7, is the number that comes after 6 in the
count. The text asks the reader to add a counter and to read the two numbers again, then to take a counter away.
After a step down the bottom row has the counter left over. The group now has one counter fewer, and its number is
the number before. The page ends on the two sentences for one pair of numbers: 7 is one more than 6, and 6 is one
less than 7.

### 320 The numbers on either side

Every number from 1 to 99 has a number before it and a number after it. The number before is one less, and the
number after is one more. The page opens on 38, between 37 and 39. The text asks the reader to tap the number
after until the group reaches 41. One more than 39 is 40, which fills a row. One more than 40 is 41, which starts
a new row. Then the reader goes back. One less than 41 is 40, the last number of the row above. One less than 40
is 39, which leaves that row one counter short again. The reader taps on down to 30, where the same thing happens
one row higher. The page says that one more and one less work the same way at every ten up to 100.

### 720 Give the top row one counter fewer

The page shows two rows and no numbers. The text asks the reader to change the top row until it has one counter
fewer than the bottom row, and then to press Check. It says how to tell without counting: pair the rows, and one
counter of the bottom row stays without a partner. When the tick shows, each row shows its number in a tag at its
end, and the text says that the top number is one less than the bottom number.

## Interactivity

### 310

- Shows: two rows of counters with a pair line from each counter to the one above or below it. The top row
  carries the name "now" and holds 7. The bottom row carries the name "before" and holds 6. A ring marks the one
  counter without a partner. A tag at the end of the top row reads "more" and a tag at the end of the bottom row
  reads "fewer". The readouts "Before" and "Now" show 6 and 7. The stepper reads "Top row", runs from 1 to 10 and
  stands in the panel under the figure.
- The reader: steps the top row up or down by one counter.
- The figure: gives the bottom row the number the top row had, then adds or removes the last counter of the top
  row. The pair lines are drawn again, the ring moves to the counter left over, and the tags and readouts follow.
  After a step up the ring is on the top row. After a step down it is on the bottom row. The two rows differ by
  one counter in every state.
- Action: step a group by one object, and a second row keeps the group as it was, so one object stands without a
  partner.

### 320

- Shows: 38 counters in rows of ten, as on the page "A hundred counters in rows of ten" of `counting`, with the
  count at the end of each full row. A ring in ink marks the last counter. A dashed outline marks the next empty
  place. Under the rows stands a strip of three tiles: the left tile shows 37 with the words "one less", the
  middle tile shows 38 in larger type, and the right tile shows 39 with the words "one more".
- The reader: taps the right tile or the left tile.
- The figure: on a tap of the right tile a counter fills the outlined place, the ring and the outline move on by
  one place, and the three tiles each show the next number. On a tap of the left tile the ringed counter leaves
  and the three tiles each show the number before. At 40 the outline stands at the start of a new row. At 1 the
  left tile shows 0, at 99 the right tile shows 100, and these two tiles take no tap.
- Action: tap the number on either side of a number, and the group steps to it by one object.

### 720

- Shows: two rows of counters with no pair lines, no tags and no numbers. One row stands wide and the other
  close, as on page 710. The top row has a stepper from 0 to 12, which shows no number until the goal holds. A
  Check button and an "Another one" button stand under the figure.
- The reader: draws pair lines by a drag between two counters, steps the top row, and presses Check.
- The figure: draws each pair line, and rings the counters without a partner once one row has no counter left to
  pair. A step adds or removes the last counter of the top row with its line. Check shows the tick, or a note that
  says in which direction the top row is off. A check on two rows of the same size gives the note for too many
  counters. A step after Check takes the tick or the note back.
- Action: change one group until exactly one object of the other is left without a partner, then commit.

## Challenge

Give the top row one counter fewer, at page 720.

- Goal: the top row has one counter fewer than the bottom row when the reader presses Check.
- Drawn each time: a bottom row of 3 to 10 counters and a top row of 1 to 10 counters that is not one fewer than
  the bottom row. The engine also draws which row stands wide, as the goal `same-rows` of page 710 does.
- Reached by: the pair drag of page 120 to find the counters without a partner, the top row's stepper from 0 to
  12, and Check to commit. The stepper is the one way to change a row.
- Hidden: every readout, the number on the stepper, the tags "more" and "fewer", and the words "The same". The
  figure shows no count of either row until the goal holds.
- Wrong answer: "The top row has too many counters." or "The top row has too few counters." Two rows of the same
  size get the first note.
- Hint: "Pair the rows. One counter fewer leaves one counter of the bottom row without a partner."
- When the goal holds: each row shows its number in a tag at its end, such as 6 and 7, and the stepper shows its
  number, so the reader sees that the top number is one less. A step of the top row hides the numbers.
- Idea used: one less, as one counter short of matching. A reader who makes the rows the same, as on page 710, or
  gives the top row one more, gets a note and has to turn the idea round.

The goal `one-less` (`new`), for `counters/match` with `look: rows`:

- It draws `{ top, bottom, wide }`: `bottom` from the page's `bottom` range, `top` from the page's `top` range and
  different from `bottom` less 1, and `wide` as `top` or `bottom`.
- The page opens with `pair: drag`, no lines, and a stepper on the top row from 0 to 12.
- It holds once the reader has pressed Check with the top row one counter shorter than the bottom row. A wrong
  check gives the note `over` when the top row holds more than that, two rows of the same size included, and
  `short` when it holds fewer.
- The stepper shows no number until the goal holds. While it holds, each row shows its count in a tag at its end.
- `kitap check` fails a page whose ranges lie outside 1 to 10 for `top` and 3 to 10 for `bottom`.

Words the scenes need in `i18n/en.yaml`: the row names "now" and "before", the readout labels "Now" and "Before",
the tile words "one less" and "one more", the goal line "Give the top row one counter fewer than the bottom row,
then press Check.", the notes "The top row has too many counters." and "The top row has too few counters.", and
the hint of the Challenge part.

- Oak National Academy, Year 1, lesson "Finding one more and one less using representations". Its keywords define
  one more as "the next number in the count" with "one more object in the set", and one less as "the previous
  number in the count" with "one fewer object in the set". Its common misconception is "getting numbers in the
  wrong order when completing the stem sentences with one more and one less", and its advice is to use the
  representations to show which value is one less and which is one more. Page 310 shows both groups at once and
  ends on both sentences for that reason. Read in
  `content/more-and-less/.references/thenational.academy-unting-to-and-from-20-lessons-finding-one-more-and-one-less-using-representations.txt`.
- Oak National Academy, Year 1, lesson "One less with manipulatives and counting". It teaches one less as the
  number before, and keeps "one less than" for a value and "one fewer than" for a quantity, the rule the title of
  page 720 follows. Its common misconception is that pupils "start counting on the number given and not recognise
  that the number before is one less", and its advice is to show the number before alongside the objects and to
  let pupils remove one from a set. The step down of page 310 and the tile of the number before on page 320 do
  both. Read in
  `content/more-and-less/.references/thenational.academy-ising-and-comparing-numbers-0-10-lessons-one-less-with-manipulatives-and-counting.txt`.
- Sage, "Year 1: One more than, one less than". Its first stage finds one more when counting objects and its
  second stage finds one less on a number track. It says that one less is harder for children than one more, so
  the challenge of this section asks for one fewer. It warns that children can fill the missing numbers of a
  number track by counting up and down, without thinking of one more or one less. Page 320 keeps the counters
  above the tiles for that reason. Read in `content/more-and-less/.references/study.sagepub.com-node-98088.txt`.
- `specs/stories/more-and-less.md`, pages 8 to 10 and the second challenge. Its pages 8 and 9, a group with a +
  button and a group with a − button, repeat the pages "Add more and count on" and "Count back as the birds fly
  away" of `counting`, so this plan puts both steps on the two rows of page 310.
