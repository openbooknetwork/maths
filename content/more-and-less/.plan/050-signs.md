# The signs <, > and =

## Header

- Assumes: the pair line, the ring on a counter without a partner and "the same" for two rows, from section
  `matching`. More and fewer for two groups, and a stepper on each row with the pair lines drawn by the figure,
  from `matching` and `more-and-fewer`. Greater and less for two numbers up to 100, the two blocks of rows of ten,
  the slider that sets a number and the frame round the rows that decide, from `comparing-numbers`. Check and
  Another one, from the challenges at 710 and 720.
- Teaches: a sign between two numbers says which is greater or that they are equal, and the line it makes is true
  or false. The sign = says that both sides are the same number.
- Introduces:
  - Terms: sign, the signs <, > and = with their readings "is less than", "is greater than" and "is equal to",
    equal, the open side and the point of a sign, a line of two numbers and a sign, true and false. The world page
    adds "leads" and "level" for two scores.
  - Controls: the sign tile, which a tap changes from < to = to > and round again. The buttons "Home goal", "Away
    goal" and "New match" of the world page. Check on a sign, in the challenge.
- Uses:
  - Rows of 5 and 8 on steppers from 0 to 10, with a sign that follows them. The page opens on 5 < 8 (page 510).
  - Fixed rows of 9 and 6 with the tile on <. The line 9 < 6 opens false, and 9 > 6 is the true line (page 520).
  - The numbers 23 and 31 on sliders from 0 to 100, with the tile on <. The line 23 < 31 opens true (page 530).
  - A football scoreboard that opens at 0 and 0, with scores up to 9 (page 620).
  - The challenge draws two rows of 4 to 10 counters that are equal or 1 to 3 apart (page 730).
  - Rows of 8 and 8 on steppers from 0 to 10. The page opens on 8 = 8 (page 810).

## Content

The section has three pages, then the world page, the challenge and the end page of the story. Every line is read
from its first number to its second, and each page gives the line in words beside the signs.

### 510 The open side faces the greater number

The page says that a sign between two numbers writes down what pairing shows. Two rows stand paired, and under them
the two numbers stand with a sign between them. With 5 and 8 the sign is <, and the line 5 < 8 reads "5 is less
than 8". The page names the three signs and their readings: < is "is less than", > is "is greater than" and = is
"is equal to". It gives the rule for < and >: the open side faces the greater number and the point faces the less.
It ties each sign to the rings. Rings in the bottom row mean the second number is greater, and the sign opens
toward it. Rings in the top row turn the sign round. With no ring the two rows are the same, the sign is =, and
both sides of = are the same number. The text tells the reader to add counters to the top row past the bottom row
and watch the sign at each step.

Example: 5 and 8. Three bottom counters have rings, and the line is 5 < 8. At 8 and 8 the line is 8 = 8, and at 9
and 8 it is 9 > 8.

### 520 A line with a sign is true or false

The page says that the reader now sets the sign, and that a line of two numbers and a sign says something that is
true or false. The line 9 < 6 says that 9 is less than 6, which is false, because the top row has the counters
without a partner. The text tells the reader to tap the sign until the line is true. For two numbers one of the
three signs makes a true line and the other two make false lines.

Example: 9 and 6. The line 9 < 6 is false, 9 = 6 is false and 9 > 6 is true.

### 530 A true line can turn false

The page moves from groups to numbers up to 100. It says that a sign stays as it is written, so a line that is true
for two numbers can be false for two others. The reader moves one number past the other, sees the line turn false,
and taps the sign to make it true again. The text then asks the reader to set both numbers the same. Then < and >
both make false lines and = makes the true one, because = says that both sides are the same number. The page
reminds the reader to judge the numbers as in `comparing-numbers`: the full rows first, then the last row.

Example: 23 and 31. The line 23 < 31 is true. With the first number at 35 the line 35 < 31 is false and 35 > 31 is
true. At 31 and 31 the true line is 31 = 31.

### 620 The scoreboard

The world page. A football match between the home team and the away team starts at 0 and 0, and the scoreboard
keeps a sign between the two scores. The page says that a match starts level, with the sign =. A goal for one team
puts that team in the lead, and the open side of the sign faces its score. A goal for the other team brings the
match level again. The reader scores goals for either team and reads who leads from the sign alone.

Example: 0 = 0 as the match starts, 1 > 0 after a goal for the home team, 1 = 1 after a goal for the away team.

### 730 Put the right sign between the two rows

The challenge. The page says that the two rows show no numbers and that the sign between them makes a false line.
The reader finds out which row has more, sets the sign and presses Check. It reminds the reader that one row
stands wide and the other close, so the ends of the rows say nothing, and that pair lines show which row has
counters without a partner.

### 810 Where comparing leads

The end page of the story. It sums the story up in the order it was taught. Pairing shows whether two groups are
the same. The counters without a partner say which group has more and how many more. Of two numbers, the one later
in the count is the greater. A sign writes the comparison down as a line. The page then names the stories that
follow: comparing lengths, adding within 20, hundreds and thousands, and times as many. The figure is the one of
page 510, opening on two rows that are the same.

Example: 8 and 8, with the line 8 = 8.

## Interactivity

### 510 The open side faces the greater number

- Shows: a top row of 5 counters and a bottom row of 8 counters, paired by the figure, with a ring on each of the
  three bottom counters without a partner. Under the rows stands the line: the top row's number on the left, the
  sign, the bottom row's number on the right. Each number has the colour of its row as a second cue. The sign
  follows the rows, so it is drawn without a box and takes no tap. The steppers "Top row" and "Bottom row" stand in
  the panel under the figure, and the panel reads the line out in words, "5 is less than 8", under "In words".
- The reader taps + and − on either stepper.
- In reply the row gains or loses its last counter, the pair lines and the rings follow, and the sign changes
  between <, = and > so that the line stays true. The reading in words follows.
- Action in general words: add or remove an object in a group, and the sign between the two numbers turns to face
  the greater one.

### 520 A line with a sign is true or false

- Shows: a top row of 9 and a bottom row of 6, paired by the figure, with rings on three top counters. The rows
  have no steppers. The line under the rows reads 9 < 6, and its sign stands on a tile. Beside the line a cross
  and the word "false" show. The panel reads out "9 is less than 6" under "In words" and "false" under "The line
  is".
- The reader taps the sign tile.
- In reply the tile shows the next sign, in the order <, =, >, and then < again. The mark beside the line shows a
  tick and "true" or a cross and "false", and the reading in words follows.
- Action in general words: tap the sign, and the line says whether it is true.

### 530 A true line can turn false

- Shows: the two blocks of page 420, each in rows of ten with its number above. The first number, 23, is the left
  block and the second number, 31, is the right block. The frame surrounds the rows that decide. The sign tile
  stands between the two numbers, on <. A tick and the word "true" show under the blocks. The sliders "First
  number" and "Second number", each from 0 to 100, stand in the panel, which reads out "23 is less than 31" and
  "true".
- The reader drags either slider, and taps the sign tile.
- In reply a block fills or empties and the frame moves as on page 420. The tile keeps its sign while the numbers
  change, and the mark turns to "false" the moment the line stops being true. A tap on the tile changes the sign as
  on page 520.
- Action in general words: change a number under a sign that stays, and the line turns false until the sign is
  changed to fit.

### 620 The scoreboard

- Shows: a scoreboard with the name and the score of two teams, "Home" on the left and "Away" on the right, and a
  sign between the scores, drawn without a box. Both scores open at 0, and the sign is =. Under each score a ball
  lies for each goal. The buttons "Home goal", "Away goal" and "New match" stand under the board. The panel reads
  the scores out in words, "0 is equal to 0".
- The reader presses a goal button, or "New match".
- In reply the score of that team goes up by one, a ball joins its row, and the sign turns to face the greater
  score or shows = when the scores are the same. A goal button stops at 9 goals. "New match" puts both scores back
  to 0.
- Action in general words: add one to either of two scores, and the sign between them shows who leads.

### 730 Put the right sign between the two rows

- Shows: the two drawn rows with no pair lines, one wide and one close, so their ends do not line up. Under the
  rows stands the line with two cards in place of the numbers, each showing a question mark, the left card for
  the top row and the right card for the bottom row. The sign tile stands between them on a sign that makes a
  false line. The buttons Check and Another one stand under it.
- The reader draws pair lines as on page 120, taps the sign tile, and presses Check.
- In reply the figure draws the lines and rings the counters without a partner as on page 120. Check shows a tick
  or says in which direction the sign is off. Once the goal holds, the two cards turn to the numbers of the rows.
  A tap on the tile takes the check back.
- Action in general words: compare two groups whose numbers are hidden, set the sign between them, and commit the
  answer.

### 810 Where comparing leads

- Shows: two rows of 8, paired by the figure with no ring, and the line 8 = 8 under the rows. The steppers "Top
  row" and "Bottom row" stand in the panel under the figure, and the panel reads out "8 is equal to 8".
- The reader taps + and − on either stepper.
- In reply the pairs, the rings and the sign follow, as on page 510.
- Action in general words: the action of page 510, on the page that sums the story up.

## Challenge

Put the right sign between the two rows, at page 730.

- Goal: the sign between the two rows makes a true line when the reader presses Check.
- Drawn each time: which of <, = and > is the true sign, each as likely as the others. Then a top row and a bottom
  row of 4 to 10 counters that fit it, 1 to 3 apart when they differ. Then which row stands wide, and which of the
  two false signs the tile opens on.
- Reached by: the pair drag and the tap of page 120 to find the counters without a partner, a tap on the sign tile
  to set the sign, and Check to commit. The rows have no steppers. A reader who counts both rows reaches it too.
- Hidden: the numbers of both rows, which the line shows as two cards with question marks, every readout, the mark
  that says true or false, and the words "The same".
- Wrong answer: "The top row has more counters than this sign says." or "The top row has fewer counters than this
  sign says." The signs stand in the order <, =, > for this, so a checked < is off toward more, a checked > toward
  fewer, and a checked = toward the row that has more.
- Solved: the tick shows, and the two cards turn to the numbers of the rows, so the line reads as a true line of
  numbers such as 7 < 9.
- Hint: "Draw a line from each top counter to a bottom counter. The row with counters left without a partner has
  more, and the open side of the sign faces it. With no counter left, the sign is =."
- Idea used: matching one to one from `matching`, with rows whose lengths mislead. More, fewer and the same from
  `more-and-fewer`. The three signs from this section.

The goal `right-sign` (`new`), for `counters/match` with the default look:

- It draws `{ top, bottom, wide, opens }`. It picks the true sign from <, = and >, each one draw in three. It draws
  one row from `rows` and sets the other equal to it, or apart by a number from `apart`, with both rows inside
  `rows`. `wide` is `top` or `bottom`, and `opens` is one of the two signs that make a false line.
- The page opens with `pair: drag`, no lines, no steppers, the tile on `opens`, and a card with a question mark in
  place of each number, the left card for the top row and the right card for the bottom row.
- It holds once the reader has pressed Check with the tile on the true sign. A wrong check gives the note `more`
  when the top row has more counters than the checked sign says and `fewer` when it has fewer. The words of the
  notes are in the Challenge part above. A tap on the tile takes the check back, and a pair line leaves it.
- When it holds, the two cards turn to the numbers of the rows.
- `kitap check` fails a page whose `rows` lies outside 1 to 10, or whose `rows` and `apart` leave one of the three
  signs with no pair of rows.

Words the scene needs in `i18n/en.yaml`: the three readings, "true" and "false", the readout labels "In words" and
"The line is", the tile's label "Sign", the stepper labels "Top row" and "Bottom row", the slider labels "First
number" and "Second number", the team names "Home" and "Away", the buttons "Home goal", "Away goal" and "New
match", the goal line "Put the sign between the two rows that makes the line true, then press Check.", the two
notes and the hint.
