# Comparing two numbers

## Header

- Assumes: counting to 100 in rows of ten, where each row ends on a ten, and the marker that stands on one counter
  of the rows, both from the story `counting`. More, fewer and the same for two groups, and one more and one less
  than a number, from the sections before this one.
- Teaches: of two numbers the one that comes later in the count is the greater, and with each number laid out in
  rows of ten the full rows are compared first and the last row only when the full rows are the same.
- Introduces: the words greater and less for two numbers, full row and last row, in order, least and greatest. The
  slider that sets a number, two markers on one count, the frame round the rows that decide, the button "Show the
  rows", and a number card dragged to a place.
- Uses: 18 and 25 as two markers on a count of 100. 34 and 52, then 47 and 43, as two blocks of rows of ten. 48 and
  51 as two numbers whose rows are covered. Three drawn cards put in order, with three
  new cards on each press of "New numbers". The text works the example 63, 36 and 39.

## Content

The section has four pages and no world page or challenge.

### 410 The number that comes later is greater

The page says that counting from 1 reaches one of two numbers first and goes on to the other. The number reached
later is the greater, and the other is the less. The greater number holds every counter of the less and some more,
which ties the new words to more and fewer from section 020. It says that greater and less are the words for
numbers, as more and fewer are the words for objects. Two numbers that stand on the same counter are the same.

Example: 18 and 25. The count passes 18 and goes on 7 counters to 25, so 25 is greater than 18 and 18 is less
than 25.

### 420 The number with more full rows is greater

The page says that a number laid out in rows of ten has full rows and a last row. The number with more full rows
is the greater, however long the last rows are. When both numbers have the same full rows, the number with more
counters in its last row is the greater. The reader looks at the full rows first and at the last row only when
the full rows are the same.

Examples: 34 and 52 as the page opens. 52 has 5 full rows and 34 has 3, so 52 is greater. The text then asks the
reader to set 47 and 43. Both have 4 full rows, the last rows hold 7 and 3, so 47 is greater.

### 430 Look at the full rows first

The page uses the rule on two numbers that are close and whose last rows point the wrong way. The reader says
which is greater from the numbers alone and then lays the rows out to see. The page says that a number tells its
full rows before they are drawn: 48 ends 8 past the fourth full row and 51 ends 1 past the fifth. The text keeps
to the words full row and last row, and the story `tens-and-ones` names them tens and ones.

Example: 48 and 51. 48 has the longer last row, 8 against 1, and 51 has more full rows, 5 against 4, so 51 is
greater. The text asks the reader to try other close pairs, such as 59 and 61, and 70 and 69.

### 440 Put three numbers in order

The page says that three numbers stand in order when each is less than the one after it. Comparing two numbers at
a time is enough: find the least, then compare the two that are left. The first place holds the least number and
the last place the greatest.

Example: 63, 36 and 39. 63 has 6 full rows, and 36 and 39 have 3 each, so the least is 36 or 39. Their last rows
hold 6 and 9, and 6 is less than 9, so 36 is the least. Of the two that are left, 39 has 3 full rows and 63 has 6,
so 39 is less than 63. The order is 36, 39, 63.

## Interactivity

### 410 The number that comes later is greater

- Shows: a hundred pale counters in rows of ten, as on the page "A hundred counters in rows of ten" of `counting`.
  The first number's marker stands on counter 18 and the second number's marker on counter 25, each with its
  number. The first number's marker is a ring with a round pin above its counter, and the second's is a square
  frame with a square pin below its counter. The counters both numbers hold are filled, the counters only the
  greater number holds are hollow with a heavy outline, and the counters past it are thin outlines. A line under
  the rows reads "18 is less than 25". The sliders "First number" and "Second number" run from 1 to 100.
- The reader drags either slider.
- In reply the marker runs along the count, the filled and hollow counters follow it, and the line changes
  between "is less than", "is the same as" and "is greater than" as one marker passes the other.
- Action in general words: drag a slider, and a marker moves along one count while the line of words between the
  two numbers follows the marker that stands later.

### 420 The number with more full rows is greater

- Shows: two blocks of counters side by side, the first number as the left block and the second number as the
  right block. Each block lays its number out in rows of ten with the number above it. Full rows are drawn solid
  and the last row lighter. A frame surrounds the full rows of both blocks, since 3 and 5 differ. The line under
  the blocks reads "34 is less than 52". The panel reads out "First, full rows", "First, last row", "Second, full
  rows" and "Second, last row". The sliders "First number" and "Second number" run from 0 to 100.
- The reader drags either slider.
- In reply the block fills or empties counter by counter, a row turns solid as it reaches ten, and the line
  follows. The frame surrounds the full rows of both blocks while their numbers of full rows differ, and moves to
  the two last rows when the full rows are the same.
- Action in general words: drag a slider, and a block gains or loses rows of ten while a frame shows which rows
  decide the comparison.

### 430 Look at the full rows first

- Shows: the numbers 48 and 51 in large figures, the first number on the left and the second on the right, with
  the sliders "First number" and "Second number" from 10 to 99. A closed cover lies where each block would be, the
  first number's on the left and the second's on the right, and the button "Show the rows" stands under them. The
  line of words and the frame are hidden.
- The reader sets the numbers, decides which is greater, and presses the button.
- In reply the covers lift, each number is laid out in rows of ten as on page 420, and the frame and the line
  appear. A move of either slider closes the covers again.
- Action in general words: press a button, and two numbers the reader has judged are laid out in rows that show
  the answer.

### 440 Put three numbers in order

- Shows: three number cards, drawn as the page opens, in a tray, and under it three empty places in a row. The left
  place is
  labelled "least" and the right place "greatest". Each card carries its number and, under it, a small drawing of
  the number in rows of ten.
- The reader drags a card to a place or back to the tray. A card dropped on a taken place trades with the card
  there, which goes to where the dragged card came from. A tap sends a tray card to the first empty place and a
  placed card back to the tray.
- In reply words appear under the row of places, centred on the gap between two neighbours, once both hold a card:
  "is less than" when the left card is less, "is greater than" when it is greater. When both gaps read "is less
  than", the three places take a frame and a tick.
- Action in general words: drag cards into places, and each pair of neighbours says which of its two numbers is
  the greater.

### Fields of `counters/order`

All are `new`.

- `top`, `bottom`: the two numbers, named as `counters/match` names its two rows. Each is a number,
  `{ slider: [low, high], start: n }` or `{ stepper: [low, high], start: n }`. The page calls `top` the first number
  and `bottom` the second number, and their controls read "First number" and "Second number". The numbers run from
  0 to 100.
- `show`: how the two numbers are drawn.
  - `count`: one run of 100 counters in rows of ten with two markers, one per number. The first number's marker
    is a ring with a round pin above its counter, and the second's is a square frame with a square pin below. The
    counters both numbers hold are filled, the counters only the greater number holds are hollow with a heavy
    outline, and the counters past it are thin outlines. Both numbers lie from 1 to 100.
  - `blocks`: each number is its own block of counters in rows of ten, the first number as the left block and the
    second number as the right block, with the number above. The rows look like the rows of `counters/count`. Full
    rows are solid and the last row lighter. 0 draws an empty block.
- `reading`, true or false: a line of words states the comparison, read from the first number to the second:
  "{top} is less than {bottom}", "{top} is the same as {bottom}" or "{top} is greater than {bottom}".
- `decide`, true or false, with `show: blocks`: a frame surrounds the rows that decide. It surrounds the full rows
  of both blocks when the numbers of full rows differ, the two last rows when they are the same and the numbers
  differ, and both whole blocks when the numbers are the same.
- `cover`, true or false, with `show: blocks`: covers hide both blocks, the line of `reading` and the frame of
  `decide` until the reader presses "Show the rows". A change of either number closes the covers.
- `cards`: three different numbers from 0 to 100. The scene draws them as cards in a tray, in the order written,
  above three places in a row labelled "least" at the left and "greatest" at the right. A card shows its number
  and a small drawing of it in rows of ten. The reader drags a card to a place or back to the tray. A card dropped
  on a taken place trades with the card there, which goes to where the dragged card came from. A tap sends a tray
  card to the first empty place and a placed card back to the tray. Under the row of places, centred on the gap
  between two neighbours that both hold a card, the scene writes "is less than" or "is greater than". When the
  three cards stand in order the places take a frame and a tick. A page
  with `cards` leaves out `top`, `bottom`, `show`, `reading`, `decide` and `cover`.
- `draw: true`: without `cards` the page opens on a draw, a new one each time. With `cards` it opens on those
  numbers. In both cases the button "New numbers" puts three new cards in the tray and empties the
  places. It draws three different numbers from 10 to 99. Two have the same number of full rows, so their last rows
  decide. The third has another number of full rows, and in about one draw of three it is one of the two with its
  digits swapped, as 63 beside 36. The tray never opens in order.
- `readout`: the measures the panel shows, from the link ids below. The panel labels `reading` "In words",
  `top-rows` "First, full rows", `top-last` "First, last row", `bottom-rows` "Second, full rows" and `bottom-last`
  "Second, last row".

## Sources

Each entry names the file it was read in.

- England, national curriculum for key stages 1 and 2, read in `.cache/curricula/england-ks1-2.pdf`. Year 1:
  "given a number, identify one more and one less" and "use the language of: equal to, more than, less than
  (fewer), most, least". Year 2: "compare and order numbers from 0 up to 100; use <, > and = signs" and "recognise
  the place value of each digit in a two-digit number (tens, ones)". Page 440 and the word least come from here.
- United States, Common Core, read in `.cache/curricula/us-common-core-math.pdf`. K.CC.C.7: "Compare two numbers
  between 1 and 10 presented as written numerals." 1.NBT.B.3: "Compare two two-digit numbers based on meanings of
  the tens and ones digits, recording the results of comparisons with the symbols >, =, and <." Pages 420 and 430
  put that standard on counters, as full rows before the last row.
- Japan, course of study 2008, grade 1, A(1), read in `.cache/curricula/japan-course-of-study-2008-en.pdf`. Item
  c: "To develop a sequence of numbers and to represent numbers on a number line by thinking about the order and
  the size of those numbers." Item g: "To consider numbers using ten as a unit." Page 410 shows order and size on
  one count, and pages 420 and 430 use ten as a unit.
- NCETM, "Early Years Typical Progression Chart: Comparison", read in
  `content/more-and-less/.references/ncetm.org.uk-media-wvqgcfqm-typical-progression-comparison.txt`. Comparing
  numbers depends on "knowing that the later counting numbers are worth more (because the next number is always
  one more)", which is the idea of page 410. Children compare "numbers that are far apart, near to, and next to
  each other", which is why the pairs run from 34 and 52 to 48 and 51 and 70 and 69.
- NCETM, "Exploring comparison in Early Years", read in
  `content/more-and-less/.references/ncetm.org.uk-features-exploring-comparison-in-early-years.txt`. "Fewer than"
  is for countable sets of objects and "less than" for numbers, and a number "can be greater than, less than, or
  equal to another". Children compare quantities before the numbers that stand for them. Page 410 says the first
  point, and every page of this section shows its numbers as counters.
- Oak National Academy, "Use equality and inequality symbols to compare the relative size of two numbers", read in
  `content/more-and-less/.references/thenational.academy-s-use-equality-and-inequality-symbols-to-compare-the-
  relative-size-of-two-numbers.txt`.
  Its sentences have the form "4 is less than 7" and "5 is greater than 3", the form of the line of `reading`.
- `specs/stories/more-and-less.md`, pages 11 to 13, for the sliders, the pair 48 and 51 and the three cards.

Three choices of the plan rest on no source read:

- The word greatest. The sources give greater, less and least. Greatest is the plain superlative of greater.
- The words "is the same as" for two equal numbers. The NCETM feature says "equal to". The brief leaves equal for
  section 050 to introduce.
- The pair 48 and 51 as a case where the longer last row belongs to the less number. It comes from the rough map
  of the story. NCETM asks only for numbers that are near to each other.
