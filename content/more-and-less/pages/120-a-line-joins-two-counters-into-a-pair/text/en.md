You can match up two groups without moving anything. Draw a line from an object in one group to an object in the other. The two objects the line joins are a pair, and each one is the partner of the other. The line is called a pair line.

An object can have only one pair line, so it has at most one partner. If some objects are left without a partner once no more pairs fit, the two groups are not the same.

Take a row of 7 counters above a row of 5. Each pair uses one counter from each row, so after 5 pairs every counter in the bottom row has a partner. A sixth pair would need another bottom counter, so no more pairs fit. Two top counters are left without a partner, so the rows are not the same.

The figure has these two rows and no lines.

- Drag from a counter in one row to a counter in the other, and let go on a counter with no line. You can also tap one counter, then a counter in the other row.
- Tap a paired counter to take its line away.
- Once no more pairs fit, a ring marks each counter without a partner.
