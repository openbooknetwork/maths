When you count from 1, you reach one of two numbers first and carry on to the other. The one you reach later is greater, and the one you reach first is less. Each number in the count is one more than the number before it. So if you count out counters, the greater number has all the counters of the smaller one, and some more. Greater and less are the words for numbers, just as more and fewer are the words for groups.

Take 18 and 25. You say 18 first, and then count on 7 more numbers to reach 25. So 25 counters are 18 counters and 7 more. This is why 25 is greater than 18, and 18 is less than 25.

On the hundred counters, a ring marks the first number and a square frame marks the second. The filled counters belong to both numbers, and the hollow ones with a thick outline belong only to the greater number.

- Drag the "First number" slider until the ring passes the square frame, and watch the words under the rows change. They now read [18 is less than 25](@reading).
- Put both markers on the same counter. The two numbers are then the same, and no counter is hollow.
