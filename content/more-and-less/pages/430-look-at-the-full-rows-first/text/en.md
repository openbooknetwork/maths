You can compare two numbers without drawing their counters, because a number tells you its full rows. 10 ends the first row, 20 ends the second, and so on. So a number from 41 to 49 has 4 full rows, and its last row has the counters after 40. Look at the full rows first, and look at the last rows only when the full rows are the same.

Take 48 and 51. 48 comes 8 after 40, so it has 4 full rows and 8 in its last row. 51 comes 1 after 50, so it has 5 full rows and 1 in its last row. The longer last row of 48 can trick you. 51 has one more full row, which is ten counters, and the last row of 48 has only 8. So 51 is greater.

A cover hides the rows of each number.

- Decide which of 48 and 51 is greater, then press "Show the rows". The frame goes round the full rows of both blocks, and the words under the blocks tell you whether you were right.
- Set another close pair, such as 59 and 61, or 70 and 69. Moving a slider closes the covers again, so you can decide before you look.
