A sign stays as you wrote it, even when a number in the number sentence changes. So a number sentence that is true for two numbers can be false for two others. If the two numbers are equal, < and > both make false number sentences. Only = makes a true one, because = says that both sides are the same number.

Take 23 and 31. 23 has 2 full rows of ten and 31 has 3, so 23 is less, and `23 < 31` is true. Now change 23 to 35. 35 and 31 both have 3 full rows, so the last rows decide. 35 has 5 counters in its last row and 31 has 1, so 35 is greater. The sign is still <, so `35 < 31` is false. Turn the sign round, and `35 > 31` is true.

Under the blocks, a tick means the number sentence is true, and a cross means it is false.

- Drag the "First number" slider to 35, and watch the tick turn into a cross.
- Tap the sign until the number sentence is true again.
- Set both numbers to 31, and find the sign that makes it true.

The "In words" readout shows [23 is less than 31](@reading), and the "True or false" readout shows [true](@verdict).
