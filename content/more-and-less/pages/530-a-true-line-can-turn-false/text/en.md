Two blocks show two numbers in rows of ten. The left block is the first number, [23](@top), and the right block is the second number, [31](@bottom). A sign stands between the two numbers and makes a line with them. The line reads [23 is less than 31](@reading), and the line is [true](@verdict).

A sign stays as it is written when a number changes. Drag the slider First number from 23 to 35. The line 35 < 31 is false, and the mark under the blocks turns from a tick with the word "true" to a cross with the word "false". Tap the sign until the line is true again, and it reads 35 > 31. A line that is true for two numbers can be false for two others.

Now set both numbers to 31. Then < and > both make false lines and = makes the true one, because = says that both sides are the same number. To judge any two numbers, compare the full rows first and then the last row. The frame surrounds the rows that decide.
