Every number from 1 to 99 has a number before it and a number after it in the count. The number before is one less, and the number after is one more.

Take 38. The count goes 37, 38, 39, so the number before 38 is 37 and the number after it is 39. It works the same way across a ten. In rows of ten, one more than 39 is 40, and that counter fills the fourth row. One more than 40 is 41, and that counter starts a new row. When you count back, one less than 41 is 40, and one less than 40 is 39, which leaves the fourth row one counter short again. The same thing happens at every ten up to 100.

A ring marks the last counter, and a dashed outline marks where the next counter goes. Under the rows, the middle tile shows how many counters there are, with the number before on its left and the number after on its right.

- Tap the "one more" tile until the middle tile shows 41. Watch the dashed outline: at 40 it moves to the start of a new row.
- Tap the "one less" tile until you get to 29. At 29 the third row is one counter short, as the fourth row was at 39.
