A number sentence says something about its two numbers, and what it says is either true or false. The first number is less than the second, equal to it, or greater than it, and only one of these can be so. Each sign says one of them, so one sign makes a true number sentence, and the other two make false ones.

Take a top row of 9 counters and a bottom row of 6, and pair them. Three counters in the top row are left over, so 9 is greater than 6. The number sentence `9 < 6` says that 9 is less than 6, so it is false. `9 = 6` is false too, because 9 and 6 are different numbers. That leaves `9 > 6`, which is true, because its open side faces 9, the greater number.

The rows on the figure stay as they are, and you choose the sign. It starts on <, and each tap changes it to the next one, from < to = to > and back to <. Beside the number sentence is a tick with "true" or a cross with "false". Tap the sign until the number sentence is true.

The "In words" readout shows [9 is less than 6](@reading), and the "True or false" readout shows [false](@verdict).
