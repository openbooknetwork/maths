To make two groups the same, you can add objects to the group with fewer, so that each new object pairs with one that was left over. Or you can take the ones left over away from the group with more. Either way, you add or take away as many objects as were left over. That number answers both "how many more" and "how many fewer", and it is called the difference between the two groups.

With 9 counters on top and 5 below, 4 top counters are left over. If you add 4 counters to the bottom row, each new one pairs with a top counter that was left over, and the rows are the same. The bottom row was 4 counters short, so it had 4 fewer. If you take those 4 away from the top row instead, the rows are the same too. The top row had 4 counters that nothing in the bottom row could pair with, so it had 4 more. Both answers are the difference, 4.

Fewer is the word for objects, and less is the word for numbers. So 5 counters are fewer than 9 counters, and 5 is less than 9.

Tap + on the "Bottom row" stepper to add counters, or − on the "Top row" stepper to take them away, or mix the two. An added counter has a small plus sign on it, and a counter taken away leaves a dashed empty place. The Added and "Taken away" readouts count them, now [0](@added) and [0](@taken). Whichever mix you use, the tags read "the same" after 4 changes in all.
