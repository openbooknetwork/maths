A sign between two numbers tells you how they compare. There are three signs. You read < as "is less than", > as "is greater than" and = as "is equal to". Two numbers are equal when they are the same number.

The < and > signs are open on one side and pointed on the other. The open side faces the greater number, and the point faces the smaller number. Two numbers and a sign between them make a number sentence, which you read from left to right.

Pair a top row of 5 counters with a bottom row of 8. Three bottom counters are left over, so the bottom row has more and 8 is greater. The open side faces the 8, and the number sentence is `5 < 8`, or "5 is less than 8". Add 3 counters to the top row, and every counter has a partner. The numbers are equal, so the number sentence is `8 = 8`.

The number sentence is under the paired rows, with the top row's number on the left. Tap + on the "Top row" stepper until the top row has more, and watch the sign. It stays < while the bottom row has counters left over, becomes = when every counter has a partner, and turns round once the counters left over are in the top row.
