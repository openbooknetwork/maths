# Terms of Adding and subtracting within 20

## 010 joining
- Introduces:
  - `join (groups)`: put two groups of objects together into one group. The brief's verb, chosen over England's
    "put together" and NCETM's "combine" as one plain word whose opposite is "take away".
  - `add (numbers)`: find the number that two numbers make together. "Adding" names the work. England Year 1 gives
    "add" and Bremen "addieren".
  - `sum (numbers)`: the number an addition gives, the number of objects in two groups joined, so the sum of 5 and 3
    is 8. NCETM gives "whole/sum" in Year 1 and England "sum" in Year 2. Chosen over England's Year 1 "total" because
    the stories after this one, `fact-families`, `equals-sign` and `adding-tens-and-ones`, build on "sum". The
    question "how many altogether" asks for the sum, and the story leaves "total" out.
  - `sign (calculation)`: one of + and −, written between two numbers. England Year 1 gives "addition (+),
    subtraction (–) and equals (=) signs". This section gives +, and 020 gives −.
  - `plus, minus (signs)`: how + and − are read. The number sentence 5 + 3 = 8 reads "5 plus 3 is equal to 8".
    NCETM's sentence "___ plus ___ is equal to ___" gives the reading with the glossary's "is equal to". This section
    gives "plus", and 020 gives "minus".
  - `number sentence (calculations, comparisons)`: the numbers and signs written in a row under the figure, a
    calculation with its answer such as 5 + 3 = 8. The same word names a comparison such as 3 < 5. Eureka Math gives
    "Write the number sentence starting with the larger number."
    (`content/adding-within-20/.references/onlinemathlearning.com-count-on-larger-addend.txt`). Chosen over NCETM's
    and IM's "equation", which is left to `equals-sign`, because one word covers the calculations of this story and
    the comparisons of `more-and-less`.
  - `addition, subtraction (calculations)`: a calculation with + such as 5 + 3, and one with − such as 12 − 5. A
    calculation with no answer yet is named so, as "the addition 7 + 5" or "the subtraction 13 − 8", and the number
    sentence writes it with its answer. England Year 1 gives "read, write and interpret mathematical statements
    involving addition (+), subtraction (–) and equals (=) signs" (`.cache/curricula/england-ks1-2.pdf`, page 7).
  - `whole, part (numbers)`: the whole is a number split in two, and its parts are the two numbers it splits into,
    which make the whole together. In 5 + 3 = 8 the parts are 5 and 3 and the whole is 8. Oak's keywords "Whole" and
    "Part", and NCETM's "part–part–whole". The reader knows them from `number-bonds`, and this story fixes their form.
  - `ten frame (figure)`: a frame of ten cells in two rows of five, each cell holding one counter. The brief's and
    `number-bonds`' form, chosen over NCETM's "tens frame" and IM's "10-frame" as the one that reads aloud as written.
    The story draws two side by side, the first frame and the second frame. The reader knows them from
    `number-bonds`.
  - `cell (ten frame)`: one place of a ten frame, which holds one counter or is empty. The `number-bonds` form.
- Uses: `one more (numbers)`, `equal (numbers)`, `is less than, is greater than, is equal to (signs)` for the
  reading "is equal to", `sign (comparison)` for =

## 020 taking-away
- Introduces:
  - `take away (groups)`: move some objects out of a group. England Year 1, NCETM and Oxford give "take away". The
    story says it of objects, as in "take 5 counters away", and reads the sign − as "minus".
  - `left (groups)`: the objects that stay in a group after some are taken away. "What is left" names their number,
    so 12 − 5 = 7 says that 7 are left. Oxford gives "how many left?" and NCETM "How many are left?".
  - `subtract (numbers)`: take one number away from another and find what is left, as 12 − 5 subtracts 5 from 12.
    "Subtracting" names the work. England Year 1 gives "subtract" and Bremen "subtrahieren".
  - `plus, minus (signs)`: the "minus" half of the entry of 010. 12 − 5 = 7 reads "12 minus 5 is equal to 7". Bremen
    and Oxford give "minus". Chosen over "take away" for the sign because NCETM warns that "the subtraction sign is
    not referred to as ‘take away’", and "minus" also fits the difference in 040.
  - `sign (calculation)`, `addition, subtraction (calculations)`: the − half of the entries of 010.
- Uses: `join (groups)`, `add (numbers)`, `sum (numbers)`, `sign (calculation)`,
  `number sentence (calculations, comparisons)`,
  `ten frame (figure)`, `cell (ten frame)`, `one fewer (groups)`, `equal (numbers)`, `is less than, is greater than, is equal to (signs)`

## 030 counting-on
- Introduces:
  - `number line (figure)`, `mark (number line)`, `point (number line)`, `jump (number line)`,
    `to the right, to the left (number line)`: the glossary senses as written, from `number-line`. One page gives
    them again, since the reader may not have read `number-line`. The line runs from 0 to 20, and the numbers stand
    at the marks without the term "label". Each jump of this story goes to the mark next to it.
  - `start (number line)`: the mark where the point stands before the first jump. A jump lands on the mark where it
    ends. NCETM gives "start at a fixed point on a number line", and the brief "a start the reader sets".
- Uses: `count on, count back (numbers)`, `add (numbers)`, `subtract (numbers)`,
  `number sentence (calculations, comparisons)`,
  `addition, subtraction (calculations)`, `the number before, the number after (numbers)`

## 040 difference
- Introduces:
  - `difference (numbers)`: of two numbers, how much greater one is than the other. It is written as a subtraction,
    the greater number minus the smaller, so the difference between 13 and 8 is 13 − 8 = 5. It is the number that
    `difference (groups)` counts when two groups of 13 and 8 objects are paired. England Year 1 gives "difference
    between" and Oak "Difference". The meaning is the brief's "the greater number minus the smaller".
  - `the greater number, the smaller number (numbers)`: of two numbers, the one that is greater and the one that is
    less. Oxford gives "start with the number to be taken away (usually the smaller number)"
    (`content/adding-within-20/.references/cdn.oxfordowl.co.uk-2014-05-13-10-29-46-467-PX-MathsContent-BK-SubtractionInSchool-01-CH.txt`).
    "Less than" stays the comparison, and "smaller" names the number before a noun, so the page "Count on from the
    smaller number to the greater" says it so.
- Uses: `difference (groups)`, `pair (objects)`, `partner (objects)`, `pair line (figure)`, `more (groups)`,
  `fewer (groups)`, `greater (numbers)`, `less (numbers)`, `subtract (numbers)`, `minus` of
  `plus, minus (signs)`, `count on, count back (numbers)`, `number line (figure)`, `mark (number line)`,
  `point (number line)`, `jump (number line)`, `start (number line)`, `number sentence (calculations, comparisons)`

## 050 making-ten
- Introduces:
  - `full ten (ten frame)`, `a full ten and some more (numbers)`: a ten frame with a counter in every cell, and each
    number from 11 to 20 laid out as a full first frame and some counters in the second, so 13 is 10 and 3. The
    `number-bonds` form, which the progression gives as "knowing all teen numbers as 10 + n". One page goes over it.
  - `pair (numbers)`: two numbers that make a given number together, as 8 and 2 are a pair that makes 10. The brief's
    and `number-bonds`' "pairs that make 10". A new sense beside `pair (objects)`. One page goes over the pairs that
    make 10.
  - `split (numbers)`: break a whole into two parts, as 5 splits into 2 and 3. The brief's and `number-bonds`' word,
    chosen over NCETM's and Oak's "partition" as the plain one.
  - `cross ten (calculations)`: an addition or a subtraction crosses ten when one of its first number and its answer
    is less than 10 and the other is greater than 10, as 8 + 5 = 13 and 13 − 5 = 8. The brief's "across ten", from
    Oak's "crosses the 10 boundary". 8 + 2 = 10 reaches ten and does not cross it.
  - `making ten (calculations)`: adding or subtracting across ten by going to 10 first. The US standard gives "making
    ten" and NCETM "a ‘make ten’ strategy", chosen over Oak's "bridging 10" as the plainer.
  - `make ten, down to ten (making ten)`: the part of the second number that brings the first number to 10 makes
    ten, in an addition and in a subtraction, as 2 makes ten with 8 and 3 taken from 13 makes ten. In a subtraction
    the part also takes the first number down to ten, as 3 takes 13 down to ten. NCETM gives "‘First we took away two
    counters to make ten.’" and "To subtract through ten, we subtract down to ten"
    (`content/adding-within-20/.references/ncetm.org.uk-media-x51ltghh-ncetm-mm-sp1-y2-se11-teach-final-ys2.txt`,
    page 19). "Make ten" replaces the brief's "fill the ten", and the page "Make ten first" says it so. "Ten" here is
    `ten (numbers)`, the number 10, which the full first frame shows.
  - `Makes ten (figure)`: the readout and stepper that hold the part of the second number that makes ten with the
    first, as 2 for 8 + 5. A figure word, named after US K.OA.A.4 "find the number that makes 10 when added to the
    given number" (`.cache/curricula/us-common-core-math.pdf`, page 11). It replaces the name "To ten".
  - `the rest (making ten)`: the other part of the second number, which is added to 10 or taken from it, so
    8 + 5 = 8 + 2 + 3 and the rest is 3. The `number-bonds` form "10 and the rest", chosen over the brief's "the part
    left over" because "left over" is the pairing word of `difference (groups)` and "left" is the word of 020.
- Uses: `ten frame (figure)`, `cell (ten frame)`, `whole, part (numbers)`, `ten (numbers)`, `add (numbers)`, `subtract (numbers)`, `sum (numbers)`,
  `take away (groups)`, `left (groups)`, `plus, minus (signs)`, `number sentence (calculations, comparisons)`,
  `addition, subtraction (calculations)`, `count on, count back (numbers)`, `equal (numbers)`

## Rules
- Join and take away are said of groups of objects, and add and subtract of numbers. The text says "join the two
  groups" and "add 5 and 3", "take 5 counters away" and "subtract 5 from 12".
- The sign − is read "minus", and taking away is what the reader does to the counters. A number sentence is read
  "12 minus 5 is equal to 7", and the text does not read − as "take away". The world page of 020 says "the jars left
  are 10 minus the jars sold".
- The number an addition gives is the sum. "Altogether" asks for it, as in "how many altogether", and "total" is
  left out of the story.
- What a take away leaves has no name of its own beside "what is left". In 040 a subtraction such as 13 − 8 = 5 also
  writes a difference, and the page says the number sentence can mean either.
- "Left" is what stays after a take away. "Left over" is said of the objects with no partner when two groups are
  paired, as in `difference (groups)`. "To the left" is a direction on the number line. The second part in making
  ten is "the rest".
- `difference (groups)` counts objects left over, and `difference (numbers)` is the same number found from the two
  numbers. The text calls it the difference. "Gap" is the piece of the number line between two marks, and this story
  leaves it out, so the rough map's titles "The difference is the gap" and "Find the gap on the number line" say
  "difference".
- `pair (objects)` is two objects joined by a pair line, and `pair (numbers)` is two numbers that make a given number.
  The text says "a pair that makes 10" for numbers. "Partner" is said only of objects, and the text says "the number
  that makes 10 with 8".
- A jump is one move of the point, and the text counts on "3 jumps". The start is the mark the jumps begin from and
  is not counted as a jump.
- "The two steps of making ten" is ordinary English for 8 + 2 = 10 and 10 + 3 = 13. `step (number line)` keeps its
  sense on a number line.
- Counting on starts from the first number. Starting from the greater number is left to `any-order`.
- "The first number" and "the second number" are the numbers before and after the sign of a calculation. In a take
  away the second number is "the number taken away".
- "Whole" is said of the number two parts make. The text says "taking all of them away leaves 0" in place of "taking
  away the whole number", since "whole number" is a later term.
- The text compares numbers with "is greater than" and "is less than". Before a noun it says "the greater number"
  and "the smaller number", so a difference is the greater number minus the smaller, and the text does not say "the
  less number".
- Each = in a number sentence joins two equal sides. The steps of making ten are written one number sentence each,
  8 + 2 = 10 and 10 + 3 = 13, and not as one chain such as 13 − 3 = 10 − 2 = 8.
- Left to later stories:
  - `equals-sign` introduces "equation", and the sense of "sum" as a calculation such as 8 + 5, as in its "a sum on
    each side". In this story a calculation written down is a number sentence, "sum" is the number, and 8 + 5 is an
    addition.
  - `fact-families` says "two take aways" in its rough map for number sentences with −. This story's word for them is
    "subtraction".
  - `any-order` says "bigger number" and "total" in its rough map, where this story says "greater number" and "sum".
  - `adding-tens-and-ones` says "find the gap" in its rough map for a difference, which this story calls the
    difference. Its crossing of 20, 30 and the others would be a new sense, "cross a ten", beside
    `cross ten (calculations)`.
  - `tens-and-ones` introduces "ten" as a group of ten ones. The "ten" of "make ten" is the number 10.
  - `number-bonds` teaches whole, part, split, ten frame, cell, full ten, the pairs that make 10 and "a full ten and
    some more" first, and takes the forms this story fixes.
