# Making ten

## Header

- Assumes: from `number-bonds`, the reader knows a whole and its two parts, splitting a number below 10 in several
  ways, the pairs that make 10, the ten frame and its cells, and each number from 11 to 20 as a full ten and some
  more. From `joining` the reader knows the sign + read as plus, the written line under the figure such as
  5 + 3 = 8, the words add and sum, the counters that wait in a tray beside the frames and join by drag or tap, and
  the steppers "First number" and "Second number". From `taking-away` the reader knows the sign − read as minus,
  the written line of a subtraction such as 9 − 4 = 5, the words subtract and left, and the tap that takes a
  counter away and leaves a dashed ring. From `counting-on` and `difference` the reader knows counting on and back
  as jumps on the number line, and the difference as the greater number minus the less.
- Teaches: an addition such as 12 + 5 keeps the full ten and adds to the counters after it, and an addition or a
  subtraction that crosses ten goes to 10 first, so 8 + 5 is 8 + 2 = 10 and 10 + 3 = 13, and 13 − 5 is 13 − 3 = 10
  and 10 − 2 = 8, each step a written line of its own.
- Introduces:
  - Terms: full ten and a full ten and some more, pair (numbers) and split (numbers), on the page that goes over
    them (510). Cross ten, making ten, fill the ten and the rest (520). Down to ten (530).
  - Controls: the two written lines of the steps under the line of the calculation, and the readouts "To ten" and
    "The rest" (520). A drag that puts a waiting counter into the frame it is dropped on (520). The seed tray with a
    packet of seeds, where a drop or a tap on an empty cell sows a seed in that row (620). The goal `steps-to-ten`,
    with the steppers "To ten" and "The rest", Check and Another one (730).
- Uses:
  - 12 red counters, a full ten and 2 more, with 5 blue counters joined after them: 12 + 5 = 17. The second number
    steps from 0 to 8, and the sum runs from 12 to 20. The second frame at 12 + 5 holds 7 counters and 3 empty
    cells, 7 and 3 a pair that makes 10, and its 7 counters split into 2 red and 5 blue (page 510).
  - 8 red counters and 5 blue counters waiting: 5 splits into 2, which fills the ten, and 3, the rest. 8 + 5 = 13
    with 8 + 2 = 10 and 10 + 3 = 13 (page 520).
  - 13 counters, from which 5 are taken: 13 − 5 = 8 with 13 − 3 = 10 and 10 − 2 = 8. The first number steps from 11
    to 19, and the text tries 15 − 7 with 15 − 5 = 10 and 10 − 2 = 8 (page 530).
  - A seed tray of 20 cells in two rows of ten, with 7 grown seedlings in the first row and a packet of 5 seeds.
    The reader works out how many seeds fill the first row, how many go to the second and how many plants the tray
    will hold, then sows: 7 + 5 = 12 with 7 + 3 = 10 and 10 + 2 = 12 (page 620).
  - The challenge draws an addition or a subtraction that crosses ten, with a second number from 3 to 9, such as
    8 + 5 or 15 − 8. The reader sets To ten and The rest, 2 and 3 for 8 + 5, and 5 and 3 for 15 − 8, which gives

  - 8 + 5 = 13 with its steps, both numbers on steppers from 0 to 10 (page 810).

## Content

### 510 The full ten stays full

The page that goes over what this section takes from `number-bonds`, then adds to a full ten and some more.

The frames hold 12 red counters. The first frame has a counter in every cell, which is a full ten, and 2 more stand
in the second frame. Each number from 11 to 20 is a full ten and some more in this way, so 12 is 10 and 2. The page
then goes over the pairs and the splits, with the second frame as its example. A ten frame has 10 cells, so the
counters in a frame and its empty cells make 10. The second frame holds 7 counters and 3 empty cells, so 7 and 3
are a pair that makes 10. Its 7 counters are 2 red and 5 blue, so 7 splits into 2 and 5, and a number below 10
splits into two parts in several ways. The next pages use these pairs and splits.

Then the addition. The 5 blue counters join after the red ones, in the second frame, and the first frame stays as it
is. The written line reads 12 + 5 = 17. The full ten stays, and the counters after it go from 2 to 2 + 5 = 7, so the
sum is a full ten and 7 more, 17. The text asks the reader to step the second number up and down. Only the second
frame changes, and at 12 + 8 = 20 both frames are full. The page ends on the rule: to add to a full ten and some
more, keep the ten and add to the counters after it.

Example: 12 + 5 = 17, then 12 + 8 = 20.

### 520 Fill the ten first

8 red counters fill most of the first frame, and 5 blue counters wait in the tray. The first frame has 2 empty
cells, because 8 and 2 are a pair that makes 10. So 2 of the 5 counters fill the ten, and 5 splits into 2 and 3.
The 3 are the rest. The text asks the reader to drag 2 counters into the first frame and the other 3 into the
second frame. A tap joins a counter to the next empty cell, so taps fill the ten first too.

Once the first frame is full and the sum goes past ten, two written lines show under the line of the calculation,
and with every counter joined they read 8 + 2 = 10 and 10 + 3 = 13. The readouts To ten and The rest then read 2
and 3, and a dash while the steps are hidden. The text writes the steps as plain numbers and names the two readouts
in words. It reads the steps in full: "5 splits into 2 and 3. 8 plus 2 is equal to 10, and 10 plus 3 is equal to
13." Each step is a written line of its own, since each = joins two equal sides.

8 + 5 crosses ten: the first number, 8, is less than 10, and the sum, 13, is greater. Making ten turns the
addition into 10 + 3, a full ten and some more, which the page before added. A reader who drops counters into the
second frame while the first frame has empty cells sees 8 + 5 = 13 with no steps, since the frames show no full
ten. Sending a counter back to the tray and dropping it into the first frame brings the steps.

Example: 8 + 5 = 13, with 8 + 2 = 10 and 10 + 3 = 13.

### 530 Go down to ten first

The frames hold 13 counters, a full ten and 3 more. Taking 5 away crosses ten, since 13 is greater than 10 and
what is left will be less. The text asks the reader to take the 3 counters of the second frame away first. That
leaves the full ten: 13 − 3 = 10, which takes 13 down to ten. 5 splits into 3 and 2, so 2 more come from the full
ten, and 10 − 2 = 8, because 8 and 2 make 10. Under 13 − 5 = 8 the two steps show: 13 − 3 = 10 and 10 − 2 = 8. The
text reads them as NCETM's children do: "First we took away 3 counters to make ten. Then we took away 2 more to
make 8."

The steps show once the second frame is empty and the take goes past ten. At 13 − 3 = 10 the line reaches ten and
writes no steps yet. The text writes the steps as plain numbers, and says that the readouts To ten and The rest read
3 and 2 with 5 taken. A reader who takes counters from the full ten first also leaves 8, and no steps show while a
counter stays in the second frame.

Then the text asks for a new case: set the first number to 15 and take 7 away. The 5 counters of the second frame
take 15 down to ten, and 7 splits into 5 and 2, so 15 − 7 = 8 with 15 − 5 = 10 and 10 − 2 = 8. The part that takes
a number down to ten is the number of counters after its full ten. Making ten works for a subtraction too.

Example: 13 − 5 = 8, with 13 − 3 = 10 and 10 − 2 = 8. Then 15 − 7 = 8.

### 620 A seed tray fills row by row

The world page. A gardener raises young plants in a seed tray of 20 cells, two rows of ten, and sows the first row
full before starting the second, so the plants of a full row can be counted at a glance. The first row holds 7
grown seedlings, and a packet of 5 seeds waits beside the tray. Under the tray the calculation stands open, 7 + 5.

Before sowing, the text asks the reader to work out three numbers: how many seeds fill the first row, how many go
to the second row, and how many plants the tray will hold. It points to the idea and leaves the numbers to the
reader: the seeds that fill the row make 10 with the seedlings, the other seeds of the packet start the second row,
and a full row and some more is a number the reader reads at once. Then the reader sows each seed into an empty
cell, in the first row until it is full and then in the second.

The text gives none of the answers, and the readouts To ten and The rest show dashes until the packet is empty. With
every seed sown and the first row full, the written line reads 7 + 5 = 12 with 7 + 3 = 10 and 10 + 2 = 12, and the
readouts read 3 and 2. The text asks the reader to compare them with the three numbers worked out before sowing. A
reader who sows into the second row while the first row has an empty cell sees 7 + 5 = 12 with no steps, and the
text says that a seed sent back to the packet and sown in the first row brings them.

Answer, for the writer and the engine author and kept out of the text: 3 seeds fill the first row, 2 go to the
second row, and the tray holds 12 plants.

### 730 Find the two steps of making ten

The challenge. The page draws an addition or a subtraction that crosses ten, such as 8 + 5 or 15 − 8, and writes
it open under the frames. The frames hold the counters of the first number. The reader sets the two steppers To
ten and The rest to the two parts the second number splits into, and presses Check. The counters stay where they
are until the goal holds, so the reader works the split out before anything moves.

The text says how. In an addition, To ten is the number that makes 10 with the first number. In a subtraction, To
ten is the number of counters after the full ten, which takes the first number down to ten. The rest is what is
left of the second number. It gives one example of each kind as plain numbers: 9 + 4 splits 4 into 1 and 3, and
16 − 9 splits 9 into 6 and 3. Once the goal holds, the counters move in the two steps and the written line and its
two steps show. The button Another one draws a new calculation.

### 810 What comes after adding and subtracting

The end page of the story. The figure shows 8 + 5 = 13 with its two steps, and the reader can set both numbers. The
text sums the story up in the order it was taught. Joining two groups gives the sum, written with +. Taking a group
away leaves fewer, written with −. A small number is added by counting on and subtracted by counting back, one jump
at a time. The difference of two numbers is the greater minus the less, and counting on finds it. An addition or a
subtraction that crosses ten goes to 10 first.

The page then names the stories that follow. Fact families writes the four facts that three numbers such as 8, 5
and 13 make. Adding tens and ones adds and subtracts bigger numbers and crosses 20, 30 and the other tens. The
equals sign puts a calculation on each side of =. Order and grouping adds numbers in any order and groups them.

Example: 8 + 5 = 13, with 8 + 2 = 10 and 10 + 3 = 13.

## Challenge

Find the two steps of making ten, at page 730.

- Goal: when the reader presses Check, the stepper To ten holds the part of the second number that takes the first
  number to 10, and The rest holds the other part.
- Drawn each time: an addition or a subtraction, each half the time, whose second number comes from `second: [3, 9]`
  and which crosses ten. An addition has a first number from 2 to 9 and a sum from 11 to 18, such as 8 + 5. A
  subtraction has a first number from 11 to 18 and what is left from 2 to 9, such as 15 − 8. Each kind has 35
  calculations. The steppers open at 0, and To ten is 1 or more in every draw, so the page does not open solved.
- Reached by: the two steppers and Check. The reader finds To ten from the first number, as the number that makes 10
  with it in an addition or the counters after its full ten in a subtraction, then splits the second number into To
  ten and the rest. The counters do not move before Check, so the frames do not count the split out for the reader.
- The answer of the calculation is not asked. Once the split is right, the second step is a full ten and some more,
  10 + 3 or 10 − 3, which page 510 and `taking-away` gave the reader, and the done line writes it.
- Hidden: every readout, and the answer of the calculation and its two steps until the goal holds. The calculation
  stands open, 8 + 5, from the start.
- Wrong answer, in this order:
  - To ten is off. The note writes the reader's first step without its answer and says which way it misses 10:
    "8 + 3 goes past 10." or "8 + 1 stops before 10.", and "15 − 6 goes past 10." or "15 − 4 stops before 10." The
    note names the reader's numbers, so it says the direction and leaves the right number to the reader.
  - To ten is right and the two steppers do not make the second number: "To ten and The rest make more than 5." or
    "To ten and The rest make less than 5."
- Hint: on an addition, "To ten is the number that makes 10 with 8. The rest is what is left of 5." On a
  subtraction, "To ten is the number of counters after the full ten of 15. The rest is what is left of 8."
- When the goal holds: the tick, the counters move in the two steps, and the written line reads the whole
  calculation with its steps, such as 8 + 5 = 13 with 8 + 2 = 10 and 10 + 3 = 13. The done line reads "8 + 5 = 13,
  with 8 + 2 = 10 and 10 + 3 = 13."
- Ideas used: the pairs that make 10 and the splits of a number below 10 from `number-bonds`, the written lines of
  an addition and a subtraction from `joining` and `taking-away`, and making ten from this section. A reader who
