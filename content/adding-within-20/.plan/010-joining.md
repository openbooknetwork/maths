# Adding joins two groups

## Header

- Assumes: from `counting`, counting a group and counting on. From `number-bonds`, a whole and its two parts, the
  ten frame and its cells, and a number from 11 to 20 as a full ten and some more. From `more-and-less`, one more as
  the next number, the sign = read "is equal to", a line of two numbers and a sign, and the stepper.
- Teaches: joining puts two groups together and the sum is how many there are altogether, the written line
  5 + 3 = 8 with the sign + between the two numbers reads "5 plus 3 is equal to 8", the sum grows by one when either
  number grows by one, and adding 0 leaves a number as it was.
- Introduces:

    on page 120. Add (numbers), addition (calculations) and sum (numbers) on page 130. The section uses whole and
    part, ten frame and cell from `number-bonds`, and one more, equal and "is equal to" from `more-and-less`.
  - Controls: two dice with a Roll button, and the tray of waiting counters, where a drag or a tap joins a counter
    to the frames and a drag or a tap on a joined counter sends it back (page 110). The written line under the
    frames, which follows the counters, and the readout "In words" (page 120). The steppers "First number" and

- Uses:
  - A board game where one move is as many squares as two dice show together. The dice show 5 and 3, 5 red

## Content

The text says "join" of groups of counters and "add" of numbers, as the terms rule. The moving thing on the board of
page 110 is "the piece", so "counter" stays the word for the frames. The word "sum" first shows on page 130, and
"total" is left out of the story.

### 110 How far does the piece move?

The page opens on a board game where a player throws two dice and moves the piece as many squares as the two dice
show together. The dice above the frames show 5 and 3. The red counters in the frames are the dots of the first
die, and the blue counters beside the frames are the dots of the second. The text asks the reader to join the blue
counters to the red ones and count how many counters the frames hold then. Each counter joined takes the next empty
cell, so the counters of the two dice sit as one group in the frames. The frames hold 8 counters, and the piece moves
8 squares. The reader presses Roll for a new throw and joins again. The page ends on the question the story answers:
two groups joined make a new number, and the story writes it down with a sign and finds quick ways to work it out,
for joining and for taking away. The page has no sign and no written line.

Links: the number on the first die, the counters still waiting, and the counters in the frames altogether, which
grows from 5 to 8 as the reader joins. The text asks the reader to count before reading that number, and a tap on it
lights every counter in the frames.

### 120 Join two groups and write it with +

The frames hold 5 red counters and 3 blue counters, and a written line stands under them: 5 + 3 = 8. The 5 is the
red group and the 3 the blue group, each written in the colour of its group, and 8 is how many there are in the
frames altogether. The sign + stands between the two numbers and says that their groups are joined. The line reads
"5 plus 3 is equal to 8", as the readout "In words" shows. The sign = is the one the reader knows from comparing.
It joins two equal sides, 5 + 3 on one side and 8 on the other, which are the same number. A group of 5 joined with
a group of 3 makes a group of 8. Two more blue counters wait beside the frames. The text
asks the reader to join one and watch the line change to 5 + 4 = 9, then to send blue counters back and watch it
follow. The numbers before and after + are called the first number and the second number. The page keeps to the
sign, its reading and the written line, and leaves the word sum and the facts about one more and 0 to page 130.

Example: 5 + 3 = 8, read "5 plus 3 is equal to 8".

### 130 The sum tells you how many altogether

The steppers set the two groups, 4 red and 3 blue, and the written line reads 4 + 3 = 7. Adding 4 and 3 gives 7.
A calculation with + such as 4 + 3 is an addition, and the number it gives is the sum, how many counters there are
altogether. In the terms of `number-bonds`, the first and second numbers are the parts and the sum is the whole. The
text asks the reader to press + on the stepper "First number": 5 + 3 = 8. One more counter in either group is one
more counter in the frames, so the sum grows by one and becomes the next number, as 4 + 4 = 8 shows from the other
stepper. Then the reader sets the first number to 4 and the second number to 0. The blue group is empty and the line
reads 4 + 0 = 4, so adding 0 leaves a number as it was. Setting the first number to 0 and the second number to 3
gives 0 + 3 = 3, the same from the first number. Last, the steppers go up to 10. The
reader sets 6 and 6, the counters fill the first frame and go on into the second, and the sum 12 shows as a full ten
and 2 more.

Examples: 4 + 3 = 7, 5 + 3 = 8, 4 + 4 = 8, 4 + 0 = 4, 0 + 3 = 3, 6 + 6 = 12.

## Interactivity

### 110

- Shows: two ten frames side by side, or one above the other on a narrow stage. 5 round red counters fill the first
  five cells of the first frame. A tray beside the frames holds 3 square blue counters. A die over the frames shows
  5, a die over the tray shows 3, and a Roll button stands in the panel. No written line and no readout.
- The reader: drags a blue counter from the tray onto the frames or taps it, drags a joined blue counter off the
  frames or taps it, and presses Roll.
- The figure: a joined counter takes the next empty cell after the counters already there. A counter sent back
  returns to the tray. Roll throws both dice from 1 to 6, puts the first die's red counters in the frames and the
  second die's blue counters in the tray, and sends every joined counter back. The links in the text follow.
- Action: throw the dice, then join the waiting group to the group in the frames one counter at a time.

### 120

- Shows: the two frames with 5 red counters and 3 blue counters after them, and 2 blue counters waiting in the tray.
  Under the frames the written line 5 + 3 = 8, with 5 in red, 3 in blue, and +, = and 8 in ink. The readout "In
  words" reads "5 plus 3 is equal to 8".
- The reader: joins a waiting counter, or sends a joined blue counter back to the tray, by drag or by tap.
- The figure: each counter joined adds one to the second number of the written line and to its answer, 5 + 4 = 9
  and 5 + 5 = 10. Each counter sent back takes one off both, down to 5 + 0 = 5. The readout "In words" follows.
- Action: join a counter or send one back, and the written line rewrites its second number and its answer.

### 130

- Shows: the two frames with 4 red counters and 3 blue counters after them, the written line 4 + 3 = 7 under them,
  the steppers "First number" at 4 and "Second number" at 3, each from 0 to 10, and the readout "Sum" at 7.
- The reader: presses + or − on either stepper.
- The figure: the first stepper adds or removes a red counter at the end of the red group, and the blue counters move
  on or back one cell to stay after it. The second stepper adds or removes a blue counter at the end. At 0 a group
  has no counter and the written line writes 0. Past 10 counters, the counters go on into the second frame. The
  written line and the readout "Sum" follow every press.
- Action: set either number with its stepper, and the counters, the written line and the sum follow.

## Challenge

The challenge 720, "Find the sum before the counters appear", sits after the world pages. It follows the brief's
line for 010: the challenge writes a drawn addition with no answer and no counters, and the reader sets its sum
before the counters appear.

- Goal: the stepper "Sum" holds the sum of the drawn addition when the reader presses Check.
- Drawn each time: a first number from 2 to 8 and a second number from 2 to 8 whose sum lies from 5 to 10, the
  section's own limit of sums within 10. The written line under the frames shows the addition open, such as 4 + 3,
  in the colours of the two groups. The frames are empty, and the stepper "Sum" opens at 0, which no drawn sum is,
  so the page does not open solved.
- Reached by: the stepper "Sum" from 0 to 20, then Check. The stepper reaches 20, the cells of the two frames, so
  its range does not tell the reader that the sum is 10 or less. A wrong Check says in which direction the number
  is off: "The sum is less than 9." or "The sum is greater than 6." The frames stay empty after a wrong Check. A
  change of the stepper takes the check back. Another one draws a new addition, empties the frames and sets the
  stepper to 0.
- Once the goal holds: the first group of round red counters fills the frames from the first cell, the second group
  of square blue counters joins after it, the written line takes its answer, 4 + 3 = 7, and the done line says "A
  group of 4 joined with a group of 3 makes 7." The tick shows. A change of the stepper empties the frames, takes the
  answer off the line and clears the tick.
- Hidden: the counters, the answer of the written line, and every readout, until the goal holds.
- The idea used: adding two numbers to find their sum, the idea of page 130. The counters do not show while the
  reader works, so the sum comes from the numbers, by knowing it, by picturing the two groups joined, or by counting
  on from the first number, and stepping a counter at a time shows nothing to count.
