# Adding and subtracting within 20

The brief of the story `adding-within-20`. `specs/stories/adding-within-20.md` was its rough map, `research.md`
holds what the sources say and `terms.md` the words the story gives its reader.

## Strategy

- **Subject:** adding and subtracting with numbers up to 20.
- **Level:** beginner. The reader knows the stories `number-bonds` and `more-and-less`, and through them
  `counting`: counting on and back, a whole and its two parts, the pairs that make 10, a full ten and some more,
  pairing two rows, how many more, and the sign =.
- **At the end the reader can:** join two groups and write the sum with + and =, take a group away and write what
  is left with − and =, read both written lines aloud with plus, minus and is equal to, add a small number by
  counting on and subtract one by counting back, write a difference as a subtraction and find it by counting on,
  and add or subtract across ten by making ten.
- **Family and figure:** `counters`, with two ten frames side by side that hold counters of two groups, and a line
  under them that writes the addition or the subtraction. Counting on and back is shown as jumps on a number line,
  from the family `line`.
- **Interaction that suits the subject:** the reader drags counters into the frames and joins two groups, taps
  counters away, sets a group with a stepper and watches the written line follow, jumps a point along a line from a
  start the reader sets, and splits a group so that its first part fills the ten.
- **Exercises that suit the subject:** the engine draws a target or a start, the reader builds the groups, the
  jumps or the take away that reach it, and commits with Check. The totals are hidden, so the reader has to add or
  subtract.

## What the story takes from earlier stories

The user ruled on 2026-11-17 that the story teaches no earlier idea again, and that one page goes over the logic
of an idea where this story needs it. The story `number-line` is no need of this story.

- Counting on and back, from `counting`. One page of `counting-on` goes over it.
- The number line and its jumps. One page of `counting-on` says what the line and a jump are, for a reader who has
  not read `number-line`.
- Pairing two rows and how many more, from `more-and-less`. One page of `difference` goes over it.
- The pairs that make 10, the splits of a number below 10 and a full ten and some more, from `number-bonds`. One
  page of `making-ten` goes over them.

## Sections

Page folders take the numbers of the section's range, in tens. World pages and challenges sit after the sections,
in the ranges given.

### 010 `joining` Adding joins two groups

- **Pages:** 110 to 190. Opens the story at 110. Challenge at 720.
- **Needs:** counting a group, and a whole and its two parts.
- **Teaches:** joining puts two groups together, and the sum is how many there are altogether. The sign + stands
  between the two numbers, and the written line 5 + 3 = 8 is read "5 plus 3 is equal to 8". The sum grows by one
  when either number grows by one, and adding 0 leaves a number as it was.
- **Hands on:** the sign + and its reading plus, the written line under the figure, the words add and sum.
- **Arc:** the opening page raises the question on a real situation, two dice thrown for one move on a board, and
  joins the two groups with no sign. The sign + has the next page to itself. The sums stay within 10 until the
  section's last page. The challenge writes a drawn addition with no answer and no counters, and the reader sets
  its sum before the counters appear.

### 020 `taking-away` Subtracting takes a group away

- **Pages:** 210 to 290. World page at 610.
- **Needs:** counting a group, and the written line of an addition.
- **Teaches:** taking some counters away leaves fewer, and what is left is counted. The sign − stands between the
  first number and the number taken away, and the written line 9 − 4 = 5 is read "9 minus 4 is equal to 5". Taking
  away 0 leaves the number as it was, and taking all of them away leaves 0.
- **Hands on:** the sign − and its reading minus, the written line of a subtraction, the words subtract and left.
- **Arc:** the first page takes counters away with no sign, and the sign − has the next page to itself. The first
  subtractions stay within 10. The world page is a shop shelf of ten jars of jam, where the jars sold in the
  morning and in the afternoon add together and the jars left are 10 minus the jars sold. No challenge.

### 030 `counting-on` Counting on and back

- **Pages:** 310 to 390. Challenge at 710.
- **Needs:** the written lines of an addition and of a subtraction.
- **Teaches:** adding a small number is counting on that many from the first number, and subtracting 1, 2 or 3 is
  counting back that many. On a number line each count is one jump, drawn as an arc from one number to the next,
  to the right to add and to the left to subtract. The start is not counted, and the jumps are. The same jumps
  from another start land the same number of jumps on.
- **Hands on:** the number line, the mark, the point, the jump and the start.
- **Arc:** one page goes over counting on and back and says what the number line and a jump are, for a reader who
  has not read `number-line`. The challenge draws a start and a target, and the reader makes the jumps that take
  one to the other.

### 040 `difference` The difference

- **Pages:** 410 to 490.
- **Needs:** the written line of a subtraction, and counting on.
- **Teaches:** the difference between two numbers is written as a subtraction, the greater number minus the less,
  so one written line can say what is left or say a difference. Counting on from the less to the greater finds
  it, which is surer than counting back a long way.
- **Hands on:** subtraction as a difference beside subtraction as what is left. The story `fact-families` adds
  the missing part.
- **Arc:** two pages. The first goes over pairing two rows and how many more, and gives the reason for the written
  line: taking the paired counters of the greater row away leaves the counters without a partner, so the
  difference of 11 and 7 is 11 − 7. The second counts on along the number line. No world page and no challenge.

### 050 `making-ten` Making ten

- **Pages:** 510 to 590. World page at 620. Challenge at 730. Ends the story at 810.
- **Needs:** the written lines, the pairs that make 10, and the splits of a number below 10.
- **Teaches:** a sum such as 12 + 5 leaves the full ten as it is and adds to the rest. To add across ten, the
  second number splits into the part that fills the ten and the rest, so 8 + 5 is 8 + 2 = 10 and 10 + 3 = 13. To
  subtract across ten, the take away goes down to ten first, so 13 − 5 is 13 − 3 = 10 and 10 − 2 = 8. Each step
  is a written line of its own.
- **Hands on:** nothing inside this story. The end page names the stories that follow: `fact-families`,
  `adding-tens-and-ones`, `equals-sign` and `any-order`.
- **Arc:** one page goes over the pairs that make 10 and a full ten and some more, and adds to a full ten with no
  crossing. The world page is a seed tray of 20 cells in two rows of ten, where the reader sows a packet of
  seeds into the cells left free. The challenge draws an addition or a subtraction that crosses ten, and the
  reader sets its two steps, the part that reaches ten and the rest, before the counters move. It combines the
  written lines, the pairs that make 10 and making ten. The end page closes the story.

## Engine

The story takes two families. `counters` gets a new scene, `add`, for the ten frames, and a new field in `match`.
`line` gets two new fields and a goal. The written line is a new part of hesap's kit, which both families draw. The
story has no page of `bonds`, `count` or `order`.

### The written line (kit `written.js`, `new`)
Draws: a row of numbers and signs under a figure, such as 5 + 3 = 8 or 9 − 4 = 5, centred, at the size of the line
of two numbers in `match`. The signs are +, − (U+2212) and =, each with a space on either side, and the numbers
follow the page's language. Each number is written in the colour of its group in the figure and carries the link id
of the number it writes, so a link lights the number together with its part of the figure. The signs and the answer
are ink. Used by: `counters/add`, `counters/match`, `line/line`.

- A written line writes its calculation, and adds "= answer" once the figure shows the answer. Until then it reads
  "13 − 8" alone. Each scene below says when its figure shows the answer.
- A challenge page shows its written lines once its goal holds, and none before.
- Each step of making ten is a written line of its own, under the line of the calculation.
- Every written line has its reading aloud, "5 plus 3 is equal to 8", "9 minus 4 is equal to 5", and "13 minus 8"
  for a line without its answer. The kit holds the four strings with `{a}`, `{b}` and `{c}`, in English and Turkish.
- The kit's model half gives a line's words and its reading from the sign and the numbers. The build and the browser
  both use it, so a text and its live value agree. A text writes − as U+2212, as the figure does.
- A readout of a written line is named "Written line", and a readout of its reading "In words", as in `match`.

### counters/add (`new`)
Draws: two ten frames side by side, or one above the other on a narrow stage, as `bonds` draws them. The first group
is round red counters, which fill the cells in order from the first cell of the first frame. The second group is
square blue counters, which fill the cells after the first group, so the two groups differ by shape and by place as
well as by colour. On an addition page the second group can wait in a tray beside the frames, and the reader drags or
taps its counters in. On a take-away page the reader taps counters away. A counter taken away leaves an empty dashed
ring in its cell, as a counter taken in `match` does, so the counters at the start stay in sight, and a tap on the
ring puts the counter back. Steppers set the numbers, and the written line under the figure follows the counters in
the frames. The looks `shelf` and `tray` draw the two world pages. Used by: `joining` (its pages and 720),
`taking-away` (its pages and 610), `difference` (a take away beside a difference), `making-ten` (its pages, 620, 730
and the end page 810).

It is a new scene because `bonds` splits a fixed whole, with its fields `whole`, `swap`, `fill`, `cup` and `list`
and readouts named "Red" and "Blue", where this figure starts from two numbers and writes a calculation. It takes
`frame.js`, `counter.js`, `places.js`, `move.js`, `driver.js` and the trays of `figures.js` from the family, and adds
a square counter to `counter.js`. `first` and `second` are the numbers before and after the sign. On an addition
they are the two parts, as in `bonds`.

### What the audit changed

A story auditor read the built story, and three plans changed. Each plan holds its `new` parts in full.

- **Goal `find-sum` replaces `make-sum` (010, page 720).** The page writes a drawn addition open, such as 4 + 3,
  over empty frames. The reader sets the stepper "Sum" and presses Check, and the two groups join in the frames
  once the sum is right. `make-sum` goes with its strings and tests.
- **Goal `steps-to-ten` replaces `take-to-leave` (050, page 730).** The page writes a drawn addition or
  subtraction that crosses ten, open. The reader sets the steppers "To ten" and "The rest" and presses Check, and
  the counters move in the two steps once the split is right. `take-to-leave` goes with its fields, strings and
  tests.
- **The seed tray takes `waiting` and `split` (050, page 620).** A packet of seeds waits beside the tray, and a
  drop or a tap sows a seed in the row it lands on. The written line writes the whole packet, 7 + 5, and takes
  its answer and its steps once the packet is empty.
- **`pairs` lights the counters (040, page 410).** On `counters/match` the link `pairs` lights the two counters
  at the ends of each pair line as well as the line.
- **The second point stays right of the start (040, page 430).** On a page of `line` with `start` and
  `written: difference`, the second point stops one mark to the right of the start and the start one mark to the
  left of the second point, and `kitap check` fails a page whose `second` is at or left of its `start`.
- **Page 420 is dropped**, so section `difference` has no page of `counters/add`.
