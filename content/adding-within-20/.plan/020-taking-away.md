# Subtracting takes a group away

## Header

- Assumes: counting a group, the ten frame and its cells from `number-bonds`, and from section `joining` the two
  ten frames side by side, the written line of an addition such as 5 + 3 = 8 under the figure, the sign + read
  "plus", the reading "is equal to" for =, the words add and sum, and that adding 0 leaves a number as it was. The
  stepper from `more-and-less` and `joining`.
- Teaches: taking some objects away from a group leaves fewer, what is left is counted, and the written line
  9 − 4 = 5, read "9 minus 4 is equal to 5", writes the number at the start, the number taken away and what is left,
  so taking away 0 leaves the number as it was and taking all of them away leaves 0.
- Introduces:
  - Terms: take away (groups), left (groups) and "what is left", subtract (numbers), minus as the reading of the sign
    −, and the − halves of sign (calculation) and of addition, subtraction (calculations).
  - Controls: a tap on a counter that takes it away and leaves an empty dashed ring in its cell, and a tap on the
    ring that puts the counter back. The stepper "Taken away". The stepper "First number" on a take-away page, which
    puts every counter taken away back. On the world page, a tap on a jar that sells it, a tap on a jar in a crate
    that puts it back, and the segment "Morning" and "Afternoon" that picks the crate.
- Uses:
  - A bowl of 9 apples, 4 of them eaten, told as a story of first, then and now. The frames open with 9 counters
    and none taken. The reader takes 4 away and 5 are left (page 210).
  - 9 counters with 4 taken away, the written line 9 − 4 = 5, and the stepper "Taken away" from 1 to 8. One more
    taken away gives 9 − 5 = 4 (page 220).
  - 7 counters with none taken, 7 − 0 = 7, then all 7 taken, 7 − 7 = 0. The stepper "First number" from 1 to 20
    sets 15, with 15 − 0 = 15 and 15 − 15 = 0 (page 230).
  - A shop shelf of 10 jars of jam. 3 jars sold in the morning as the page opens, the reader sells 5 in the
    afternoon, and the written lines read 3 + 5 = 8 for the jars sold and 10 − 8 = 2 for the jars left. Selling
    every jar gives 10 − 10 = 0 (page 610).

As built after the audits: page 610 asks the reader to say the jars sold and the jars left before selling, and
then to sell 5 jars in the afternoon. With 4, the answers 7 and 3 were the two numbers the page opens with, 3 and
7, in swapped places. A tap on the shelf sells its last jar, and a tap on a crate puts its last jar back.

## Content

The section has three pages and the world page 610. The first two subtractions stay within 10, and page 230 takes
the first number up to 20. The text says "take away" of counters and jars, and "subtract" and "minus" of numbers. It
reads the sign − as "minus".

### 210 Take counters away and count what is left

The page tells a story in three parts. First there are 9 apples in a bowl. Then 4 apples are eaten. Now how many are
left? The counters in the frames stand for the apples. A tap on a counter takes it away, and an empty ring stays in
its cell, so the 9 at the start stay in sight. The text asks the reader to take 4 counters away and count the
counters left, which are 5. Taking some away leaves fewer than there were at the start. A tap on a ring puts its
counter back, so the reader can take a different number away and count again. The page writes no sign and no
written line.

Example: 9 counters, 4 taken away, 5 left.

### 220 Write a subtraction with a minus sign

The page writes the take away of 210 as a written line, as `joining` wrote 5 + 3 = 8 for two groups joined. The line
reads 9 − 4 = 5. The first number, 9, counts the counters and the rings together, which are the counters at the
start. The sign − stands after it, then the number taken away, 4, then = and the number left, 5. The line is read
"9 minus 4 is equal to 5". This is a subtraction, and it subtracts 4 from 9. The first number is the counters at the
start, so it comes first, and 4 − 9 says something else. The text then asks the reader to tap + on the stepper
"Taken away". Each counter taken away leaves one fewer, so the number after = goes down by one while the first
number stays 9: 9 − 5 = 4, then 9 − 6 = 3.

Example: 9 − 4 = 5, then 9 − 5 = 4 and 9 − 6 = 3.

### 230 Take away none, or take away all

The page asks what happens at the two ends. 7 counters stand in the frames with none taken, and the line reads
7 − 0 = 7. Taking away 0 leaves the number as it was, as adding 0 did in `joining`. The text asks the reader to tap
every counter away. The line reads 7 − 7 = 0, so taking all of them away leaves 0. Then the reader sets the
stepper "First number" to 15. Every counter comes back, the line reads 15 − 0 = 15, and taking all 15 away gives
15 − 15 = 0. Both rules hold for every number the stepper sets.

Example: 7 − 0 = 7 and 7 − 7 = 0, then 15 − 0 = 15 and 15 − 15 = 0.

### 610 The jam shelf

The world page. A shop shelf holds 10 jars of jam. In the morning 3 jars sold, and they stand in the crate
"Morning". The text asks the reader to pick "Afternoon" and sell 5 more jars. The jars sold in the morning and in the
afternoon add together, so the shop sold 3 + 5 = 8 jars. The jars left on the shelf are 10 minus the jars sold, so
10 − 8 = 2 jars are left. The text then asks how many jars the shop can still sell, and the reader sells jars until
the shelf is empty, where the line of the jars left reads 10 − 10 = 0.

Example: 3 + 5 = 8 sold and 10 − 8 = 2 left, then 10 − 10 = 0 with every jar sold.

## Interactivity

### 210 Take counters away and count what is left

- Shows: the two ten frames side by side, or one above the other on a narrow stage, with 9 round red counters in
  the first frame, none taken. The panel has the readouts "Taken away" at 0 and "Left" at 9. No written line.
- The reader taps a counter, or taps an empty ring.
- In reply a tapped counter leaves its cell and an empty dashed ring takes its place. A tapped ring takes its counter
  back. "Taken away" and "Left" follow.
- Action in general words: tap an object, and it is taken away and leaves an empty ring, and tap a ring to put the
  object back.

### 220 Write a subtraction with a minus sign

- Shows: the frames with 9 counters, of which the last 4 are taken away and show as rings, so 5 counters are left.
  Under the frames stands the written line 9 − 4 = 5. The stepper "Taken away" from 1 to 8 stands in the panel, and
  the readouts "Written line" at 9 − 4 = 5 and "In words" at "9 minus 4 is equal to 5". The counters take no taps.
- The reader taps + or − on the stepper "Taken away".
- In reply + turns the last counter left into a ring, and − turns the first ring from the end back into a counter.
  The written line and its reading follow, and the first number stays 9.
- Action in general words: step the number taken away, and the objects turn into rings from the end while the
  written line changes its second number and what is left.

### 230 Take away none, or take away all

- Shows: the frames with 7 counters and no rings, the written line 7 − 0 = 7, the stepper "First number" from 1 to
  20, and the readouts "Written line" and "In words".
- The reader taps counters and rings, and taps + or − on the stepper "First number".
- In reply a tap takes a counter away or puts it back as on 210, and the written line follows. A change of the
  stepper sets that many counters, filling the first frame before the second, and puts every counter taken away back,
  so the line reads the new number − 0 = the new number.
- Action in general words: set how many objects there are, then take none or every one of them away, and the
  written line ends in the number or in 0.

### 610 The jam shelf

- Shows: a shop shelf of 10 jars of jam with two crates under it, "Morning" holding 3 jars and "Afternoon" empty, so
  7 jars stand on the shelf. The panel holds the segment "Morning" and "Afternoon", on "Morning" as the page opens.
  Under the figure stand two written lines, 3 + 0 = 3 for the jars sold and 10 − 3 = 7 for the jars left, and the
  readouts show both lines.
- The reader picks a crate in the segment, taps a jar on the shelf, or taps a jar in a crate.
- In reply a jar tapped on the shelf moves into the crate the segment picks, and a jar tapped in a crate goes back
  on the shelf. Both written lines follow.
- Action in general words: sell objects into one of two groups, and one written line adds the two groups while the
  other subtracts their sum from 10.
