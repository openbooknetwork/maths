# Counting on and back

## Header

- Assumes: from sections `joining` and `taking-away`, the reader reads and writes the written lines 5 + 3 = 8 and
  9 − 4 = 5, reads + as plus and − as minus, and knows the words add, subtract, sum and left. From the story
  `counting`, the reader counts on and back from any number. The reader may not have met a number line.
- Teaches: adding a small number is counting on that many jumps from the first number, and subtracting 1, 2 or 3 is
  counting back that many, where each jump goes from one number to the next, to the right to add and to the left to
  subtract, the start is not counted and the jumps are, and the same jumps from another start land the same number
  of jumps on.
- Introduces:
  - Terms: number line, mark, point, jump, to the right and to the left, given again from `number-line` on page 310
    for a reader who has not read it. Start, new on page 320.
  - Controls: Jump right and Jump left on a number line, with the drag of the point (page 310). The start, a ring
    round a mark that holds the first number, with the written line under the number line that follows the jumps
    (page 320). The drag of the start, which carries the point and its jumps along (page 330).
- Uses:
  - A number line from 0 to 20 with the point on 14. Three jumps to the right count on 15, 16, 17, and five presses
    of Jump left take those jumps back and count back 13, 12. From 14 to 17 the point passes four marks and makes
    three jumps (page 310).
  - A number line from 0 to 20 with the start on 9. Three jumps to the right write 9 + 3 = 12, and three jumps to
    the left write 9 − 3 = 6. Before the first jump the written line reads 9 + 0 = 9 (page 320).
  - The same line opening on 9 + 3 = 12. The start dragged to 4 writes 4 + 3 = 7, to 15 writes 15 + 3 = 18, and
    stops at 17 with 17 + 3 = 20. Three jumps to the left from 15 write 15 − 3 = 12, and the start dragged to 8
    writes 8 − 3 = 5 (page 330).
  - The challenge draws a start from 2 to 18 and a number to add from 2 to 4 or to subtract from 1 to 3, such as
    7 + 4 or 12 − 3 (page 710).

The section has three pages and the challenge at 710. The pages 340 to 390 stay free.

## Content

### 310 Count on and back on a number line

The page goes over counting on and back and says what the number line and a jump are, for a reader who has not
read `number-line`. The figure is a number line from 0 to 20. Each number sits at a mark, a short stroke across the
line, and the numbers run in the order of the count from left to right, starting at 0. The marks are the same
distance apart. The point is the dot on the line, and it is on 14.

The text asks the reader to press Jump right three times. A jump moves the point to the mark next to it, and an arc
over the line shows the jump. The point lands on 15, then 16, then 17. Saying those numbers is counting on from 14,
and each number said is one jump to the right. From 14 to 17 the point passes four marks, 14, 15, 16 and 17, and
makes three jumps, so the reader counts the jumps and leaves out the mark the point began on.

Then the reader presses Jump left until the point is on 12. The first three presses take the jumps back, one each,
and the next two jump to the left, with their arcs under the line, to 13 and 12. That is counting back from 14. A
jump to the right reaches the number after, and a jump to the left reaches the number before. A drag of the point
to another mark clears the arcs, so the reader can count on or back from any number.

### 320 Count on to add, count back to subtract

The point stands on 9 inside a ring. The ring marks the start, the mark where the point stands before the first
jump. Under the number line the written line reads 9 + 0 = 9, because the point has made no jump yet. The text asks
the reader to press Jump right three times. With each jump the written line adds one: 9 + 1 = 10, 9 + 2 = 11,
9 + 3 = 12, read "9 plus 3 is equal to 12". The start is the first number of the addition, and the number of jumps
is the number added. To add 3 to 9, say the start, 9, and count on one number for each jump, 10, 11, 12. The start
is not a jump, and the reader does not count it.

Then the reader presses Jump left. Each press takes a jump back, and after three presses the point is on the start
and the written line reads 9 + 0 = 9 again. Three more presses jump to the left: 9 − 1 = 8, 9 − 2 = 7, 9 − 3 = 6,
read "9 minus 3 is equal to 6". To subtract 3 from 9, count back 3 from 9, 8, 7, 6. Jumps to the right add, and
jumps to the left subtract. The page ends by saying that counting on suits adding a small number, and counting back
suits subtracting 1, 2 or 3. A longer count back is easy to get wrong, and section `difference` finds such answers
by counting on.

### 330 The same jumps from a different start

The page opens on three jumps to the right from the start 9, and the written line reads 9 + 3 = 12. The text asks
the reader to drag the start to 4. The point and its three arcs move with it, and the written line reads
4 + 3 = 7. Dragged to 15, it reads 15 + 3 = 18. The jumps stay three wherever the start goes, so the point lands on
the number three after the start, and adding 3 gives the number 3 after the first number in the count. The start
stops at 17, where the written line reads 17 + 3 = 20, because the point would leave the line.

Then the reader drags the start back to 15 and presses Jump left six times, three to take the jumps back and three
to jump to the left. The written line reads 15 − 3 = 12. Dragged to 8, it reads 8 − 3 = 5. Three jumps to the left
land on the number three before the start, wherever the start is. The page ends on the rule for both directions:
the number of jumps is the number added or subtracted, and the start is the first number.

## Interactivity

### 310

- Shows: a number line from 0 to 20 with a number at every mark. The point sits on 14. The buttons Jump left and
  Jump right stand in the panel, with readouts of the number at the point, 14, and of the jumps, 0.
- The reader: presses Jump right and Jump left, and may drag the point to another mark.
- The figure: Jump right moves the point to the mark on its right and draws an arc over the line between the two
  marks. Jump left moves it to the mark on its left, and takes back the last arc when that jump went to the right,
  or draws a new arc under the line when the jumps go to the left. The jumps readout counts the arcs from the mark
  where the reader began to jump. A drag of the point snaps it to the nearest mark, clears the arcs and sets the
  jumps to 0. At 0 Jump left does nothing, and at 20 Jump right does nothing.
- Action: press a jump button, and the point moves to the next mark with an arc, and the jumps are counted.

### 320

- Shows: the number line from 0 to 20 with a number at every mark. The point sits on 9 inside a ring of the point's
  colour, larger than the point, the start. Under the line the written line reads 9 + 0 = 9. The panel holds Jump
  left and Jump right and a readout of the jumps, 0.
- The reader: presses Jump right and Jump left.
- The figure: the point jumps as on page 310 and the ring stays on 9. The written line writes the start, the sign
  of the direction of the jumps, the number of jumps and the number at the point. Jumps to the right write
  9 + 3 = 12, jumps to the left write 9 − 3 = 6, and the point on the start writes 9 + 0 = 9. The jumps readout
  follows. The point takes no drag on this page. The start takes a drag, which page 330 teaches, and the text does
  not ask for it here.
- Action: jump the point from a fixed start, and the written line writes the jumps as an addition or a subtraction.

### 330

- Shows: the number line from 0 to 20. The start ring on 9, the point on 12, and three arcs over the line from 9 to
  12. Under the line the written line reads 9 + 3 = 12. The panel holds Jump left and Jump right and readouts of the
  start, 9, and of the jumps, 3.
- The reader: drags the start ring along the line, and presses Jump left and Jump right.
- The figure: the start snaps to the nearest mark, and the point and its arcs move with it, so the jumps keep their
  number and their direction. The start stops where the point would pass an end of the line: at 17 for three jumps
  to the right, and at 3 for three jumps to the left. The written line changes its first number and its answer, and
  the number of jumps stays. The jump buttons work as on page 320. The start readout follows.
- Action: drag the start, and the point and its jumps move with it, the same number of jumps.

## Challenge

At 710, "Jump from the start".

- Goal: put the point on the answer of a drawn addition or subtraction by jumping from its first number, then press
  Check.
- Drawn each time: an addition or a subtraction, each as likely as the other. A start from 2 to 18, and a number to
  add from 2 to 4 or a number to subtract from 1 to 3, such that the answer is on the line. 7 + 4 and 12 − 3 are
  two draws. The amount is at least 1, so the page does not open with the goal held.
- How it is reached: the goal line names the start and the calculation without its answer, "Jump from 7 to find
  7 + 4, then press Check." The point opens on the start inside its ring, which stays where it is. The reader jumps
  the point with Jump left and Jump right and presses Check. The goal holds when the point is on the answer. A wrong
  Check says how the jumps are off: "The point jumped the wrong way." when the jumps go against the sign, else
  "Too few jumps." or "Too many jumps." A jump after Check takes the check back. Another one draws again.
- Hidden: the jumps readout, the number at the point as a readout, and the written line until the goal holds. Every
  mark keeps its number, which the reader reads once the jumps land. Once the goal holds the written line shows the
  calculation with its answer, such as 7 + 4 = 11.
- The idea used: + is counted on to the right and − counted back to the left, the start is the first number and is
  not counted, and the number of jumps is the number added or subtracted. The reader has to count the jumps, since
  nothing on the page counts them, and a reader who knows the answer can jump straight to it.

The brief's goal draws a target `apart: [2, 9]` from the start, shows it as a flag and asks "Make the jumps from 7
to 12". This plan changes that, for the reasons under Sources. The goal keeps its name `jump-to` and its field
`start`, and `add` and `subtract` take the place of `apart`.

## Sources

The files sit in `content/adding-within-20/.references/` unless the path starts with `.cache/`.

- Counting on starts from the first number and counts only the numbers added. The progression: "counting the 9
  things can be “taken as done” if we begin the count from 9", with "“Niiiiine, ten, eleven, twelve, thirteen.”"
  and the counts 1 to 4 under the numbers after 9, and "Elongating the first counting word (“Niiiiine...”) is
  natural and indicates that the student differentiates between the first addend and the counts for the second
  addend." Read in `isbe.net-Documents-counting-cardinality-k-5.txt`, page 15 of the draft. Page 320 says the start
  and counts one number for each jump, and the hint of 710 says the same.
- The start on the number line, and counting on and back from it. NCETM: "They can start at a fixed point on a
  number line and will either count on (augmentation) or back (reduction). Initially, they may move in smaller
  amounts". Read in `ncetm.org.uk-media-pkxnnz0r-pska-add-q2.txt`. Pages 320 and 330 draw the start as a ring that
  stays fixed while the point jumps, with jumps of one.
- Count the jumps and not the start. NRICH: "Make sure that children are counting the jumps they make, and not the
  starting point." Read in `nrich.maths.org-5652.txt`. The NRC report quoted by OISE: "They may count the starting
  point 0 and then be off by one, or they focus on the spaces and are confused by the location of the numbers at
  the ends of the spaces", and "lots of students just count the hash marks". Read in
  `oise.utoronto.ca-robertson-blog-power-number-line-model-scales-2023-06-04.txt`. Page 310 names four marks and
  three jumps from 14 to 17, page 320 writes 9 + 0 = 9 on the start, and the notes of 710 name too few and too many
  jumps.
- Jumps backwards on a line that includes 0. Oxford works 7 − 4 "backwards on a number line" with the hops −1 to
  −4, says children "hop backwards down the numbers", and advises "Always include 0 on your number line." Read in
  `cdn.oxfordowl.co.uk-2014-05-13-10-29-46-467-PX-MathsContent-BK-SubtractionInSchool-01-CH.txt`. Every page draws
  the line from 0, and counting back is jumps to the left.
- Small numbers to count on and back. US grade 1: "Relate counting to addition and subtraction (e.g., by counting on
  2 to add 2)." Read in `.cache/curricula/us-common-core-math.pdf`, page 15, 1.OA.C.5. IM: "There is an emphasis on
  adding and subtracting 1 or 2 to encourage students to count on or count back" and "being able to count on up to
  3 and make 10 are helpful steps toward fluency". Read in
  `curriculum.illustrativemathematics.org-k5-teachers-grade-1-course-guide-scope-and-sequence.txt`, Unit 1 Section A
  and Unit 3 Section A. The pages count 3 jumps, and the challenge adds 2 to 4 and subtracts 1 to 3.
- Counting back kept to 1, 2 or 3. The progression: "Many children attempt to count down for subtraction, but
  counting down is difficult and error-prone." Read in `isbe.net-Documents-counting-cardinality-k-5.txt`, page 36,
  Appendix. With the brief's decision, page 320 says that a longer count back is easy to get wrong and points to
  section `difference`.
- The same jumps from a different start. NCETM topic 1.7: "Adding one gives one more; subtracting one gives one less."
  and "Adding two to an odd number gives the next odd number". Read in
  `ncetm.org.uk-classroom-resources-primm-107-addition-and-subtraction-strategies-within-10.txt`. These hold the
  number added and change the start, as page 330 does with 3. The drag of the start that shows it is the plan's
  own.
- The line from 0 to 20 for a first reader, with a jump drawn from one number to the next. NRICH uses "a 0-10 number
  line" and extends to "a number line which goes to up to 20". Read in `nrich.maths.org-5652.txt`. The brief's line

- What page 310 says of the line and the jump follows `content/number-line/.plan/010-in-order.md`, pages 120 and

The plan's own choices:

- Counting on and counting back share page 320. Their actions are the same, jumps from a fixed start with the
  written line following, and only the direction differs, so the rule that merges pages with the same action makes
  them one page. The three jumps each way, 9 + 3 = 12 and 9 − 3 = 6, show the same jumps adding and subtracting.
- Page 310 has no start ring and no written line. It goes over the line, the jump and counting on and back, and
  page 320 adds the start and the written line, so each page has its own thing to do.
- The numbers 14, 9, 3, 4, 15, 17 and 8 are the plan's own. 9 is the brief's engine example, and 14 keeps page 310
  apart from 320. Page 310 counts back two after taking three jumps back, so the reader sees a press against the
  last jump take it back.
- The challenge departs from the brief's `jump-to`. With the target shown as a flag, a reader reaches it by
  pressing a jump button until the point stands on the flag, which needs no knowing, and the spec asks that "trial
  alone does not get there". Its `apart: [2, 9]` to the left counts back up to 9, against the brief's decision that
  counting back subtracts 1, 2 or 3, which rests on the progression's quote above. A target named as a calculation
  makes the reader count the jumps from the start in the direction of the sign, which is the section's idea. The
  ranges, start from 2 to 18, add 2 to 4, subtract 1 to 3, are the plan's own within the sources' small numbers.
  The progression's example counts on 4, which sets the top of `add`.
- The three notes of a wrong Check, the wrong way, too few and too many jumps, say in which direction the answer is
  off in the words of the section, where the left and right notes of `place` would not say what to change.
