When you pair up two rows of counters, the number of counters left over is the difference between the rows. For two numbers, the difference is how much greater one is than the other. You write it as a subtraction, with the greater number first.

Take a row of 11 counters and a row of 7. The 7 pair up with 7 of the 11, and 4 are left over. If you take the 7 paired counters away from the 11, those 4 are what is left. That is why the difference is the subtraction `11 − 7 = 4`. The greater number comes first because the paired counters are taken away from the row with more.

The number sentence `11 − 7 = 4` answers two kinds of question.

- "There are 11 apples in a bowl, and 7 are eaten. How many are left?" Taking 7 apples away from 11 leaves 4.
- "There are 11 apples and 7 pears. How many more apples are there than pears?" Each pear pairs with an apple, and 4 apples are left over, so there are 4 more apples than pears.

The second question says "more", but adding 11 and 7 would give 18, which counts all the fruit.

On the figure, change the bottom row.

- Press + on the "Bottom row" stepper. Each press makes the difference 1 less, down to `11 − 11 = 0` when both rows have 11.
- Press + once more, and the bottom row has more. You cannot take 12 counters away from 11, so the number sentence turns round to `12 − 11 = 1`.
