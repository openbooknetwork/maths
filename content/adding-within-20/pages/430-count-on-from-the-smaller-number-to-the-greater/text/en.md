You can also find a difference by counting on. Start on the smaller number and count up to the greater one. The difference is how many numbers you say, because each number you say is one more than the last. On a number line, each number you say is one jump to the right, and the start is not counted. For a subtraction, you start on the number after the − sign and count the jumps up to the number before it.

Take `13 − 8`. Start on 8 and say 9, 10, 11, 12, 13. That is five jumps, so `13 − 8 = 5`. If you count back 8 from 13 instead, you say eight numbers going down. Saying numbers backwards is harder than saying them forwards, and it is easy to lose count.

The ring round 8 is the start. The square second point is on 13, and under the line the number sentence reads [13 − 8](@written). It gets its answer when the point lands on the second point.

- Press "Jump right" and say a number at each jump.
- Drag the square second point to 15 and jump on until the point lands on it. The number sentence reads `15 − 8 = 7`, because the point is 7 jumps from the start.
