You can also find a difference by counting on. Start on the smaller number and count on until you reach the greater one. Each number you say adds 1, so how many numbers you say is how much you add to the smaller number to reach the greater one. That is the difference. On a number line, each number you say is one jump to the right, and the start is not counted. For a subtraction, you start on the number after the − sign and count the jumps to the number before it.

Take `13 − 8`. Start on 8 and say 9, 10, 11, 12, 13. That is five jumps, so `13 − 8 = 5`. If you count back 8 from 13 instead, you say eight numbers going down. Saying numbers backwards is harder than saying them forwards, and it is easy to lose count.

The point starts on 8, inside the start ring, and the square second point starts on 13. Under the line, the number sentence reads [13 − 8](@written), and it gets its answer when the point reaches the second point.

- Press "Jump right" and say a number at each jump. When the point reaches 13, the Jumps readout shows 5 and the number sentence reads `13 − 8 = 5`.
- Drag the square second point to 15 and jump on until the point reaches it. The number sentence reads `15 − 8 = 7`, because the point is 7 jumps from the start.
