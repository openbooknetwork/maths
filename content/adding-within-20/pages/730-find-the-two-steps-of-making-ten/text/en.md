Your goal is to find the two steps of making ten for a calculation that crosses ten. You split its second number into two parts. The first part takes the first number to 10, and the other part is the rest.

- In an addition, the first part makes ten with the first number.
- In a subtraction, the first part is the number of counters after the full ten, because taking them away leaves 10.

Take `9 + 3`. 9 and 1 make 10, so 3 splits into 1 and 2, and the steps are `9 + 1 = 10` and `10 + 2 = 12`. For `12 − 3`, 12 is a full ten and 2 more. So 3 splits into 2 and 1, and you work out `12 − 2 = 10`, then `10 − 1 = 9`.

The counters do not move until your split is right.

- Set the first part on the "Makes ten" stepper ("Down to ten" in a subtraction). Set the other part on "The rest".
- Press Check. If the split is wrong, a note says whether your first step misses 10 or your two parts do not make the second number.
- When the tick appears, the counters move in the two steps. Press "Another one" for a new calculation.
