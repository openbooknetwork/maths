A subtraction crosses ten when its first number is greater than 10 and the number left is less than 10. You work it out by going down to ten first. You split the number you take away into two parts. The first part is the number of counters after the full ten, so taking it away leaves 10. Then you take the rest from the full ten.

Take `13 − 5`. 13 is a full ten and 3 more, so you split 5 into 3 and 2. Taking away the 3 leaves the full ten, so the first step is `13 − 3 = 10`. Then you take the other 2 from the full ten, and `10 − 2 = 8` because 8 and 2 are a pair that makes 10. You can say the steps aloud as you go: "First I took away 3 counters to make ten. Then I took away 2 more to make 8."

The frames hold 13 counters.

- Tap a counter to take it away, and tap its dashed ring to put it back.
- Take the 3 counters in the second frame first, then 2 from the full ten. The two steps appear under the number sentence, with 3 and 2 on the "Down to ten" and "The rest" readouts.
- If you take a counter from the full ten while the second frame still has counters, no steps appear, because you have not gone down to ten first. Empty the second frame, and they appear.
- Set the "First number" stepper to 15 and take 7 away. The second frame now has 5 counters, so this time 7 splits into 5 and 2.
