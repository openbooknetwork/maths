You can write taking away as a number sentence. You write how many there were at the start, then the − sign, then the number taken away. After the = sign comes the number that is left. The − sign is read "minus". A calculation with −, such as `9 − 4`, is called a subtraction. Taking 4 away from 9 is called subtracting 4 from 9.

Think of a bowl of 9 apples where 4 were eaten and 5 were left. You write that as `9 − 4 = 5`, and you read it as "9 minus 4 is equal to 5". The number you start with comes first, because `4 − 9` would mean eating 9 apples from a bowl that has only 4.

The frames show the bowl, with a counter for each apple. The last 4 counters have been taken away, so their cells hold empty rings. The first number, 9, counts the counters and the rings together, because that is how many there were at the start. Under the frames, the number sentence reads [9 − 4 = 5](@written).

Press + on the "Taken away" stepper. One more counter turns into a ring, so the number after − goes up by one and the number after = goes down by one. The first number stays 9.
