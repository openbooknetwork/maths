When you join two groups, you can write it down as a number sentence. First comes the number in the first group, then the + sign, then the number in the second group. The + sign shows that the two groups are joined, and you say "plus" for it. Last come the = sign and how many there are altogether.

A group of 5 joined with a group of 3 makes a group of 8. You write that as `5 + 3 = 8`, with 5 as the first number and 3 as the second number. `5 + 3` and 8 are equal, so the = sign goes between them, just as it did when you compared numbers. You read it "5 plus 3 is equal to 8".

The figure has 5 round red counters and 3 square blue counters in the frames, and 2 more blue counters in the tray. Under the frames is the number sentence [5 + 3 = 8](@written). The "In words" readout writes it as [5 plus 3 is equal to 8](@reading).

- Drag or tap a counter in the tray to join it. The second number and the number after = both go up by 1.
- Tap a blue counter in the frames to send it back. Both numbers go down by 1.
