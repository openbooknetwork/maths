# Terms of The number line

## 010 in-order
- Introduces:
  - `number line (figure)`: a straight line with marks, where each mark stands for one number and the numbers go in
    the order of the count from left to right. IM, Oak and England all give "number line". After a page names it,
    "the line" refers back to it.
  - `mark (number line)`: a short stroke across the line at the place of one number. England and Oak say "marks",
    chosen over IM's "tick mark" as the shorter word the brief also uses.
  - `label (number line)`: the number written at a mark. IM gives "labeled tick marks".
  - `point (number line)`: the dot the reader moves along the line, which sits at one number. IM gives "A dot,
    called a point".
  - `gap (number line)`: the piece of the line between two marks. The brief's word, chosen over IM's "space" and
    Oak's "interval" because "interval" has a later sense in the book.
  - `equal (gaps)`: two gaps are equal when they have the same length. The gaps between marks next to each other on
    a number line are equal. Oak gives "equally spaced" and IM "equal spacing".
  - `jump (number line)`: a move of the point along the line from one mark to another, drawn as an arc. In this
    story each jump goes to the mark next to it and crosses one gap. Oak gives the reader "jumps of ten", chosen over
    Teppo's "hop" because the later stories of the line say "jump".
  - `to the right, to the left (number line)`: toward the numbers that come later in the count, and toward the ones
    that come earlier. Of two points, the one further right is at the greater number. IM gives "to the left" and "to
    the right".
  - `greater (numbers)`, `less (numbers)`: the glossary senses as written, first taught in `more-and-less`. `in-order`
    teaches them again, since `number-line` needs only `counting`: the greater of two numbers is the one further
    along the count, so on the line it is further to the right, and the other is less. The story says "is greater
    than" and "is less than". IM gives "the number farther to the right represents a greater value than the number
    to the left" (`content/number-line/.references/curriculum.illustrativemathematics.org-k5-teachers-grade-2-unit-4-lesson-4-lesson.txt`).
  - `the number before, the number after (numbers)`: the glossary sense as written, first taught in
    `more-and-less`. `in-order` teaches it again, since `number-line` needs only `counting`. On the line they are one
    jump to the left and one jump to the right.
  - `in order (numbers)`: the glossary sense as written, first taught in `more-and-less`. `in-order` teaches it
    again, and the text puts it as "in the order of the count", because "least" and "greatest" are outside the
    story's level.
- Uses: `count on, count back (numbers)`

## 020 steps
- Introduces:
  - `step (number line)`: the number that a jump to the next mark on the right adds, the same all along one line.
    Two labels next to each other give it. The brief's word, which Bremen also gives as "Schritte".
  - `count in tens, fives, twos (number line)`: a line counts in tens when its step is 10, and so on for the others.
    England gives "count in twos, fives and tens", chosen over IM's "count by" as the British form. The term lists
    them in the order the section teaches them.
  - `long mark, short mark (number line)`: a long mark carries a label and a short mark has none. The brief's words.
- Uses: `number line (figure)`, `mark (number line)`, `label (number line)`, `point (number line)`,
  `gap (number line)`, `jump (number line)`, `to the right, to the left (number line)`, `ten (numbers)`,
  `count on, count back (numbers)`

## 030 part-of-the-line
- Introduces:
  - `end (number line)`: the first or the last mark of the line as drawn, called the left end and the right end.
    England gives "both ends of the number line".
  - `the ten before, the ten after (numbers)`: of a number that is not a ten, the ten that comes just before it in
    the count and the ten just after it. For the numbers 1 to 9 the ten before is 0. England's Year 2 says "previous
    and next multiple of 10" and Oak "previous" and "next". The form follows the glossary's "the number before, the
    number after", which keeps Oak's point of avoiding "more" and "less".
  - `label card (figure)`: a card with a number on it that the reader drags onto a mark to label it. The brief's
    words.
- Uses: `number line (figure)`, `mark (number line)`, `label (number line)`, `gap (number line)`,
  `step (number line)`, `jump (number line)`, `ten (numbers)`, `the number before, the number after (numbers)`,
  `count on, count back (numbers)`

## 040 placing
- Introduces:
  - `middle (number line)`: the place halfway between two numbers on the line. The middle of a line is halfway
    between its ends, so 50 is the middle of a line from 0 to 100, and 45 is the middle of the gap from 40 to 50.
    Oak and England give "midpoint" and "halfway", and Oak defines midpoint as "the middle of something". "Middle"
    is chosen because "midpoint" is a later term of geometry.
- Uses: `number line (figure)`, `end (number line)`, `mark (number line)`, `label (number line)`,
  `point (number line)`, `step (number line)`, `gap (number line)`, `count on, count back (numbers)`

## 050 estimating
- Introduces:
  - `unmarked number line (number line)`: a number line with no marks between its two ends, which carry the only
    labels. England's Year 1 guidance gives "identify or place numbers up to 20 on marked and unmarked number lines"
    (`content/number-line/.references/assets.publishing.service.gov.uk-ads-system-uploads-attachment-data-file-954511-Maths-guidance-year-1.txt`),
    and Oak "unmarked number line". Chosen in full over the rough map's "bare line", because it says what the line
    lacks in the story's own word "mark".
  - `estimate (number line)`: as a noun, a place judged for a number, or a number judged for a point, from its
    distance to the ends and the middle. As a verb, to find one. An estimate is close to the place the marks would
    give. England gives "estimate the value/position" and Oak "Estimate".
  - `estimate mark (figure)`: the pale mark that moves to the place of the number the reader steps to. Named by
    what it shows, over the rough map's "ghost mark".
- Uses: `number line (figure)`, `end (number line)`, `middle (number line)`, `point (number line)`,
  `label (number line)`, `mark (number line)`, `equal (gaps)`

## Rules
- A mark stands for a number, and a gap lies between two marks. A jump crosses a gap, so the jumps from 0 to 5 are
  five while the marks from 0 to 5 are six. To find how far, count the jumps.
- The step is a number, and the gap is a piece of the line. A line that counts in tens has gaps the same length as
  one that counts in ones, and a jump on it adds ten.
- The mark is fixed on the line, the label is the number written at it, and the point is the dot the reader moves.
- The text says "the point is on 13" or "the number at the point". "The number of" says how many, as in "the number
  of counters".
- The story says "is greater than" and "is less than" of two numbers, and explains greater as further along the
  count, since it does not need `more-and-less`. "Comes later in the count" explains the word and does not stand in
  for it.
- The story says "the number after" and "the number before", as the glossary does, and keeps "next" out of them.
- The place that Show reveals is "where the number is". "True" has the glossary sense of a number sentence.
- "The line" in this story is the number line. A comparison written down, such as 3 < 5, is a number sentence.
- The term is "middle". "Halfway" appears in its meaning, and "midpoint" is left to a later story of geometry.
- The number the reader steps to on an unmarked number line is the reader's estimate, and the text calls it so in place of
  a guess.
- The term is "unmarked number line" for the line with no marks between its ends, and "number line" for any line of
  this story.
- Left to later stories:
  - `skip-counting` introduces "skip counting" and jumps of 2, 5 or 10 across several marks, which fit the sense
    of `jump (number line)`.
  - `tens-and-ones` introduces "ten" as a group of ten ones, a sense apart from `ten (numbers)`.
  - `rounding-and-estimating` decides "halfway". Its rough map says "the ten below" and "the ten above", where the
    book's form is `the ten before, the ten after (numbers)`.
  - `adding-within-20` calls a difference "the gap" in its rough map. "Gap" is said of the line, and the number left
    over is the `difference (groups)`.
  - `fractions-on-a-line` says "tick" in its rough map, where the book's word is "mark".
