# Marks that count in steps

## Header

- Assumes: from the story `counting`, the reader counts to 100 and knows the tens as the ends of the rows of ten:
  10, 20, 30 and on to 100. From section `in-order` the reader knows the number line, the mark, the label, the
  point, the gap, the jump, to the right and to the left, and the + and − buttons that jump the point one gap. The
  reader knows that a jump to the right goes to the number after, and that the jumps from 0 to a number are as many
  as the number.
- Teaches: two labels next to each other give the step, the number that each jump adds, and on a line where only
  the tens carry labels, a number between two tens sits on a short mark between them.
- Introduces:
  - Terms: step, count in tens, fives, twos, long mark and short mark.
  - Controls: the step picker, a row of four buttons 1, 2, 5 and 10 under the figure, which changes every label of
    the line and keeps the gaps. The lens above the point, which shows the marks around the point at full size
    while the reader drags or jumps it on a line from 0 to 100 in ones.
- Uses:
  - A line of 10 gaps that counts in tens from 0 to 100. The reader jumps the point from 0 three times, to 30, and
    ten jumps reach 100. Then the reader picks the step 5, and the line counts 0, 5, 10 and on to 50, with the
    point on 15. With the step 2 the line reaches 20 and the point is on 6. With the step 1 the line reaches 10
    and the point is on 3 (page 210).
  - A line from 0 to 100 in ones, with long labelled marks at the tens and short marks between. The point starts on
    30, the reader jumps it on to 37, then on to 40 in ten jumps from 30. Then the reader drags it to 80 and jumps
    back once to 79 (page 220).
  - The challenge draws a target from 11 to 99 that is no ten, on the line of page 220 (page 710).

The section has two teaching pages. Page 210 works on lines whose every mark carries a label, and page 220 on a
line whose short marks carry none. The pages 230 to 290 stay free.

As built: the buttons read "− Jump left" and "Jump right +", and the texts name them Jump left and Jump right, where
this plan writes − and +. The lens shows while the reader drags the point and for 1.6 seconds after a jump.

## Content

### 210 Every jump adds the same step

The page opens on a line from 0 to 100 with a label at every mark: 0, 10, 20 and on to 100. The point is on 0.
The text asks the reader to jump the point to the right. One jump reaches 10, two reach 20, three reach 30. The
labels are the count in tens that the reader knows from the rows of ten. Each jump adds ten, and ten jumps reach
100. The number that each jump adds is the step of the line. Two labels next to each other give it: 20 and 30
are ten apart, since counting on ten from 20 reaches 30, so the step is 10 and the line counts in tens.

Then the text asks the reader to pick the step 5. Every label changes, and the line now counts 0, 5, 10, 15 and on
to 50. The gaps keep their length, and the point stays on its mark with its three jumps, so it is now on 15. The
labels 15 and 20 sit next to each other and counting on five from 15 reaches 20, so the step is 5 and the line counts in fives. The step
2 makes the line count in twos, from 0 to 20, with the point on 6. The step 1 gives the line of the first section,
which counts in ones from 0 to 10, and the point is on 3.

The page ends on the rule: the step is a number, and the gap is a piece of the line. The gaps are equal whatever
the step, and a jump adds one step.

### 220 Short marks between the tens

The line runs from 0 to 100 and counts in ones, with a mark for every number. Only 0 and the tens carry labels, so a
label sits on a long mark and the marks between are short marks with no label. The point is on 30.

The text asks the reader to jump the point to the right seven times. The point is on 37, the seventh short mark
after 30. Three more jumps reach 40, so ten jumps lie between two long marks next to each other, and each jump
adds one. The nine short marks between 30 and 40 are the numbers 31 to 39.

Then the text asks the reader to drag the point onto the long mark of 80 and to jump once to the left. The point is
on 79, the number before 80, on the short mark just left of the long mark. The page ends on the way to find any
number between two tens: find the two long marks it sits between, then count jumps on from the long mark on its
left, or back from the long mark on its right when that one is nearer. Counting every mark from 0 also gets there,
and the labels make the count short.

### 710 Put the point on the number

The page shows the line of page 220 and asks for a number from 11 to 99 that is no ten, such as 64. The text says
how to find it: 64 sits between the long marks of 60 and 70, so the point goes four jumps to the right of 60. The
reader presses Check when the point is on the number.

## Interactivity

### 210

- Shows: a number line of 10 equal gaps with a long labelled mark at each of its 11 marks, 0 to 100, and the point
  on 0. Under the figure stand the step picker, labelled "Step" with the buttons 1, 2, 5 and 10 and 10 picked, and
  the − (Jump left) and + (Jump right) buttons. The readouts "Jumps" and "Point" show 0 and 0.
- The reader: presses + and −, and picks a step.
- The figure: on a press of + or − the point moves one gap and the jump is drawn as an arc, and the readouts follow.
  On a pick of a step every label changes to its count in the new step, the last label becomes ten times the step,
  and the gaps keep their length. The point stays on its mark and the arcs stay over their gaps, so "Jumps" keeps
  its value and "Point" shows the number of the new label under the point.
- Action: pick the step, and every label changes while the gaps, the point and its jumps stay in place.

### 220

- Shows: a number line from 0 to 100 with a mark at every number. The marks of the tens are long and carry their
  labels, and the other marks are short. The point is on the long mark of 30. On a phone the gaps come out about
  3.5px wide, so while the reader drags or jumps the point a lens above it shows about ten gaps around the point at
  full size, with the long and short marks and the labels of the tens there. The − and + buttons stand under the
  figure. The readouts "Point" and "Jumps" show 30 and 0.
- The reader: presses + and − to jump the point one mark at a time, and drags the point.
- The figure: a jump moves the point one gap, draws the arc and counts it in "Jumps". A drag snaps the point to the
  nearest mark, short or long, and clears the arcs. "Point" shows the number at the point, on a short mark as on a
  long one.
- Action: jump from a labelled mark to a mark without a label, and read its number from the count of jumps.

### 710

- Shows: the line of page 220, with the point on 0 and no number at the point. The goal line names the target. The
  − and + buttons, a Check button and an "Another one" button stand under the figure. No readouts.
- The reader: drags and jumps the point, then presses Check.
- The figure: moves the point as on page 220, with the lens on a phone. Check shows the tick, or a note that says
  whether the point is to the left or to the right of the number. A move of the point after Check takes the tick or
  the note back.
- Action: put the point on a number between two labelled marks, then commit.

## Challenge

Put the point on the number, at page 710.

- Goal: the point is on the target when the reader presses Check.
- Drawn each time: a target from 11 to 99 that is no ten. The line is fixed: 0 to 100 in ones with labels at the
  tens, the point on 0.
- Reached by: finding the two long marks the target sits between, then dragging the point near them and jumping it
  on or back, as on page 220. Check commits the answer.
- Hidden: the number at the point and the count of jumps. The page has no readouts and no flag. The lens shows the
  marks and the labels of the tens, as the line has them.
- Wrong answer: "The point is to the left of the number." or "The point is to the right of the number."
- Hint: "Find the two long marks the number sits between. Count jumps on from the one on the left, or back from the
  one on the right."
- When the goal holds: the point shows its number in a flag above it, and the goal line says it is on the number. A
  move of the point hides the number.
- Idea used: a number between two tens sits on a short mark between their long marks. A reader who counts every
  mark from 0 gets there slowly and can slip, and the labels make the count short.

## Contract

The section uses the one scene of the family `line`, which the brief's "Engine" part defines, and writes
`scene: line`. The folder name without its number is the page's id.

### Fields this section adds to the scene

The brief's build order gives these to 020: `step` with its segment, `labels` as a number, long and short marks,
the lens, and the goal `place`. Two behaviours the brief leaves open are fixed here:

| Field | What it draws and does |
|---|---|
| `step` as a segment | The picker is labelled "Step" and shows one button for each value of `segment`, the value of `start` picked. A pick keeps the number of gaps and the length of each gap, labels every mark with `from` plus its count of gaps times the new step, and moves the `to` link to the new right end. The point keeps its mark, and the arcs of `jump` stay over their gaps, so the `jumps` link keeps its value and the `point` link reads the new label under the point. |
| goal `place`, the reveal on the tick `new` | While the goal holds, the point shows its number in a flag above it, as `flag` draws it. A move of the point hides the number with the tick. |

Words the scene needs in `i18n/en.yaml` for this section: the picker label "Step", the goal line "Put the point on
{target}, then press Check.", the notes "The point is to the left of the number." and "The point is to the right
of the number.", and the hint of the Challenge part. The readout labels "Point" and "Jumps" and the button names
"Jump left" and "Jump right" come from `in-order`.

### Pages

#### `210-every-jump-adds-the-same-step`

- Id: `every-jump-adds-the-same-step`
- Title: "Every jump adds the same step"
- Links: `step` starts at 10, `to` at 100, `point` at 0, `jumps` at 0. After the three jumps of the text, `point`
  reads 30 and `jumps` 3. With the step 5 `to` reads 50 and `point` 15, with the step 2 `to` reads 20 and `point`
  6, and with the step 1 `to` reads 10 and `point` 3. `point` is `jumps` times `step` in every state.

```yaml
hesap:
  scene: line
  to: 100
  step: { segment: [1, 2, 5, 10], start: 10 }
  jump: true
  readout: [jumps, point]
```

#### `710-put-the-point-on-the-number`

- Id: `put-the-point-on-the-number`
- Title: "Put the point on the number"
- Links: none. A challenge page draws its target as it opens.

```yaml
title: "Put the point on the number"
hesap:
  scene: line
  from: 0
  to: 100
```

- Choices that are the plan's own:
