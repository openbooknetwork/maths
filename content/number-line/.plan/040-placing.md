# Placing a number

## Header

- Assumes: from section `in-order`, the reader knows the number line, the mark, the label, the point that snaps to
  the marks when dragged, the gap, and the jump with the − and + buttons, whose arcs count the gaps from the mark
  where the jumps began. From section `steps`, the reader knows long marks with labels beside short marks without,
  and the lens that shows the gaps at full size on a line from 0 to 100 in ones. From section `part-of-the-line`,
  the reader knows the two ends of a line and finds a number by counting gaps from a label. From the story
  `counting`, the reader counts on and back to 100.
- Teaches: on a line with few labels, a number is found by counting gaps from the guide nearest to it, an end of
  the line or its middle, and the middle of a line from 0 to 100 is 50.
- Introduces:
  - Terms: middle.
  - Controls: the Find readout, which names the number to place. The Show button, which shows where the number is
    and writes the number at the point on the point. The Next button, which gives the next number to place. The
    Middle button, which draws the long mark of the middle and takes it away. The look `street`, with houses at
    the marks, numbers on some doors and a van as the point.
- Uses:
  - A line from 0 to 20 with a mark for every number and labels at 0 and 20 only. The numbers to place are 3, 19
    and 14 (page 410).
  - A line from 0 to 100 with a mark for every number and labels at 0 and 100 only. The Middle button draws 50.
    The numbers to place are 48, 53 and 96 (page 420).
  - A street of houses numbered 1 to 60 in order along one side, with numbers on the doors of 1, 20, 50 and 60.
    The van goes to house 46, then 22, then 57 (page 610).

## Content

### 410 Count from the nearer end

A line from 0 to 20 has a mark for every number, but only its two ends carry labels. The Find readout names a
number, 3. Three is near 0, so the reader counts three gaps on from 0 and puts the point there. Show then shows
where 3 is, and the point shows the number it is on. Next asks for 19. From 0 that is nineteen jumps, but 19 comes
just before 20, so one jump back from 20 finds it. The text asks the reader to drag the point to 20 and jump back
once. Next asks for 14. From 0 it is fourteen jumps and from 20 it is six: 19, 18, 17, 16, 15, 14. The page ends
on the rule: count from the end that is nearer to the number, because there are fewer gaps to count.

### 420 50 is halfway between 0 and 100

A line from 0 to 100 has a mark for every number and labels at 0 and 100 only. The Find readout asks for 48. From
0 that is 48 jumps and from 100 it is 52, too many to count. The text asks the reader to press Middle. A long mark
with the label 50 appears halfway between the ends, with as many gaps from 0 to 50 as from 50 to 100. 48 comes
just before 50, so the reader drags the point to the middle and jumps back two gaps, then presses Show. Next asks
for 53, three jumps on from 50. Next asks for 96, which is near the right end, so four jumps back from 100 find it.
The page ends on the rule: a line has three guides, its two ends and its middle, and the reader counts from the
guide nearest to the number.

### 610 The delivery van

A van brings parcels to a street of 60 houses numbered in order along one side, from 1 to 60. Only four doors
show their numbers: 1, 20, 50 and 60. The first parcel is for house 46. The nearest door with a number is 50, and 46
comes four houses before it, so the driver counts back four houses from 50. The reader drives the van there and
presses Show to see which house is 46. Next gives a parcel for 22, two houses on from 20, and then one for 57,
three houses back from 60. The page says that a driver who knows a few house numbers finds every other house by
counting from the nearest one.

## Interactivity

### 410

- Shows: a number line from 0 to 20 with 21 marks. The marks at 0 and 20 are long and carry their labels, and the
  19 marks between are short. The point sits on 0. The Find readout shows 3 and the Jumps readout shows 0. The
  − (Jump left) and + (Jump right) buttons and the Show button stand in the panel.
- The reader: drags the point onto a mark or jumps it with − and +, then presses Show. After Show the reader
  presses Next.
- The figure: the point snaps to the nearest mark and each jump draws its arc. Show puts a flag at the place of
  the number to find and writes the number at the point on the point. When the point is on that place, the flag
  stands over the point. A move of the point takes the flag and the written number away. Next gives the next
  number of the list, 19 and then 14, in the Find readout and clears the reveal.
- Action: place the point where you think a number is, then press Show to see where the number is.

### 420

- Shows: a number line from 0 to 100 with 101 marks. The marks at 0 and 100 are long and carry their labels, and
  the marks between are short. The point sits on 0. The Find readout shows 48 and the Jumps readout shows 0. The
  Middle button stands in the panel beside the − and + buttons and Show. While the reader drags or jumps the point,
  the lens above it shows about ten gaps at full size with the marks and labels the line has there.
- The reader: presses Middle, drags the point to the middle mark, jumps it, and presses Show and then Next.
- The figure: a press of Middle draws the long mark of 50 with its label, halfway between the ends, and a second
  press takes it away. The lens shows the mark of 50 with its label while the point is near it. The point, the
  jumps, Show and Next behave as on page 410. Next gives 53 and then 96.
- Action: press a button, and a long mark with its label appears at the middle of the line.

### 610

- Shows: a street from left to right with a house at each of 60 marks. The doors of houses 1, 20, 50 and 60 show
  their numbers and the other doors are blank. A van stands at house 1. The Find readout shows 46 and the Show
  button stands in the panel. While the reader drags the van, the lens above it shows about ten houses at full
  size with the door numbers the street has there.
- The reader: drags the van along the street to a house and presses Show, then Next.
- The figure: the van snaps to the nearest house. Show puts a flag over house 46 and writes the number of the
  house the van stands at on the van. A move of the van takes the reveal away. Next gives 22 and then 57.
- Action: the world page joins the drag of the point and Show on a street, where the point is a van and the
  labels are door numbers.

## Sources

- England, Year 1 guidance, 1NPV–2: "Pupils should use efficient strategies and appropriate reasoning, including
  working backwards from a multiple of 10, to identify or place numbers on marked number lines." Its Figure 5 places
  5, 12 and 19 on a marked 0 to 20 line, with "c is 19 because it is one less than 20." Page 410 takes the line from
  0 to 20, the number 19 and the jump back from 20. Read in
  `content/number-line/.references/assets.publishing.service.gov.uk-ads-system-uploads-attachment-data-file-954511-Maths-guidance-year-1.txt`,
  and the figure in the `.pdf` of the same name, page 14.
- Petitto 1990 scores counting as appropriate when a child counts "from the left endpoint to the leftmost- or
  midtarget, or from the right endpoint to the rightmost- or midtarget", and finds that "inappropriate counting is
  relatively frequent at lower grades". Page 410 asks for 3 near 0 and for 19 and 14 near 20 for that reason. The
  same paper says children counted "from one known position value (usually one endpoint but sometimes the midpoint,
  using the value 5 or 50, depending on numerical range)", which backs 50 as a place to count from on page 420. It
  also says "they never used the target's position relative to any other known position to check or to adjust the
  result of the count", which Show answers by showing where the number is. Read in
  `content/number-line/.references/lchcautobio.ucsd.edu-uploads-2015-10-Petitto-1990-Development-of-Numberline-and-Measurement-Concepts.txt`.
- NCETM, Mastering Number Year 2, Term 2: "focusing on multiples of 10 and the midpoint of 50." Read in
  `content/number-line/.references/axis.ncetm.org.uk-media-l1ygv0oz-mastering-number-year-2-overview.txt`.
- Oak National Academy, "Explain where 6, 7, 8 and 9 lie on a number line": "On a 0 to 10 number line, 5 is in the
  middle. It is the midpoint." Its teacher tip asks pupils to see that "4 is closer to 0 than 10 on the number line",
  which the text of page 410 uses to choose the nearer end before counting. Read in
  `content/number-line/.references/thenational.academy-osition-of-numbers-6-to-10-lessons-explain-where-6-7-8-and-9-lie-on-a-number-line.txt`.
- Illustrative Mathematics, grade 2 unit 4 lesson 3, Activity 1, asks the reader who counts every mark "How did you
  use the labeled tick marks?" Read in
  `content/number-line/.references/curriculum.illustrativemathematics.org-k5-teachers-grade-2-unit-4-lesson-3-lesson.txt`.
- The plan's own choices:
  - The line of page 420 has a mark for every number. NCETM and Oak place the tens on a 0 to 100 line. A line in
    ones makes counting from either end too long for 48 and 53, so the middle is the guide the reader needs, as the
    brief asks for a number near the middle.
  - Page 410 labels only 0 and 20, as the brief says. England's Figure 5 also labels 10, the middle of that line,
    and the plan keeps the middle for page 420.
  - The numbers to place, 3, 19, 14, 48, 53 and 96, and the jumps on both teaching pages.
  - The street numbers its houses in order along one side, so that one house is one step. Many real streets put
    odd numbers on one side and even numbers on the other. The door numbers 1, 20, 50 and 60 and the parcels for 22
    and 57 after 46 are the plan's own.
