# Research for The number line

Files named with a `.pdf` from `.cache/curricula/` are the curricula. Every other file is in
`content/number-line/.references/`.

## Curricula

- England teaches the number line in Year 1 next to counting in twos, fives and tens: "count, read and write numbers
  to 100 in numerals; count in multiples of twos, fives and tens" and "identify and represent numbers using objects
  and pictorial representations including the number line" (england-ks1-2.pdf, page 6, Year 1 number and place
  value).
- England adds estimating in Year 2: "identify, represent and estimate numbers using different representations,
  including the number line" (england-ks1-2.pdf, page 11, Year 2 number and place value).
- Japan teaches it in grade 1: "To develop a sequence of numbers and to represent numbers on a number line by
  thinking about the order and the size of those numbers" (japan-course-of-study-2008-en.pdf, page 6, Grade 1
  A(1)c).
- The United States teaches it in grade 2, under measurement, as lengths from 0: "Represent whole numbers as lengths
  from 0 on a number line diagram with equally spaced points corresponding to the numbers 0, 1, 2, ..."
  (us-common-core-math.pdf, page 20, 2.MD.B.6).
- Bremen teaches counting in steps in years 1 and 2 and names the empty line for calculation only: "Zählen in
  Schritten (z. B. 2er- 5er, 10er-, 100er-Schritte)" and "strukturierte Punktefelder, Rechenstrich"
  (germany-bremen-primary-2025.pdf, page 20, 3.1 Menge, Zahl und Operation).

## 010 in-order

- Order: the line follows counting and a numbered track. "Being able to count fluently (1NPV–1), both forwards and
  backwards, is necessary for pupils to be able to reason about the location of numbers in the linear number
  system." (assets.publishing.service.gov.uk-ads-system-uploads-attachment-data-file-954511-Maths-guidance-year-1.txt,
  1NPV–2 Making connections)
- Order: the experience before the line is a track game. "Play games that involve moving along a numbered track, and
  understand that larger numbers are further along the track." (assets.publishing.service.gov.uk-ads-system-uploads-attachment-data-file-954511-Maths-guidance-year-1.txt,
  table of previous experience, 1NPV–2)
- Order: NCETM puts the track and the line side by side in the first term of Year 1. "explore number tracks and
  number lines and identify the differences between them" (axis.ncetm.org.uk-media-zclpoezm-mastering-number-year-1-overview.txt,
  Term 1)
- Order: IM opens with what the line is: order, equal spacing and the point, then the reader labels marks.
  "the sequence of numbers moving from left to right and equal spacing between tick marks to locate and represent
  whole numbers" (curriculum.illustrativemathematics.org-k5-teachers-grade-2-unit-4-lesson-1-lesson.txt, Activity 1)
- Order: comparing by position comes in IM's lesson 4, after unlabelled marks. "the number farther to the right
  represents a greater value than the number to the left" (curriculum.illustrativemathematics.org-k5-teachers-grade-2-unit-4-lesson-4-lesson.txt,
  Activity 1)
- Examples: a frog or rabbit hopping along the line. "Give students a context to relate the number line to. For
  example, a frog jumping on lily pads, or a rabbit hopping." (curriculum.illustrativemathematics.org-k5-teachers-grade-2-unit-4-lesson-4-lesson.txt,
  Activity 1, Engagement)
- Examples: a line from 0 to 20 with a point on 13 and on 3. "Make a number line that goes from 0 to 20. Locate 13
  on your number line." (curriculum.illustrativemathematics.org-k5-teachers-grade-2-unit-4-lesson-1-lesson.txt,
  Activity 2)
- Examples: a class line of string with cards 0 to 30 hung in random order. "Today, you are going to create a class
  number line to represent the numbers from 0 to 30." (curriculum.illustrativemathematics.org-k5-teachers-grade-2-unit-4-lesson-2-lesson.txt,
  Activity 1)
- Examples: a board of 10 equal squares numbered 1 to 10, moved along one or two squares at a time. "Both included
  10 horizontally arranged, different colored squares of equal size." (cmu.edu-dietrich-psychology-cs-research-teaching-docs-SieglerBoardGamesCDPerp2009.txt,
  An Initial Demonstration)
- Words: "number line", "tick mark", "point". "A dot, called a point, on the number line represents a specific
  number." (curriculum.illustrativemathematics.org-k5-teachers-grade-2-unit-4-lesson-1-lesson.txt, Activity 1)
- Words: "Number line - A line with numbers placed in their correct position." and "one more", "one less", "order"
  (thenational.academy-osition-of-numbers-6-to-10-lessons-explain-where-6-7-8-and-9-lie-on-a-number-line.txt,
  Keywords)
- Words: "to the left" and "to the right". "using the language “to the left” and “to the right”"
  (curriculum.illustrativemathematics.org-k5-teachers-grade-2-unit-4-lesson-4-lesson.txt, Activity 1)
- Words: a hop. "a ‘‘hop of 5’’ is operationalized by moving a finger in an arc from one tick mark to the next, 5
  times" (fisme.science.uu.nl-publicaties-literatuur-2013-teppo-number-line-zdm.txt, 4.1)
- Errors: learners count the marks with no regard to the gaps. "she ‘‘used the tickmark with a counting function
  without regard to the distance between tickmarks’’" (fisme.science.uu.nl-publicaties-literatuur-2013-teppo-number-line-zdm.txt,
  4.1)
- Errors: learners take a number to be the point and miss the length. "Numbers are represented on the numberline by
  lengths-instead, numbers are thought to be represented by the points they label" (lchcautobio.ucsd.edu-uploads-2015-10-Petitto-1990-Development-of-Numberline-and-Measurement-Concepts.txt,
  introduction)
- Errors and advice: learners miss that the marks must be equally spaced. "Pupils do not understand that intervals or
  marks on a number line need to be equally spaced and that in these cases each mark represents one number. Make
  connections between number tracks and number lines showing that the intervals are counted on a number line."
  (thenational.academy-osition-of-numbers-6-to-10-lessons-explain-where-6-7-8-and-9-lie-on-a-number-line.txt,
  Common misconception)
- Errors and advice: IM shows lines with unequal gaps or wrong labels for the reader to correct. "Students analyze
  number line diagrams that do not have equal unit intervals or have tick marks that are not properly labeled."
  (curriculum.illustrativemathematics.org-k5-teachers-grade-2-unit-4-lesson-2-lesson.txt, Activity 2)
- Errors: a hop of 5 from 0 crosses six marks, and the count switches between gaps and marks. "the beginning of the
  initial jump of ‘‘5’’ starts at zero, and thus includes six tick marks over the total jump of five spaces."
  (fisme.science.uu.nl-publicaties-literatuur-2013-teppo-number-line-zdm.txt, 4.1)
- Errors and advice: learners miss a gap in the count. "Pupils may not realise that a count with a missing number or
  number in the wrong place is incorrect." (thenational.academy-unting-recognising-and-comparing-numbers-0-10-lessons-finding-the-missing-numbers.txt,
  Common misconception)

## 020 steps

- Order: England teaches counting in twos, fives and tens in Year 1 with a number line as its picture. "counting
  with the support of visual representations and gestural patterns, for example pupils can point to numerals on a
  number line or 100 square" and "Figure 10: number line to support counting in multiples of 2"
  (assets.publishing.service.gov.uk-ads-system-uploads-attachment-data-file-954511-Maths-guidance-year-1.txt,
  1NF–2 Teaching guidance)
- Order: IM counts by 5 out loud first, then shows lines that count by 5 and by 10. "Count by 5, starting at 0."
  (curriculum.illustrativemathematics.org-k5-teachers-grade-2-unit-4-lesson-2-lesson.txt, Warm-up) and "These number
  lines help us see what it looks like when we count by different numbers."
  (curriculum.illustrativemathematics.org-k5-teachers-grade-2-unit-4-lesson-3-lesson.txt, Warm-up synthesis)
- Order: after the count, IM labels only the fives or tens and the reader locates numbers between them. "students
  work with number lines that only have multiples of 5 or 10 labeled" (curriculum.illustrativemathematics.org-k5-teachers-grade-2-unit-4-lesson-3-lesson.txt,
  Activity 1)
- Order: NCETM places the tens on 0 to 100 from the line to 10. "use their understanding of the linear number system
  to 10 to position multiples of 10 on a 0 - 100 number line and reason about midpoints"
  (axis.ncetm.org.uk-media-l1ygv0oz-mastering-number-year-2-overview.txt, Term 2)
- Examples: 24, 37, 48 and 83 on lines labelled at fives or tens. "Locate 37 on the number line."
  (curriculum.illustrativemathematics.org-k5-teachers-grade-2-unit-4-lesson-3-lesson.txt, Activity 1)
- Examples: 36 and 79 on a 0 to 100 line. "“a is 36 because it is one more than the midpoint of 35.” “b is 79
  because it is one less than 80.”" (assets.publishing.service.gov.uk-ads-system-uploads-attachment-data-file-954796-Maths-guidance-year-2.txt,
  2NPV–2 Language focus)
- Examples: cubes that change colour every 5. "Build a number line that changes color back and forth at intervals of
  5." (curriculum.illustrativemathematics.org-k5-teachers-grade-2-unit-4-lesson-3-lesson.txt, Activity 2)
- Words: "jumps of ten", "equally spaced". "There are ten jumps of ten on a line from 0 to 100" and "The multiples of
  ten are equally spaced." (thenational.academy-es-of-10-lessons-estimate-the-position-of-multiples-of-ten-on-a-0-100-number-line.txt,
  Key learning points)
- Words: "interval", "spaces", "tick mark", "pattern", "distance from zero". "number line, distance from zero, in
  order, interval, spaces, tick mark, point, and pattern" (curriculum.illustrativemathematics.org-k5-teachers-grade-2-unit-4-lesson-3-lesson.txt,
  Activity 1, MLR2)
- Words: "skip-count". "label only the tick marks that show the numbers you say when you skip-count by 10"
  (curriculum.illustrativemathematics.org-k5-teachers-grade-2-unit-4-lesson-3-lesson.txt, Lesson Synthesis)
- Errors and advice: learners count every mark in place of using the labels. "If students count every tick mark to
  locate numbers, consider asking: “How could you use the labeled tick marks to help you find a number?”"
  (curriculum.illustrativemathematics.org-k5-teachers-grade-2-unit-4-lesson-3-lesson.txt, Advancing Student
  Thinking)
- Errors: counting in tens on a line lags the spoken count by more than a year. "the use of counting by 10s does not
  increase significantly until over a year after children master the verbal counting skill"
  (lchcautobio.ucsd.edu-uploads-2015-10-Petitto-1990-Development-of-Numberline-and-Measurement-Concepts.txt,
  Experiment 1 results)
- Errors: learners think only next numbers can be put in order. "Children may think that only consecutive numbers can
  be ordered. Order non-consecutive numbers, then place back with other numbers on number track"
  (thenational.academy-in-tens-decade-numbers-lessons-order-and-compare-decade-numbers-on-number-tracks.txt, Common
  misconception)

## 030 part-of-the-line

- Order: IM gives lines that start past 0 in the same lesson as lines labelled at fives and tens. "Today we made sense
  of number lines that do not have each number labeled and number lines that do not start with 0."
  (curriculum.illustrativemathematics.org-k5-teachers-grade-2-unit-4-lesson-3-lesson.txt, Lesson Synthesis)
- Order: England uses the line from 10 to 20 in Year 1, after 0 to 10 and 0 to 20. "beginning with a 0 to 10 number
  line, then moving on to 0 to 20 and 10 to 20 number lines" (assets.publishing.service.gov.uk-ads-system-uploads-attachment-data-file-954511-Maths-guidance-year-1.txt,
  1NPV–2 Teaching guidance)
- Order: in Year 2 the reader names the two tens a number sits between, to prepare rounding. "pupils should also
  learn to identify which 2 multiples of 10 a given two-digit number is between."
  (assets.publishing.service.gov.uk-ads-system-uploads-attachment-data-file-954796-Maths-guidance-year-2.txt,
  2NPV–2 Teaching guidance)
- Examples: a line of 31 marks from 10, with 25 and 30 labelled and other labels blank, and 17 to place. "First tick
  mark labeled 10, sixth mark labeled blank, eleventh mark labeled blank, sixteenth mark labeled 25"
  (curriculum.illustrativemathematics.org-k5-teachers-grade-2-unit-4-lesson-3-lesson.txt, Activity 2)
- Examples: 35 on a line labelled only at 10 and 50. "How could you find 35 without filling in the missing labels?"
  (We could count back from 50. We could count on from 10.)" (curriculum.illustrativemathematics.org-k5-teachers-grade-2-unit-4-lesson-3-lesson.txt,
  Activity 2 synthesis)
- Examples: missing numbers in a count. "37 38 40 42 43" and "63 62 60 58 57"
  (assets.publishing.service.gov.uk-ads-system-uploads-attachment-data-file-954511-Maths-guidance-year-1.txt,
  1NPV–1 Example assessment questions)
- Examples: 46 between 40 and 50, with 45 as the middle of the gap. "The midpoint we refer to is halfway between each
  multiple of ten." and "The midpoint we refer to has a ones digit of five."
  (thenational.academy-ers-20-to-99-lessons-use-a-number-line-to-position-and-estimate-the-numbers-20-99.txt, Key
  learning points)
- Words: "previous" and "next" multiple of ten. "avoid the language of 'the multiple of ten that is more/less' and
  instead use 'previous' and 'next'" (thenational.academy-ers-20-to-99-lessons-use-a-number-line-to-position-and-estimate-the-numbers-20-99.txt,
  Teacher tip)
- Errors and advice: learners take the ten before the ten as the previous ten. "they may think 30 is before 40, so
  think 30 is the multiple of ten before 45. Make deliberate errors to discuss and encourage children to visualise,
  then label, their own number lines marked in tens." (thenational.academy-ers-20-to-99-lessons-use-a-number-line-to-position-and-estimate-the-numbers-20-99.txt,
  Common misconception)
- Errors: counting back across a ten. "When counting backwards, pupils often find it challenging to identify which
  number they should say after they have said a multiple of 10. A partially marked number line can be used for
  support." (assets.publishing.service.gov.uk-ads-system-uploads-attachment-data-file-954511-Maths-guidance-year-1.txt,
  1NPV–1 Teaching guidance)
- Errors and advice: labels that do not match the step. "I see you labeled this point as ___. How can you use the
  number line to prove that is the location of that number?" (curriculum.illustrativemathematics.org-k5-teachers-grade-2-unit-4-lesson-3-lesson.txt,
  Activity 2, Advancing Student Thinking)

## 040 placing

- Order: on a marked line the reader works back from a ten as well as on from 0. "Pupils should use efficient
  strategies and appropriate reasoning, including working backwards from a multiple of 10, to identify or place
  numbers on marked number lines." (assets.publishing.service.gov.uk-ads-system-uploads-attachment-data-file-954511-Maths-guidance-year-1.txt,
  1NPV–2 Teaching guidance)
- Order: NCETM introduces midpoints with the teen numbers in Year 1. "including identifying the midpoints of 5, 10
  and 15" (axis.ncetm.org.uk-media-zclpoezm-mastering-number-year-1-overview.txt, Term 3)
- Order: Oak names 5 as the middle of 0 to 10 before placing 6 to 9. "On a 0 to 10 number line, 5 is in the middle.
  It is the midpoint." (thenational.academy-osition-of-numbers-6-to-10-lessons-explain-where-6-7-8-and-9-lie-on-a-number-line.txt,
  Key learning points)
- Examples: 5, 12 and 19 on a marked 0 to 20 line. "“a is 5 because it is halfway between 0 and 10.” “b is 12
  because it is 2 more than 10.” “c is 19 because it is one less than 20.”"
  (assets.publishing.service.gov.uk-ads-system-uploads-attachment-data-file-954511-Maths-guidance-year-1.txt,
  1NPV–2 Language focus)
- Examples: 50 as the middle of 0 to 100. "focusing on multiples of 10 and the midpoint of 50."
  (axis.ncetm.org.uk-media-l1ygv0oz-mastering-number-year-2-overview.txt, Term 2)
- Examples: tens on a 0 to 100 line read from an arrow. "What multiple of 10 is the arrow pointing to on this number
  line?" with answers 20 and 60 (thenational.academy-es-of-10-lessons-estimate-the-position-of-multiples-of-ten-on-a-0-100-number-line.txt,
  Assessment exit quiz)
- Words: "midpoint", "halfway". "Midpoint - The middle of something. The point halfway along."
  (thenational.academy-numbers-6-to-10-lessons-estimate-where-6-7-8-and-9-lie-on-an-unmarked-number-line.txt,
  Keywords)
- Words: "marked and unmarked number lines". "identify or place numbers up to 20 on marked and unmarked number lines"
  (assets.publishing.service.gov.uk-ads-system-uploads-attachment-data-file-954511-Maths-guidance-year-1.txt,
  1NPV–2 Teaching guidance)
- Errors: learners count from one end only, also when the other end is nearer. "Sequential estimation strategies
  neglect the matter of distance between positions and take into account only one endpoint value"
  (lchcautobio.ucsd.edu-uploads-2015-10-Petitto-1990-Development-of-Numberline-and-Measurement-Concepts.txt,
  Experiment 1)
- Errors: the counting goes one way and is not checked against other marks. "they never used the target's position
  relative to any other known position to check or to adjust the result of the count"
  (lchcautobio.ucsd.edu-uploads-2015-10-Petitto-1990-Development-of-Numberline-and-Measurement-Concepts.txt,
  Results)
- Errors: counting from the far end drops with age, and the middle as a guide comes late. "inappropriate counting is
  relatively frequent at lower grades and drops sharply by late-second grade" and "These data show a marked increase
  in use of the midpoint values across grades, particularly from late-second to late-third grade."
  (lchcautobio.ucsd.edu-uploads-2015-10-Petitto-1990-Development-of-Numberline-and-Measurement-Concepts.txt,
  Results)
- Errors and advice: the reader who counts every mark is asked how the labels help (see 020).
  "How did you use the labeled tick marks?" (curriculum.illustrativemathematics.org-k5-teachers-grade-2-unit-4-lesson-3-lesson.txt,
  Activity 1)

## 050 estimating

- Order: England estimates in Year 1 on lines with no marks, from 0 to 10 first. "Pupils should learn to estimate the
  value/position of a number relative to both ends of the number line, beginning with a 0 to 10 number line, then
  moving on to 0 to 20 and 10 to 20 number lines." (assets.publishing.service.gov.uk-ads-system-uploads-attachment-data-file-954511-Maths-guidance-year-1.txt,
  1NPV–2 Teaching guidance)
- Order: Year 2 moves the same work to 0 to 100. "Pupils should also be able to estimate the value or position of
  two-digit numbers on unmarked numbers lines" with "Figure 4: placing 60 on an unmarked 0 to 100 number line"
  (assets.publishing.service.gov.uk-ads-system-uploads-attachment-data-file-954796-Maths-guidance-year-2.txt,
  2NPV–2 Teaching guidance)
- Order: IM estimates the number at a point first, then adds a middle mark and the reader revises. "draw a tick mark
  at the halfway point and label with 20. Students can revise their thinking based on this additional information."
  (curriculum.illustrativemathematics.org-k5-teachers-grade-2-unit-4-lesson-5-lesson.txt, Warm-up)
- Order: in IM the labelled marks grow further apart from line to line. "For each successive number line, the given
  tick marks are farther apart" (curriculum.illustrativemathematics.org-k5-teachers-grade-2-unit-4-lesson-5-lesson.txt,
  Activity 1)
- Examples: 16 on a bare 0 to 20 line and on a bare 10 to 20 line. "“16 is about here because it is just over
  halfway between 10 and 20.”" (assets.publishing.service.gov.uk-ads-system-uploads-attachment-data-file-954511-Maths-guidance-year-1.txt,
  1NPV–2 Teaching guidance)
- Examples: 60 on a bare 0 to 100 line. "“60 is about here on the number line because it’s just over half way”."
  (assets.publishing.service.gov.uk-ads-system-uploads-attachment-data-file-954796-Maths-guidance-year-2.txt,
  2NPV–2 Teaching guidance)
- Examples: cards placed on a 0 to 40 line marked at 10, 20 and 30. "draw tick marks at the beginning (label 0) and
  the end (label 40)" and "draw tick marks and label: 10, 20, 30."
  (curriculum.illustrativemathematics.org-k5-teachers-grade-2-unit-4-lesson-5-lesson.txt, Activity 2)
- Examples: targets 2, 4 and 8 on 0 to 10, and 16, 44 and 78 on 0 to 100. "In the 0-to-10 range, the targets were at
  2, 4, and 8; in the 0-to-100 range, the targets were at 16, 44, and 78."
  (lchcautobio.ucsd.edu-uploads-2015-10-Petitto-1990-Development-of-Numberline-and-Measurement-Concepts.txt,
  Procedure)
- Examples: lengths compared with a 100 cm line. "Look at lines A, B and C. Estimate how long they are by comparing
  them to the 100cm lines?" (assets.publishing.service.gov.uk-ads-system-uploads-attachment-data-file-954796-Maths-guidance-year-2.txt,
  2NPV–2 Example assessment questions)
- Words: "estimate", "too low", "about right", "too high". "Record an estimate that is: too low about right too
  high" (curriculum.illustrativemathematics.org-k5-teachers-grade-2-unit-4-lesson-5-lesson.txt, Warm-up)
- Words: "Estimate - To find a value that is close enough to the right answer, usually with some thought or
  calculation involved." (thenational.academy-numbers-6-to-10-lessons-estimate-where-6-7-8-and-9-lie-on-an-unmarked-number-line.txt,
  Keywords)
- Words: "unmarked". "It can be horizontal or vertical and can be marked or unmarked."
  (thenational.academy-es-of-10-lessons-estimate-the-position-of-multiples-of-ten-on-a-0-100-number-line.txt,
  Keywords)
- Errors and advice: learners count on from one end, and bunch the large numbers. "When pupils are asked to mark all
  numbers on, for example, an unmarked 0 to 10 number line, they typically start at 1 and run out of space as they
  approach 10, and so bunch the larger numbers together. Pupils should learn to look at the full length of the
  number line and mark on the midpoint first." (assets.publishing.service.gov.uk-ads-system-uploads-attachment-data-file-954511-Maths-guidance-year-1.txt,
  1NPV–2 Teaching guidance)
- Errors and advice: estimating is proportion, and counting from an end is the error to steer away from. "using
  appropriate proportional reasoning, rather than counting on from a start point or back from an end point"
  (assets.publishing.service.gov.uk-ads-system-uploads-attachment-data-file-954796-Maths-guidance-year-2.txt,
  2NPV–2 Teaching guidance)
- Errors: on 0 to 100, young learners spread the small numbers and squeeze the large ones. "On 0–100 number lines,
  kindergartners consistently produce logarithmically increasing estimates, whereas second graders produce linearly
  increasing estimates" (cmu.edu-dietrich-psychology-cs-research-teaching-docs-SieglerBoardGamesCDPerp2009.txt,
  page 120)
- Errors: the range matters, and the same learner is accurate on a short range and not on a long one. "The same child
  often produces linearly increasing estimates on smaller numerical scales (e.g., 0–100) and logarithmically
  increasing ones on larger scales (e.g., 0–1,000" (cmu.edu-dietrich-psychology-cs-research-teaching-docs-SieglerBoardGamesCDPerp2009.txt,
  page 120)
- Errors: learners nudge a far guess by small steps. "children occasionally persisted in using small incremental
  adjustments to an original estimate, even when that original estimate was visibly far off the mark"
  (lchcautobio.ucsd.edu-uploads-2015-10-Petitto-1990-Development-of-Numberline-and-Measurement-Concepts.txt,
  introduction)
- Errors and advice: drawing one's own line with unequal gaps. "Place the first, last and midpoint numbers first and
  use fingers or cubes to space the other numbers out equally." (thenational.academy-numbers-6-to-10-lessons-estimate-where-6-7-8-and-9-lie-on-an-unmarked-number-line.txt,
  Common misconception)
- Advice: a reasonable place is enough. "There is no need for pupils to be completely accurate in their estimation of
  value or position of numbers." (assets.publishing.service.gov.uk-ads-system-uploads-attachment-data-file-954511-Maths-guidance-year-1.txt,
  1NPV–2 Teaching guidance)
- Advice: judge by eye before computing. "At this stage, rely on visual perception (e.g. visually 4 is closer to 0
  than 10 on the number line) rather than calculation" (thenational.academy-osition-of-numbers-6-to-10-lessons-explain-where-6-7-8-and-9-lie-on-a-number-line.txt,
  Teacher tip)
- Advice: a playing board of equal numbered squares makes estimates on the line more even. "After playing the game,
  the best fitting linear function accounted for an average of 61% of the variance."
  (cmu.edu-dietrich-psychology-cs-research-teaching-docs-SieglerBoardGamesCDPerp2009.txt, An Initial Demonstration)

## Against the brief

- Question 1, steps and skip counting: the sources teach the line in twos, fives and tens together with counting in
  twos, fives and tens, as the picture of that count. England puts both in Year 1 and points pupils "to numerals on a
  number line" while they count in multiples (assets.publishing.service.gov.uk-ads-system-uploads-attachment-data-file-954511-Maths-guidance-year-1.txt,
  1NF–2 Teaching guidance). IM has the class "Count by 5, starting at 0" in the lesson before the lines labelled at
  fives (curriculum.illustrativemathematics.org-k5-teachers-grade-2-unit-4-lesson-2-lesson.txt, Warm-up). The brief
  places `steps` before the story `skip-counting`, so the line in twos and fives meets a count the reader has not
  learned. The count in tens is in `counting`, the count in twos and fives is not.
- Question 1, reading a scale: England leaves reading a line whose labelled gaps split into 2, 4, 5 or 10 parts to
  Year 3, with hundreds. "read scales/number lines marked in multiples of 100 with 2, 4, 5 and 10 equal parts"
  (assets.publishing.service.gov.uk-ads-system-uploads-attachment-data-file-954796-Maths-guidance-year-2.txt,
  ready-to-progress table, 3NPV–4). IM reads lines labelled at fives and tens in grade 2
  (curriculum.illustrativemathematics.org-k5-teachers-grade-2-unit-4-lesson-3-lesson.txt, Activity 1).
- Question 2, track before line: the track comes first. England lists "Play games that involve moving along a
  numbered track" as the experience before 1NPV–2 (assets.publishing.service.gov.uk-ads-system-uploads-attachment-data-file-954511-Maths-guidance-year-1.txt,
  previous experience table), and NCETM has pupils "explore number tracks and number lines and identify the
  differences between them" (axis.ncetm.org.uk-media-zclpoezm-mastering-number-year-1-overview.txt, Term 1). The
  brief's stepping stones are a track, a square for each number. A track numbers its squares from 1: "the numbers
  1–10 listed consecutively from left to right" (cmu.edu-dietrich-psychology-cs-research-teaching-docs-SieglerBoardGamesCDPerp2009.txt,
  An Initial Demonstration). The rough map starts the frog on stone 0, which mixes the two.
- Question 2, counting marks in place of gaps: the sources report it and advise linking the track to the line.
  "Make connections between number tracks and number lines showing that the intervals are counted on a number line."
  (thenational.academy-osition-of-numbers-6-to-10-lessons-explain-where-6-7-8-and-9-lie-on-a-number-line.txt,
  Common misconception). Teppo shows the error in a reader who "used the tickmark with a counting function without
  regard to the distance between tickmarks" and the mismatch in a hop of 5 from 0, which "includes six tick marks
  over the total jump of five spaces" (fisme.science.uu.nl-publicaties-literatuur-2013-teppo-number-line-zdm.txt,
  4.1). The brief names no page for the gaps between marks. The hops of `in-order` are where the gap is counted.
- Question 3, range for estimating: the sources start on short lines. England goes "0 to 10 number line, then moving
  on to 0 to 20 and 10 to 20" in Year 1 and to 0 to 100 in Year 2 (assets.publishing.service.gov.uk-ads-system-uploads-attachment-data-file-954511-Maths-guidance-year-1.txt,
  1NPV–2 Teaching guidance). IM estimates on lines to 40 with tens marked
  (curriculum.illustrativemathematics.org-k5-teachers-grade-2-unit-4-lesson-5-lesson.txt, Activity 2). Petitto
  tests 0 to 10 and 0 to 100 (lchcautobio.ucsd.edu-uploads-2015-10-Petitto-1990-Development-of-Numberline-and-Measurement-Concepts.txt,
  Procedure). The brief's bare lines all run within 0 to 100, which the sources support as the upper limit, since
  "On 0–1,000 number lines, second graders’ estimates increase logarithmically"
  (cmu.edu-dietrich-psychology-cs-research-teaching-docs-SieglerBoardGamesCDPerp2009.txt, page 120). The brief
  opens `estimating` on 0 to 100 with no shorter line before it.
- Question 3, errors on the bare line: counting on from one end, bunching the larger numbers, and squeezing the
  larger numbers on 0 to 100. "they typically start at 1 and run out of space as they approach 10, and so bunch the
  larger numbers together" (assets.publishing.service.gov.uk-ads-system-uploads-attachment-data-file-954511-Maths-guidance-year-1.txt,
  1NPV–2 Teaching guidance) and "kindergartners consistently produce logarithmically increasing estimates"
  (cmu.edu-dietrich-psychology-cs-research-teaching-docs-SieglerBoardGamesCDPerp2009.txt, page 120). The brief's
  "ends and the middle" matches the advice to "mark on the midpoint first" (same Year 1 file).
- Order of `placing` and `estimating`: England teaches estimating on the bare line in the same criterion as placing on
  a marked line, from Year 1. "identify or place numbers up to 20 on marked and unmarked number lines"
  (assets.publishing.service.gov.uk-ads-system-uploads-attachment-data-file-954511-Maths-guidance-year-1.txt,
  1NPV–2 Teaching guidance). The brief keeps them in two sections, which the sources allow.
- The middle as a guide: Petitto finds the midpoint strategy rises "particularly from late-second to late-third
  grade" (lchcautobio.ucsd.edu-uploads-2015-10-Petitto-1990-Development-of-Numberline-and-Measurement-Concepts.txt,
  Results), later than the brief's beginner level. England teaches it from Year 1: "mark on the midpoint first"
  (assets.publishing.service.gov.uk-ads-system-uploads-attachment-data-file-954511-Maths-guidance-year-1.txt,
  1NPV–2 Teaching guidance). The brief follows the teaching, and the research says the middle needs to be shown.
- The line as length: the United States and IM teach the line as lengths from 0, after measuring with rulers. "Each
  number represents a length from 0." (curriculum.illustrativemathematics.org-k5-teachers-grade-2-unit-4-lesson-1-lesson.txt,
  Activity 1) and 2.MD.B.6 sits under "Relate addition and subtraction to length" (us-common-core-math.pdf, page 20).
  The brief treats the line as positions in the count and leaves length to `centimetres-and-metres`, after this
  story.
- Previous and next ten: England's Year 2 criterion names them and Oak sets the words. "identifying the previous and
  next multiple of 10" (assets.publishing.service.gov.uk-ads-system-uploads-attachment-data-file-954796-Maths-guidance-year-2.txt,
  2NPV–2) and "use 'previous' and 'next'" (thenational.academy-ers-20-to-99-lessons-use-a-number-line-to-position-and-estimate-the-numbers-20-99.txt,
  Teacher tip). The brief's `part-of-the-line` teaches the gap between two tens without these words.
