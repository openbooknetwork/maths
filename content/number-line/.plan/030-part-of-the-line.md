# A line that starts past zero

## Header

- Assumes: from the story `counting`, the reader counts on and back and counts to 100 in rows of ten. From section
  `in-order` the reader knows the number line, the mark, the label, the point and its drag, the gap, and the jump with
  the buttons Jump left and Jump right. From section `steps` the reader knows the step, reads it from two labels next to
  each other, knows lines that count in tens, fives and twos, knows long marks with labels beside short marks without,
  and knows the line from 0 to 100 in ones labelled at the tens, where the nine short marks between two tens are the
  nine numbers between them (page 220). From challenge 710 the reader knows Check.
- Teaches: a line can show a piece of the count, with a left end and a right end past zero, a number that is no ten
  sits between the ten before it and the ten after it, and two labels next to each other give the step that fills
  in all the missing labels.
- Introduces:
  - Terms: end (left end, right end), the ten before and the ten after, label card.
  - Controls: a tap on a gap that opens it as a line of its own (310). The bracket from the ten before the point to
    the ten after it (320). Blank label boxes and a row of label cards that the reader drags onto them, and the
    button New line, which draws another line with its cards (330).
- Uses:
  - The line from 0 to 100 that counts in tens. The reader opens the gap from 40 to 50, a line with the left end 40
    and the right end 50, and then another gap such as 70 to 80, with the ends 70 and 80 (page 310).
  - A line from 30 to 60 in ones, labelled at 30, 40, 50 and 60, with the point on 46 between 40 and 50. The reader
    puts the point on 45, with 30, 40 and 45 on the line together, and jumps it from 53 back across 50 to 49
    (page 320).
  - A drawn line of 6 gaps, past zero and ending at 100 or below, that counts in twos, fives or tens. Two marks next
    to each other carry labels, the other five carry blank boxes, and six cards hold the five missing labels and
    one number that fits no mark. The page opens on a draw and New line draws again. The text works the line from
    25 to 55 in fives, labelled at 35 and 40, where the card 41 fits no mark (page 330).
  - The challenge draws a step of 2, 5 or 10, a line of 10 gaps whose left end is from 0 to 50 and whose right end
    is 100 or below, two labels next to each other and a target mark (page 720).

The section has three teaching pages. The pages 340 to 390 stay free.

## Content

### 310 A line can start past zero

The page shows the line from 0 to 100 that counts in tens, as the reader met it in `steps`. The text asks the reader
to tap the gap from 40 to 50. It opens under the line as a line of its own. That line starts at 40 and stops at 50,
so 40 is its left end and 50 is its right end. A line can start past zero, and it shows only the piece of the count
between its two ends, here 40, 41 and on to 50. The thin lines from its ends to 40 and 50 on the long line show
where the piece was taken from. The text asks the reader to open another gap, such as 70 to 80, and to name its
left end and its right end. Each gap from 10 upward opens as a line that starts past zero. The text leaves the
count of the numbers between two tens to page 220, and calls 0 no ten.

### 320 The ten before and the ten after

A line from 30 to 60 shows a piece of the count. Its left end is 30 and its right end is 60. It counts in ones, and
only its tens carry labels. The point is on 46. A plain bracket over the line reaches from 40 to 50 and lights both
labels. 40 is the ten before 46 and 50 is the ten after it. The readouts Ten before and Ten after show the same two
tens. The text asks the reader to put the point on 45. The bracket still reaches from 40 to 50, so the ten before 45
is 40. 30 is on the line too, to the left of 40. 30 comes earlier in the count than 45, and 40 sits between 30 and
45, so 40 is the ten just before 45. Then the reader puts the point on 53 and presses Jump left three times, to 52,
51 and 50. On 50 the bracket goes, because 50 is a ten, and the readouts Ten before and Ten after show a dash. One
more press of Jump left takes the point to 49, and the bracket now reaches from 40 to 50. The text ends on the rule
that every number between two tens has the ten before it on its left and the ten after it on its right.

### 330 Two labels tell you the step

The page opens on a drawn line, and the button New line draws another. Each line has only two labels, on two marks
next to each other. The other marks carry empty boxes, and a row of label cards waits under the line. The text says
that two labels next to each other give the step, and the step gives all the missing labels. Counting on in steps
from the right label reaches the right end, and counting back from the left label reaches the left end. The text
asks the reader to drag the cards onto their marks, then to press New line for another line.

The text works one example apart from the line on show. Take a line with the labels 35 and 40. 35 to 40 is a step of
5. The mark after 40 is 45, and the mark one step to the left of 35 is 30. Counting on reaches 55 at the right end, and counting back
reaches 25 at the left end. Every line the page draws has one card that fits no mark. On this example it is 41, the
number after 40 in the count, while the mark after 40 is one step further on. The text names the readout, which
counts the cards on their own marks out of 5, and puts no number of the drawn line in a sentence.

### 720 Find the step and place the number

The page shows a line with two labels next to each other and a goal line that names a number, such as "Put the point on
34, then press Check." The text says how to start. The reader reads the step from the two labels, then counts the jumps
from a label to the number. When the tick shows, every mark carries its label, and the reader sees the count that the
step made.

## Interactivity

### 310

- Shows: a line from 0 to 100 with a long mark and a label at every ten, 10 gaps, and no point.
- The reader: taps a gap, then taps another gap or the open one.
- The figure: under the line the tapped gap is drawn again as a line of its own, from its left ten to its right
  ten, at the width of the stage, with a labelled mark for each number from its left end to its right end. Two thin
  lines join its ends to the two tens on the line above. A tap on another gap opens that gap in place of the first.
  A tap on the open gap closes it.
- Action: tap a gap, and it opens below as a line of its own with its own left end and right end.

### 320

- Shows: a line from 30 to 60 in ones, 30 gaps, with long labelled marks at 30, 40, 50 and 60 and short marks
  between. The point is on 46. A plain bracket over the line, with no mark of its own between its ends, reaches from
  40 to 50, and the labels 40 and 50 are lit. The buttons Jump left and Jump right stand in the panel. The readouts
  read the point, the ten before and the ten after: 46, 40 and 50.
- The reader: drags the point along the line, and jumps it with Jump left and Jump right.
- The figure: the point snaps to the marks. The bracket follows the point and lights the ten before and the ten
  after it. It hides while the point is on a ten, and the readouts Ten before and Ten after read a dash. A jump
  across a ten moves the bracket to the next pair of tens. The jumps draw their arcs, and a drag clears them.
- Action: move the point, and a bracket over the line reaches from the ten before it to the ten after it.

### 330

- Shows: a drawn line of 6 gaps that counts in twos, fives or tens, as the field `draw` describes. Two long marks
  next to each other carry labels. The other five marks carry empty label boxes. Under the line a row of six cards
  stands in a drawn order. No point. The readout reads the cards placed, 0 of 5. The button New line stands in the
  panel.
- The reader: drags a card onto a mark with an empty box, and presses New line.
- The figure: a card dropped on its own mark stays there as that mark's label, and the readout counts it. A card
  dropped on any other mark, or off the line, slides back to the row. The card that fits no mark always slides back.
  When all five boxes hold their cards, the line reads in full and the one card is left in the row. New line draws
  another line with its labels, boxes and cards, and the readout returns to 0 of 5.
- Action: drag a card onto an empty label, and it stays where its number belongs. Draw a new line to fill again.

### 720

- Shows: a line of 10 gaps with a short mark at each end and at every step between, two long labelled marks next to each
  other, and the point on one of them. An end is a short mark while it carries no label. The goal line names the target.
  A Check button and an "Another one" button stand under the figure. No readout.
- The reader: drags the point and presses Check.
- The figure: the point snaps to the marks. Check shows the tick, or a note that says whether the point is to the
  left or to the right of the number. A drag after Check takes the tick or the note back. When the goal holds, every
  mark shows its label and the point shows its number.
- Action: read the step from two labels, move the point to a number, and commit.

## Challenge

Find the step and place the number, at page 720.

- Goal: the point is on the target when the reader presses Check.
- Drawn each time: a step from 2, 5 and 10, a left end from 0 to 50 that is a multiple of the step, and 10 gaps,
  with the right end at 100 or below. So a step of 10 starts at 0, a step of 5 at 0 to 50, and a step of 2 at 0 to
  50. Two marks next to each other carry labels. The target is another mark, at least three steps from both
  labelled marks. The point starts on one of the two labelled marks.
- Reached by: the drag of the point, which snaps to the marks, and Check. The reader reads the step from the two
  labels and counts the jumps from a label to the target. The line has no Jump left and Jump right buttons.
- Hidden: the number of the point, every label but the two drawn ones, and every readout.
- Wrong answer: "The point is to the left of the number." or "The point is to the right of the number."
- Hint: "Two labels next to each other give the step. Count the jumps from a label to the number."
- When the goal holds: every mark shows its label and the point shows its number. A drag of the point hides them
  again.
- Idea used: two labels give the step, and the step finds a number on a line that may start past zero. A reader who
  counts the marks in ones lands on the wrong mark and gets a note.

## Contract

brief's "Engine" part. The folder name without its number is the page's id.

### New in this section

| Scene | Field | What it draws and does |
|---|---|---|
| `line` | `draw` `new` | Values `true`, `false` (default). A line of label cards drawn at random, and a button New line that draws another. The page opens on a draw. Each draw takes a step from 2, 5 and 10, a left end that is a multiple of the step from the step up to 100 less 6 steps, and 6 gaps, so the right end is 100 or below and the line starts past zero. A step of 10 starts at 10 to 40, a step of 5 at 5 to 70, and a step of 2 at 2 to 88. Two marks next to each other carry labels, the second to the fifth mark with the one after it, so at least one blank lies on each side. The other five marks are blank. The cards are the five blanks and one number that fits no mark, the number after the right label or the number before the left label, picked at random. The six cards stand in a drawn order that is not from least to greatest. A draw resets the whole line: the step, the ends, the labels, the blanks, the cards and their order, and it takes every placed card back, so `placed` reads 0. There is no Check and no tick. A card on its own mark shows at once. Each line of 6 gaps has gaps about 59px wide on a 390px phone, and the six cards fit in one row. `draw` leaves out `from`, `to`, `step`, `labels`, `blank`, `cards`, `point`, `marks`, `second`, `flag`, `reading`, `jump`, `bracket`, `open`, `middle`, `find`, `estimate` and `challenge`, and takes `look: line` and `readout`. Its one link id is `placed`. |

- The goal `place-two-labels` keeps the right end at 100 or below, so a step of 10 starts at 0. The brief's
