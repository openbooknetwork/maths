# Numbers in order on a line

## Header

- Assumes: from the story `counting`, the reader says the numbers in order, counts on and counts back from any
  number, and knows the count to 100 in rows of ten. The reader has met no number line in this book.
- Teaches: the numbers of the count sit at equally spaced marks along a line from left to right, starting at 0, and
  a jump crosses one gap, so a jump to the right reaches the number after, a jump to the left the number before,
  the jumps from 0 to a number are as many as the number, and the number further to the right comes later in the
  count. On a line whose marks stand out of place the gaps differ, a jump still adds one while it covers more or
  less of the line, and the marks have to move until every gap is equal.
- Introduces:
  - Terms: number line, mark, label, point, gap, equal (gaps), jump, to the right and to the left, the number
    before and the number after, in order (said as "in the order of the count").
  - Controls: the + and − buttons, Jump right and Jump left, which jump the point one gap and draw an arc over it
    (page 110). The drag of the point, which snaps to the nearest mark (page 120). Two points with a flag each and
    a line of words under the line that says which comes later (page 140). The drag of a mark along the line, with
    a line of words under the line that says whether every gap is equal (page 150).
- Uses:
  - A row of 10 stepping stones numbered 1 to 10 with the frog on the bank. Four jumps take the frog to stone 4,
    and the jumps count back to the bank (page 110).
  - The same 10 stones over a number line from 0 to 10, the bank over 0. The reader drags the point from 0 to 6
    and back to 0 (page 120).
  - A number line from 0 to 20. Five jumps from 0 reach 5 and pass six marks. From 12 a jump to the right reaches
    13 and a jump to the left reaches 11 (page 130).
  - A number line from 0 to 20 with points on 13 and 3. The reader moves the point on 3 past 13 to 18, and puts
    both points on the same mark (page 140).
  - A number line from 0 to 10 whose mark 4 stands 0.4 of a gap too far right and whose mark 7 stands 0.4 of a gap
    too far left. The jump from 3 to 4 is long and the jump from 4 to 5 short. The reader drags both marks back
    until every gap is equal (page 150).

The section has five pages and no challenge. The pages 160 to 190 stay free.

As built: the buttons read "− Jump left" and "Jump right +", and the texts name them Jump left and Jump right, where
this plan writes − and +. Page 120 says in its text that the gaps of every number line are equal.

## Content

### 110 How far has the frog jumped?

A frog sits on the bank of a pond. A row of stepping stones crosses the water, and the stones carry the numbers 1
to 10 in the order of the count. The frog jumps from the bank to the first stone, then from stone to stone. The text
asks the reader to make the frog jump four times and to read the stone it lands on. The frog stands on stone 4 after
4 jumps. The number on the stone tells how many jumps the frog has made from the bank. The reader jumps the frog on
to stone 7 and checks it again, then jumps it back to the bank, one jump for each stone. The page ends on the
question the story answers. Can the numbers sit along a line in the same way, so that the place of a number tells
how far it is along the count?

### 120 Each number has its own mark

The stones now stand over a straight line. Under each stone the line has a short stroke across it, called a mark,
and the number under the mark is its label. The bank stands over the first mark, whose label is 0. A line like this
is a number line. The point is the dot on the line, and the frog moves with it. The page opens with the point on
0, the frog on the bank. The text asks the reader to drag the point to stone 6. The point stops on a mark and not
between two, and the frog lands on the stone above it. The piece of the line between two marks next to each other
is a gap. The stones are the same size and the same distance apart, so the gaps are equal. The numbers run in the
order of the count from left to right. 0 sits one gap before 1, just as the bank sits one jump before the first
stone.

### 130 A jump crosses one gap

The stones are gone, and the number line runs from 0 to 20. The point is on 0. A jump moves the point to the mark
next to it, and the arc over the line shows the gap it crosses. The text asks the reader to press + five times. The
point is on 5 after 5 jumps, so the jumps from 0 to a number are as many as the number. The text points out that
the marks from 0 to 5 are six, counting 0, and that the jumps are five. To find how far the point has gone, count
the jumps. Then the reader drags the point to 12, which clears the arcs. A jump to the right reaches 13, the number
after 12. A press of − takes that jump back, and one more press of − jumps to the left, to 11, the number before
12. From any mark, a jump to the right reaches the number after, and a jump to the left the number before.

### 140 The number further right comes later

The number line from 0 to 20 carries two points, a round one on 13 and a square one on 3, each with its number in a
flag. The words under the line say that 13 comes later in the count than 3. The point on 13 is further to the
right. From 0 it takes 13 jumps to the right to reach 13 and 3 jumps to reach 3, and the point that took more jumps
is further along. The text asks the reader to drag the point on 3 to the right, past 13, to 18. The words change
to say that 18 comes later than 13. Of two numbers, the one further to the right comes later in the count, and the
one further to the left comes earlier. Last, the reader puts both points on the same mark, and the words say that
both points are on that number. The numbers of the count lie in order along the line.

### 150 A number line needs equal gaps

The number line runs from 0 to 10, every mark with its label, and the point is on 0. The words under the line say
that some gaps are longer than others. Two marks stand out of place, and the reader finds which. The text asks
the reader to press Jump right until the point is on 10 and to look at the arcs. The jump from 3 to 4 is long and
the jump from 4 to 5 is short, and the reader finds the other two jumps next to each other that differ. Each jump still goes to the
number after and adds one, whatever its length. On this line, though, 4 looks closer to 5 than to 3, while it is
one from each in the count, so the place of a number no longer shows how far along the count it is. The text asks
the reader to drag each mark that is out of place until its two gaps are as long as the others. The reader judges
the gaps by eye, and the arcs move with the marks, so a long arc and a short arc side by side show a mark still out
of place. When the gaps are close to equal, the marks settle on their places and the words under the line say that every
gap is equal. Now every jump covers the same length of the line, and the place of each number shows how far along
the count it is. The page ends on the rule of page 120: on every number line the gaps between marks next to each
other are equal.

## Interactivity

### 110

- Shows: the bank of a pond on the left with a frog on it, and a row of 10 stepping stones to its right, the same
  size and the same distance apart, numbered 1 to 10. The bank carries no number. The + and − buttons stand in the
  panel under the figure, with a readout of the jumps, which reads 0.
- The reader: presses + or −.
- The figure: + makes the frog jump onto the next stone to the right, and an arc over the water shows the jump.
  − makes the frog jump one stone to the left. A − after a + takes the last arc away. The readout counts the jumps
  from the bank. At the bank − does nothing, and at stone 10 + does nothing.
- Action: press a button, and the frog jumps one stone forward or back, with the jumps from the start counted.

### 120

- Shows: the 10 stones of page 110 over a number line from 0 to 10. Each stone stands over the mark of its number,
  and the bank stands over the mark of 0. Every mark carries its label. The point sits on 0 and the frog on the
  bank above it. A readout shows the number at the point, 0.
- The reader: drags the point along the line.
- The figure: the point follows the finger and snaps to the nearest mark when the reader lets go. The frog moves
  with the point and stands on the stone over that mark, or on the bank at 0. The readout follows.
- Action: drag the point along the line, and it snaps to the nearest mark.

### 130

- Shows: a number line from 0 to 20 with every mark labelled, the point on 0, the + and − buttons, and readouts of
  the number at the point and of the jumps, both 0.
- The reader: presses + or −, and drags the point to a new mark.
- The figure: + jumps the point one gap to the right and draws an arc over that gap above the line. − jumps it one
  gap to the left and draws the arc under the line, or takes back the last jump when that jump went to the right.
  The jumps readout counts the arcs from the mark where the reader began to jump. A drag of the point clears the
  arcs and sets the jumps to 0, so the next jump starts from the new mark.
- Action: drag the point to a start, then jump it, and the arcs count the gaps crossed from that start.

### 140

- Shows: a number line from 0 to 20 with every mark labelled. A round point sits on 13 and a square point of
  another colour sits on 3, each with a flag above it that shows its number. Under the line a line of words reads
  "13 comes later in the count than 3".
- The reader: drags either point.
- The figure: the dragged point snaps to the nearest mark, its flag follows and shows its number, and the words
  under the line change to name the number that comes later. When both points are on the same mark, the words say
  that both points are on that number.
- Action: drag one of two points, and a sentence says which point's number comes later in the count.

### 150

- Shows: a number line from 0 to 10 with every mark labelled. The mark of 4 stands 0.4 of a gap to the right of its place, between the places of 4
  and 5, and the mark of 7 stands 0.4 of a gap left of its place, each with its label under it. Nothing marks
  these two as different from the rest. The point sits on 0. The buttons Jump left and Jump right stand in the
  panel, with readouts of the number at the point and of the jumps, both 0. Under the line a line of words reads
  "Some gaps are longer than others."
- The reader: presses Jump right and Jump left, and drags a mark by its stroke or its label along the line.
- The figure: the jumps work as on page 130, from mark to mark at the places where the marks stand, so an arc over
  a long gap is wider and higher than an arc over a short one. A dragged mark moves along the line with its label,
  between its two neighbours and not past them. The arcs over its two gaps stretch and shrink with it, and the
  point moves with it when the point is on it. The mark stays where the reader lets go. When every mark between the
  ends lies within a tenth of a gap of its place, all the marks settle on their places, the arcs come out the same
  size, and the words read "Every gap is equal." A drag that takes a mark out of place again brings back "Some gaps
  are longer than others."
- Action: drag a mark along the line, and the two gaps on either side of it grow and shrink.

## Challenge

The brief gives this section no challenge. The section's controls, the drag and the jumps, return in the challenge
`place` at 710 of section `steps`.

## Contract

The section uses the one scene of the family `line`, which a page names as `scene: line`. The fields and link ids
are those of the brief's "Engine" part. The folder name without its number is the page's id.

It uses from the build order's first step: the line, the marks, the labels, the point and its drag, `second`,
`flag`, `reading`, `jump`, and the looks `stones` and `stones-on-line`. Page 150 needs one field and one link id
beyond the brief, `moved` and `gaps`, both `new`, which join the first step of the build order.

### New in this section

| Field | Values | Draws and does |
|---|---|---|
| `moved` `new` | a list of `{ mark, by }`, where `mark` is a number of the line and `by` a part of a gap from −0.6 to 0.6 | Each listed mark starts `by` gaps off its place, to the right for a positive `by` and to the left for a negative one, with its label under it, drawn like every other mark. Every mark between the two ends takes a drag, and the ends stay where they are. A dragged mark moves freely along the line with its label and stays a fifth of a gap from each neighbour where that neighbour stands, so the marks keep their order. It stays where the reader lets go. While some mark between the ends lies more than a tenth of a gap from its place, the words under the line read "Some gaps are longer than others." When every one lies within a tenth of a gap of its place, all the marks settle on their places and the words read "Every gap is equal." A drag that takes a mark out of place again brings the first words back. The point snaps to the marks where they stand and moves with a mark it sits on. The arcs of `jump` run between the marks where they stand and follow a dragged mark (150). |

| Link id | Reads |
|---|---|
| `gaps` `new` | The words under the line on a page with `moved`, "Some gaps are longer than others." or "Every gap is equal." `readout` leaves it aside, as it leaves `reading`. |

Fields that go with `moved`:
- `moved` takes `look: line`, a line from 0 with a step of 1, every mark labelled, and `to` from 4 to 12, so a
  gap stays at least 17px wide on a 390px stage. It takes `point`, `jump` and `readout`.
- A page with `moved` leaves out `second`, `flag`, `reading`, `step` as a segment, `labels` other than `all`,
  `blank`, `cards`, `bracket`, `open`, `middle`, `find`, `estimate` and `challenge`.
- Each listed mark lies between the ends, no two listed marks are next to each other, and each `by` is at least
  0.3 and at most 0.6 in size, so the page opens with a gap that looks wrong. A page whose marks all start within
  a tenth of a gap of their places fails `kitap check`.

By touch, a mark is grabbed by its stroke or its label, in a band under the line as wide as the stretch halfway to
each neighbour and at least 44px tall, and the point is grabbed by its dot on the line. On 0 to 10 at 390px each
band is about 35px wide. By keyboard, each mark between the ends takes the focus after the point and the jump
buttons, from left to right, as a slider named "Mark 4", and the Left and Right arrow keys move it a tenth of a
gap. Its value text compares its two gaps, "The gap on its left is longer", "The gap on its right is longer" or
"Its two gaps are equal", so a reader who does not see the figure has what the eye gives. The value text gives no
number of gaps or of pixels.

The page opens with `gaps` at "Some gaps are longer than others.", `point` at 0 and `jumps` at 0.

Two readings of the brief that the engine author needs:

- `readout` on the looks `stones` and `stones-on-line`. The brief lists `to`, `point` and `jump` as the fields those
  looks take. This plan reads that list as the line fields they take, and gives pages 110 and 120 a `readout` as
  well. The readout `jumps` on 110 and `point` on 120 read as they do on the line.
- The look `stones` draws the jumps of `jump` as arcs over the water from stone to stone, and the bank as the
  point's place at 0, with no label.

Words the scene needs in `i18n/en.yaml` for this section: the button names "Jump left" and "Jump right", the
readout labels for `point` and `jumps`, and the sentences of `reading`, "<a> comes later in the count than <b>" and
"Both points are on <a>". For page 150: the words of `gaps`, "Some gaps are longer than others." and "Every gap is
equal.", the mark's name "Mark <n>", and its value texts "The gap on its left is longer", "The gap on its right is
longer" and "Its two gaps are equal".
