# Estimating on an unmarked line

## Header

- Assumes: from section `in-order`, the reader knows the number line, the mark, the label, the point and its drag,
  the gap and equal gaps. From section `part-of-the-line`, the reader knows the two ends of a line, a line that
  starts past zero, and the bracket from the ten before the point to the ten after it. From section `placing`, the
  reader knows the middle, that the middle of a line from 0 to 100 is 50, the Find readout, the Show button that
  shows where the number is, the Next button and the Middle button. From challenge 710, the reader knows Check.
- Teaches: a line with no marks between its ends still has a place for each number, and an estimate finds that place,
  or the number at a point, by judging the distance from the ends and from the middle, which is marked first, with
  the place halfway between the middle and an end as a further guide.
- Introduces:
  - Terms: unmarked line, estimate (as a noun and as a verb), estimate mark.
  - Controls: the free drag of the point on an unmarked line, which stops anywhere along it, and Show on that line,
    which draws the flag where the number is and leaves the point without a number (page 510). The Estimate
    stepper, whose number Show draws as the estimate mark, a pale dashed mark, beside the point (page 530). The look
    `book`, with a bookmark as the point and the button "Open the book" in place of Show (page 620).
- Uses:
  - An unmarked line from 0 to 10. The numbers to place are 8, 2 and 4 (page 510).
  - An unmarked line from 0 to 100 with the Middle button. The reader puts the point where the middle seems to be
    and presses Middle to see 50. The numbers to place are 60, 44 and 78. The place of 78 is judged from 75, halfway
    between the middle and the right end (page 520).
  - An unmarked line from 10 to 20 with the point on 16 and the Middle button, which draws 15. The Estimate stepper
    runs from 10 to 20 and starts at 15, the middle (page 530).
  - The edge of a closed book of 100 pages, with the front cover at 0 and page 100 at the back. The bookmark goes to
    page 75, then 25, then 90 (page 620).
  - The challenge draws an unmarked line with a left end from 0 to 30 and a right end from 40 to 100, both tens and
    at least 40 apart, and a target that is at least a tenth of the line's length from each end and from the
    middle (page 730).
  - A line from 0 to 100 in ones labelled at the tens, with the point on 64 and the bracket from 60 to 70
    (page 810).

The section has three teaching pages, the world page 620, the challenge 730 and the end page of the story 810. The
pages 540 to 590 stay free.

## Content

### 510 Estimate where a number is

The page takes the marks away. A line from 0 to 10 has a long mark with its label at each end and no mark between,
and the page calls it an unmarked line. The numbers 1 to 9 have no marks, but each still has its place, the place
its mark would have on a line with ten equal gaps. To estimate is to judge that place from how far it is from the
ends. The Find readout names 8. Eight is much nearer 10 than 0, so its place leaves a short piece of line up to 10.
The reader drags the point there. The point moves freely and stops wherever the reader lets go, also between two
numbers, so it carries no number. Show then puts a flag where 8 is. The reader compares the point with the flag. A
point a short way from the flag is a close estimate, and a point far from it is not. An estimate does not have to be
exact. It has to be close. Next asks for 2, which leaves the same short piece from 0 that 8 leaves to
10. Next asks for 4, which is harder because it is far from both ends. Four is nearer 0 than 10, but only a little,
so its place is a little left of the middle of the line.

Example: on the line from 0 to 10, 8 sits near 10, 2 sits near 0, and 4 sits a little left of the middle.

### 520 Mark the middle first

The line now runs from 0 to 100 and has no marks between its ends. The Find readout names 60. Counting on from 0 by
eye does not work here. A reader who counts small steps from 0 makes the first steps too wide, runs out of room
before 100, and squeezes the large numbers together near the right end. The page gives a better start, which is to
mark the middle first. The text asks the reader to drag the point to where the middle seems to be, then press
Middle. A long mark with the label 50 appears halfway between the ends, and the reader sees how near the point came
to it. Now 60 is easy to place. It comes just after 50, so its place is just past the middle. The reader drags the
point there and presses Show, then compares the point with the flag of 60. Next asks for 44, just before the
middle. Next asks for 78, which lies between the middle and 100. The page shows a second guide for it. The right
half of the line, from 50 to 100, has a middle of its own, halfway between the middle of the line and the right end,
and that place is 75. The reader judges it by eye, halfway along the right half, and puts 78 just past it. The same
works on the left half, where 25 is halfway between 0 and the middle. The page ends on the rule. On an unmarked line,
mark the middle first, then judge the distance from the middle and from the nearer end. Halfway between the middle
and an end is a guide too.

Example: 60 is just past the middle 50, and 44 is just before it. 75 is halfway between 50 and 100, and 78 is just
past 75.

### 530 From a point to its number

The page turns the question round. The point already sits on the line, and the reader estimates its number. The line
runs from 10 to 20, so its ends are not 0 and 100, and its middle is halfway between 10 and 20. The reader first
judges, then sets, then shows. The text asks the reader to judge whether the point is before or after the middle,
and whether it is near an end. Then the reader presses Middle. The long mark of 15 appears, and the point is just
past it, so the number at the point is just over 15. The Estimate stepper starts at 15, the middle, and the reader
steps from that guide to the estimate. Nothing on the line moves while the reader steps. Show then draws the
estimate mark, a pale dashed mark, at the place of the estimate, and writes the number at the point on the point,
16. The reader sees how far the estimate mark stands from the point. When the estimate mark is to the left of the
point, the number at the point comes later in the count than the estimate. When it is to the right, the number at
the point comes earlier. When it stands on the point, the estimate is the number. The page ends on the rule. The ends
and the middle work in both directions, from a number to its place and from a place to its number, whatever the two
ends are.

Example: on the line from 10 to 20 the middle is 15. The point at 16 is just past the middle, and an estimate of 16
puts the estimate mark on the point.

### 620 A bookmark at page 75

The world page. A closed book of 100 pages shows only its edge, a line with no marks. The pages are all there, in
order from the front to the back. The front cover is 0, as the bank was 0 for the stepping stones, and page 100 is at
the back. A reader who wants page 75 does not count the pages one by one. Page 50 is in the middle of the edge, and
75 is halfway between the middle and the back, the guide of page 520. The reader finds the middle by eye, since the
book has no Middle button, and slides the bookmark halfway from there to the back. "Open the book" puts a flag where
page 75 is, and the reader compares the bookmark with the flag to see how close it came. Next asks for page 25,
halfway between the front and the middle, and then page 90, near the back.

Example: page 75 is halfway between the middle and the back, page 25 is halfway between the front and the middle,
and page 90 is close to the back.

### 730 Place a number on an unmarked line

The challenge. The page says that the line has no marks between its two ends and that the point shows no number.
The goal line names a number. The reader presses Middle to see the middle, decides whether the number comes before
or after it, judges how far it is from the middle and from the nearer end, drags the point there and presses Check.
The page says that an estimate counts when it is close, within a small piece of the line. When it is close
enough, the goal line says so and a flag shows where the number is, so the reader sees how far the point stands from
it.

### 810 What comes after the number line

The end page of the story. It sums the story up in the order it was taught. Each number of the count sits at a mark,
the marks have equal gaps, and a jump crosses one gap. The labels give the step, a line can start past zero, and a
number sits between the ten before it and the ten after it. A few labels, the ends and the middle, are enough to
place a number, and with no marks at all an estimate still finds its place. The page then names the stories that
follow and what each does with the line. `centimetres-and-metres` reads the line as lengths from 0, the way a ruler
does. `fractions-on-a-line` opens the gap from 0 to 1 and finds numbers inside it. `rounding-and-estimating` takes
the ten before and the ten after and asks which is nearer. `bar-charts` stands the line up beside the bars and
reads their heights from it. The figure is the line from 0 to 100 labelled at the tens with the bracket, the line
`rounding-and-estimating` starts from.

Example: the point on 64, between the ten before, 60, and the ten after, 70. The text links the number at the point
and names the two readouts of the tens in words, without links.

## Interactivity

### 510 Estimate where a number is

- Shows: a line from 0 to 10 with a long mark and its label at each end and no mark between. The point sits on 0.
  The Find readout shows 8, and the Show button stands in the panel.
- The reader: drags the point along the line, presses Show, and then Next.
- The figure: the point follows the finger along the line and stops wherever it is let go between the ends. It
  carries no number. Show puts a flag at the place of the number to find, and the point stays without a number. A
  move of the point takes the flag away. Next gives 2 and then 4 in the Find readout and clears the reveal.
- Action: drag a point to any place on a line with no marks, and Show puts a flag where the number is, so the
  reader sees how far the point stands from it.

### 520 Mark the middle first

- Shows: a line from 0 to 100 with a long mark and its label at each end and no mark between. The point sits on 0.
  The Find readout shows 60. The Middle button and the Show button stand in the panel.
- The reader: drags the point to where the middle seems to be, presses Middle, then drags the point to 60 and
  presses Show and Next.
- The figure: a press of Middle draws the long mark of 50 with its label, halfway between the ends, and a second
  press takes it away. The point, Show and Next behave as on page 510. Next gives 44 and then 78.
- Action: judge the middle of a line with no marks by eye, check it with the button, and place numbers from the
  middle.

### 530 From a point to its number

- Shows: a line from 10 to 20 with a long mark and its label at each end and no mark between. The point sits at the
  place of 16 and carries no number. The Estimate stepper, at 15, the Middle button and the Show button stand in the
  panel. No estimate mark is drawn.
- The reader: presses Middle, taps + and − on the Estimate stepper to set an estimate, and presses Show.
- The figure: the point stays where it is. A tap on the stepper changes its number and draws nothing on the line.
  Middle draws the long mark of 15 and takes it away, as on page 520. Show draws the estimate mark, a pale dashed
  mark, at the place of the estimate, and writes the number at the point on the point, 16. A tap on the stepper
  takes both away.
- Action: set a number for a fixed point, and Show draws its place beside the point, so the reader sees how far the
  estimate is and whether the number at the point comes earlier or later.

### 730 Place a number on an unmarked line

- Shows: an unmarked line with its two drawn ends labelled, the point at the left end with no number, and the goal
  line naming the target. The Middle button, Check and "Another one" stand in the panel. No readout, no Find
  readout and no Show button.
- The reader: presses Middle, drags the point, and presses Check.
- The figure: the point moves freely. Middle draws and takes away the long mark of the middle with its label. Check

  the drawn target, a flag shows where the number is, and the point stays without a number.
- Action: place a drawn number on a line with no marks between drawn ends, and commit.

### 810 What comes after the number line

## Challenge

Place a number on an unmarked line, at page 730.

- Goal: the point is close enough to the target when the reader presses Check, within a tenth of the line's
  length. On a line from 0 to 100 that is 10 either side, and on a line from 20 to 60 it is 4 either side. The two
  ends are 40, 60, 80 or 100 apart, so the middle is a ten. It was a twentieth and any two tens 40 apart or more,
  which the user found impossible to do by eye.
- Drawn each time: a left end from 0 to 30 and a right end from 40 to 100, both tens and at least 40 apart. The
  middle of two tens is a whole number. The target is a whole number at least a tenth of the length from each end
  and from the middle, so neither an end nor the middle gives it away. The point starts at the left end.
- Reached by: the free drag of page 510, the Middle button of `placing`, and Check. The reader finds the middle,
  sees on which side of it the target lies, and judges the distance from the middle and from the nearer end.
- Hidden: the number at the point, Show and the flag, the Find readout and every other readout. The goal line names
  the target.
- Wrong answer: "The point is to the left of the number." or "The point is to the right of the number." Every goal
  of the story answers Check this way.
- Hint: "Press Middle. Is the number before or after the middle? Then judge how far it is from the middle and from
  the nearer end."
- When the goal holds: the goal line says "The point is close to 34.", with the drawn target, and a flag shows where
  the number is. The point stays without a number, so the reader compares it with the flag. A drag of the point
  hides the flag and the tick again.
- Idea used: the ends of a line that may start past zero, from `part-of-the-line`. The middle as a guide, from
  `placing`. The estimate on an unmarked line, from this section. It is the story's last challenge and combines the
  three.
