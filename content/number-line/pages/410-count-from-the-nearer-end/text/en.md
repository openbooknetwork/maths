When only the two ends of a line have labels, you find any other number by counting the gaps from one of the ends. Either end works, but one of them is nearer to the number. Count from that one, because there are fewer gaps to count and fewer chances to lose your place.

Take a line from 0 to 20 with a mark for every number and labels at 0 and 20 only. To find 14 from 0, you would count fourteen jumps to the right. From 20 you count back instead: 19, 18, 17, 16, 15, 14. That is only six jumps to the left, so 20 is the nearer end for 14.

The figure has that line, and the point starts on 0. The Find readout shows the number to find, [3](@find).

- 3 is close to 0, so move the point three gaps to the right, by dragging it or with "Jump right". Then press Show. A flag appears where the number is, and the point shows the number it is on.
- Press "Next number" to get 19, which comes just before 20. Drag the point to 20, press "Jump left" once, and press Show.
- "Next number" then gives 14. Count back from 20 for that one.
