The middle of a line is the place just as far from one end as from the other, with as many gaps on each side. On a line from 0 to 100, the count from 0 to 50 is five tens, and the count from 50 to 100 is five tens more, so the middle is 50. The middle and the two ends give you three places to count from, and you count from the one nearest the number.

Take 48 on a line from 0 to 100 with a mark for every number and labels only at the ends. From 0 it is 48 jumps, and from 100 it is 52. Both are long counts. But 48 comes just two numbers before 50, so from the middle you count back two jumps, to 49 and then 48.

The figure has the same line, with the point on 0. The Find readout asks for [48](@find).

- Press Middle, and a long mark with the label 50 appears halfway between the ends.
- Drag the point onto that mark, press "Jump left" twice, and then press Show.
- Press "Next number" to get 53, and then 96. Count each one from whichever of 0, 50 and 100 is nearest to it.
