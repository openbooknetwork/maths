When you compare two numbers, the one that comes later in the count is greater, and the other one is less. On a number line, the greater number is further to the right. That is because you reach a number from 0 in as many jumps as the number says. A greater number takes more jumps to reach, and every jump to the right takes you further along the line.

Take 13 and 3. From 0 you need 13 jumps to the right to reach 13, and only 3 jumps to reach 3. So the mark for 13 is further to the right, and 13 is greater than 3. It works the other way round too: 3 is further to the left, so 3 is less than 13.

The number line runs from 0 to 20. The round point starts on 13 and the square point on 3, and each shows its number in a flag. The words under the line compare the two numbers, and now they read [13 is greater than 3](@reading).

- Drag the square point to the right until it is on 18. Once it is past 13, the words name its number as the greater one.
- Drag one point onto the other point's mark, and read what the words say then.
