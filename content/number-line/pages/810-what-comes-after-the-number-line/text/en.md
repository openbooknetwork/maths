This story has shown you how numbers are laid out in order along a line.

- Each number has its own mark, the gaps between the marks are equal, and a jump crosses one gap.
- Two labels next to each other tell you the step, and a line can start past zero.
- A number that is not a ten is between the ten before it and the ten after it.
- The ends and the middle are enough to place a number. Even on an unmarked number line, you can estimate where a number goes.

The point is now on [64](@point). It starts between 60 and 70, so the bracket reaches from 60 to 70. Drag the point, and the bracket and the "Ten before" and "Ten after" readouts follow it. When the point is on a ten, the bracket goes away.

These stories come next:

- **Centimetres and metres** reads the line as lengths from 0, the way a ruler does.
- **Fractions on a line** opens up the gap from 0 to 1 and finds numbers inside it.
- **Rounding and estimating** takes the ten before and the ten after, and asks which one is nearer.
- **Bar charts** stands the line upright and reads the heights of bars from it.
