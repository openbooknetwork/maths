A number line is a straight line with the numbers of the count along it, from left to right. Each number has its own mark, a short line drawn across the number line, and the number written under the mark is its label. The piece of the line between two marks next to each other is a gap, and all the gaps are equal, which means they have the same length.

The line starts at 0, one gap before 1. To see why, put the frog's stones over a number line, each stone over the mark for its number. The frog has made no jumps while it is on the bank, so the bank is over the mark for 0. One jump takes the frog to stone 1, one gap along, and six jumps take it to stone 6, six gaps along. The stones are all the same distance apart, and that is why the gaps are equal too.

The dot on the line is the point, and the frog moves with it. The point starts on 0.

- Drag the point to the mark under stone 6, and watch the frog. It stays above the point, so the number on its stone is the same as the label under the point.
- Let go between two marks, and the point moves to the nearest one.
