On an unmarked number line you can also go from a point to its number. To estimate the number at a point, first decide whether the point is before or after the middle of the line. Then judge how far it is from the middle and from the nearer end.

Say a line runs from 10 to 20. Its middle is 15, because 15 is five more than 10 and five less than 20. Now picture a point very close to the right end. It is past 15 and just short of 20, so its number is about 19.

The point on the figure stays put and shows no number.

- Press Middle to see where 15 is.
- Set your estimate with − and + on the Estimate stepper, which now shows [15](@estimate).
- Press Show. A pale dashed mark, the estimate mark, appears at your estimate, and the point shows its number. If you change your estimate, both disappear.

Numbers further to the right are greater. So if the estimate mark is to the left of the point, the number at the point is greater than your estimate. If it is to the right, that number is less. If it is on the point, your estimate is the number at the point.
