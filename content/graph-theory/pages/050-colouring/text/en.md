Think of each vertex as a country on a map, with an edge between two countries that share a border. Neighbours get different colours, so the map is easy to read.

Pick a colour under the graph and tap a vertex to paint it. When an edge joins two vertices of the same colour, the edge turns dashed and both vertices shake. Paint every vertex with no clash. The number of blank vertices is [7](@uncoloured).

Try it with three colours first. [Vertex A](@A) touches every vertex of the ring B, C, D, E, F. The ring has five vertices, an odd number, so two colours cannot take turns round it. The ring needs three colours and A needs a fourth. This graph needs [4](@chromatic) colours.

In 1976 two mathematicians proved that four colours are enough for any map drawn on a flat sheet. Theirs was the first famous proof that needed a computer to check all its cases.
