Long before rulers had marks, Greek geometers built figures with two tools. A straightedge draws a straight line through two points. A compass draws a circle about one point through another. Neither tool measures a length.

Pick the [ruler](@ruler), tap [A](@A) and then [B](@B). You can also press on A and drag to B. The line runs on past both points to the edge.

Pick the [compass](@compass), tap A for the centre and B for a point on the circle. The circle passes through B, so its radius is the length AB.

Where two objects cross, a small ring shows a new point. The ruler and the compass can start from it, and the [point](@point) tool marks it with a letter. Mark a point where your line meets your circle.

The step counter reads [0](@steps) so far, and the figure holds [0](@objects) lines and circles. Undo takes back the last step. Hint draws the next step of one way to the goal faintly, for you to draw over.
