Euclid's Elements, written about 2,300 years ago, opens with this construction. Given the segment [AB](@AB), build a triangle whose three sides all have the length AB.

A circle about A through B holds every point that lies as far from A as B does. A circle about B through A holds every point that lies as far from B as A does.

Draw both circles. Where they cross, a point lies at the length AB from A and from B. Join that point to A and to B with the ruler.

The goal needs [4](@fewest) steps, and the counter shows [0](@steps) so far. The circles cross below AB too, and a second triangle waits there.
