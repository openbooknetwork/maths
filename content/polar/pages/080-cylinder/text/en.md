Polar coordinates work in space too. The simplest way keeps the polar plane as the floor and adds a height.

Find the spot on the floor with r = [3](@r) and θ = [60°](@angle), then go up by z = [3](@z). The three numbers (r, θ, z) are cylindrical coordinates.

On the floor nothing has changed, so x and y come from the same two formulas:

[x = r cos θ = 3 cos 60° = 1.5](@formula-x)

[y = r sin θ = 3 sin 60° ≈ 2.6](@formula-y)

Keep r still and change the other two. The point stays on the wall of a cylinder. Drag the figure to turn the view.
