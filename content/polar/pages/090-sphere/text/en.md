The other way uses one distance and two angles. ρ is the straight distance from the origin to the point. Here it is [4](@rho).

θ turns round the floor as before, and here it is [110°](@angle). The new angle φ tilts down from the z axis, and here it is [60°](@angle-phi). At φ = 0° the point is straight up, and at 90° it lies on the floor.

The three numbers (ρ, θ, φ) are spherical coordinates. The height comes from φ alone:

[z = ρ cos φ = 4 cos 60° = 2](@formula-z)

The shadow of the point on the floor is ρ sin φ from the origin, and θ splits that into x and y:

[x = ρ sin φ cos θ = 4 sin 60° cos 110° ≈ −1.18](@formula-x)

[y = ρ sin φ sin θ = 4 sin 60° sin 110° ≈ 3.26](@formula-y)

Keep ρ still and change the two angles. The point stays on a sphere. Places on Earth are given this way, by two angles.
