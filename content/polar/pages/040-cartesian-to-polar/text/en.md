Now go the other way. The point is at x = [4](@x) and y = [3](@y).

Its distance from the origin is the long side of the right triangle, so Pythagoras gives r:

[r = √(x² + y²) = √(4² + 3²) = √25 = 5](@formula-r)

The angle comes from the two short sides, since tan θ = y / x. Here θ is [36.9°](@angle).

Two directions share each value of y / x, one opposite the other. The signs of x and y say which one the point is in.
