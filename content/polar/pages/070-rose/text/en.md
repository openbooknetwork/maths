Let r swing back and forth as θ grows, and the curve folds into petals:

[r = 4 cos(k θ) = 4 cos(3 × 40°) = −2](@formula-r)

Sweep the angle from 0° to 360°. When the cosine turns negative, so does r. A negative r tells you to face the direction θ and then walk backwards through the origin. The dashed line shows the direction the angle points in.

With k = [3](@k) the rose has 3 petals. An odd k gives k petals, and an even k gives twice as many.
