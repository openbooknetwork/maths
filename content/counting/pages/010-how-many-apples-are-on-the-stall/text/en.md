A seller wants to know how many apples are on the stall, so you count them. You point to one apple at a time and give it the next number: 1, 2, 3 and so on. Each apple gets exactly one number. The last number you say tells you how many apples there are.

This works because the numbers go up by one for each apple you count. So the number you say for an apple tells you how many apples you have counted so far, that one included. When the last apple gets its number, you have counted all of them, so that number is how many there are.

Take three apples. You point to the first one and say 1, to the next one and say 2, and to the last one and say 3. Every apple has a number, and the last one you said is 3, so there are three apples.

Tap an apple to give it the next number, and the number appears on it. If you tap it again, it keeps its number. The "Last number said" readout now shows [0](@count). The "How many" readout shows a question mark until every apple has a number, and then it shows how many there are.
