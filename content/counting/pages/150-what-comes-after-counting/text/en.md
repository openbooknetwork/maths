This story has shown you how to count, and why counting tells you how many. You give each object one number, and the last number you say is how many there are. You get the same number whatever order you count in. You can count on from a number you already know, and count back as objects leave.

The apples from "How many apples are on the stall?" are back, and each one already has its number. The last number is 8, so there are eight apples.

Counting is where many other ideas start:

- In "More and less", you pair up two groups to see which has more.
- In "The number line", you put the numbers in order along a line.
- In "Number bonds", you split a number into two parts.
- In "Adding and subtracting within 20", you join groups and take them away.
- In "Tens and ones", you read 54 as 5 tens and 4 ones.
- In "Skip counting", you count in twos, fives or tens.
- When you cut a group in half, you count each half to see whether they are the same.
- On a calendar, you count the days until a birthday.
- In a chart, you count how many there are of each kind.
