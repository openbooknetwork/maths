A reflection flips the plane over a line, the mirror. Each point jumps straight across the line and lands as far on the other side as it started.

The mirror starts out as the y-axis. It makes an angle of [90°](@tilt) with the x-axis, and P = [(1, 1)](@P) lands on P′ = [(−1, 1)](@image). The dashed lines join points to their images, and each one meets the mirror at a right angle.

Look at the F and its image. The image reads backwards. A reflection turns a shape over, which no slide or turn can do.

Drag either end of the mirror to tilt it. At 45° the mirror is the line y = x, which runs through P. A point on the mirror stays where it is.
