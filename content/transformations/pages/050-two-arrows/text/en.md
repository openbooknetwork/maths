So far each move had its own rule. One idea covers many of them. Think of two short arrows. The arrow i runs from (0, 0) to (1, 0), and the arrow j runs from (0, 0) to (0, 1).

Drag the tips of i and j. The whole grid follows them. Its lines stay straight, lines that were parallel stay parallel, and (0, 0) stays put. A move like that is a linear transformation.

The two arrows decide where every point goes. P = [(2, 1)](@P) means 2 steps along i and then 1 step along j. Move the arrows and P′ still takes 2 steps along the new i and 1 along the new j:

[A P = 2 × (1, 0.5) + 1 × (−0.5, 1) = (1.5, 2)](@formula-image)

So we write where i and j land side by side and call it the matrix A. Its first column is i = [(1, 0.5)](@i), its second is j = [(−0.5, 1)](@j). These four numbers say where every point of the plane goes.
